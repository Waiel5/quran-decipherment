**Key intellectual ancestors:**
- **Burhān al-Dīn al-Biqāʿī** (d. 1480), *Naẓm al-Durar* — first systematic coherence/ring commentary. Farrin and Cuypers credit him.
- **Al-Zarkashī** (d. 1392), *al-Burhān*, nawʿ 52 — defined *al-mutashābih al-lafẓī*. Our `mutashabih-lafzi` agent operationalized his 14th-century claim.
- **Al-Suyūṭī** (d. 1505), *al-Itqān* — encyclopedic. Nawʿ 9 diagnosed verse-length-by-period 500 years before Sadeghi 2011. Nawʿ 58 catalogued iltifāt.
- **Al-Kirmānī** (d. 1106), *Asrār al-Tikrār* — 1,100+ pair catalog for mutashabih.
- **Ibn al-Muʿtazz** (d. 908), *Kitāb al-Badīʿ* — founded *radd al-ʿajuz ʿalā al-ṣadr*, the chiasmus category our agents rediscovered at Q 13:28.

**Recalibration (from classical-balagha audit):** Of 42+ major findings, 17 are classically catalogued under a known name (novelty is application to specific verses), 14 are implicit in classical tradition but never quantified, 11 are genuinely novel with no classical category — those live in numerology, Zipf/info-theory, chronological metrics, Bonferroni-corrected chiasmus and graph analysis.

**Primary-source archive:** 453 MB at `data/literature/` — citation chain spans 7 centuries (al-Zarkashī 14th c. → al-Biqāʿī 15th c. → al-Suyūṭī 16th c. → Khalifa 1982 → Philips 1987 → Witztum-Rips-Rosenberg 1994 → McKay 1999 → Cuypers 2015 → Farrin 2014 → Sinai 2017 → this project 2026).

**The project's core contribution in one sentence:** Classical scholarship has the full conceptual vocabulary (munāsabat, ring, tarṣīʿ, jinās, tikrār, radd al-ʿajuz, sajʿ) but lacks the computational tooling. This project is what happens when 1,400 years of classical categories meet 20th-century concordance infrastructure.
