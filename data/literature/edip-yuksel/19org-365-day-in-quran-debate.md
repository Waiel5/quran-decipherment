# 365 Day in the Quran (1996 debate)

**Source:** https://19.org/blog/365days/
**Authors:** Edip Yüksel (and AbdulraHman Lomax)
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

This lengthy debate from 1996 examines claims about mathematical patterns in the Qur'an, specifically focusing on the word "yawm" (day).

## Key Claims

**Original assertion:** The Arabic word for "day" appears exactly 365 times in the Qur'an, matching the solar year's day count.

**Supporting numerical claims:**
- Plural/dual forms of "day" appear 30 times (matching average monthly days)
- All forms combined total 475 occurrences (divisible by 19)

## The Debate

Daniel Lomax initially challenged these counts, conducting a meticulous concordance analysis. He identified what he considered "counting inconsistencies" in Yuksel's methodology, particularly regarding:

- Forms with suffixed pronouns ("their day," "your day")
- The phrase "yawma-idh" (the day when)
- Various grammatical variations

**Resolution:** After detailed analysis, both parties eventually acknowledged discrepancies stemmed from differing definitions of what constitutes the singular "day." Lomax conceded that excluding certain grammatical forms (pronouns, specific phrases) does yield approximately 365 occurrences.

## Methodology Dispute

The fundamental disagreement centered on which word forms should be counted. Yuksel maintained a consistent method excluding forms with attached pronouns, while Lomax initially counted all singular variations, only adjusting his approach after recognizing the unstated counting rules.
