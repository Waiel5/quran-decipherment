# Discussion of numerical miracles in the Qur'an and use of the solar calendar

**Source:** https://islamqa.info/en/answers/69741
**Publisher:** Islam Question & Answer (mainstream Sunni)
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

This Islam Q&A response addresses claims about numerical patterns in the Qur'an, specifically the assertion that the word "yawm" (day) appears 365 times.

## Main Arguments

**On Numerical Miracles:**
The fatwa argues that "numerical miracles" as presented in popular literature are not authentic. Scholars like Dr. Ashraf 'Abd al-Razzaaq Qatanah concluded these claims use "selective conditions" to reach predetermined conclusions, sometimes contradicting established principles of Arabic linguistics and Qur'anic orthography.

The response notes that researchers "ignored words" to reach specific numbers. For example, they counted only certain forms while excluding grammatical variations.

**On Calendar Systems:**
The fatwa emphasizes that Islamic timekeeping is fundamentally lunar-based, not solar. Multiple Qur'anic verses establish the moon and new moons as the basis for reckoning time, not the sun. The response references Qur'an 10:5 and 2:189 to support this position.

Scholars cited include Ibn al-Qayyim, al-Qurtubi, and Ibn 'Uthaymeen, who argue the lunar calendar is more precise and accessible than solar systems inherited from non-Islamic traditions.

## Conclusion

The fatwa rejects both the numerical miracle claims and the implication that Qur'anic patterns validate solar calendars.
