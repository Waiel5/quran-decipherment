# On the Qur'an's "Numerical Miracles" (J. Luis Dizon, Pros Aletheian)

**Source:** https://prosaletheian.wordpress.com/2022/02/07/on-the-qurans-numerical-miracles/
**Author:** J. Luis Dizon
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

## Core Argument

The article argues that claims about mathematical patterns in the Qur'an represent a logical fallacy rather than evidence of divine origin. The author identifies this as "The Texas Sharpshooter Fallacy"—selectively finding patterns after the fact to support a predetermined conclusion.

## Key Criticisms

**Methodological Issues:**
- No fixed criteria exist for what constitutes a "numerical miracle," making the enterprise entirely subjective
- Proponents cherry-pick data, selecting certain word forms while excluding others based on arbitrary rules
- The word "day" (يوم) illustrates this: while occurring 475 times total, miracle-claimers cite only 365 instances by excluding plurals and selectively removing words with certain suffixes

**Historical Problems:**
- Such miracles were unknown to Muslims before the 20th century
- They require textual manipulation or data alteration
- Originators (like Rashad Khalifa) eventually adopted heterodox Islamic positions

**Scholarly Rejection:**
Conservative Muslim scholars reject numerical miracle arguments. Bilal Philips authored an entire book refuting them: *The Qur'an's Numerical Miracle: Hoax and Heresy*.

## Comparative Analysis

The article notes identical arguments appear supporting biblical numerology, yet these lack persuasive force for Christianity's truth claims, suggesting such patterns prove nothing about religious authenticity.
