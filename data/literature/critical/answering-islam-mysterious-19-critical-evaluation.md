# The Mysterious 19 in the Qur'an: A Critical Evaluation

**Source:** https://answering-islam.org/Nehls/Ask/number19.html
**Author:** Gerhard Nehls (answering-islam.org)
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

This article presents a detailed critique of Dr. Rashad Khalifa's mathematical claims about the number 19 appearing throughout the Qur'an.

## Main Arguments

**The Core Claim:**
Khalifa alleged that the number 19 appears systematically throughout the Qur'an as mathematical proof of divine authorship, triggered by Sura 74:30 which reads "Over it are nineteen."

**Six Primary Objections:**

1. **Historical/Moral Context Matters** - Mathematical patterns cannot override documented sources predating Muhammad or content benefiting him personally.

2. **Exclusionary Proof System** - A proof method requiring computers would have excluded believers during 1,400 years of Qur'anic history.

3. **Vague Probability Claims** - The stated odds of "626 Septillions to one" rest on imprecisely formulated questions unsuitable for rigorous mathematical analysis.

4. **Coincidences Occur** - Remarkable numerical alignments happen randomly in various texts without indicating divine origin.

5. **Reproducibility in Other Texts** - Similar patterns could likely be found in any literature through selective searching and computer analysis.

6. **Biblical Precedent** - The number seven appears systematically throughout the Bible with comparable statistical strength, yet this never convinced Muslims of Biblical authenticity.

## Critical Findings

The evaluation identified **specific manipulations** in Khalifa's data:

- Selectively excluding certain letters from calculations when results didn't align with multiples of 19
- The Sura 7 analysis omitted 98 S's to achieve divisibility
- The ALR analysis differed from independent computer verification by over 1,000 units
- Claims about word frequencies ignored the Bismillah (opening verses) appearing in 113 Suras

## Broader Concerns

The authors note that verse division systems varied across five different traditions and weren't established by Muhammad himself. Sura ordering occurred under Caliph Uthman, undermining historical claims about intentional numerical design.

When asked to provide methodology for verification, Khalifa responded dismissively, suggesting the extensive computer analysis he'd emphasized was unnecessary—contradicting his original presentation.

**Conclusion:** While the numerical patterns appear superficially impressive, systematic manipulation, statistical misrepresentation, and reproducible errors in verifiable claims suggest the evidence is "impressive, yes; divine, no."
