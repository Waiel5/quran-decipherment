# Allah's Timing? — Critical Analysis of Claimed Quranic Mathematical Miracles

**Source:** https://www.answering-islam.org/authors/katz/rebuttals/taslaman/allahs_timing.html
**Author:** Jochen Katz
**Subject:** Rebuttal to Caner Taslaman's *The Quran: Unchallengeable Miracle*
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

## Overview

Jochen Katz's article examines purported mathematical miracles in the Quran, particularly claims about the number 19 and their connection to modern historical dates. The analysis targets Dr. Caner Taslaman's assertions regarding coded discovery dates and Apollo 11 mission timing.

## Key Critiques of the "19 Miracle" Discovery Claims

### Calendar Inconsistencies

Katz identifies a fundamental problem: proponents mix solar (Gregorian) and lunar (Islamic) calendar systems inconsistently. The article notes: "Using sometimes the Gregorian calendar and sometimes the Islamic calendar looks arbitrary. Not consistency but expediency seems to be the principle."

The Islamic year 1393 AH actually concluded on January 25, 1974, meaning most of 1974 corresponded to Islamic year 1394, not 1393. This calculation error undermines the claimed connection between 19 × 74 = 1406.

### Textual Manipulation

Taslaman allegedly omitted repeated letters when calculating numerical values. The verse "Ya ayyuha almuddaththir" contains doubled consonants, but Taslaman counted them only once. The article states: "In order to perform an honest addition, he would have to add another Ya (10) + Dal (4)..." yet failed to do so.

### Quranic Teaching Contradiction

The Quran explicitly commands adherence to a twelve-month lunar calendar in Sura 9:36-37. Using the Gregorian solar calendar violates this directive. Katz argues: "The author was strongly against using a calendar that deviated from a year that consisted of 12 lunar months."

### Linguistic and Contextual Problems

The verses in question (74:1-7) contain personal commands to Muhammad addressing moral conduct—purification, generosity, and patience. These verses simply cannot metaphorically reference an abstract numerical system. The article notes: "A number or concept cannot hear a command, it cannot act, it is not defiled."

Additionally, Sura 74 was among the earliest revelations. At that time, only twelve Quranic verses existed, making it impossible for these commands to reference a complete mathematical structure.

### Technical Issues with Verse Counting

The claim that 741 verses exist from Sura 74:1 to the Quran's end contains several flaws:

- **Arrangement uncertainty**: Sura ordering was determined after Muhammad's death and varies in historical manuscripts
- **Verse numbering systems**: Different traditions count the Basmalah differently, creating inconsistencies
- **Arabic notation reversal**: In Arabic script (read right-to-left), the reference "74:1" becomes "1:74" (١:٧٤), yielding 174 rather than 741—a number not divisible by 19

## Apollo 11 Mission Claims Analysis

### Date Format Arbitrariness

The claim that 21071969 (July 21, 1969 in a specific format) equals 19 × 1109051 relies on choosing particular notation conventions. Writing the same date as 2171969 yields a number not divisible by 19.

Converting to the Islamic calendar: July 21, 1969 = 6 Jumaada al-awal 1389 AH. Testing 6051389, 5051389, and 7051389 (accounting for potential one-day conversion errors) produces no multiples of 19.

### Arbitrary Time Standards

The article emphasizes that Universal Time (based on Greenwich meridian) resulted from an 1884 international conference dominated by Western nations—centuries after Quranic revelation. Why would Allah employ this arbitrary convention rather than Islamic calendar systems or Mecca Time (UTC+02:39:18.2)?

### Verse-Time Correspondence Problems

Proponents note that the lunar module departed at 17:54:01 UT, matching Sura 54:1 superficially. However:

- The Quran mentions "the hour" metaphorically, not literal minutes and seconds
- Sura 54 contains 55 verses, not 60 seconds
- Arabic notation writes this reference as "1:54," reversing the English format
- The connection lacks textual support within the Quranic passage itself

## Methodological Critique

The overall approach exhibits confirmation bias: numerologists test numerous mathematical operations and notation schemes until finding one divisible by 19, then declare it miraculous. Katz observes: "out of a host of options, there is quite often one mathematical operation that produces a result that can be viewed as 'meaningful'."

This selective presentation ignores failed attempts and cherry-picks successful coincidences while ignoring that large numbers produce occasional multiples of 19 by statistical probability.

## Conclusion

Katz argues these supposed miracles rest on textual manipulation, inconsistent calendar application, disregard for Quranic teaching about timekeeping, and reliance on arbitrary modern conventions. Rather than demonstrating divine design, they reflect human pattern-seeking imposed upon ambiguous numerical data.
