**Author:** Abduldaem Al-Kaheel

This article by Abduldaem Al-Kaheel presents 20 numerical claims about Surat Al-Fatiha (the opening chapter of the Qur'an), asserting that multiples of seven appear throughout its structure.

## Key Claims

**Structure:** The article contends that "Surat Al-Fatiha consists of 7 Verses" and contains "31 words," with these numbers forming patterns divisible by seven when arranged in specific ways.

**Letter Patterns:** The analysis examines letter frequencies, claiming that "21 Arabic letters are used in this Chapter" (3 × 7), and that specific letter combinations consistently yield multiples of seven.

**Cross-references:** The author argues connections exist between Al-Fatiha and other Qur'anic chapters through their chapter numbers and verse counts, all yielding multiples of seven.

**Phonetic Features:** The article explores "shadda" (consonant doubling marks), claiming "14 such letters" appear in the chapter, representing numerical patterns divisible by seven.

## Methodology & Claims

The analysis arranges numbers in Arabic reading order (right-to-left) and claims that even massive 31-digit numbers divide perfectly by seven without decimals. The author states: "the probability of this being mere coincidence is 1/7 x 7 x 7 x 7…30 times," arguing this proves divine authorship.
