# Introduction To The Numeric Miracles

**Author:** Abduldaem Al-Kaheel
**Source:** https://www.kaheel7.com/eng/index.php/numeric-miracle/68-introduction-to-the-numeric-miracles
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

## Article Overview

This article by Abduldaem Al-Kaheel examines claimed numeric patterns in the Quran while addressing criticisms of this field of study.

## Key Claims and Methodology

**Primary Arguments:**
- The Quran allegedly contains numeric arrangements that serve as evidence of divine authorship
- Numeric miracles complement linguistic and interpretive studies rather than replace them
- Examples include the number 114 (chapters, a multiple of 19) and 365 mentions of "day" (matching calendar days)

**Criticisms of Rashad Khalifa:**
The article disputes Khalifa's foundational work, claiming his counts were inaccurate. For instance: "He says the first word 'bism'...was mentioned 19 times, yet it was mentioned 22 times."

**Methodology Standards Proposed:**
The author advocates for:
- Data sourced directly from the Quran (verse/chapter counts, letter frequencies)
- Scientific rather than speculative methods
- Avoidance of the "counting of letters" (assigning numerical values to Arabic letters)

## Critical Distinctions

The article distinguishes between:
- **Valid claims**: Countable textual elements (verses, chapters, specific word repetitions)
- **Invalid approaches**: Numerological letter-value systems lacking scriptural basis
- **Problematic practices**: Predictions about future events or political outcomes

## Limitations Acknowledged

The author notes numeric miracles remain a nascent field with ongoing errors, requiring continued scholarly refinement rather than dismissal.
