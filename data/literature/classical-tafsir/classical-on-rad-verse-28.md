# Classical Commentary on Q 13:28 — The Hearts-at-Rest Verse

## The verse

الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَتَطْمَئِنُّ قُلُوبُهُم بِذِكْرِ اللَّهِ ۗ أَلَا بِذِكْرِ اللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ الْقُلُوبُ

"Those who have believed and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of Allah. Verily,
in the remembrance of Allah hearts find rest."

## Our finding

Root sequence (stem tokens, minus particles): {Ṭmn qlb dkr Alh | dkr Alh Ṭmn qlb}
— a perfect mirror palindrome at the root level. 8 of 9 stem tokens mirror each other.
Form enacts content: the verse about finding rest STRUCTURALLY enacts that rest through
its chiastic closure.

This is the single highest length-normalized jinas-density verse in the entire Quran
(0.889).

## What classical commentators DO say

- **Ibn Kathir**: "Hearts find comfort on the side of Allah, become tranquil when He is
  remembered."
- **al-Jalalayn**: "Hearts are reassured by God's remembrance."
- **Kashani** (Sufi): discusses multiple levels of remembrance (soul, heart, spirit)
  and how each level of dhikr settles the corresponding level of the self.
- **al-Tustari**: "remembrance with knowledge: there is tranquil repose."
- **al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, al-Tabari, al-Baydawi**: all emphasize the THEOLOGICAL import —
  hearts find their true home only in recollection of God.

## What they do NOT observe

**No classical commentator we could find identifies the palindromic/chiastic root-level
structure of the verse.** The mirror-enactment of content by form — "the verse about
rest structurally rests" — is a novel observation.

This is striking because Q 13:28 is one of the most popular verses in Muslim devotional
practice (inscribed on walls, recited in sufi sessions, used as a screensaver). Its
content is classically beloved. Its *form* — that it is literally the most structurally
self-referential verse in the Quran — has apparently escaped notice until computational
analysis surfaced it.

**Note on classical balagha:** The classical rhetoric concept **radd al-ʿajuz ʿalā al-ṣadr**
("bringing the end back to the beginning") is exactly the right classical category for
this phenomenon at the verse level. Classical balagha works — al-Jurjani's Dala'il and
Asrar, Sakkaki's Miftah al-'Ulum — discuss this device in poetry. We did NOT find a
classical scholar applying this label to Q 13:28 specifically. But a sophisticated
classical rhetorician asked "does this verse have radd al-ʿajuz ʿalā al-ṣadr?" would
have to say yes.

## Verdict

The verse's theological self-referentiality (content = hearts at rest, effect = listener's
heart rests) is classical. The verse's STRUCTURAL self-referentiality at the root-sequence
level is novel. The classical category radd al-ʿajuz ʿalā al-ṣadr would have covered it
if anyone had thought to look.
