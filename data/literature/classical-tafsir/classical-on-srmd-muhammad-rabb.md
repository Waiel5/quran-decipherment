# Classical Commentary on Q 28:71-72 Sarmad, Muhammad as Proper Name, and Rabb Chronology

## Q 28:71-72 — sarmada hapax pair

"Say: What do you think, if Allah made the night **perpetual (sarmadan)** over you until
the Day of Resurrection, which god other than Allah could bring you light? ... Say:
What do you think, if Allah made the day **perpetual (sarmadan)** over you until the
Day of Resurrection, which god other than Allah could bring you a night in which you
could rest?"

### Our finding
The root s-r-m-d (sarmad, "perpetual") occurs exactly twice in the entire Quran, BOTH
in adjacent verses, forming a perfect hapax-pair organized by the night/day inversion.

### What classical commentators DO say
al-Tabari, al-Razi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir all comment on these verses theologically:
- God alternates night and day as a mercy (ni'ma).
- The hypothetical (perpetual night / perpetual day) is rhetorical, demonstrating God's
  unique creative power.
- The parallelism between v71 and v72 is noted as a rhetorical doubling.
- Some commentators discuss the lexical meaning of sarmad and note that it is "continuous"
  or "uninterrupted".

### What they do NOT observe
**No classical commentator we could find notes that sarmad is a hapax in the Quran**
(occurring only twice and only here). The quantitative uniqueness escapes them.

A modern concordance (ʿAbd al-Baqi 1945) lists both occurrences trivially, but the
**observation** that these two exhaust the root's entire Quranic inventory is not made
in classical tafsir.

The rhetorical parallelism (the pair of counterfactuals) IS noted by al-Razi and al-Tabari.
They stop short of noting the lexical hapax status.

## "Muhammad" as proper name appears only in Medinan surahs

### Our finding
The proper name Muhammad occurs only 4 times in the Quran, all Medinan:
- 3:144 (Aal 'Imran — Medinan)
- 33:40 (al-Ahzab — Medinan)
- 47:2 (Muhammad — Medinan)
- 48:29 (al-Fath — Medinan)

All 86 pre-Hijra Meccan surahs avoid the proper name, preferring "al-rasūl" (the messenger),
"al-nabiyy" (the prophet), "al-nadhīr" (the warner), or second-person address.

### What classical sources discuss
**Qadi 'Iyad in al-Shifa** has an exhaustive treatment of the Prophet's names and epithets,
listing dozens. He observes that the Prophet has many names in the Quran (Rasulullah,
al-Nabi, al-Mustafa, al-Mujtaba, al-Ummi, al-Shahid, al-Mubashshir, al-Nadhir, etc.) but
**does NOT organize this list by Meccan/Medinan provenance.** al-Suyuti, in his enumeration
of the Prophet's names, likewise does not make the chronological observation.

### What they do NOT observe
No classical biographer (Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Sa'd, al-Waqidi, al-Tabari's Ta'rikh) we could
find notes explicitly that the PROPER NAME "Muhammad" enters the Quran only post-Hijra
— this despite an extensive classical sira tradition that does distinguish Meccan and
Medinan material.

**The observation is genuinely novel to classical scholarship.** The tools to see it
(Nöldeke-style chronological ordering PLUS concordance lookup of "Muhammad") only
mature in the 20th century.

## Rabb declining chronologically

### Our finding
Root ر-ب (rabb, "Lord") is the only frequent root with a negative Spearman ρ against
chronological order. Early Meccan = densest; Medinan = thinnest. Every other frequent
root is flat or increases.

### Modern scholarship
Modern scholars (Izutsu, Toshihiko, *God and Man in the Quran*; Sadeghi 2011, chronological
stylometric work) have observed thematic shifts Meccan→Medinan. The specific Rabb/Allah
distributional shift is documented in modern semantic-analysis papers (e.g., the paper
surveyed at https://www.academia.edu/44099488).

### Classical sources
al-Tabari and others distinguish Meccan/Medinan for asbab al-nuzul purposes. They note
that early Meccan surahs are "theological" and Medinan surahs are "legislative." This
qualitative observation is classical and directly adjacent to our finding. **But the
quantitative claim "Rabb specifically declines" with a per-surah density curve is modern.**

Classical scholars noted:
- Meccan address is direct ("Say...", "O mankind...", "O children of Adam...")
- Medinan address is communal ("O you who believe...")
- Meccan content is creedal, Medinan is legal
- The name *al-Rahman* (as an alternative divine name) is characteristic of Middle Meccan

These are all consistent with our Rabb finding, but none IS our Rabb finding.

## Verdict

- sarmad hapax pair: **novel** (classical noted rhetorical pairing, not lexical hapax)
- Muhammad post-Hijra: **novel** (classical listed the names, not their chronology)
- Rabb declining: **classical precedent at qualitative level** (Meccan = theological,
  Medinan = legal), quantitative finding novel.
