# Classical Commentary on Surah Yusuf and the word سجن (sijn, "prison")

## Our finding

The root س-ج-ن (s-j-n, "prison/imprison") appears exactly **12 times** in the entire Quran,
and **all 12 occurrences are in Surah 12 (Yusuf)**. Surah 12 is the prison narrative.
This is a triple coincidence: the root count, the surah index, and the exclusivity.

Sister findings: qmS (shirt) = 6x all in Surah 12; khf (cave) = 6x all in Surah 18.

## What classical commentators DO say

Classical tafsirs on Surah Yusuf are extensive (Razi's treatment runs to dozens of pages;
al-Qurtubi, al-Zamakhshari, Ibn Kathir all wrote at length).

They typically discuss:
- The theological significance of Yusuf preferring prison over sin ("Prison is more beloved
  to me than what they invite me to" — 12:33).
- The prison as a place where Yusuf demonstrates prophecy through dream interpretation.
- Classical Arabic linguists discuss the root س-ج-ن lexically (sijn = prison, sajjana =
  imprison, sajjin = a record-book in 83:7).
- al-Tabari discusses the historical setting of the Egyptian prison and Yusuf's da'wa to
  fellow inmates.

## What they do NOT say

- **No classical tafsir we could find identifies that s-j-n occurs exactly 12 times.**
- **None notes that all 12 occurrences are confined to Surah 12.**
- **None notes the coincidence with the surah's index number.**

A classical commentator attentive to repetition (e.g., Zamakhshari, who is highly rhetoric-
focused) would have remarked on lexical fingerprinting by subject matter — and they do,
in a qualitative way, when they note that the prison theme pervades the surah. But the
exact 12 = surah 12 match is a post-concordance observation, and concordances of the
Quran postdate the classical period.

## Why this matters for distinguishing classical from novel observation

A 12th-century observation that "Surah Yusuf has lots of prison references" is NOT the same
as the finding "root s-j-n occurs exactly 12 times in the whole Quran, all in Surah 12."
The qualitative observation is ancient. The quantitative triple-coincidence is novel.
