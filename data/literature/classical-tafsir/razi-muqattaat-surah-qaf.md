# Classical Commentary on the Muqatta'at and Surah Qaf

## Did classical scholars notice the letter-density effect?

**Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (d. 1210)** gives the most extensive classical discussion of the
huruf muqatta'at, listing some twenty opinions. He notes that:
- The letters may be names of the surahs they open
- They may be among God's names
- They spell words when combined
- The Arabs would name things after single letters ('ayn for "eye", ghayn for "clouds",
  nun for "whale")

**al-Razi does NOT report anyone computing the letter-frequency density.** The classical
range of explanation is semantic and symbolic, not statistical.

## Surah Qaf (Surah 50)

Surah Qaf opens with the single letter qaf. Classical commentators discuss meanings
(a mountain, a name of God, etc.) but none appear to have counted qaf occurrences inside
the surah.

Our project's finding:
- Surah 50 has exactly 57 qaf's
- Surah 42 has exactly 57 qaf's
- 57 + 57 = 114 = surah count
- 57 = 19 × 3
- This is the only Khalifa-family muqatta'at claim that survives strict testing.

No classical scholar counted this. The tools (easy letter tallying) did not exist; classical
letter-counting focused on total-Quran letters, not per-surah-per-letter matrices.

## Our density effect (chi² = 228.78, p < 10⁻¹⁵)

The finding that muqatta'at-opening letters are statistically over-represented in their
host surahs is a MODERN computational observation. It is driven primarily by Surah 50
(qaf, z=+4.68), Surah 2 (alm, z=+3.43), Surah 29 (alm, z=+2.74). Of the 29 muqatta'at surahs,
many are individually weak or anti-enriched — but the aggregate signal is overwhelming.

**Classical scholars did not observe this.** Even al-Razi's exhaustive twenty-opinion
catalog does not include a "letter-density" hypothesis. The conceptual vocabulary of
"statistical over-representation under a null model" is 20th-century.

**Partial classical adumbration:** Classical Arabic linguists did observe that some letters
are rarer than others (e.g., in their discussions of tanasub al-hurūf / compatibility of
consonants), and they knew perfectly well that alif, lam, mim, etc. are extremely common
Arabic letters in general. That is why part of our tempering fact — 9/14 "Luminous Letters"
are among the 14 most frequent Arabic letters anyway — is *not* surprising to them. But
they did not compute the per-surah enrichment ratio.
