# al-Suyuti, al-Itqan fi Ulum al-Qur'an — Word/Letter Count Chapters

**Author:** Jalal al-Din al-Suyuti (d. 911/1505)
**Work:** al-Itqan fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an (80 chapters / nau')
**Searched for:** chapters on word counts, letter counts, frequency of specific lemmas, and any pre-Khalifa attention to rahma=114.

## What classical Quranic-sciences literature actually counted

Classical scholars — al-Suyuti in al-Itqan and al-Zarkashi (d. 794/1392) in al-Burhan — did
compile **TOTAL** counts of the Quran (total words, total letters, total verses, total surahs).
They recorded multiple narrations, differing by 10s to 100s, reflecting divergence in counting
rules (shadda, whether a clitic counts as a separate word, etc.). These are the famous numbers:

- Total words: 77,934 / 77,437 / 77,277 (multiple narrations reported by al-Suyuti, Itqan p.166
  in the Algar/Bili/Schub/Abdel Haleem Garnet edition)
- Total surahs: 114 (by consensus; al-Zarkashi states this explicitly)
- Total verses: 6,000 / 6,204 / 6,214 / 6,219 / 6,236 / etc. (depending on kufan/basri/madani/shami school)

## What they did NOT do

1. **They did not publish per-lemma frequency tables.** There is no classical analog of a
   concordance listing each Quranic lemma with its exact occurrence count. The first
   known alphabetic concordance of the Quran is Muhammad Fuad 'Abd al-Baqi's *al-Mu'jam
   al-Mufahras li-Alfaz al-Qur'an al-Karim* (1945), which draws on Fluegel's earlier 1842
   German concordance. These are modern works.

2. **They did not identify rahma = 114 as a structural coincidence.** The canonical
   classical works (Itqan, Burhan) do not remark on rahma's count. Where the number 114
   appears in classical literature, it refers to the surah count itself or (via al-Shafi'i)
   to the number of times the basmala is recited in the mushaf (113 surah-headers + 1
   inside Surat al-Naml).

3. **The "rahma = 114" claim reached popular circulation through Khalifa-adjacent
   numerology in the 1970s-80s.** Where it is claimed today, it is typically derived by
   *cherry-picking* — e.g., "80 of 81 instances of adjective 'raheem' (excluding the one
   referring to Muhammad) + 34 instances of name al-Raheem = 114." See
   https://114chambers.wordpress.com/2023/12/03 for a representative example. That is
   NOT the same as our finding, which is a uniform lemma count: lemma `raHomap` = 114,
   no filtering, no handpicking, the unique lemma with count 114 out of 4,832 distinct
   Quranic lemmas.

## Conclusion: the rahma = 114 finding is NOVEL relative to classical scholarship

No classical scholar identified the coincidence. Al-Suyuti and al-Zarkashi had total
counts but not per-lemma frequency tables, so even if the observation had been interesting
to them, the computational infrastructure for making it did not exist until the 1945
Mu'jam al-Mufahras.
