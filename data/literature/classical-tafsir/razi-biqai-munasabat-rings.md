# Classical Quranic Structure Analysis — al-Razi, al-Biqa'i, and Ring Composition

## The classical tradition of 'ilm al-munasabat

The science of munasabat (inter-verse coherence) is classical:
- **al-Naysaburi (Abu Bakr)** — 11th c., among the earliest to argue verses must be read
  in structural relation to each other.
- **Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (d. 606/1210)** — the major pioneer. In Mafatih al-Ghayb / al-Tafsir
  al-Kabir, al-Razi establishes verse-to-verse coherence throughout most surahs. His method
  identifies nine types of munasabah: within a sentence, between closing and content,
  between verses in one surah, between multiple verses, between a surah's opening and
  closing, between surah contents, between a surah's opening and the previous surah's
  closing, between surah openings, and thematic unity within a surah. Source: Central
  Asian J. Soc. Sci. Hist. — https://cajssh.casjournal.org/index.php/CAJSSH/article/view/1309
- **al-Zarkashi (d. 794/1392)** in al-Burhan has a chapter on munasabat.
- **al-Biqa'i (d. 885/1480)** — Nazm al-Durar fi Tanasub al-Ayat wa-l-Suwar. The magnum opus
  of this tradition. 8 volumes. al-Biqa'i famously said, "Sometimes I sit pensively for
  months just to know the connection between one verse and another."
- **al-Suyuti** treats munasabat in al-Itqan chapter 62 (Fi Munasabat al-Ayat wa-l-Suwar).

## What did classical scholars see at the Al-Baqarah 131-144 (Abraham/qibla) center?

al-Razi's method establishes LINEAR verse-to-verse coherence: v1→v2→v3, etc. That differs
from *chiastic* ring composition (ABCBA). A linear coherence method cannot by itself
detect that verses 131 and 144 are paired around a central axis.

**al-Biqa'i** is the strongest classical candidate. He did identify what is arguably a
ring-compositional observation: that the LAST 9 surahs of the Quran mirror the FIRST 9
(e.g., al-Ikhlas [surah 112, 3rd from the end] mirrors Al-'Imran [surah 3 from the front]).
This is a whole-mushaf macro-ring claim and it is 500 years older than Farrin.

**Important:** Our computational audit DISCONFIRMED this whole-mushaf ring (Farrin's
macro-ring, z = −4.87, less ring-like than random under lexical test). So al-Biqa'i's
claim is itself an interesting historical case of a ring observation that does not
survive quantitative testing.

We did NOT find a classical statement that Al-Baqarah 131-144 is a ring centered on the
qibla-change verse, nor that this is the strongest ring in the Quran. That quantitative
claim is distinctively modern (Farrin 2014, and our algorithmic re-discovery 2026).

## Cuypers and Farrin explicitly credit classical predecessors

Cuypers and Farrin acknowledge al-Biqa'i and Islahi as predecessors in the Quranic-structural-
analysis tradition. But they are the first to systematically apply ABBA ring composition
with explicit pairing at the verse-sequence level.

## Conclusion for our chiastic findings

- **Farrin's whole-mushaf ring (our disconfirmation):** has a classical precursor in
  al-Biqa'i, also disconfirmed by our data. Both modern Farrin and classical Biqa'i made
  a claim that the quantitative lexical-level test does not support.

- **Al-Baqarah 131-144 as the single strongest ring:** NOVEL quantitative identification.
  Classical scholars (al-Razi, al-Biqa'i, al-Zarkashi) had the conceptual vocabulary of
  munasabat but their method was linear rather than chiastic-quantitative. The
  Bonferroni-surviving z = +9.69 identification of this specific 14-verse window as
  the center of Al-Baqarah's strongest ring is a modern computational result.

- **Sub-surah rings (al-Qamar 21-30, 'Abasa 1-9, al-Kahf 83-91):** no classical mention
  found of these specific rings.
