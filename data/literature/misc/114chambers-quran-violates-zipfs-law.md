# Quran Violates Zipf's Law, Unlike Any Human-Authored Book

**Source:** https://114chambers.wordpress.com/2022/02/26/quran-violates-zipfs-law-unlike-any-human-authored-book/
**Author:** "Siham Karami" (114 Chambers blog)
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

## Article Summary

The article claims the Quran breaks Zipf's Law—a linguistic principle stating word frequency is inversely proportional to rank—unlike all human-authored texts. The author argues this constitutes scientific evidence of divine authorship.

## Key Claims

**Primary assertion:** "The Quran does not follow Zipf's Law" while all human languages and books do.

**Supporting evidence presented:**
- Frequency comparison charts showing Quranic word rankings versus Arabic language rankings
- The two most frequent words in the Quran are "min" (from) and "Allah"
- These words rank much lower in general Arabic usage
- A line graph allegedly demonstrates deviations from predicted ratios

## Critical Issues

**Methodological problems:**

The article conflates language-wide patterns with text-specific distributions. Zipf's Law describes aggregate linguistic behavior across large corpora, not individual texts. Any specialized document—religious, technical, or narrative—will show frequency variations based on subject matter.

**The "signature" claim:**

The article presents as remarkable that "min Allah" (from Allah) appear as the top two words. This is unsurprising: Quranic discourse fundamentally emphasizes divine origin and Allah's attributes.

**Unresolved counterargument:**

A commenter provided a Wikipedia link showing Arabic itself demonstrates Zipfian properties across multiple scripts. The author acknowledged this but reframed the analysis without addressing whether their original methodology was flawed.

## Conclusion

The analysis lacks rigorous statistical comparison between the Quran's deviation and normal variation in specialized texts. Subject-matter frequency clustering alone cannot demonstrate supernatural authorship.
