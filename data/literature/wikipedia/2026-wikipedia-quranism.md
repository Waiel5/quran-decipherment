## Definition and Core Beliefs

Quranism is an Islamic denomination that considers the Qur'an as the sole authoritative religious source. Adherents believe "the Qur'an is clear and complete and can be fully understood without recourse to external sources."

The movement rejects the binding authority of Hadith (prophetic traditions) and Sunnah, dividing into two main branches: those treating non-Quranic sources as secondary, and those rejecting them entirely.

## Methodology

Quranists employ *tafsir al-qur'an bi al-qur'an* (explaining the Qur'an through the Qur'an itself) rather than relying on traditional commentaries or historical sources.

## Rashad Khalifa and the United Submitters

Egyptian-American Rashad Khalifa developed numerological analysis claiming divine proof through the number 19, found throughout Quranic structure. He termed this the "Qur'an code."

Khalifa founded **United Submitters International** (USI), publishing *Submitter's Perspective* from 1985 onward. He proclaimed Hadith and Sunnah "pure speculation" and "satanic traps," excluding Qur'an verses 9:128–129 as incompatible with his numerical theory.

Controversially, Khalifa declared himself "God's Messenger of the Covenant" in 1989, generating widespread outrage. He was murdered in 1990 at Tucson Mosque, though USI persists with followers primarily active online across North America.

## Notable Figures and Organizations

**Edip Yüksel:** Turkish-Kurdish activist who joined the Submitters after encountering Khalifa's works. He authored Quranic translations promoting the number-19 doctrine and "de-Arabization" of Islam, subsequently leaving USI but continuing independent advocacy.

**Ahmad Subhy Mansour:** Egyptian Islamic historian removed from Al-Azhar University in 1985 for Quranist views, imprisoned, then granted U.S. asylum in 2002. He founded the International Quranic Center emphasizing democracy and human rights.

**Kassim Ahmad:** Malaysian thinker whose 1997 work *Hadith: A Re-evaluation* called for scientific Hadith assessment.

**Yaşar Nuri Öztürk:** Turkish theologian and Quranic translator advocating abandonment of post-prophetic innovations.

## Modern Manifestations Summary

Yan Tatsine in Nigeria (Maitatsine, d. 1980), Iranian reformist Shia scholars, and Libya under Gaddafi who declared the Qur'an Libya's "constitution" all represent variant Quranist trajectories.

In late 1990s Kano, Nigeria, educated professionals formed *Qurʾānawa*, adopting Khalifa's mathematical-miracle teachings without recognizing him as prophet. The movement subsequently expanded across northern Nigerian cities via internet dissemination.
