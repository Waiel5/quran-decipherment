## Overview

The **Quran code** (also called **Code 19**) represents a belief that mathematical patterns—specifically based on the number 19—are embedded throughout the Quranic text. Proponents argue these patterns serve as proof of divine authorship. The most prominent advocate was biochemist Rashad Khalifa.

## History

In 1969, Khalifa began studying the separated letters (Muqattaʿat) within the Quran. His 1973 book *Miracle of the Quran: Significance of the Mysterious Alphabets* explored these initials through numerical analysis.

By 1974, Khalifa claimed discovery of a mathematical code centered on 19, publishing *The Computer Speaks: God's Message to the World*. He referenced Surah 74, verse 30—"Over it is nineteen"—as scriptural support for the number's significance.

Supporters include the United Submitters International (founded by Khalifa), various Quranists, and some traditional Muslims.

## Key Claims

According to Edip Yüksel's *Nineteen: God's Signature in Nature and Scripture*, the evidence includes:

- The Bismillah contains exactly 19 letters
- "Allah" appears 2,698 times (19×142)
- "Rahman" appears 57 times (19×3)
- The Quran comprises 114 chapters (19×6)
- Total verses number 6,346 (19×334)

## The 29 Initialized Surahs

The Quran contains 29 chapters beginning with separated letters. Khalifa argued these initials reveal complex mathematical patterns divisible by 19 within their respective chapters.

## Quranic Gematria

Each Arabic letter corresponds to a numerical value (Abjad numerals). This system allows proponents to assign numbers to words and calculate their gematric values, supporting claims about mathematical patterns.

## Western Reception

Khalifa's work received minimal Western attention until 1980 when Martin Gardner mentioned it in *Scientific American*. Gardner revisited the topic in a 1997 *Skeptical Inquirer* column.

## Criticism

**Major objections include:**

- **Cherry-picking textual variants**: Early Quranic manuscripts differ orthographically. Khalifa selected versions with letter frequencies divisible by 19, avoiding those that didn't fit
- **Nonstandard spellings**: Khalifa proposed spelling changes unsupported by any manuscript, like altering Surah 68's initial letter
- **Selective inclusion**: He excluded Surah 9, verses 128-129, claiming they were "humanly added," enabling his total of 6,346 verses (which yields multiples of 19) rather than the traditional 6,348
- **Pattern-finding bias**: Critics invoke stochastic processes—large datasets naturally contain apparent patterns
- **Manuscript inconsistencies**: Without a universally agreed "original text," letter enumerations remain contested

Scholar Bilal Philips characterized the theory as "hoax based on falsified data, misinterpretations of the Quran's text, and grammar inconsistencies."
