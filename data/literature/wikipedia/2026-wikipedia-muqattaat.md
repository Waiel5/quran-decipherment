## Overview

The **Muqattaʿat** (Arabic: حُرُوف مُقَطَّعَات), also called "mysterious letters" or "disconnected letters," are "combinations of between one and five Arabic letters that appear at the beginning of 29 out of the 114 chapters (surahs) of the Quran." These letters form the opening verses following the Bismillāh phrase and are alternatively known as *fawātiḥ* or "openers."

Four to five chapters bear names derived from their muqattaʿat: Ṭā-Hā, Yā-Sīn, Ṣād, Qāf, and sometimes Nūn.

## Interpretation Theories

### Enigmatic/Obscurative Approach

Traditional Islamic scholarship generally maintains that "the general belief of most Muslims is that their meaning is known only to God." Classical scholars like Abd Allah ibn Abbas and Abdullah ibn Masud suggested these letters represent words or phrases connected to divine attributes. Contemporary analysis by Ahsan ur Rehman identifies "phonological, syntactic and semantic links between the prefixed letters and the text of the chapters."

### Numerological Interpretations

Rashad Khalifa proposed the "Quran code" theory, claiming "these initials occur throughout their respective chapters in multiples of nineteen." This interpretation links to surah 74:30, which references 19 guardian angels of Hell.

### Linguistic and Historical Theories

**Christoph Luxenberg's hypothesis** suggests the letters are "remnants of indications for the liturgical recitation for the Syriac hymns that ended up being copied into the Arabic text."

**Theodor Nöldeke's original theory** proposed these letters served "as marks of possession, belonging to the owners of Qur'anic copies" and later revised his position to suggest "mystical reference to the archetypal text in heaven."

**Keith Massey's theory** argues the letters function as "initials or monograms of the scribes who originally transcribed the sūras," though acknowledging exceptions to his ranking system.

**Hebrew Theory** posits that *Alif-Lam* corresponds to Hebrew "El" (god).

### Mystical and Esoteric Interpretations

Sufi scholars, particularly within Batiniyya sects, view the mysterious letters as extensions to the ninety-nine names of God, with individual letters carrying hidden mystical significance.

**Hurufism**, an Ismaili ghulat sect, emphasized letter-based and numerological mysticism surrounding the muqattaʿat, influencing poets like Nasimi and Fuzuli despite being considered heretical.

**Baháʼu'lláh** (founder of the Baháʼí Faith) authored the *Commentary on the Isolated Letters* (1857-58), describing cosmic creation through the letters, with special emphasis on *alif* as central to divine worlds.

**The Báb** utilized muqattaʿat in his *Qayyúmu'l-Asmáʼ*, claiming the first seven surahs' letters possess numerical value 1267, yielding the year 1844 (his declaration date).

## Structural Analysis

The muqattaʿat appear in 14 distinct combinations across 29 surahs, using exactly half of the 28 Arabic letters. The fourteen letters employed are: *ʾalif*, *hā*, *ḥā*, *ṭā*, *yā*, *kāf*, *lām*, *mīm*, *nūn*, *sīn*, *ʿain*, *ṣād*, *qāf*, *rā*.

Key patterns include:
- *ʾAlif Lām Mīm* appears six times (most frequent)
- *Ḥāʾ Mīm* appears six times (most frequent)
- *ʾAlif* invariably precedes *Lām*
- Most combinations begin with either *ʾAlif Lām* or *Ḥāʾ Mīm*

Nearly all surahs containing these letters immediately reference Quranic revelation in following verses, with 8 cases beginning "These are the signs..."

Importantly, "all but 3 of these suras are Meccan surat" (exceptions: al-Baqarah, Āl ʾImrān, ar-Raʻd).
