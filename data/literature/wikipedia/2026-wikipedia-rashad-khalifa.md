## Overview

**Rashad Khalifa** (November 19, 1935 – January 31, 1990) was an Egyptian-American biochemist primarily known for founding the United Submitters International (USI) and promoting "Quran-alone" Islam.

## Life and Education

Khalifa earned an honors degree from Ain Shams University in Egypt before immigrating to the United States in 1959. He obtained a Master's degree in biochemistry from the University of Arizona and a Ph.D. from UC Riverside. He became a naturalized U.S. citizen and settled in Tucson, Arizona, where he worked in various scientific roles, including as a science adviser for Libya and a senior chemist for Arizona's State Office of Chemistry.

He was married to an American woman and had two children: son Sam Khalifa and daughter Beth Bujarski.

## Doctrinal Positions

Khalifa's core belief involved rejecting hadith and sunnah as corruptions, instead advocating that Islamic practice should derive exclusively from the Quran. He applied this principle broadly, arguing that "previous revelations of God, such as the Bible, contained contradictions due to human interference."

### Code 19 Research

Beginning in 1968, Khalifa used computers to analyze textual patterns in the Quran. His publications included:

- *Miracle of the Quran: Significance of the Mysterious Alphabets* (1973)
- *The Computer Speaks: God's Message to the World* (1981)
- *Quran: Visual Presentation of the Miracle* (1982)

He claimed the Quran contained an incorruptible mathematical structure based on the number 19. He argued this mathematical code "rejected the Quranic verses 9:128-129." However, mainstream Islamic scholars strongly opposed these interpretations. Martin Gardner reviewed his work critically in *Scientific American* (1980) and later publications.

## Assassination

On January 31, 1990, Khalifa was found stabbed to death inside the Mosque of Tucson, which he had founded.

### Investigation and Trial

The murder remained unsolved for 19 years until Calgary Police arrested Glen Cusford Francis in 2009. Francis had initially studied under Khalifa under the alias "Benjamin Phillips" but disappeared after the killing. DNA evidence linked him to the crime scene.

The trial concluded December 19, 2012, with a jury finding Francis guilty of first-degree murder after three hours of deliberation, resulting in a life sentence.

Prior to Francis's arrest, James Williams—allegedly affiliated with Jamaat ul-Fuqra—was convicted of conspiracy in the slaying. Williams disappeared on his sentencing day in 1994, was recaptured in 2000 attempting to re-enter the United States, and received a 69-year sentence.
