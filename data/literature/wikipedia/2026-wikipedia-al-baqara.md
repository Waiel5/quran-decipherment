## Overview

Al-Baqarah (الْبَقَرَة), meaning "The Heifer" or "The Cow," constitutes the second and longest chapter of the Quran. This extensive surah contains 286 verses and spans three of the Quran's thirty segments (juzʼ).

## Key Statistics

- **Position**: Chapter 2 of the Quran
- **Verse Count**: 286 verses (āyāt)
- **Word Count**: 6,116 words
- **Letter Count**: 25,900 letters
- **Structural Divisions**: 40 rukus (sections)
- **Juzʼ Distribution**: Chapters 1-3

## Opening Letters

The chapter begins with the muqattaʿat letters: alif (ا), lām (ل), and mīm (م).

## Classification and Revelation

Al-Baqarah is classified as a **Medinan surah**, meaning it was revealed in Medina rather than Mecca. Muslim tradition holds that the chapter was revealed gradually over approximately ten years, beginning in 622 CE following the Hijrah (migration to Medina).

## Major Themes (verse-range structure)

- **Verses 1-7**: God-fearing individuals rewarded; unbelievers reproved
- **Verses 8-20**: Hypocrites characterized
- **Verses 21-38**: Exhortation to worship the true God
- **Verses 39-101**: Jews and Christians urged to accept Muhammad's prophethood
- **Verses 102-112**: Opposition from Jews and Christians addressed
- **Verses 113-114**: Doctrine of abrogation
- **Verses 116-141**: Abraham's story and true religion
- **Verses 142-153**: Change of prayer direction from Jerusalem to Mecca (includes the famous "wasata"/middle nation verse 143)
- **Verses 154-163**: Comfort for bereaved friends of Battle of Badr casualties
- **Verses 164-172**: Faith exhortations and dietary laws
- **Verses 173-176**: Laws concerning lawful and unlawful foods
- **Verses 178-182**: Retaliation law and bequests
- **Verses 183-187**: Fasting regulations and Ramadan
- **Verses 188-202**: Pilgrimage and religious striving
- **Verses 214-242**: Charitable giving, warfare, wine prohibition, and orphan care
- **Verses 243-254**: Religious defense and prophetic history
- **Verses 261-274**: Encouragement for charitable giving
- **Verses 275-277**: Prohibition against usury
- **Verses 278-283**: Debt regulations
- **Verses 284-286**: Final three verses regarding Paradise

## Structural Organization

The surah employs a **chiastic structure** (ring composition), arranging themes in mirrored sequences to emphasize central concepts and highlight relationships between corresponding elements. This is the structure that Raymond Farrin (2010, 2014) and Cuypers analyze as a ring.

## Notable Verses

**Verse 255 - The Throne Verse (Ayatul Kursi)** receives special prominence in Islamic tradition.

**Verse 256 - No Compulsion in Religion** establishes the principle that religious faith cannot be coerced.

**Verse 282 - The Verse of Loan** is recognized as the longest single verse in the Quran.
