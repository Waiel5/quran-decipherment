# Ring Theory: the Quran's Structural Coherence

**Source:** https://www.islam21c.com/islamic-thought/ring-theory-the-qurans-structural-coherence/
**Author:** Abu Zakariya
**Fetched:** 2026-04-12

## Overview

The article by Abu Zakariya examines how the Qur'an exhibits sophisticated literary structure despite its unique revelation circumstances.

## Key Claims

**Ring Composition Definition**: The author explains that ring composition involves correspondence between beginning and end, structured as "A, B, C, D, C', B', A'" with "The meaning is in the middle."

**Surah Al-Baqarah Structure**: The longest Qur'anic chapter (286 verses) divides into nine themed sections that mirror each other:
- Faith vs. unbelief (sections 1 & 9)
- Allah's creation (sections 2 & 8)
- Law delivery (sections 3 & 7)
- Testing themes (sections 4 & 6)
- Central pivot: Ka'ba qibla change (section 5, verse 143)

## Significant Verses Referenced

- **2:143**: Describes the qibla change and Muslims as "a middle nation," positioned at the chapter's exact center
- **2:255** (Ayat al-Kursi): Called "the most excellent verse" with its own ring composition containing sub-rings

## Arguments for Divine Origin

The author contends that Prophet Muhammad's inability to read or write, combined with the Qur'an's gradual 23-year revelation in response to unpredictable events, made such structured composition humanly impossible without editorial revision.

## Scholarly Support

Raymond K. Farrin's analysis concluded the structure exhibits "marvellous justness of design" with precise arrangement guiding readers to central themes.
