# Qalb-Theology

## 1. The Root and Its Self-Demonstration

The root **q-l-b** carries a single underlying semantic gesture: *to turn, overturn, reverse, flip.* Physical meanings include turning meat on a spit (the source for *qalb* = the organ that pulses/turns), turning pages, reversing direction, and military rout. Cognate extensions: *inqalaba* = to be overturned, to return; *qallaba* = to turn over (transitive, intensive); *taqallub* = turning-about, oscillation; *munqalab* = the place/destination one turns to.

Crucially, the *heart* in the Quran is not a static seat. It is named *qalb* precisely because it is the organ that **turns** — between faith and disbelief, softness and hardness, assurance and fear. The Prophet's recorded prayer *Yā muqallib al-qulūb thabbit qalbī ʿalā dīnik* ("O Turner of hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion") activates this lexicon directly: the hearts are being-turned, and God is *al-muqallib*.

This is not a hidden pun — it is foregrounded. **Q 24:44**: *yuqallibu llāhu l-layla wa-n-nahār* — "Allah turns/alternates the night and day" — uses the same verb *qallaba* that Q 6:110 uses for hearts: *wa-nuqallibu afʾidatahum wa-abṣārahum* — "We turn their hearts and their eyes." And **Q 18:42**: *fa-aṣbaḥa yuqallibu kaffayhi* — the ruined man "began turning his hands over" in dismay. The *same motion* applied to day/night cycle, to hearts, to hands of despair: **turning is the universal metaphysics of the created order**, and the heart is its human instance.

Of 132 nominal tokens: **112 are plural** *qulūb* (hearts); only **~20 are singular** *qalb*. The *individual* heart is rarely foregrounded. The Quran overwhelmingly speaks of *hearts-as-classes* (hearts of the believers, hearts of the disbelievers, hearts of the hypocrites, hearts of those in whose hearts is disease). This is already theologically significant: in Quranic anthropology, heart-states are *communal taxonomies*, not individual biographies.

**Distribution: 56 Meccan verses, 99 Medinan verses.** The heart becomes more thematically central in Medina than Mecca — correlating with the rise of the hypocrite-category (a Medinan phenomenon requiring interior/exterior doubling).

**Verses with two qlb-tokens in one ayah** (where the heart is doubly emphasised): Q 3:144, 5:41, 8:63, 9:110, 13:28, 22:46, 22:53, 24:37, 26:227, 33:53, 48:12, 57:16, 83:31. The Q 13:28 double (*taṭmaʾinnu qulūbuhum bi-dhikri llāh, alā bi-dhikri llāhi taṭmaʾinnu l-qulūb*) is the single most structurally compressed qalb-statement in the book — *the verse says "hearts" twice in chiastic mirror*, which is why it registers so strongly in the chiastic audit.

## 3. Verbs Acting on the Heart: A Classification

I classify every qalb-verse by the grammatical action applied to the heart. Patterns emerge cleanly.

### 3.1 SEAL / STAMP (ختم khatama, طبع ṭabaʿa, رين rāna)

12 verses: Q 2:7, 4:155, 6:46, 7:100, 7:101, 9:87, 9:93, 10:74, 16:108, 30:59, 40:35, 42:24, 45:23, 47:16, 63:3, 83:14 (*rāna ʿalā qulūbihim*).

**Grammatical subject of sealing**: always Allah (except Q 83:14 where "their earnings" is the subject — but still an Allah-permitted consequence). **Grammatical object**: always disbelievers, deniers, or those who "returned to disbelief after belief" (Q 63:3). No believer is ever described as having a sealed heart. The seal is a divine response to prior human act of refusal; Q 7:101 and Q 47:16 make this explicit by naming cause before effect ("because they refused ... their hearts were sealed").

### 3.2 TURN / REVERSE (qallaba, inqalaba, tataqallabu)

The dominant action-verb on hearts, matching the root's own semantics.

- Q 6:110 — *nuqallibu afʾidatahum* ("We turn their hearts") — divine subject, disbelievers as patients, as consequence of refusal.
- Q 24:37 — *tataqallabu fīhi l-qulūbu wal-abṣār* — hearts and eyes "turning about" on the Day (terror).
- Q 2:144 — *taqalluba wajhika fī s-samāʾi* — the Prophet's *face* turning in the sky (*qibla* episode).
- Q 3:196, 16:46, 40:4 — *taqallub* = movement in the land (disbelievers' "restless motion").
- Q 18:18 — *nuqallibuhum* — the Sleepers of the Cave "turned right and left."

The same root ties the qibla-turning, the Sleepers' turning, the Day of turning-hearts, and the disbelievers' restless earthly turning into one semantic field: *all turning is God-caused, and the heart is the organ of turning*.

### 3.3 FILL / DESCEND-INTO (amlaʾa, anzala, ulqiya)

- Q 48:4, 48:18, 48:26 — *anzala s-sakīnata fī qulūbihim* ("He sent down tranquillity into their hearts"). **Believer hearts.**
- Q 3:151, 8:12, 33:26, 59:2 — *alqā fī qulūbihim ar-ruʿb* ("He cast terror into their hearts"). **Disbeliever hearts.**
- Q 3:167 — *fī qulūbihim mā laysa fī afwāhihim* (what is in their hearts is not in their mouths) — *hypocrite hearts.*
- Q 48:26 — *al-ḥamiyyata ḥamiyyata l-jāhiliyyati* (chauvinism of Jahiliyya) — *disbeliever hearts, set in opposition to sakīna*.

Critical parallel: **Q 48:26 puts both movements in the same verse** — the disbelievers *put chauvinism into* their hearts (*jaʿala* — they themselves the agent); Allah *sent tranquillity down into* the hearts of the Messenger and believers. The heart is a receptacle with two possible contents, and the *agency-direction* differs: anxiety/chauvinism is self-generated; tranquillity is descended-from-above.

### 3.4 REST / BE REASSURED (iṭmaʾanna) vs FEAR (wajila / wajifa)

- *Iṭmaʾanna* (rest) applied to qalb: Q 13:28 (twice), 2:260, 3:126, 5:113, 8:10, 16:106.
- *Wajila* (fearful tremor at God's mention): Q 8:2, 22:35 — **the mark of believing hearts**. Paradoxically, the *same* believer hearts that are reassured by *dhikr* (Q 13:28) *tremble* at God's mention (Q 8:2; 22:35). This is *not* a contradiction: the movement is first awe (*wajila*), then rest (*iṭmaʾanna*) — the Q 39:23 verse compresses both motions: "skins shiver ... then their skins and hearts relax at the remembrance of Allah."

### 3.5 HARDEN (qasā), BE-HARD (aqsā)

Q 2:74, 5:13, 6:43, 22:53 ("qāsiyat al-qulūb"), 39:22, 57:16. The *opposite* of the believing-heart-chiasmus. Q 57:16 is diagnostic: "Has the time not come for those who believed that their hearts should become humble-submissive (*takhshaʿa*) at the remembrance of Allah ... and that they not become like those who were given the Scripture before, whose time was prolonged, so their hearts hardened, and many of them are defiantly disobedient." Here hardening is the *temporal* failure mode of believers — the longer revelation is held without humility, the harder the heart gets.

### 3.6 DISEASE (maraḍ)

See §4 below — the single most important formula.

### 3.7 DEVIATE (zāgha)

Q 3:7 (*fī qulūbihim zaygh* — "in their hearts is crookedness" — the *zaygh* is the hermeneutical distortion, following the *mutashābih* verses for discord), Q 9:117, Q 61:5, Q 66:4 (*ṣaghat qulūbukumā* — "your hearts have deviated" — addressed to the Prophet's wives).

### 3.8 BLIND (ʿamiya)

Q 22:46 — *fa-innahā lā taʿmā l-abṣāru wa-lākin taʿmā l-qulūbu llatī fī ṣ-ṣudūr* — "it is not eyes that are blinded, but blinded are the hearts within the breasts." **This is the *defining* heart-metaphysics verse**: sight is internal, and what is "in the breast" (ṣadr) is the seat of seeing. Note the nesting: *qulūb fī ṣ-ṣudūr* — hearts *inside* breasts. Ṣadr is the larger container; qalb is the perceptual organ inside it. The Quran embeds one heart-term inside another grammatically.

### 3.9 GUIDE (hadā)

Q 64:11 — *man yuʾmin bi-llāhi yahdi qalbahu* — "whoever believes in Allah, He will guide his heart." Belief precedes guidance of the heart — a sequencing that undercuts determinism: the heart is guided *in response to* prior faith, not prior to it.

### 3.10 JOIN / UNITE (allafa)

Q 3:103, Q 8:63 — *allafa bayna qulūbihim* — "He joined between their hearts." The social miracle: the heart is not only the seat of divine-human relation but the organ of human-human reconciliation. Q 8:63: "if you had spent all that is in the earth, you could not have joined between their hearts — but Allah joined between them."

### 3.11 OPENED / EXPANDED (sharaḥa) — Applied to *ṣadr*, NOT *qalb*

Q 6:125, Q 20:25, Q 39:22, Q 94:1 — the verb *sharaḥa* ("expand, open") is applied exclusively to *ṣadr* (breast/chest), never to *qalb*. This is a **terminology split** (see §6).

## 4. The "Disease in Hearts" Formula (marad fī qulūbihim): 10 Occurrences

Every instance of *fī qulūbihim maraḍ* ("in their hearts is disease") or its variants occurs in a **Medinan** sura:

| Verse | Sura Type | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Q 2:10 | Medinan | Hypocrites, opening of Baqara |
| Q 5:52 | Medinan | Those "hastening" into alliance with disbelievers |
| Q 8:49 | Medinan | Hypocrites at Badr |
| Q 9:125 | Medinan | Hypocrites at Tabūk, disease increases them in evil |
| Q 22:53 | Medinan | "Those in whose hearts is disease and those hard of heart" |
| Q 24:50 | Medinan | "Is there disease in their hearts or have they doubted?" |
| Q 33:12 | Medinan | Hypocrites at Trench (Aḥzāb) |
| Q 33:32 | Medinan | Prophet's wives warned re: men "in whose heart is disease" |
| Q 33:60 | Medinan | Hypocrites and "rumour-spreaders" of Madina |
| Q 47:29 | Medinan | Hypocrites in Muhammad |

**Verification: all 10 Medinan.** The formula is absent from the entire Meccan corpus. This is not incidental: the *hypocrite* (munāfiq) category is itself a Medinan emergent — you cannot have a hypocrite where there is no visible community to hide inside. The *marad fī qulūbihim* formula is the lexical marker of that sociological reality.

Grammatically, *fī qulūbihim maraḍ* is always a predication of *concealed* interior state ("in their hearts is disease") — never a verbal action. The disease is not *done* to the heart; it is *located* in the heart. This differs from seal (*khatama*, an action) and softening (*anzala s-sakīna*, an action). Disease is a latent container-state that Allah either exposes (Q 47:29: "would Allah never expose their feelings of hatred?") or increases (Q 2:10: "Allah has increased their disease").

## 6. Qalb vs Ṣadr vs Fuʾād vs Lubb — The Heart-Terminology System

The Quran distinguishes **four** Arabic heart-terms with non-overlapping domains. A corpus-level census:

### 6.1 قلب Qalb (168 occurrences, 155 verses, 132 nominal)

The *theological* heart: locus of faith, disease, sealing, reassurance, hardening. When the Quran speaks of what Allah *does to* the interior (seal, soften, fill with tranquillity, fill with terror, turn, guide), it uses *qalb/qulūb*. Qalb is the **agent-patient term**: the heart is always being-acted-upon by Allah or by its own owner.

### 6.2 صدر Ṣadr (46 occurrences, 43 verses)

The **breast/chest** — the larger enclosure. Domains:

- **Expansion-for-Islam** (*sharḥ aṣ-ṣadr*): Q 6:125, 20:25, 39:22, 94:1. The verb *sharaḥa* NEVER appears with *qalb* — only with *ṣadr*.
- **Concealment**: "what the breasts conceal" (*mā tuṣīru aṣ-ṣudūr*): Q 3:29, 3:119, 3:154, 5:7, 8:43, 11:5, 27:74, 28:69, 29:10, 31:23, 35:38, 39:7, 40:19, 57:6, 64:4, 67:13, 100:10 — a huge set.
- **Constriction vs ease**: Q 7:2, 11:12, 15:97, 26:13 — "let not your breast be constrained."
- **Container-of-resentment**: Q 7:43, 15:47 — "We will remove whatever is in their breasts of resentment" (paradise).
- **Healing**: Q 10:57 — "healing for what is in the breasts."
- **Whispering site**: Q 114:5 — "who whispers [evil] into the breasts of mankind."

Ṣadr is the **container that knows secrets** — what God sees that humans hide. It is also where the Prophet's physical chest-opening occurs (Q 94:1: Moses asks to have his *ṣadr* expanded in Q 20:25; the Prophet's *ṣadr* is already opened in Q 94:1). The *ṣadr* is thus the space prepared to receive revelation — *qalb* is what lives inside the ṣadr and does the receiving.

Q 22:46's formulation **nests them**: *al-qulūb allatī fī ṣ-ṣudūr* — "the hearts that are within the breasts." This is the Quran's own explicit anatomy.

### 6.3 فؤاد Fuʾād (16 occurrences, 15 verses, all singular or plural *afʾida*)

The **perceptual/witnessing** heart. Domains:

- **Sensorium triad**: fuʾād appears in *samʿ + baṣar + fuʾād* lists (Q 16:78, 17:36, 23:78, 32:9, 46:26, 67:23) — heart as an *epistemic faculty* parallel to hearing and sight.
- **Binding/firmness**: Q 11:120, 14:37, 25:32, 28:10 — "*We make firm your heart*" (*nuthabbitu bihi fuʾādaka*). When the Quran describes firming the Prophet against doubt, it uses *fuʾād*, not *qalb*.
- **Witnessing**: Q 53:11 — *mā kadhaba l-fuʾādu mā raʾā* — "the heart did not lie [about] what it saw." This is the only Quranic description of the Prophet's direct vision (Night Journey / lote-tree event). The seeing-heart is *fuʾād*, not *qalb*.
- **Emptying** (vulnerability): Q 28:10 — Moses's mother's *fuʾād* became empty; Q 14:43 — Resurrection terror empties fuʾāds (*afʾidatuhum hawāʾ*).

Fuʾād = the heart as **witness and carrier of conviction**. It is what can be strengthened or emptied. Where *qalb* is sealed by disbelief, *fuʾād* is *emptied* by terror — a different kind of failure.

### 6.4 لبّ Lubb (16 occurrences, ALL PLURAL *albāb*, always in the construct *ulū l-albāb*)

The **intellect-heart** — the pure discerning interior. Domains:

- Only ever in the phrase **"O people of understanding"** (*yā ulī l-albāb*): Q 2:179, 2:197, 2:269, 3:7, 3:190, 5:100, 12:111, 13:19, 14:52, 38:29, 38:43, 39:9, 39:18, 39:21, 40:54, 65:10.
- *Lubb* never takes an action-verb, never is the grammatical object, never appears singular. It is a *category of people*, not a thing-acted-upon.
- Co-occurs with: *dhikrā* (reminder), *ʿibra* (lesson), *ḥikma* (wisdom), *tadabbur* (reflection).

The Quran effectively says: **qalb** is what Allah acts on; **ṣadr** is the container that hides/reveals; **fuʾād** is what perceives directly; **lubb** is what has already arrived at discernment. Four distinct stages/aspects of one interiority.

**Classical precedent**: Al-Ḥakīm al-Tirmidhī's *Bayān al-Farq bayna aṣ-Ṣadr wa al-Qalb wa al-Fuʾād wa al-Lubb* (c. 3rd AH) proposed a nested onion: ṣadr outside, then qalb, then fuʾād, then lubb at the innermost. The Quranic data is largely consistent with his scheme, though the Quran uses *fuʾād* somewhat more for *active perception* than Tirmidhī's "seat of maʿrifa." Al-Ghazālī's *Iḥyāʾ* Book 21 (*Sharḥ ʿAjāʾib al-Qalb*) builds on this: the qalb has two faces, one toward the unseen (spiritual) and one toward the bodily. Ibn al-Qayyim distinguishes three heart-states in *al-Fawāʾid* (a schema derivative from the nafs-states — see §8).

## 7. Q 50:37 — The Thesis Verse

*Inna fī dhālika la-dhikrā li-man kāna lahu qalb aw alqā s-samʿa wa-huwa shahīd*
"Indeed in that is a reminder for whoever has a *qalb*, or casts [forward] the ear while he is present [as witness]."

This verse is uniquely powerful for three reasons:

1. **Indefinite *qalb***: the only verse where *qalb* is the *subject* of having ("whoever *has* a heart"). Elsewhere, hearts are always possessed plural-collectives (*their hearts, our hearts*). Here, it is the bare condition: "to have a heart" = to be capable of receiving reminder.
2. **Heart OR hearing**: the verse splits receptive capacity into two minimal instruments — either you have *qalb* (inward organ), or you *lend an ear while present*. Reception requires either interior or focused attention. Nothing else.
3. **Locus of "dhikrā"**: *dhikrā* (reminder) lands on the qalb. This is the **pre-condition** for the Q 13:28 phenomenon: hearts that rest *at* remembrance must first *have* enough reality to receive it.

Q 50:37 is thus the silent axiom behind the whole dhikr-qalb cluster. It defines *being human* as *having a heart capable of admonition*.

## 8. Heart-States ↔ Nafs-States Correspondence

The Quran names three explicit *nafs* (soul) states:

1. **al-nafs al-ammāra bi-s-sūʾ** — the soul commanding to evil (Q 12:53)
2. **al-nafs al-lawwāma** — the self-reproaching soul (Q 75:2)
3. **al-nafs al-muṭmaʾinna** — the reassured/tranquil soul (Q 89:27)

These correspond **exactly** to three dominant qalb-states mapped from the corpus:

| Nafs-State | Corresponding Qalb-State | Verse Exemplar |
|---|---|---|
| *ammāra* (commanding evil) | **maṭbūʿ / makhtūm** (sealed/stamped), **qāsiya** (hardened) | Q 2:7, 6:43, 47:16 |
| *lawwāma* (self-reproaching) | **qāliba** / **muṣība bi-maraḍ** (deviating, in disease, oscillating) | Q 3:7 zaygh; Q 9:45 doubt; Q 24:50 |
| *muṭmaʾinna* (tranquil) | **muṭmaʾinna** (same root!) — Q 13:28 | Q 13:28, 22:35, 57:16 |

The *same Arabic root* — **ṭ-m-ʾ-n** — is used for both the tranquil soul (Q 89:27) and the tranquil heart (Q 13:28). This is **not a coincidence**: the soul reaches *iṭmiʾnān* precisely *because* the heart does so first via *dhikr*. Q 13:28 is thus the *mechanism verse* for Q 89:27's final reception. The tranquil soul of the Afterlife is the tranquillised heart of this life grown into its final name.

The intermediate state — *lawwāma* / diseased-wavering heart — is the Medinan hypocrite's condition, which is why *all* disease-in-hearts formulas are Medinan. The hypocrite has a *lawwāma nafs* *without* turning toward dhikr; the result is oscillation without rest (*mudhabdhabīna bayna dhālika*, Q 4:143 — "wavering between").

## 9. The Belief/Disbelief Container Grammar

The heart is the **receptacle**. But the grammar differs systematically:

- **Allah as active subject, heart as patient**: *anzala fī qulūbihim* (sent down into), *alqā fī qulūbihim* (cast into), *allafa bayna qulūbihim* (joined between), *khatama ʿalā qulūbihim* (sealed upon), *ṭabaʿa ʿalā qulūbihim* (stamped upon), *zāda fī qulūbihim* (increased in).
- **Human as active subject, heart as patient**: *aqsat qulūbuhum* (their hearts hardened — intransitive/middle), *ṣaghat qulūbukumā* (your hearts deviated), *ḥamalat qulūbuhum* (their hearts bore).
- **Heart as logical subject (intransitive verbs)**: *taṭmaʾinnu l-qulūb* (hearts rest), *wajilat qulūbuhum* (hearts tremble), *qulūbuhum wajifa* (hearts throb).

Pattern detected: when Allah is the agent, the preposition is **فى/على** (*fī* — into/in, or *ʿalā* — upon). When hearts are subjects of their own state, the verbs are *intransitive* (*ṭ-m-ʾ-n*, *w-j-l*, *q-s-w*). The Quran thereby encodes a two-way grammar: receptive hearts take prepositions (God pours in); expressive hearts conjugate (they themselves tremble or rest). **The grammatical voice tracks the theological agency.**

## 10. The Q 13:28 Dhikr-Qalb Family

Q 13:28's chiasmus (*taṭmaʾinnu qulūbuhum bi-dhikri llāhi, alā bi-dhikri llāhi taṭmaʾinnu l-qulūb*) is not isolated. It belongs to a family of **6 verses** that link *qalb + dhikr*:

1. **Q 13:28** — hearts rest by remembrance (the chiasmus anchor)
2. **Q 18:28** — "do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance" (negative shadow)
3. **Q 24:37** — "men whom neither commerce nor sale distracts from the remembrance of Allah ... they fear a Day when hearts and eyes will turn about" (dhikr vs taqallub opposition)
4. **Q 39:22** — "whose breast Allah has expanded to Islam ... vs those whose hearts are hardened against the remembrance of Allah" (sharḥ vs qasā on dhikr-axis)
5. **Q 39:23** — "their skins and their hearts soften at the remembrance of Allah" (soft-skin + soft-heart, the Quranic self-description)
6. **Q 57:16** — "has the time not come for believers that their hearts humble at the remembrance of Allah" (temporal imperative)

**Additional penumbra** (qalb + dhakara or similar): Q 2:152 (me-remembering-you remembrance), Q 3:191, Q 33:21, Q 63:9. These don't contain *qalb* lexically but operate in the same semantic field.

The family's internal logic: *dhikr is the mechanism by which hearts move from hardening (Q 39:22) → humble-submission (Q 57:16) → shiver + relax (Q 39:23) → rest (Q 13:28).* The stages are sequenced; the Quran is describing a *process*.

## 12. Three Striking Findings

1. **Disease-hearts are a Medinan sociological marker, grammatically distinct.** All 10 *fī qulūbihim maraḍ* verses are Medinan; all co-occur with hypocrite-contexts; all use locative predication rather than verbal action. The Quran's vocabulary of interior pathology emerges *only* where there is a community visible enough to hide within. This is a structural lexical-theological finding, not merely historical.

2. **The root q-l-b self-demonstrates**: the heart is named after *turning*, and the Quran's verb-actions on the heart are dominated by turning verbs (qallaba, inqalaba, taqallub), and the universal metaphysics of alternation — day/night (Q 24:44), Sleepers' turning (Q 18:18), qibla-turning (Q 2:144), Day-of-turning-faces (Q 33:66) — uses the same root. **Turning is not a heart-accident; it is the heart's essence. God is *al-Muqallib*, the Turner, and heart-theology is turning-theology.**

3. **Four heart-terms, four different grammatical profiles**: *qalb* is a theological patient (seals, disease, tranquillity); *ṣadr* is a secret-container (concealment, expansion); *fuʾād* is a perceptual faculty (in triads with hearing/sight; strengthened or emptied); *lubb* is never singular, never acted-upon — it is *the category of the discerning*, appearing only as *ulū l-albāb*. The Quran does not collapse these. Read together, they form a nested anthropology: *you* (the person) have a *ṣadr* (outer container) inside which is a *qalb* (turning organ); when that heart perceives directly it is called *fuʾād*; when it has matured into steady discernment it earns membership in *ulū l-albāb*. This is a complete psychological-hierarchy encoded at the lexical level, anticipating by three centuries the classical Sufi nested-heart schemas of Tirmidhī and Ghazālī.

## 13. Classical Prior Art (Situating the Reading)

- **Al-Ḥakīm al-Tirmidhī** (d. ~295 AH), *Bayān al-Farq bayna aṣ-Ṣadr wa al-Qalb wa al-Fuʾād wa al-Lubb* — proposes nested onion (ṣadr → qalb → fuʾād → lubb); the Quranic grammatical profile largely vindicates him.
- **Al-Ghazālī**, *Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn* Book 21 (*Sharḥ ʿAjāʾib al-Qalb*) — two-faced heart, upward/downward; names the heart as *the viceregent* that can be cleaned (*mujāhada*) or rusted (*rayn*, picking up Q 83:14).
- **Ibn al-Qayyim**, *Iʿlām al-Muwaqqiʿīn* and *al-Fawāʾid* — three heart-states: *qalb ṣaḥīḥ* (sound), *qalb mayyit* (dead), *qalb marīḍ* (diseased). The last is lifted directly from Medinan hypocrite-verses.
- **Ibn ʿAṭāʾillāh al-Iskandarī**, *Ḥikam* — heart-as-mirror; purification (*tazkiyat al-qalb*) via presence (*ḥuḍūr*) keyed to the Q 50:37 *qalb aw shahīd* formula.

The Quran supplies each of these classical thinkers with distinct raw grammatical-lexical data, and each emphasises one axis of the heart-system they developed from the text.

## 14. Coda: Why q-l-b is #2 Bridge Root

In the graph-theory co-occurrence analysis, *khalq* (creation) is #1 bridge because it connects cosmology ↔ anthropology ↔ resurrection. *q-l-b* is #2 because it connects:

- **Faith-cluster** (ʾ-m-n, dh-k-r, sakīna) ↔ **disbelief-cluster** (kufr, zaygh, khatm)
- **Inner psychology** (qalb, ṣadr, fuʾād) ↔ **outer action** (battle verses, hypocrites at Badr/Uḥud/Trench)
- **Individual state** (Q 50:37) ↔ **community taxonomy** (hypocrites-plural, believers-plural)
- **Present life** (Q 13:28 rest by dhikr) ↔ **Afterlife** (Q 89:27 tranquil soul)

Every major theological axis of the Quran crosses through the heart. Remove *q-l-b*, and the network fragments. That is the topology that makes it #2.
