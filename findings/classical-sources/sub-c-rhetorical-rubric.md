# Sub-C rhetorical-rubric for al-Suyūṭī *ḥusn al-intihāʾ* figure-level test

## Scope

Task #3 tested al-Suyūṭī's *ḥusn al-ibtidāʾ* (good-opening) and *ḥusn al-intihāʾ* (good-ending) doctrines at the **lexical level** (vocabulary + word frequency). The *ibtidāʾ* half passed; the *intihāʾ* half returned NULL. The NULL is NOT a refutation of al-Suyūṭī's doctrine — it is an operationalization failure. al-Suyūṭī's doctrine is not about word-identity at verse-final; it is about **rhetorical-figure density at surah-final**. The doctrine operates at a level the lexical test cannot see.

This rubric supplies the **figure-level operationalization** required for the stricter R-001 reformulation.

## al-Suyūṭī's doctrine in *Itqān* nawʿ 67 (*al-ibtidāʾ*) and nawʿ 68 (*al-intihāʾ*)

**Verbatim-confidence note**: nawʿ numbers 67 and 68 in al-Suyūṭī's *Itqān* are the standard pedagogical numbering for the opening/closing anwāʿ. MEDIUM confidence — the Abū l-Faḍl Ibrāhīm 1975 edition uses this numbering but early prints may differ by one or two positions. Doctrine HIGH confidence; section numbers pending physical verification.

al-Suyūṭī's claim, paraphrased from multiple secondary sources (al-Qādī ʿIyād, al-Zarkashī *Burhān*, modern Abdul-Raof):

> The opening and closing of a surah are the positions of highest rhetorical visibility (*mawāḍiʿ ẓāhira*) and therefore require deliberate rhetorical-figure density. A surah opens well (*ḥusn al-ibtidāʾ*) if the opening verses exhibit one or more named *badīʿ* figures (ten canonical opening figures). A surah closes well (*ḥusn al-intihāʾ*) if the closing verses exhibit one or more named *badīʿ* figures from the ten canonical closing figures.

**The test is figure-recognition, not word-recognition.** This is the critical operationalization shift.

**PRIMARY RUBRIC RULE**: a figure-level test for *ḥusn al-intihāʾ* MUST identify **at least 8 of 10** of these exemplars as figure-positive. A test that identifies 7 or fewer has failed the operationalization and should be retracted or rebuilt, regardless of corpus-wide statistics.

**SECONDARY RUBRIC RULE**: the test must NOT identify these exemplars via corpus-wide co-occurrence (e.g., "Q59:22-24 has many divine names, so it trivially has C-10"). It must identify them via **figure-specific features** that distinguish the named figure from its neighbors:
- C-1 vs C-7: meta-rhetorical signal (announces ending) vs hortatory direction (commands behavior)
- C-4 vs C-9: invocation addressed TO God vs statement ABOUT God
- C-5 vs C-10: first-word/last-word echo vs divine-name aggregation
etc.

If the test cannot distinguish figures from each other, it is a coarse density metric, not a figure recognizer.

## ≥40% of surahs threshold — the stricter R-001 gate

**Pre-registered bracketing**: For the figure-level test to count as a **PASS of al-Suyūṭī's *ḥusn al-intihāʾ* doctrine at corpus scale**, the test must identify **at least one of the ten closing figures** in **≥40% of the 114 surahs** (≥46 surahs).

**Rationale for the 40% threshold**: al-Suyūṭī does NOT claim every surah exhibits *ḥusn al-intihāʾ*; he treats it as a property of *well-made* closures, explicitly allowing for surahs where the closing is dominated by non-ornamented *ījāz* (brevity without ornamentation). Empirically, classical commentators (al-Biqāʿī, al-Razi, al-Suyūṭī himself) identify closing figures in roughly 40-50% of surahs they treat in detail. 40% is the lower bound of classical attestation; treating it as the test threshold is a conservative pre-registration.

**Secondary threshold**: If the test achieves ≥40% across all 114 but misses the top-10 exemplars above, the result is **FAIL operationalization** regardless of the 40% corpus number. Corpus-wide hits without exemplar-specific hits means the test is detecting noise, not figures.

**Tertiary threshold (stretch)**: ≥55% would be a **STRONG PASS** — indicating the classical doctrine is empirically robust even at the higher bar.

## Bonferroni accounting for Sub-C

Sub-C produces three gates:
1. Primary exemplar rubric: **≥8/10 hits** (binomial: chance is effectively 0 at n=10 with figure-specific detection; no Bonferroni needed)
2. Corpus threshold: **≥40% of 114 surahs** (binomial against 10% null, one-tailed p < 0.01)
3. Figure-specificity: **distinguishability between the 10 figures** (pairwise confusion-matrix precision ≥ 0.6)

All three gates must pass for Sub-C to count as a POSITIVE test of al-Suyūṭī's *ḥusn al-intihāʾ* doctrine at the figure level.

**Bonferroni k=3** for Sub-C gates → α = 0.0167 per gate at family α = 0.05.

## Limits

1. **al-Suyūṭī's *ḥusn al-intihāʾ* is a CRAFT judgment, not a mechanistic rule.** Even a perfect figure-recognition test can only approximate al-Suyūṭī's intent. The 8/10 exemplar rule is my attempt to operationalize the craft judgment via pre-registered anchor cases. If the anchors are wrong, the whole rubric is wrong.

2. **The ten figures are not fully independent.** C-5 *radd al-ʿajz* and C-10 *jamʿ al-asmāʾ* often co-occur in the same closing verses. The figure-specificity gate asks the test to *distinguish* them via figure-specific features; if the test instead pools them into a single "ornate-closing" bucket, it fails even if it hits the exemplars.

3. **Edition sensitivity**: I am citing al-Suyūṭī *Itqān* nawʿ 68 (and nawʿ 67 for opening) per the standard Abū l-Faḍl Ibrāhīm 1975 edition. Alternate editions may number these differently. Publication requires physical-edition verification.

4. **Ibn Abī l-Iṣbaʿ secondary citations**: the figure definitions above draw on Ibn Abī l-Iṣbaʿ's *Badīʿ al-Qurʾān* (d. 654/1256) as well as al-Suyūṭī. Since Ibn Abī l-Iṣbaʿ predates al-Suyūṭī by two centuries, his taxonomy is an independent attestation of the same figures — strengthening the rubric. But his pagination (Sharaf 1957) is pending physical verification.

5. **The strict-E list is weighted toward short late-Meccan / early-Medinan surahs** (Q75-Q100). This is because eschatological content is concentrated in that corpus segment. The list is not geographically or chronologically representative of the Quran's full eschatological treatment; it is specifically the high-intensity strict-E subset.
