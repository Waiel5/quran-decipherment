# The Honest Limits Ledger

## Preface — why a null-results volume

A research project's integrity is fixed not by the volume of its confirmed
findings but by the fidelity with which it reports its refutations.
Where apologetic literature on the Quran selects its successes and is
silent on its failures, this project publishes both. The rules the
project binds itself to are stated in `docs/statistical-rigor-protocol.md`:
every claim carries a rules tuple (orthography, word-definition,
letter-definition, basmala-policy, verse-numbering, abjad-table,
null-model); every novel finding must pass at least two independent
null models; matched-corpus baselines are drawn from a 13.4 million
token classical Arabic pool (Bukhari ḥadīth with Quran-quote trigrams
stripped; Ibn Hishām's *Sīra*; al-Jāḥiẓ's *Ḥayawān*; the seven
*Muʿallaqāt* and the full *Dīwān*s of Imruʾ al-Qays, Ṭarafa, Zuhayr,
Labīd, ʿAntara, al-Ḥārith and ʿAmr ibn Kulthūm; al-Mutanabbī's Dīwān).
Multiple-comparison control is Bonferroni or Holm step-down over the
pre-stated test family. The Quranic Arabic Corpus (Leeds v0.4) is the
lemma/root source.

This ledger collects, in one place, every claim against which the
project's testing went *against* the claimant. Part I covers modern
numerology. Part II covers *iʿjāz ʿilmī*, the scientific-foreknowledge
literature. Part III covers structural theses from academic Qurʾānic
studies that did not survive our test instruments. Part IV logs
questions the project has not been able to resolve, openly. Part V
enumerates the methodological caveats that qualify every finding —
confirmed or disconfirmed. Part VI states the integrity clause: why
these refutations reinforce rather than undermine the project's
surviving findings.

The locked anchors every count in this ledger respects:

```
114 surahs · 6,236 verses · 77,797 real-word tokens · 330,709 graphemes
(no-tashkeel orthography, U+0621..064A ∪ U+0671..06D3, recitation marks
U+06D6..06ED filtered)
Bismillah: 19 letters, 4 words, abjad (mashriqi) = 786
Lemma support (QAC v0.4): 4,832 distinct lemmas
```

# Part I — Falsified modern numerology claims

## 1. Rashad Khalifa's Code-19

Rashad Khalifa (1935–1990), an Egyptian biochemist, published
*Quran: Visual Presentation of the Miracle* (1982) and *Quran: The
Final Testament* (1989), asserting that a pervasive mathematical
structure based on the integer 19 saturates the text. The movement
drew on Q 74:30 ("Over it is nineteen") as self-referential proof.
The project ran a full audit; the results are in
`findings/phase-a-replications/code19-khalifa-full-audit.md` and its
companion `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/prime-mod-scan.md`. The rules
tuple: no-tashkeel primary (cross-checked against full-tashkeel and
min-tashkeel), grapheme letter definition, QAC-LEM word definition,
basmala-counted-only-in-Surah-1, Hafs-Kufan numbering, Mashriqī abjad,
binomial-divisibility and 1.5-permutation null models.

### 1.1 The surviving anchors are all trivial or small-number coincidences

Five Khalifa Family-A claims do verify under standard counting, but
none carry statistical weight on their own:

- **Bismillah = 19 letters.** Anchor-locked; this is the seed of the
  program, not a derived result.
- **114 surahs = 19 × 6.** Small-integer arithmetic. Under a uniform
  null, the probability that a randomly-chosen small integer divides by
  19 is 1/19 ≈ 5.26%; the datum is consistent with chance.
- **Surah 96 is the 19th from the end.** Trivially follows from 114.
- **Q 74:30 contains the word "nineteen" (تسعة عشر).** A textual fact,
  not a numerical one; the classical tafsīr (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr)
  reads this as the number of angels of Hell. Khalifa's
  self-referential reinterpretation is exegetical and unfalsifiable.
- **al-Raḥmān occurs 57 times = 19 × 3.** One of four basmala-word
  counts claimed; the only one that verifies without textual editing.
  On four tests, the raw Bonferroni-corrected p for one hit is
  4 × (1/19) ≈ 0.21, not significant.

### 1.2 The ALM surah letter counts: comprehensive failure

Khalifa published exact alif/lām/mīm counts for the six ALM-prefixed
surahs in *Final Testament* Appendix 1 (1989). For every one of these
surahs, his alif count falls *between* our no-tashkeel count and our
full-tashkeel count, matching no scribal tradition at all:

| Surah | Khalifa total | no-tashkeel total | full-tashkeel total | Khalifa % 19 | Our % 19 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 9,899 | 10,109 | 9,604 | 0 | 1, 9 |
| 3 | 5,662 | 5,797 | 5,485 | 0 | 2, 13 |
| 29 | 1,672 | 1,703 | 1,603 | 0 | 12, 7 |
| 30 | 1,254 | 1,263 | 1,197 | 0 | 9, 0† |
| 31 | 817 | 851 | 800 | 0 | 15, 2 |
| 32 | 570 | 583 | 548 | 0 | 13, 16 |

† Surah 30 under full-tashkeel gives 1,197 = 19 × 63, but the
multiplier does not match Khalifa's claimed 19 × 66, so this partial
arithmetic hit is not the one he published either.

The pro-Khalifa community (Quran Talk Blog 2020-08-21) responds that
*"the total counts that Rashad provided were divinely revealed by
God, and not the individual numbers he inputed"* — an unfalsifiable
move in which every numerical disconfirmation becomes, by assertion,
a miraculous confirmation that God corrected Khalifa's typographical
errors inside his computer. This is the canonical pattern of an
unfalsifiable program.

### 1.3 The ALM sum per surah: 1-in-29 divisible by 19 (not 29-in-29)

The broad claim that every one of the 29 muqaṭṭaʿāt surahs should have
its opening-letter count sum to a multiple of 19 is tested
surah-by-surah. Only **1 of 29 surahs** (Surah 50, qāf, count = 57)
passes. The binomial expectation under a uniform-residue null is
29 × (1/19) ≈ 1.53; observing 1 has P(X ≥ 1) ≈ 0.79 — dead centre of
the null. The claim that all 29 should pass has p ≈ (1/19)^29 ≈
8 × 10^−38: Khalifa's hypothesis is about 37 orders of magnitude off
from observation.

### 1.4 The basmala word-counts

Of the four basmala-word claims, `ism = 19` is off by 20 under every
natural QAC filter (closest count: 39 LEM instances; no
bi-prefix-only / definite-only / divine-only filter recovers 19).
`Allāh = 2,698` is off by 1 under standard counting and passes *only*
if Q 9:128–129 are deleted — the famous Khalifa controversy. Every
extant manuscript, including the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest, the Birmingham
folio, and the Topkapı codex, contains these two verses.
`al-Raḥīm = 114` is off by 2 under standard counting and off by 1 even
with the 9:128–129 deletion. Of the four claims: one passes, two fail,
one passes only under non-canonical verse deletion. The advertised
"all four divide by 19" result is false.

### 1.5 The grand-total claim

The claim 346,199 = 19² × 959 (sum over surahs of *surah-number +
verses + Σᵢ i* for i = 1..verses) gives 346,458 under canonical 6,236
verse numbering. The difference is exactly 259 — exactly the
contribution of Q 9:128–129. As with the Allāh count, the arithmetic
passes if and only if those two verses are deleted. The deletion is
precisely the size of the discrepancy, which is the load-bearing move.

### 1.6 Other individual letter claims

- **Surah 68 (nūn).** Khalifa reports 133 = 19 × 7; our count is 131.
  To reach 133, Khalifa needs a non-attested spelling نون in the
  initial muqaṭṭaʿa, adding two phantom nūns. This spelling occurs in
  no manuscript. **FAILS.**
- **Surah 19 (kāf-hā-yāʾ-ʿayn-ṣād).** Sum = 740; 740 mod 19 = 18.
  **FAILS.**
- **Surah 42 (ḥā-mīm-ʿayn-sīn-qāf).** Sum = 556; 556 mod 19 = 5.
  **FAILS.**
- **Ḥā-Mīm cluster (Surahs 40–46) joint sum.** 2,112; mod 19 = 16.
  **FAILS.**

### 1.7 The 2,743-verse total across the 29 muqaṭṭaʿāt surahs

2,743 / 19 = 144.37. **FAILS.** No natural 19-arithmetic relates
this number to the Khalifa family.

### 1.8 The qāf-50 / qāf-42 anomaly

The one non-trivial Khalifa claim that survives standard counting: in
Surah 50, qāf occurs 57 = 19 × 3 times; in Surah 42, qāf occurs 57
times; the two-surah sum equals 114 = 19 × 6. Under the locked rules
tuple, these three arithmetic facts all verify. Treating this as a
single joint pre-registered test, we estimate p ≈ 0.001–0.005, which
would survive Bonferroni over the 32 prime-mod-scan tests *if it had
been pre-registered*. It was not; it was found by inspection of
Khalifa's own published list, so under the §3.4 robustness
requirement of the statistical-rigor protocol this is exploratory, not
confirmatory. It is flagged for future pre-registered replication.

### 1.9 The open prime-mod scan

For p ∈ {7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29, 31} and for each per-surah
statistic m ∈ {letters, words, verses, abjad}, we counted how many of
the 114 surahs have m ≡ 0 (mod p). Under the null (uniform residue
mod p), the expected count is 114/p. For p = 19 the four observed
counts are 3, 8, 4, 5 versus expected 6, 6, 6, 6 — no deviation is
statistically significant. The minimum raw p-value across all 32
tests is 0.056 (per-surah words mod 7 and per-surah verses mod 11);
the Bonferroni threshold is 0.05/32 = 0.00156. **Zero tests survive
correction.** The 19-special-status claim has no general support:
p = 19 is not distinguished from any other small prime by any
whole-book metric. The result is consistent with uniform-residue
behaviour under any prime the reader might have tested instead of 19.

### 1.10 Letter-divisibility-by-19 rate at corpus level

Across 15 classical Arabic texts in our baseline pool, the rate at
which individual letters have total counts divisible by 19 ranges
from 0.0% to 11.1%, with the Quran at 5.6% (2 of 36 letters). The
expected random rate is 1/19 ≈ 5.3%. The Quran sits at the median
below both Muʿallaqat al-Labīd (11.1%) and Muʿallaqat ʿAmr ibn
Kulthūm (11.1%). There is no Quran-distinctive 19-signature at this
aggregate.

**Net verdict on Code-19.** Of Khalifa's 22 catalogued family-A
claims: 5 trivially verify; 2 verify only under deletion of canonical
verses; 1 verifies only under a single non-standard orthography with
wrong multiplier; 1 partially verifies (letter count under
full-tashkeel; word count fails under every tokenisation); 13 fail
outright. The qāf-50/42/57 triangle is flagged for pre-registered
follow-up. No peer-reviewed McKay-style audit existed; this is, to
our knowledge, the first.

## 2. Al-Kaheel and Nawfal word-pair claims

ʿAbd al-Dāʾim al-Kaheel (kaheel7.com) and ʿAbd al-Razzāq Nawfal
(*al-Iʿjāz al-ʿadadī li-l-Qurʾān*, 1983) catalogued ~15 famous
"symmetric word-pair" claims — distinct lemmas that supposedly occur
the same number of times in the Quran. Caner Taslaman (*The Quran:
Unchallengeable Miracle*, 2006) reprised the list. The project tested
every famous pair against the QAC lemma and root counts and computed
a baseline-corpus denominator for "how many such coincidences exist
by chance." Primary file:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/word-pair-symmetry.md`. Baseline file:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/cross-textual-baseline.md`.

### 2.1 The McKay denominator: 2,817 tied root-pairs

The root-cartographer agent enumerated all unordered pairs of
*distinct* roots in the Leeds QAC whose total occurrence counts are
exactly equal with both ≥ 10. There are **2,817 such root-pairs** in
the Quran. At the word-token level the denominator is **16,997
tied pairs**. In length-matched 77,797-token slices of comparable
Arabic, the word-token-pair denominator is **10,860 (Sīra) to 13,177
(Bukhari) to 13,157 (Jāḥiẓ)** — same order of magnitude. Anyone
selecting a single specific pair is selecting from a population of
10⁴ such coincidences; choosing one as miraculous is a selection bias
of the same kind McKay, Bar-Natan, Bar-Hillel & Kalai (1999)
diagnosed in the Bible Codes.

### 2.2 Famous-pair verdicts

| Pair | Claim | QAC observation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| yawm / layl | = 365 (solar year) | yawm lemma 405; layl+layla 92 | FAILS |
| rajul / imraʾa | both = 24 | rajul 29; imraʾa 26 | FAILS |
| baḥr / barr | 32/13 (water/land ratio) | baḥr 41; barr 22 | FAILS |
| al-dunyā / al-ākhira | both = 115 | no 115 at any lemma level | FAILS |
| malāʾika / shayāṭīn | both = 88 | malak 88; shayṭān 88 | VERIFIES |
| al-ḥayāt / al-mawt | both = 145 | ḥayāt 76; mawt 50 | FAILS |
| ṣāliḥāt / sayyiʾāt | symmetric | ṣāliḥ 65; sayyiʾ-family 62 | PARTIAL |
| iblīs / malāʾika | symmetric | iblīs 11; malak 88 | FAILS |
| "seven heavens" as phrase | appears 7× | 7 exact | VERIFIES as phrase |
| qul / qāla | both = 332 | filter-dependent | PARTIAL |
| Ādam / ʿĪsā | both = 25 | Ādam 25; ʿĪsā 25 | VERIFIES |

Of 11 tested famous claims, **2 fully verify at lemma level**
(malak/shayṭān 88/88; Ādam/ʿĪsā 25/25), **2 partially verify** under
strict filters (qul/qāla; ṣāliḥāt/sayyiʾāt), **1 verifies as a phrase
rather than a lemma** (seven heavens), and **6 fail outright**. Given
the 2,817-pair denominator, two clean coincidences out of ~15
tested is **below the Bonferroni-expected rate**.

### 2.3 Expected hits under chance

The computation: we pre-select ~15 semantically related pairs. Each
pair has probability ≈ (2,817 / C(1,642, 2)) ≈ 2,817 / 1,347,161 ≈
0.00209 of being a tied pair at random root-selection. Over 15
selections the expected number of accidental ties is 15 × 0.00209 ≈
0.031 — but this computation assumes uniform random pair selection.
In practice the pairs are selected *after* inspecting the data, which
collapses the McKay denominator back down to the raw tie-count: any
tied pair with semantic resonance can be retrospectively framed as a
"symmetry miracle." Under this more honest framing, the expected
number of semantically-resonant ties among 2,817 pairs is bounded
only by human pattern-matching capacity, which is effectively
unbounded. The observed count (2–4 semantically-striking pairs) is
thus consistent with chance.

Interpretation: the Al-Kaheel/Nawfal pair-program survives only in
the strong sense of "two verifiable coincidences exist"
(malak/shayṭān, Ādam/ʿĪsā). It does not survive as evidence of
design. The other 9 famous pairs fail under hands-off QAC counting.

## 3. Yūsuf-sjn = 12 ("the thematic anchor")

The root-cartographer agent's candidate headline finding: the root
*s-j-n* (prison) appears 12 times in the Quran, all 12 in Surah 12
(Yūsuf). Surah 12 narrates Joseph's imprisonment. The triple
coincidence of *count = surah-index = thematic-location* looked
striking.

The test: chop each baseline corpus into 114 chunks whose sizes match
the Quranic surah-length distribution. For each frequency N, how
often does a word-type appearing exactly N times land entirely in
one chunk?

| f | Quran | bukhari-77k | sīra-77k | jāḥiẓ-77k | poetry (84k) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 0.5 % | 5.4 % | 6.7 % | 3.0 % | 1.8 % |
| 6 | 0.7 % | 2.5 % | 6.2 % | 2.5 % | 2.1 % |
| 8 | 0.0 % | 1.0 % | 2.1 % | 1.3 % | 5.4 % |
| 10 | 0.0 % | 0.0 % | 1.4 % | 0.0 % | 3.0 % |
| **12** | **0.0 %** | 0.0 % | **4.5 %** | 0.0 % | 1.5 % |

**The Quran's single-chunk-concentration rate at every frequency from
5 to 20 is lower than or equal to the rate in length-matched Arabic
prose.** Sīra ibn Hishām, at f = 12, has 4.5 % single-chunk words —
i.e., if you chop the Sīra into 114 surah-shaped pieces, 2 of the 44
word-types occurring exactly 12 times will, by chance, land all 12
occurrences in one chunk. The Yūsuf-sjn finding is one such
single-chunk event.

The reason is simple: Quranic surahs are thematically coherent by
design. A surah *about Joseph in prison* will naturally concentrate
prison-vocabulary. *khaybar* in the *Sīra* is similarly concentrated
in the Khaybar-campaign chapter; no one calls that miraculous because
no one is scanning the *Sīra* for miracles. The *count = surah-index*
layer (both equal 12) is a 1/114 coincidence on top of a pattern that
is already expected. **The claim is demoted.**

## 4. rahma = 114

The root-cartographer agent surfaced: among the 4,832 distinct QAC
lemmas, exactly one has count 114 — `raḥomap` (raḥma, mercy). 114 is
the number of surahs. The Quran self-describes as *raḥmatan
lil-ʿālamīn* ("a mercy to the worlds," Q 21:107). Candidate headline
finding. The project tested it rigorously in
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/rahma-114-baseline-rigor.md`.

### 4.1 Baseline rate: 34.1%

In 1,000 random contiguous 77,797-token slices of the 1.15-million-
token (non-Quran) classical Arabic pool, **34.1% of slices have
exactly one word-type at count 114**. In length-matched 77k slices of
each of bukhari-77k, sīra-77k, jāḥiẓ-77k and the poetry pool, *every
slice* has exactly one word-type at count 114. Uniqueness-at-114 is
a one-in-three property of random 77k Arabic slices.

### 4.2 The Quran's distribution of singleton-at-count lemmas

Of the Quran's 181 distinct lemma-counts, **89 are singletons** — "a
lemma is unique at its count" is true for 49.2 % of the count
distribution. The question "is the surah count 114 privileged?" falls
flat: if the surah count had been 110, 115, 116 or 119 the claim
would equally work and give a different (likewise semantically-weighty)
lemma — Ẓāliman (oppression), al-dunyā (worldly life),
al-Raḥīm (the Merciful), al-mubīn (manifest). The parameter is
post-hoc.

### 4.3 Famous-number overlap: Quran under-delivers

Under a hypergeometric null (89 Quran-singleton-at-count lemmas
distributed over 181 distinct counts, 10 of which are "famous" —
{7, 12, 19, 28, 30, 40, 77, 99, 114, 147, 313, 365, 786} with 10 in
the supported range), the expected number of overlaps is **4.92**;
observed is **2** (at N = 99 and N = 114). The Quran
under-delivers, not over-delivers, on famous-number singletons.

### 4.4 Mercy is not Quran-distinctive

The *r-ḥ-m* root-token rate per 77k tokens: Quran ~103 (strict
rahma-forms); matched-bukhari-77k 246 (2.4× higher, almost entirely
from ḥadīth-opening basmala formulae); sīra-77k ~100 (comparable).
Mercy is a normal classical-religious-register Arabic word.

### 4.5 Bonferroni-corrected verdict

Against the 13 famous numbers, the corrected p-value for "the Quran
has a unique lemma at 114" is **1.000**. The raw per-N p under the
empirical null is 0.341. The claim is **demoted from a candidate
headline finding to a base-rate coincidence** and removed from the
"top 10 striking coincidences" list. The semantic weightiness of
the word *raḥma* cannot carry the numerical claim alone, because no
quantitative null for semantic-centrality exists.

## 5. The 147 triple (ghayr / ilāh / janna)

In `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/word-pair-symmetry.md` we noted that
three distinct lemmas — *ghayr* (other), *ilāh* (deity), *janna*
(garden/paradise) — each occur 147 times in the Quran. The phrase
*lā ilāha ghayruhū* ("there is no god other than Him") is a core
tawḥīd formula; *janna* is the thematic destination of reward. The
observation was tentatively flagged.

The cross-textual-baseline test shows the Quran has 16,997 tied
word-pairs at frequency ≥ 10, and 83 count-buckets with ≥ 2 lemmas
among the Quran's ~988 frequent word-types. The probability that
*any* specific count value will host ≥ 3 lemmas (a "triple") is
non-trivial by pigeonhole alone. In matched-bukhari-77k there are
10,860–13,177 tied pairs and a comparable density of triples. The
147-triple is **killed at baseline** — matched Arabic has
approximately the same order of tied-pair density as the Quran, and
a triple at some specific count is a pigeonhole certainty.

The *content* observation (the formula *lā ilāha ghayruhū*) is a
real rhetorical feature of the Quran, but "three distinct words each
occurring 147 times" carries no weight beyond the ambient tied-pair
rate of comparable Arabic prose.

## 6. al-Rāzī's muqaṭṭaʿāt = 99 divine-names theory

Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī (d. 606/1210) in his *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* raised
the hypothesis (attributed to earlier theologians) that the 14
muqaṭṭaʿāt letters are an index into the 99 divine names. The test:
of the 99 names, 66 begin with a "luminous" (muqaṭṭaʿāt) letter. A
naive binomial test gives p = 0.000593 — highly significant.

Proper null (random 14-letter subset of the 28-letter alphabet, re-
sampled 10⁵ times): the p-value rises to **0.139** — not significant.
The reason: mīm alone accounts for 136 % of the excess because the
Arabic participial prefix *mu-* over-samples for content nouns; mīm
is a high-frequency content-initial letter and its inclusion in the
muqaṭṭaʿāt set is almost sufficient to explain the overlap. The
effect dissolves under a properly-bounded subset null. The
divine-name-abbreviation theory is **REJECTED**. The muqaṭṭaʿāt are
a frequent-letter signature, not a divine-name index. File:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/razi-99names-test.md`.

## 7. Surah-length-predicts-Meccan-origin Zipf hypothesis (H14)

The hypothesis: per-surah Zipf α heterogeneity should predict
Meccan > Medinan (shorter, denser surahs = higher α). Test:
per-surah Zipf α versus Nöldekean chronology. Result: ρ(α, surah
length) = +0.962 — α is essentially length-determined. The Nöldeke
phase-means rise monotonically **through the Meccan period** — the
exact inverse of the proposed direction. The hypothesis is rejected
as a length artifact. File:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/zipf-per-surah.md`.

## 8. "Meccan saj' is denser than Medinan" (folk wisdom)

A widespread claim in introductory balāgha literature: Meccan surahs
are more rhyme-dense than Medinan. Our rhyme-uniformity metric under
label permutation (10⁴ permutations of the Meccan/Medinan labels)
gives p > 0.3 for every density metric tested. The intuition is
**FALSE as stated**. The folk wisdom operates through verse brevity
(Meccan mean 8.10 words/verse versus Medinan 16.93 words/verse):
shorter verses force rhyme closer together, but the per-verse rhyme-
placement density is no higher. File:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/saj-rhyme-analysis.md`.

## 9. Khalifa's aggregate Zipf claim

The Quranic Zipf exponent (α) under standard orthographic-token
counting is 0.97; prose baselines span 0.94 (Jāḥiẓ) to 1.07 (Bukhari).
The Quran is **not distinctive** on this measure at the standard
rule. Earlier information-theory work in the project reported α = 1.318
using a QAC-lemma convention — a different counting rule. The
discrepancy is flagged transparently in
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/cross-textual-baseline.md`: the choice
of tokenisation asymmetry between the Quran (lemma) and baselines
(orthographic token) is a forking-paths decision; under the same rule
the Quran is not special.

## 9b. "sabʿ samāwāt appears exactly 7 times" (classical folk-tradition) — RETRACTED 2026-04-17

A widely-recited classical-adjacent numerological observation claims
that the phrase *sabʿ samāwāt* ("seven heavens") appears **exactly 7
times** in the Quran — a self-referential "seven-times-seven" coincidence
sometimes cited in popular *iʿjāz* discourse and folk tradition. The
project's 90-claim classical-quantitative audit (2026-04-13) listed it
as CONFIRMED on the strength of earlier informal tallies.

Under a proper lexical audit conducted 2026-04-17 (`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/h-new-119-seven-fold.md`,
Cell C1), the claim does not hold. Under the strict Arabic reading
`سبع + سماء/سماوات` (seven + singular/plural "heaven"), the corpus
contains **5** occurrences:

- Q 2:29, Q 41:12, Q 65:12, Q 67:3, Q 71:15.

Under an extended cosmic-synonym reading (adds *سبع شداد* "seven firm
ones", *سبع طرائق* "seven pathways", and variants at Q 12:48, Q 23:17,
Q 78:12), the count is **8**. The rule tuple determines whether the
answer is 5 or 8; no defensible lexical rule yields 7.

The "exactly 7" tally is a folk-convergence — a cultural projection of
7-symbolism onto an iconic phrase, not a textual fact. The underlying
classical cosmological doctrine of the seven heavens (*al-samāwāt
al-sabʿ* as an ontological claim about a seven-layer cosmos; Q 65:12
the locus classicus) is **untouched** by this retraction. What is
retracted is only the specific claim that the phrase *sabʿ samāwāt*
occurs "exactly 7 times." The doctrine survives; the numerological
count does not.

This retraction is consistent with M-5 classical-doctrine decomposition
pattern (*MASTER-FINDINGS-LEDGER.md* §6 item 7 / H-META-1 item #5):
classical doctrines typically decompose into structural/thematic
sub-claims that survive audit and numerological sub-claims that fail
it. The *samāwāt al-sabʿ* doctrine is the former; the "exactly 7
occurrences" sub-claim is the latter.

# Part II — Failed *iʿjāz ʿilmī* (scientific-foreknowledge) claims

The *iʿjāz ʿilmī* movement traces to Maurice Bucaille's *La Bible, le
Coran et la science* (1976), amplified by Keith L. Moore's embryology
endorsements in the 1980s, the Turkish publisher Harun Yahya / Adnan
Oktar, and the Egyptian geologist Zaghloul al-Naggar. The movement
asserts that the Quran contains specific descriptions of natural
phenomena unavailable to 7th-century Arabia. The project's
rigorous audit is in
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/scientific-foreknowledge.md` and
subsidiary files
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/embryology-audit.md`,
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/cosmology-audit.md`,
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/earth-sciences-audit.md`, and
`findings/phase-c-structures/hadid-deep-dive.md`. The critique draws
on Pervez Hoodbhoy (*Islam and Science*, 1991), Taner Edis (*An
Illusion of Harmony*, 2007), Nidhal Guessoum (*Islam's Quantum
Question*, 2011), Basim Musallam (*Sex and Society in Islam*, 1983),
Andrew Rippin (*The Qur'an and Its Interpretative Tradition*, 2001),
and Angelika Neuwirth's Corpus Coranicum programme.

The two framing points the audit holds throughout: (a) 7th-century
Mecca was not a scientific vacuum — Syriac-speaking Christians in
Najrān and Mesopotamia had translated Galen and Aristotle, Jewish
rabbinic learning was present in Medina, Sassanid Persian natural
philosophy was accessible through trade, and pre-Islamic Arabic
poetry is rich in astronomical, zoological and meteorological
imagery; (b) the apologetic literature is selection-biased — it
quotes the verses that look prescient after modern science caught
up and is silent about those that look odd by the same literal
reading (sperm produced between backbone and ribs, Q 86:6–7; sun
setting in a muddy spring, Q 18:86; stars as missiles against
eavesdropping jinn, Q 67:5; seven layered earths, Q 65:12; a
creature-of-the-earth eating Solomon's staff, Q 34:14).

## 10. Embryology (Q 23:12–14, 22:5, 39:6, 76:2)

The apologetic reading (Bucaille; Moore 1982) maps the Quranic
sequence *nuṭfa → ʿalaqa → muḍgha → ʿiẓām → laḥm* onto modern
embryology.

Basim Musallam (*Sex and Society in Islam*, 1983) documents that
this is **Galenic inheritance, in native Arabic dress**. Galen (*On
the Formation of the Foetus*, *On Semen*) articulated a four-stage
embryology — semen ("like milk") → sanguineous mass → unformed flesh
→ articulation — that became dominant in Greek, Syriac, Latin and
Arabic medicine. The mapping:

- *nuṭfa* ↔ Galen stage 1 (semen / two-seed mixture). Direct.
- *ʿalaqa* (blood-clot sense) ↔ Galen stage 2 (sanguineous mass). Direct.
- *muḍgha* (chewed lump of flesh) ↔ Galen stage 3 (unformed flesh). Direct.
- *ʿiẓām* then *laḥm* ↔ Galen stage 4 (articulation / ossification).

Galenic embryology was in Syriac medical transmission through the
School of Nisibis and the Nestorian missions; Mecca, as a trade hub,
had access. The *amshāj* ("mingled") language of Q 76:2 is exactly
Galenic two-seed theory (male and female fluids mingling), glossed
that way by classical mufassirūn including al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī and
al-Qurṭubī — not as sperm-ovum fusion.

The ordering *bones-then-flesh* (*ʿiẓām* then *laḥm*) is a
specifically scriptural twist and does not match modern embryology,
where mesoderm differentiation produces myogenesis and osteogenesis
concurrently; literal readings of this ordering yield an inaccuracy
the apologetic literature does not address. Verdict:
**PRE-EXISTING KNOWLEDGE + partially inaccurate**. The claim of
miraculous modernity is refuted by the availability of Galen in the
7th-century milieu and by the partial mismatch with modern staging.

## 11. Iron "sent down" (Q 57:25)

> "We sent down iron, wherein is great might and benefits for
> humankind…" (*anzalnā al-ḥadīda fīhi baʾsun shadīdun wa-manāfiʿu
> lil-nāsi*).

Apologetic reading (Bucaille; Naggar): iron arrives via meteoritic
delivery from supernovae; the Arabic *anzala* ("sent down") literally
describes cosmic nucleosynthesis.

Counter-analysis (`phase-c-structures/hadid-deep-dive.md`):

1. *anzala* is the standard Quranic verb for divine grant of any
   resource — rain (Q 2:22), sustenance (Q 6:99), cattle (Q 39:6),
   clothing (Q 7:26), scales of justice (Q 57:25 in the same verse),
   the Torah (Q 3:3) and the Quran itself (Q 2:23). It is not
   specifically cosmological; everything God provides is "sent down."
   Reading only *iron* as cosmic while cattle and cloth are
   metaphorical is cherry-picking.
2. Surah 57 is numbered 57; the iron atomic number is 26; the
   apologetic swapping between "al-ḥadīd is surah 57" and "iron is
   Fe-26" and "Fe-57 is one of four stable isotopes" is a classic
   survivor-bias selection. Fe-56 is the dominant isotope (91.75 %),
   not Fe-57. The one statistic matching the surah number was
   selected after the fact.
3. Meteoritic iron was well-known to the 7th-century world: the
   Ancient Egyptians called meteoritic iron *biʿja-en-pet* ("metal of
   the sky"); Tutankhamun's iron dagger is meteoritic. The observation
   that iron can "come from the sky" in the narrow sense of meteorite-
   fall is ancient background knowledge, not modern foreknowledge.

Verdict: **APOLOGETIC OVERREACH**.

## 12. Big Bang (Q 21:30)

> "Did those who disbelieve not see that the heavens and the earth
>  were a closed-up mass (*ratqan*), then We parted them (*fa-fataqnā*)…"

Apologetic reading (Bucaille): the Quran anticipates Lemaître's
primeval-atom cosmology. Counter: *ratq* and *fatq* are a
cosmogonic-split motif found throughout Near Eastern religion —
Genesis 1 (separation of waters), Enūma Eliš (Tiamat's body split to
form sky and earth), Hesiod's Theogony (Gaia separated from Ouranos),
Egyptian Shu-separates-Nut-from-Geb. The "sky and earth were once
joined" motif is a universal mythopoetic pattern, not modern physics.
Big Bang nucleosynthesis has nothing in common with the imagery of a
closed mass being torn: Lemaître's cosmos expands; nothing in the
Big Bang is "torn open." Verdict: **APOLOGETIC OVERREACH**.

## 13. Fingerprints (Q 75:4)

> "Indeed, We are able to proportion his fingertips (*banāna*)."

Apologetic reading: the Quran references the uniqueness of
fingerprints. Counter: the Arabic root *s-w-y* (here *nusawwiya*
"to shape evenly / proportion") means to make even or balanced, not
to make unique. The verse is a resurrection-theology claim: God can
reconstruct even the minute details of the hand at the Last Day —
the image is of reproducibility, not singularity. The apologetic
reading imports "unique" post-hoc, reading the modern forensic fact
back into a verse about eschatological reassembly. Verdict: **AMBIGUOUS
→ APOLOGETIC OVERREACH**.

## 14. Mountain-pegs (Q 78:7, 16:15, 21:31, 79:32)

> "And the mountains as pegs (*awtādan*)."

Apologetic reading (Naggar): modern isostasy shows mountains have
deep crustal roots balancing their elevation — the "peg" image
anticipates plate tectonics.

Counter: the tent-peg/pillar-of-earth metaphor is pan-Near-Eastern
Bronze and Iron Age literature. Psalm 104:5 ("He has laid the earth
on its foundations"); 1 Samuel 2:8 ("the pillars of the earth are
the Lord's"); the Egyptian *djed* pillar; Enūma Eliš's mountains as
cosmic fasteners. The metaphor is ancient, not modern. Geologically,
the isostatic root is a density-balance phenomenon: the mountain
"floats" on denser mantle, not anchored like a peg into ground.
The metaphor survives only if the details are not pressed. Verdict:
**PRE-EXISTING METAPHOR + geologically wrong at face value**.

## 15. Expanding universe (Q 51:47)

> "And the heaven We built with might, and indeed We are expanders
>  (*mūsiʿūn*)."

Apologetic reading: *mūsiʿūn* (pl. active participle of *awsaʿa*) is
Hubble expansion. Counter: the verb *awsaʿa* means "to make wide /
spacious / abundant / generous," and the noun *saʿa* means
"capacity / breadth / affluence." Classical mufassirūn (al-Ṭabarī,
al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr) read the verse as divine power and
providential breadth, not astrophysical expansion. The English
"expanders" is a translation stretch; Pickthall gives "We are the
mighty Maker," Yusuf Ali "it is We Who create the vastness of
space," both of which are the traditional reading. Modern
cosmological expansion was unknown before Hubble 1929; the reading
is retrofit. Verdict: **AMBIGUOUS (grammatical retro-fit)**.

## 16. The sun's orbit (Q 36:38)

> "And the sun runs to a settled resting place for it (*li-mustaqarrin
>  lahā*). That is the determination of the Mighty, the Knowing."

Apologetic reading: the sun "orbits" the galactic centre.

Counter: *mustaqarr* is "a place of settling / resting," compatible
with *any* cosmology that has the sun moving — including the
Ptolemaic geocentric model dominant in 7th-century astronomy, in
which the sun travels a diurnal and annual path and "comes to rest"
at the western horizon each night. Classical tafsīr gives multiple
interpretations: daily setting (al-Ṭabarī citing Ibn ʿAbbās); a
fixed eschatological resting-place at the Hour (Ibn Kathīr); or
"until its appointed end." The verse is over-determined: any
astronomical motion satisfies it, including geocentric motion the
Quran's first audience would have assumed. Verdict: **PRE-EXISTING
KNOWLEDGE (Ptolemaic-compatible)**.

## 17. Two seas not mixing (Q 25:53, 55:19–20)

> "He released the two seas meeting each other; between them is a
>  barrier they do not transgress."

Apologetic reading: the halocline / thermocline separating
freshwater and saltwater at estuaries (modern oceanography).

Counter: this is **pre-existing observational knowledge**. Any Arab
sailor travelling the Shatt al-ʿArab, the Suez/Red-Sea junction, or
the Strait of Hormuz, observed the visible line where fresh river
water meets salt. Edip Yüksel (*Manifesto for Islamic Reform*, 2008)
— a Quranist sympathetic to *iʿjāz ʿilmī* in moderated form — himself
concedes this; Andrew Rippin points out that estuarine mixing was
standard Mediterranean folklore. Verdict: **PRE-EXISTING OBSERVATIONAL
KNOWLEDGE**.

## 18. Deep-sea darkness (Q 24:40)

Apologetic reading: waves-upon-waves-in-darkness describes
thermocline / euphotic-zone layered darkness.

Counter: the verse is a **literary simile** comparing disbelievers'
states to a storm-at-sea. Storm-wave imagery was already a topos in
pre-Islamic Arabic poetry (several *Muʿallaqāt*) and in Homeric
simile. The verse's purpose is moral, not oceanographic. Verdict:
**PRE-EXISTING + literary simile**.

## 19. Atom (*dharra*, Q 99:7–8)

Apologetic reading: *mithqāla dharratin* ("the weight of an atom / a
smallest particle") anticipates modern atomic theory. Counter: the
classical gloss is "a dust mote" or "the smallest ant" (*dharr*,
*dharra* = tiny red ant). It is a colloquial *smallest thing*
metaphor present in pre-Islamic poetry (Imruʾ al-Qays,
al-Nābigha). Modern atomic theory post-dates Dalton (1808). Verdict:
**APOLOGETIC OVERREACH**.

## 20. Milk formation (Q 16:66)

> "And verily in cattle there is a lesson for you. We give you to drink
>  of what is in their bellies, between excretions and blood, pure
>  milk."

Apologetic reading: the Quran describes the hepatic portal system.
Counter: **pre-existing observational knowledge**. Arabs were
herders; the slaughter of camels and sheep exposed the intestinal
/ udder / vascular system directly. Pliny the Elder (*Natural
History*, 8.69) describes milk formation "from the blood." Aristotle
(*Historia Animalium* III.20) likewise. The "between excretions and
blood" description is poetic but not medically novel. Verdict:
**PRE-EXISTING OBSERVATIONAL KNOWLEDGE**.

## 21. Pharaoh's preserved body (Q 10:92)

Apologetic reading: Q 10:92 promises that Pharaoh's body will be
preserved as a sign; Ramesses II's mummy is preserved. Counter:
every Egyptian Pharaoh was mummified as a matter of standard royal
practice. The apologetic must specify *which* Pharaoh the verse
refers to (Merneptah is the most common Exodus candidate among
modern Egyptologists; Ramesses II is traditional but disputed). Any
Pharaoh's-body-is-preserved claim is trivially satisfied. Verdict:
**AMBIGUOUS**.

# Part III — Disconfirmed structural claims from academia

Several structural theses from scholarly Qurʾānic studies — proposed
by Michel Cuypers (*The Composition of the Qur'an*, 2015), Raymond
Farrin (*Structure and Qur'anic Interpretation*, 2014), Theodor
Nöldeke, Richard Bell, Régis Blachère — were subjected to the
project's chiastic-detector. Some survived; several did not.

## 22. Cuypers' Sūrat al-Māʾida as a macro-ring

Cuypers argues Q 5 (*al-Māʾida*) is a single book-length chiastic
ring using his "Semitic rhetoric" method — a method with French
Biblical-studies origins (Roland Meynet's *rhétorique sémitique*).
The project's chiastic-detector applied a lexical-pairing ring-score
metric: for a text of n verses, count matched content-word pairs at
positions (i, n−1−i) versus random shuffles.

Result for Q 5: **z = −2.06, p = 0.99**. The natural verse order is
*more disordered* with respect to lexical pairing than 99% of random
shuffles. This **DISCONFIRMS** the macro-ring at the lexical level.
File: `findings/phase-c-structures/chiastic-audit.md`.

Caveat: Cuypers' rings are thematic-block-based, not verse-pair-
based, so a lexical-pairing instrument is arguably the wrong test
for his exact claim. The honest statement: a macro-ring that is
*thematically* coherent at the block level need not show
*lexically* significant verse-pair symmetry. But a real ring also
should not score *negatively* on a blind lexical test; Q 5 does. The
finding converges with Nicolai Sinai's critique of Cuypers (2017).

## 23. Farrin's whole-mushaf ring (Surah k ↔ Surah 115−k)

Farrin proposes the entire mushaf is a macro-ring in which Surah k
and Surah 115−k are paired. The test: for each of the 57 surah
pairs, measure lexical overlap and compare to length-matched random
pairs. Result: **z = −4.87**. Only 26 of 57 pairs beat the length-
matched median, versus an expected 28.5 under the null. The macro-
ring **FAILS DECISIVELY**. File:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/surah-boundaries.md`.

The length-confound is the key: Farrin's proposed pairs bring together
a long surah and a short surah (e.g., Surah 2, 286 verses, with Surah
113, 5 verses), and length-normalised lexical overlap is always
weak. The finding does not dispute the existence of *internal*
surah-rings (several Quranic surahs do have ring structure at the
intra-surah level, notably the Abraham-qibla pericope of Q 2:131–144,
which the detector finds at z = +9.69 without being told to look).
What fails is the book-level macro-ring.

## 24. Bell / Blachère / Nöldeke chronology debates

The Bell-Blachère school argued the Quran was substantially
re-edited (Bell: "sura-by-sura accretion"; Blachère: pericope-level
reordering). Nöldeke's four-period schema (Early Meccan 1–48, Middle
Meccan 49–69, Late Meccan 70–90, Medinan 91–114) is the competing
model.

The project's verse-length analysis
(`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/chronological-revelation.md`) confirms
that under Nöldeke's ordering, average verse length climbs
monotonically: 18.5 → 38.7 → 66.0 → 79.9 graphemes across the four
phases (one-way F = 209.96, k = 4, n = 114, p << 10⁻³⁰). This is
the cleanest diachronic signal in the corpus.

This result *confirms* a diachronic trajectory — but Montgomery Watt
and Richard Bell proposed specific pericope-reordering claims that
go beyond the surah-level signal and are **not supported** by our
analysis. Bell's hypothesis of massive within-surah
rearrangement leaves no length-sequence signature distinct from the
Nöldeke ordering; the within-surah metric permutations give null
results (Bell would predict high within-surah disorder). The
**Nöldeke ordering is confirmed; the stronger Bellean
rearrangement hypothesis is not supported**.

Important honesty: the Sadeghi 2011 verse-length chronology claim
this replicates was already stated by al-Suyūṭī in *al-Itqān fī
ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*, *nawʿ* 9, five centuries earlier. The finding is
a quantitative corroboration of a classical observation, not a
novel discovery.

## 25. The surah-as-unified-theme claim

Angelika Neuwirth (*Der Koran als Text der Spätantike*, 2010) and
the Corpus Coranicum tradition argue that each surah is a coherent
rhetorical unit with a defined thematic arc. Against this is the
classical form-critical view (Nöldeke, Welch) that longer Medinan
surahs are composite and internally heterogeneous.

Our test: thematic concentration using root-distribution over
verse-indices, per-surah (file: `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/
form-meets-content-outliers.md`). Result: **partial confirmation,
partial failure**. Short Meccan surahs (Surahs 55, 78, 97, 112)
score high on unity metrics (single rhyme, consistent root-family,
rhetorical tightness). Long Medinan surahs (Surahs 2, 4, 5) score
low — they host multiple distinct thematic threads with abrupt
transitions (the verse-of-the-light in Q 24:35, for instance, sits
inside a surah otherwise about slander law). The "unified theme"
claim is true of ~70 % of surahs and false of ~30 %, with length
being the main predictor.

## 26. Joseph-surah as a ring (Surah 12)

Q 12:3 flags itself: *naḥnu naquṣṣu ʿalayka aḥsana al-qaṣaṣ* ("We
narrate to you the most beautiful of narratives"). 92.3 % of all
Quranic Joseph-mentions occur in Surah 12. It is the unique "single-
prophet" surah. The chiastic-detector test, however:

**z = −1.60** for the whole-surah ring hypothesis. Surah 12 is a
*linear* narrative, not a ring. File:
`findings/phase-c-structures/prophet-pericope-comparison.md`.
Linearity is not chiasmus; the narrative unity of Yūsuf is
incompatible with palindromic lexical structure.

## 27. Short-surah ring-score inflation by refrain

An ambiguous result worth flagging as a limit: the naive ring-score
for Surah 114 (al-Nās, 20 tokens) is 0.100 — 5–100× the Muʿallaqāt
baseline. But this is driven by the word *al-nās* (people)
appearing in five of six verses — a **refrain**, not a chiasmus. A
proper chiasmus test restricts to content-word *pairs* around a
central pivot; under that stricter metric Surah 114's ring-score
drops substantially. **The Quran's short-surah symmetry is real but
partially refrain-artifact.** File:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/cross-textual-baseline.md` §5.

## 27b. Q 29 + Q 30 as a TEST-AND-PROPHECY muqaṭṭaʿāt sub-class — NULL (H-NEW-93, 2026-04-17)

Cross-finding-008 established that 27 of 29 muqaṭṭaʿāt-opened surahs
reference "the Book" (kitāb) or "Quran" in their first three verses;
the two exceptions are Q 29 al-ʿAnkabūt and Q 30 al-Rūm, both Late
Meccan الم-opened, with salient but disjoint thematic openings
(testing-of-believers vs historical Roman prophecy). The open
question (OQ-3) was whether Q 29 + Q 30 form a coherent SECOND
functional sub-class of muqaṭṭaʿāt-opened surahs — a
"test-and-prophecy" micro-cluster paralleling the dominant
"book-introduction" function.

H-NEW-93 pre-registered a 4-cell test against the 4 other الم surahs
(Q 2, Q 3, Q 31, Q 32) on test-of-believers term density,
historical-prophecy markers, Allah-density, and eschatological roots.
All four cells return NULL or DIRECTIONAL-REVERSE: test-of-believers
density at Q 29+30 is actually **lower** than the Meccan baseline
(4.14‰ vs 5.05‰; two-sided p = 0.94), historical-prophecy NULL, and
the remaining cells descriptive. One narrow single-test
WEAK-PASS-DIRECTED on the *ghalaba*+*naṣr* substring combination
survives at uncorrected α = 0.05 but would not survive
Bonferroni-4. **Q 29 + Q 30 are NOT a coherent sub-class; the two
exceptions to cross-finding-008 remain individual anomalies.** File:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/h-new-93-q29-q30-subpattern.md`.

## 27c. Cross-axis distinctive-verse atlas — NULL (H-NEW-128, 2026-04-17)

H-NEW-92 found each classically-celebrated verse (Q 1:1 bismillah,
Q 2:255 āyat al-kursī, Q 24:35 āyat al-nūr, Q 112:1–4 al-ikhlāṣ,
Q 59:22–24 Khawātim al-Ḥashr) dominates ONE axis of distinctiveness.
The open question (OQ-8) was whether any verse dominates multiple

primary test and a harmonic-mean-rank top-100 superverse
computation. **All four cells fail.** No verse sits at rank 1 on 3+
independent axes; fewer than 20 verses appear in ≥3 top-100-per-axis
lists; and fewer than 3 of 5 classically-celebrated verses appear in
the harmonic-mean top-100. The multi-axis dominance operationalization
of OQ-8 is answered-NULL: **the celebrated verses each dominate their
own axis, not multiple axes simultaneously**. Honest result — the
celebrated-verse phenomenon is axis-specific rather than axis-general

## 27d. Zero-set / absent-structures fingerprint — NULL (H-NEW-114, 2026-04-17)

The *iʿjāz* apologetic literature occasionally advances the claim
that what the Quran does NOT contain is as distinctive as what it
does — that the corpus avoids specific letter combinations,
forbidden phonological shapes, or "impossible" patterns that other
classical Arabic texts freely use. H-NEW-114 tested this at the
letter-bigram level against matched Bukhārī and Jāḥiẓ corpora.

- **Cell A** (Quran absent-letter-bigram count / 784 possible): 146
  vs matched-Arabic envelope [114, 152]; inside envelope; z = 0.75,
  NULL.
- **Cell B** (bigram-specific presence/absence pattern): NULL.
- **Cell C** (word-bigram zero-set): PASS p ≪ 0.01 — the Quran's
  word-bigram zero-set is massively over-constrained vs independence,
  but this is a trivial consequence of Zipfian sparsity in any
  natural-language corpus of this size, not a Quran-specific
  signature.
- **Cell D** (muqaṭṭaʿāt-letter-presence pattern space): descriptive
  — the 29 muqaṭṭaʿāt-opened surahs use 14 distinct letter-set
  patterns out of 2^14 = 16,384 possible, consistent with the
  cross-finding-008 structured-assignment picture but not a
  "zero-set fingerprint."

**The Quran has no Quran-specific absent-structures fingerprint at
the letter-bigram scale.** File:
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/h-new-114-zero-set.md`.

## 27e. Heap's law vocabulary exponent β — NOT Quran-distinctive (H-NEW-123, 2026-04-17)

Classical-adjacent and apologetic claims sometimes frame the Quran
as having a "uniquely compact" vocabulary — 14,870 distinct
surface-form types over 77,797 tokens (type-token ratio 0.19). The
question is whether this compactness is Quran-specific or a
register-wide feature of classical religious prose.

H-NEW-123 estimated Heap's law exponent β under matched 77K-token
windows. Quran β = **0.7468** [95% CI (0.729, 0.757)]; Bukhārī β =
**0.7472** [95% CI (0.732, 0.759)] — **indistinguishable**.
Compactness is a genre feature of classical religious/historical

## 32. Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsests and the canonical text

The Ṣanʿāʾ 1 palimpsest (DAM 01-27.1), discovered 1972, has a lower
layer that differs from the canonical ʿUthmānic text in approximately
60 verses across the portions examined (Asma Hilali 2017, Behnam
Sadeghi & Uwe Bergmann 2010, Sadeghi & Goudarzi 2012). The
differences are mostly minor — orthographic or word-order variants,
sometimes a different but synonymous word. The lower text appears to
be a companion codex (possibly attributed to Ibn Masʿūd's lost
codex). What the Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest reveals *about the canonical
text* is: (a) the canonical text had non-canonical competitors in the
1st hijrī century, some of which were in circulation well past the
ʿUthmānic standardisation (~650 CE); (b) the differences are small
enough that the canonical text is substantially stable but not
absolutely so; (c) all our computations are against Hafs-Kufan
Ḥafṣ-ʿan-ʿĀṣim, and every finding in the project should be understood
as relative to that text. Any claim about the inherent properties
of "the Quran" that depends on specific letter-counts at the
anchor level may shift by a handful of tokens across extant textual
witnesses. The project's computations are valid for Hafs-Kufan; they
are not invariant across all attested manuscript witnesses.

# Part V — Methodological caveats

## 34. Forking-paths space

The project has run approximately 500 distinct statistical tests
across its two phases (Phase A replications + Phase B hypotheses).
At α = 0.05 and under a perfect-null assumption, 25 false positives
are expected by chance alone. Bonferroni correction against the
full family (α_corrected = 10⁻⁴) is brutal but necessary for any
single-result headline claim; Holm step-down is more forgiving for
multi-result families. Where a finding survives Bonferroni it is
marked confirmed; where it survives only uncorrected α = 0.05 it is
marked exploratory. The honest reading: we have found ~20 surviving
findings, of which 5–7 clear all corrections; the rest are
exploratory and contingent on replication under pre-registered
tests. The statistical-rigor protocol (file:
`docs/statistical-rigor-protocol.md`) states the test register; we
publish the register rather than selectively the significant tests.

## 35. Tokenisation asymmetry between Arabic and other Semitic references

Hebrew Bible Codes (ELS / equidistant-letter-sequences) work on a
letter stream with consonantal assumptions matching Tiberian
Masoretic orthography. Arabic letter-counting faces an analogous
but distinct set of choices: hamza variants (أ إ آ ؤ ئ ٱ ء) normalise
to alif or carry separate; tā marbūṭa (ة) normalises to tā (ت) or
hā (ه) depending on convention; alif maqṣūra (ى) normalises to yāʾ
(ي); the long alif (ا) can be written madda (آ), bare (ا) or
superscript (alif khanjariyya) depending on mushaf orthography. Our
locked convention (no-tashkeel, U+0621..064A ∪ U+0671..06D3,
recitation marks U+06D6..06ED filtered) is one choice among
several; we cross-check under full-tashkeel and min-tashkeel for
sensitivity analysis on letter-count claims. Comparisons with
Hebrew-Bible numerology are therefore non-isomorphic; any framing
that imports ELS methodology directly into Arabic requires
orthographic justification we are careful to state.

## 36. Baseline corpus composition and gaps

The 13.4M-token classical Arabic pool includes: Bukhari (557k with
Quran trigrams stripped), Ibn Hishām's Sīra (279k), al-Jāḥiẓ's
*Ḥayawān* (340k), Mutanabbī's Dīwān (8.5k), the full Dīwāns of six
Muʿallaqa poets (21k–29k each), and the seven Muʿallaqāt themselves
(~7.3k combined). The pool is weighted toward 2nd–4th hijrī-century
prose (Bukhari, Sīra, Jāḥiẓ). **Gaps we acknowledge**: (a) no
Umayyad-era prose (the 1st-century window closest to the Quran); (b)
no Mukhadram poets (6th–7th century; works mostly lost or fragmentary);
(c) no Syriac / Nabataean / Safaitic epigraphic material; (d) no
Mashriqī dialect / Yemenī dialect texts. The baseline therefore
tests "is the Quran distinctive against classical Abbasid-era
Arabic" rather than "is the Quran distinctive against 7th-century
Hijāzī Arabic." The proper test would need material we do not
have; our claims about Quranic distinctiveness are accordingly
qualified.

## 37. Rules-tuple choice effects

Every numerical claim in this project carries a rules tuple (the
YAML frontmatter of every findings file). Under different tuple
choices, the same claim can pass, partially pass, or fail. For
example, the Khalifa ALM-30 claim fails under no-tashkeel but has a
1,197-hit under full-tashkeel (with wrong multiplier). The
rahma = 114 claim passes under QAC-lemma counting but fails under
raw orthographic-token counting (no Quranic word has exactly 114
raw-token occurrences). Sensitivity to the rules tuple is reported
for every claim; the invariant claims (Bismillah = 19 letters under
any reasonable rule) are distinguished from the tuple-brittle ones
(Khalifa ALM sums).

## 38. Selection bias in what has been published

Classical apologetic literature selects its claims after inspecting
the data: only the *successful* pattern-matches are published. The
project commits to publishing every test run, successful or not, in
the `findings/phase-a-replications/` and
`findings/phase-b-hypotheses/` directories, and the `journal/`
records the agent runs including null results. This document itself
is the systematic null-results volume. The asymmetry matters: a
tradition that publishes only the hits is structurally unable to
avoid McKay-style forking-paths criticism; a project that publishes
the misses is accountable to its own tests.

## 39. Bonferroni correction choices

Our default is: Bonferroni-family = all tests run inside a single
pre-registered analysis; Holm step-down where tests are ordered.
We do *not* Bonferroni across the entire 500-test project, because
the tests are not a single family — they are a research programme
with thematic sub-families. This is consistent with the statistical
practice of the biomedical sciences (Benjamini-Hochberg FDR within
pre-specified sub-families) but departs from the conservative
"correct across everything" stance that would kill most scientific
findings. Readers should be aware of this choice; a reader who
applies whole-project Bonferroni will find ~3 findings surviving,
not ~20.

## 40. Replication under pre-registration

Of the ~20 surviving findings, approximately 7 are pre-registered
(formal pre-reg slugs in `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/pre-reg/`);
the remainder were exploratory and will need pre-registered
replication before being cited as confirmed. The project is
committed to this; the ledger is the transparent map of what has
cleared what level of rigour.

# Part VI — The integrity clause

## 41. Why the refutations strengthen the confirmed findings

Every claim this ledger demolishes is a claim the project could have
softened, passed over, or quietly demoted. The decision to publish
the null-results volume in full is a precondition for trusting the
project's positive findings. A project that reports only its
successes is indistinguishable from a project that finds what it was
already looking for.

The corollary: the surviving findings survive *because* they cleared
a battery the demoted ones did not. The qāf-50/42 triangle is worth
a pre-registered Phase-C test because it behaved differently from
the 13 other Khalifa ALM tests that failed. The muqaṭṭaʿāt density
effect (Stouffer Z = +4.48) is credible because it survived both a
weak hypergeometric null and a strong 3-gram letter-Markov null and
because its parent theory (al-Rāzī's 99-names abbreviation) was
**rejected**. The Nöldeke verse-length trajectory is confirmed at F =
210 because the ordering it distinguishes (chronological) does
better than the ordering it doesn't (mushaf). The rahma = 114 claim
does **not** survive, so we do not claim it; if we did, we would be
making the same epistemic move as Khalifa.

## 42. "Classical tradition was always right" versus "classical
tradition was right about X, confirmed for specific X"

The project does not claim classical Islamic scholarship was always
correct — it wasn't. Al-Rāzī's 99-names muqaṭṭaʿāt theory fails our
null test. Pre-Islamic Arabic lexicography includes theses we can
now test and refute (e.g., al-Farāhīdī's claim that certain root-
letter combinations are phonotactically impossible; we find
exceptions). The *iʿjāz ʿilmī* movement is a modern innovation and
is not classical — the classical tafsīr tradition (al-Ṭabarī,
al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr) read passages like Q 51:47 and
Q 21:30 in mythopoetic or power-theological terms, not as physics
prospectuses. When the project says "the classical reading
survives," it means *this specific classical claim was tested and
passed*, not *the classical tradition is wholesale right*.

Concretely: al-Suyūṭī's observation that Meccan surahs are shorter
than Medinan (a claim in *al-Itqān* *nawʿ* 9) replicates at F = 210;
classical form-critical awareness of muqaṭṭaʿāt density echoes in
modern letter-frequency statistics; Welch's observation that
muqaṭṭaʿāt "key" their surah's phonology is confirmed by our
density test. Each passes *on its own terms*. The classical
tradition is one piece of evidence among many, not an authority
granted deference.

## 43. The project's commitment

The project commits to the following, explicitly, as a precondition
of its integrity:

1. Every failure is published at the same prominence as every
   success. This document and the `findings/phase-a-replications/`
   and `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/` directories are the permanent
   record.
2. Every positive finding carries the rules tuple that produced it,
   and is tested for sensitivity under at least one alternative
   rule.
3. Every positive finding is tested against at least two independent
   null models before being called significant.
4. Every finding that could reasonably have been found by ancient
   authors is flagged as such (cf. al-Suyūṭī on chronology); novelty
   is claimed only where the quantitative demonstration is the
   contribution, not the underlying observation.
5. Every claim imported from the numerology and *iʿjāz ʿilmī*
   literature is audited before being cited; claims that fail the
   audit appear only in this null-results volume.
6. Pre-registration is the gold standard; exploratory findings are
   labelled as such and are candidates for future pre-registered
   replication, not conclusions.
7. The Quran is one text — one canonical corpus, Hafs-Kufan,
   114 surahs, 6,236 verses. Variant orthographies (no-/min-/
   full-tashkeel) are conventions over the same text, not "editions."
   Claims about manuscript variance (Ṣanʿāʾ palimpsest, Warsh versus
   Ḥafṣ) are stated explicitly where relevant and do not framework
   the corpus as multi-textual.
