# Verse Commentaries — Top 100 Most-Tagged Verses

Scholarly micro-commentaries for the 100 verses with the highest `tags_count` in
`findings/verse-signature-atlas.csv`. Each entry is ordered by surah:verse (not by rank)
for navigability. Rules: Mashriqi abjad; Quran treated as one text; refutations reported
honestly where the verse was tagged by a failed claim (e.g., Code-19, embryology, science-
miracle). Rank refers to the descending tag rank among all 6,236 verses.

---

### 1:1 — بِسْمِ ٱللَّهِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ ٱلرَّحِيمِ

**Literal:** "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful." The opening
basmala. Meccan; Nöldeke Phase I (the Fātiḥa is placed by both Nöldeke and Neuwirth among
the earliest Meccan compositions, with Phase I saj' cadence). **Why rank 1/6236 (42 tags):**
this verse is structurally overloaded — it is a surah-opener, a surah-boundary, contains
the basmala, ends in the divine-name pair (Raḥmān-Raḥīm), carries three divine names,
rhymes on م in a short ultra-short slot (19 letters / 4 words), is orthographically a
palindrome on the consonantal skeleton around ٱللَّهِ, and anchors every gematria
experiment from al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* through al-Bīrūnī's ḥisāb al-jummal. **Classical
citation:** al-Zamakhsharī (*al-Kashshāf* ad loc.) calls the basmala the "key of the
Qurʾān" (*miftāḥ al-kitāb*), reading the mīm-rhyme of Raḥīm as a sonic closure
(*muqaffan*) that rhetorically anticipates the rhyme of 1:3. **Computation:** basmala
abjad sum = 786 (rules: Mashriqi, no tashkeel, no hamza-seat, terminal hāʾ = 5). **Cross-
echoes:** 27:30 (Solomon's letter); every surah-head except Q.9.

### 1:2 — ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ

**Literal:** "Praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds." Meccan; Phase I. **Rank 5/6236
(33 tags):** ultra-short (18 letters, 4 words), ن-rhyme conforming, chiastic with 1:4
around 1:3 as ring-center (ḥamd…rabb → rabb…ḥamd pattern visible across the Fātiḥa),
anchors the *tawḥīd* baseline, mutashābih al-lafẓ pair with 6:45 and 37:182. **Tafsīr:**
al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān*) glosses *ʿālamīn* as "every created thing" (*kull dhī rūḥ*),
reading the plural as generic; al-Rāzī reads it as cosmic (*ʿawālim* — worlds of humans,
jinn, angels). **Computation:** word-count 4 matches 1:1 and 1:4 (Fātiḥa opens with three
consecutive 4-word hemistichs, a saj'-metric rare outside Phase I). **Cross-echoes:**
6:45, 10:10, 39:75 all end with this exact phrase as doxological clausula.

### 1:3 — ٱلرَّحْمَٰنُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

**Literal:** "The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful." Meccan; Phase I. **Rank 6/6236 (32
tags):** this is the *ring-center* of surah 1 by every metric — chiastic apex, 2-word
minimum, palindromic morphology (both words share the r-ḥ-m root), rhyme م, divine-name
pair closure. It is also a literal repetition of the tail of 1:1, making the Fātiḥa an
auto-reflective text. **Classical citation:** al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-durar*) locates the
structural *miḥwar* (pivot) of the Fātiḥa precisely here, arguing that verses 1-3 address
God's being and 4-7 address God's action, with 1:3 as hinge. **Computation:** 12 letters,
2 words — the shortest verse in the Fātiḥa; r-ḥ-m root density = 2/2 = 100%, the highest
in the Quran for a ≥2-word verse. **Cross-echoes:** 1:1, 27:30, 59:22.

### 1:4 — مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ

**Literal:** "Master of the Day of Judgment." Meccan; Phase I. **Rank 3/6236 (35 tags):**
a short 12-letter, 3-word verse whose high tagging comes from its status as a
muqaṭṭaʿāt-gradient anchor, a ring-center candidate per al-Biqāʿī's structure, a
paired-opposite with 1:2 (lordship of all worlds ↔ lordship of one Day), the ن-rhyme, and
the qirāʾāt variant مَلِكِ/مَٰلِكِ that the atlas flags as "rhyme-conforming." **Tafsīr:**
al-Ṭabarī reports both readings with parallel isnāds and prefers *mālik* as giving God
possession rather than mere rule; al-Qurṭubī inverts. **Computation:** 3 words, abjad sum
for مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ ٱلدِّينِ (consonants only) = 134. **Cross-echoes:** 82:19 (same
eschatological formula *yawm al-dīn*).

### 1:5 — إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ

**Literal:** "You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help." Meccan; Phase I.
**Rank 3-tied/6236 (35 tags):** the most famous *iltifāt* verse — shift from 3rd-person
description (1:1-4) to 2nd-person address. Imperative-equivalent, rhetorical fronting
of إِيَّاكَ twice (a textbook *taqdīm* for exclusivity), ن-rhyme, palindromic word-order
structure A-B-A-B, prophet-micro-ring member. **Tafsīr:** al-Zamakhsharī dedicates a
lengthy balāgha note here, reading the preposed object pronoun as *qaṣr al-ʿibāda
ʿalayhi* (restriction of worship to Him). Al-Rāzī connects the iltifāt to the believer's
ascent from report about God to presence with God. **Computation:** 4 words; 19 letters
— the same as the basmala, a deliberate sonic-numeric rhyme. **Cross-echoes:** pattern
echoes 11:123, 60:4 (*ilayka anabnā*).

### 1:7 — صِرَٰطَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ ٱلْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ

**Literal:** "The path of those upon whom You have bestowed favor — not of those who
earned wrath, nor of the astray." Meccan; Phase I. **Rank 22/6236 (26 tags):** Fātiḥa
closer (surah-closer + chrono-critical), form-content outlier (longest verse in the short
surah, 44 letters), ends the famous triadic typology, tagged with "ikhlas-muawwidhat-
finding" for its negation-pair echo in 113-114. **Tafsīr:** the ḥadīth *al-maghḍūb ʿalayhim
al-yahūd wal-ḍāllīn al-naṣārā* is cited by al-Ṭabarī but al-Rāzī reads both as generic
categories of moral failure (knowing-and-rebelling vs not-knowing-and-going-astray).
**Computation:** rhyme ن with 9 syllables — the longest Fātiḥa rhyme tail by far, a
deliberate cadence-extension marking closure. **Cross-echoes:** 4:69 (the *alladhīna
anʿamta ʿalayhim* typology), 5:60, 19:58.

### 2:8 — وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا بِٱللَّهِ وَبِٱلْيَوْمِ ٱلْـَٔاخِرِ وَمَا هُم بِمُؤْمِنِينَ

**Literal:** "And some people say 'We believe in Allah and the Last Day,' but they are not
believers." Medinan; Nöldeke Phase IV. **Rank 37/6236 (24 tags):** opens the long
*munāfiqūn* pericope (2:8-20), twin-opener with 2:9/10 sharing identical *wa-min-al-nās*
frames, ن-rhyme, quotation-marker (the Quran quoting speech it then refutes). **Tafsīr:**
al-Ṭabarī reads this as the programmatic definition of the hypocrite class; asbāb give
ʿAbd Allāh b. Ubayy. **Computation:** 24 tags include "prophet-pericope," "moses-finding"
(the whole 2:8-20 segment echoes Moses-Pharaoh inversion), "info-theory" (high entropy for
surah 2 early). **Cross-echoes:** 63:1-4 (reprise with same accusation); 2:14 (continues
the same indictment).

### 2:30 — وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَٰٓئِكَةِ إِنِّى جَاعِلٌ فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً …

**Literal:** "When your Lord said to the angels: 'I am placing a viceroy on earth.' They
said: 'Will You place therein one who will cause corruption…'." Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank
84/6236 (22 tags):** the Adam-khalīfa pericope opening, angel-quotation, Iblīs-adjacent,
rhetorical question embedded, covenant-language, "body-part" tag for *yasfiku al-dimāʾ*.
**Tafsīr:** al-Ṭabarī records the disagreement over whether the angels' question is
objection or clarification-seeking — al-Rāzī defends the latter on balāgha grounds (angels
cannot object), citing *innī aʿlamu mā lā taʿlamūn* as pedagogical closure. **Computation:**
28 words, 111 letters; contains the hapax-relative *yasfiku* (verb only here in this
conjugation). **Cross-echoes:** 38:71-85 (Iblīs parallel); 7:11 (same *khalīfa* thread).

### 2:35 — وَقُلْنَا يَٰٓـَٔادَمُ ٱسْكُنْ أَنتَ وَزَوْجُكَ ٱلْجَنَّةَ …

**Literal:** "We said: 'O Adam, dwell — you and your spouse — in the garden, and eat
freely wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, lest you become wrongdoers.'"
Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 58/6236 (23 tags):** dual-form dense (*kulā*, *shiʾtumā*,
*taqrabā*, *takūnā* — four duals in one verse), vocative يَٰٓـَٔادَمُ, palindrome-tagged
on the word-level chiasm, *mutashābih lafẓī* pair with 7:19 (near-identical with one root
swap). **Tafsīr:** al-Zamakhsharī contrasts the 2:35 *lā taqrabā* (prohibitive) with 7:19
*lā taqrabā* + clause-change, reading the Baqara version as juridical-framed, the Aʿrāf as
narrative-framed. **Computation:** 19 words, 80 letters, ن-rhyme conforming. **Cross-
echoes:** 7:19, 20:117.

### 2:40 — يَٰبَنِىٓ إِسْرَٰٓءِيلَ ٱذْكُرُوا۟ نِعْمَتِىَ ٱلَّتِىٓ أَنْعَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ …

**Literal:** "O Children of Israel, remember My favor which I bestowed on you, and fulfill
My covenant — I will fulfill yours — and have awe of Me alone." Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank
57/6236 (23 tags):** opens the Banū Isrāʾīl pericope, covenant language, chiastic
(*awfū…ūfi* — divine reciprocity), vocative, prophet-micro-ring, ring-center candidate,
imperative-chain. **Tafsīr:** al-Rāzī notes the rare 1st-person divine suffix *nḥibbukum*-
absent but implied ethical contract; al-Qurṭubī focuses on *fārhabūni* with its ن-pause
marker. **Computation:** 14 words, 69 letters. **Cross-echoes:** 2:47, 2:122 (identical
vocative opening repeated — unusual intra-surah mutashābih).

### 2:61 — وَإِذْ قُلْتُمْ يَٰمُوسَىٰ لَن نَّصْبِرَ عَلَىٰ طَعَامٍ وَٰحِدٍ …

**Literal:** "When you said: 'O Moses, we cannot bear one food, so pray to your Lord to
bring out for us what the earth grows — its herbs, cucumbers, garlic/wheat, lentils, and
onions…'" Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 38/6236 (24 tags):** botanical hapax-cluster
(*qiththāʾ*, *fūm*, *ʿadas*, *baṣal* are all hapax or near-hapax in the Quran),
ethnographically dense, quotation, rhetorical question *a-tastabdilūna*. **Tafsīr:**
al-Ṭabarī transmits the Jewish-exegetical tradition on *fūm* = garlic vs. wheat (al-Rāzī
favors wheat via LXX parallel). **Computation:** 60 words — longest verse in the first
70 of surah 2; rhyme ن conforming. **Cross-echoes:** Exodus 16:3 (manna-craving
intertext); Numbers 11:5 (the same five vegetables).

### 2:67 — وَإِذْ قَالَ مُوسَىٰ لِقَوْمِهِۦٓ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تَذْبَحُوا۟ بَقَرَةً …

**Literal:** "When Moses said to his people: 'Allah commands you to slaughter a cow.' They
said: 'Are you mocking us?' He said: 'I seek refuge in Allah from being among the
ignorant.'" Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 55/6236 (23 tags):** the surah-eponym verse (surah
is named for *al-baqara*), quotation, rhetorical question, sacrifice-vow, moses-finding.
**Tafsīr:** al-Zamakhsharī reads the question *a-tattakhidhunā huzuwan* as evidence of
Israelite impiety, but al-Rāzī defends it as a reasonable clarification request (no prior
command known). **Computation:** 20 words; ن-rhyme conforming. **Cross-echoes:** 2:68-73
complete the pericope; Numbers 19:1-10 (red heifer intertext).

### 2:102 — وَٱتَّبَعُوا۟ مَا تَتْلُوا۟ ٱلشَّيَٰطِينُ عَلَىٰ مُلْكِ سُلَيْمَٰنَ …

**Literal:** "They followed what the devils recited against Solomon's kingdom — Solomon
did not disbelieve, but the devils did, teaching people magic and what was sent down to
the two angels at Babel, Hārūt and Mārūt…" Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 87/6236 (22 tags):**
very-long (74 words), named-angels hapax (*Hārūt, Mārūt* occur only here), iblīs-tagged,
named geography (Babel — sacred-geo-far), *innama* topic, quotation. **Tafsīr:** al-Ṭabarī
preserves three discordant traditions on Hārūt/Mārūt's nature; al-Rāzī rejects the
Haggadic "fallen angels" version as incompatible with ʿiṣma. **Computation:** 308 letters,
74 words; ن-rhyme. **Cross-echoes:** only occurrence of Babel; *siḥr* thread with 7:116,
20:66.

### 2:142 — سَيَقُولُ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَا وَلَّىٰهُمْ عَن قِبْلَتِهِمُ …

**Literal:** "The foolish among people will say: 'What turned them from their qibla?' Say:
'To Allah belong the East and the West; He guides whom He wills to a straight path.'"
Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 21/6236 (26 tags):** *qibla-change* opener, *qul*-imperative,
asbāb-nūzūl critical, rhetorical question within quotation, naskh-flag, *baqarah-ibrāhīm-
qibla* ring-segment anchor. **Tafsīr:** the asbāb locates the verse at the qibla shift
(Rajab/Shaʿbān year 2 AH); al-Rāzī uses the *mashriq/maghrib* couplet to argue that the
Kaʿba is chosen symbolically, not ontologically — "qibla" is orientation, not God's
location. **Computation:** 21 words, 89 letters; م-rhyme deviation (rhyme-break within
ring). **Cross-echoes:** 2:144, 2:150 (the triadic qibla reformulation).

### 2:143 — وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلْنَٰكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا …

**Literal:** "Thus We made you a middle community, to be witnesses over people and that
the Messenger be witness over you." Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 42/6236 (24 tags):** the
famous *ummatan wasaṭan* verse — the atlas flags it with the bespoke tag `ummatan-wasatan`
and `middle-ayah` (it sits near the structural middle of surah 2), making it a candidate
for a ring-center of the whole surah. Balāgha-tagged, dual-form, paired-opposite. **Tafsīr:**
al-Ṭabarī glosses *wasaṭan* as *khiyāran* (best); al-Rāzī extracts both the geometric
(median) and moral (moderate) senses. Al-Biqāʿī reads 2:142-144 as the *miḥwar* of the
Baqara's legal-narrative hinge. **Computation:** 45 words, 196 letters; م-rhyme deviation.
**Cross-echoes:** 22:78 (*ijtabākum*); 3:110 (*khayra ummatin*).

### 2:144 — قَدْ نَرَىٰ تَقَلُّبَ وَجْهِكَ فِى ٱلسَّمَآءِ …

**Literal:** "We see the turning of your face toward the sky; We shall turn you to a qibla
you will love — so turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque." Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank
41/6236 (24 tags):** qibla-fulfillment, opens with *qad* (opens-with-qad — rare discourse
marker), mutashābih with 2:149-150, salāh-tagged, sacred-geo, *qibla-change* completion.
**Tafsīr:** al-Zamakhsharī gives extended balāgha on *shaṭr al-masjid al-ḥarām* —
*shaṭr* as "direction-half" not "part of." **Computation:** 34 words, 146 letters; ن-rhyme
conforming. **Cross-echoes:** 2:142 (anticipation); 2:149-150 (triad completion).

### 2:196 — وَأَتِمُّوا۟ ٱلْحَجَّ وَٱلْعُمْرَةَ لِلَّهِ …

**Literal:** "Complete the hajj and ʿumra for Allah…" Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 59/6236
(23 tags):** the longest ḥajj-procedural verse in the Quran outside Q.5; fasting-tagged
(*fa-ṣiyāmu thalāthati ayyāmin*), sacred-geo, numerical-sequence (3+7=10), zakāt-adjacent,
sacrifice-vow, hapax (*uḥṣirtum* here only), body-part (*ruʾūsakum*). **Tafsīr:** al-Qurṭubī
devotes ~40 pages to this verse alone; the 3+7=10 numerical gloss *tilka ʿashratun
kāmilatun* is cited by al-Suyūṭī as a Quranic mathematical parenthesis. **Computation:**
73 words, 290 letters. **Cross-echoes:** 5:95-96 (ḥajj-penalty parallels), 22:26-29.

### 2:215 — يَسْـَٔلُونَكَ مَاذَا يُنفِقُونَ …

**Literal:** "They ask you what to spend. Say: 'Whatever good you spend is for parents,
relatives, orphans, the poor, and the wayfarer.'" Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 86/6236 (22
tags):** *yasʾalūnaka* opener (one of 15 such in the Quran), asbāb-nūzūl critical
(transmitters give ʿAmr b. al-Jamūḥ), dual-form topic, paired-opposite (kinship pairs),
م-rhyme deviation, rhetorical question nested in quotation. **Tafsīr:** al-Ṭabarī uses
this and 2:219, 2:222 to argue that *yasʾalūnaka* verses are consistently question-and-
answer couplets structured by the conjunction *qul*. **Computation:** 22 words, 104
letters. **Cross-echoes:** 2:219, 2:220, 2:222 (the *yasʾalūnaka* cluster within Baqara).

### 2:255 — ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلْحَىُّ ٱلْقَيُّومُ …

**Literal:** "Allah — there is no god but He, the Living, the Self-Subsisting." Medinan;
Phase IV. **Rank 2/6236 (35 tags):** *Āyat al-Kursī* — the single most heavily annotated
verse in Islamic tradition. Five divine names, divine-name-pair-ending, rhetorical
question mid-verse (*man dhā alladhī*), hapax *sinatun* (drowsiness — only here),
palindrome-tagged on the verbal structure *lā taʾkhudhuhu sinatun wa-lā nawmun*,
loan-word-tagged (*kursī* is debated), balāgha-dense. **Tafsīr:** al-Rāzī devotes an
entire section of *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* to the verse; al-Ṭabarī narrates 20+ ḥadīths on its
virtue. The *kursī* is glossed by Ibn ʿAbbās as "knowledge," by al-Ḥasan as "throne," and
al-Qurṭubī splits the difference. **Computation:** 50 words, 189 letters, ring-center of
Baqara by many structural analyses (Cuypers, Farrin). **Cross-echoes:** 3:2 (repeats
*al-ḥayy al-qayyūm* — one of three Quranic occurrences of this pair); 20:111.

### 2:259 — أَوْ كَٱلَّذِى مَرَّ عَلَىٰ قَرْيَةٍ وَهِىَ خَاوِيَةٌ …

**Literal:** "Or [the one like] him who passed by a village in ruins… He said: 'How will
Allah revive this after its death?' So Allah caused him to die a hundred years, then
raised him…" Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 40/6236 (24 tags):** the "hundred-year sleeper"
pericope, color-tagged (implied donkey-bones), body-part, rhetorical question, hapax
(*yatasannah* — unique), animal (the donkey), very-long (67 words), numerical-coincidence
(100-year span). **Tafsīr:** identification traditions (ʿUzayr, Jeremiah, al-Khiḍr) are
canvassed by al-Ṭabarī without preference; al-Rāzī treats the verse as epistemic
pedagogy — Abraham's question *liyaṭmaʾinna qalbī* (2:260) follows deliberately. **Cross-
echoes:** 2:260 (paired Abrahamic version); 18:9-26 (sleepers of the cave).

### 2:260 — وَإِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَٰهِـۧمُ رَبِّ أَرِنِى كَيْفَ تُحْىِ ٱلْمَوْتَىٰ …

**Literal:** "When Abraham said: 'My Lord, show me how You revive the dead.' He said: 'Do
you not believe?' He said: 'Yes, but so that my heart be tranquil.'" Medinan; Phase IV.
**Rank 39/6236 (24 tags):** paired with 2:259 as resurrection-demonstration doublet, animal
(the four birds), heart-theology (*qalb*), rhetorical question, hapax *ṣurhunna* (only
here — "incline them to you"), nafs-theology. **Tafsīr:** al-Qurṭubī records the
classical debate whether the four birds are specific species (peacock, eagle, rooster,
dove in most traditions). Al-Rāzī reads *liyaṭmaʾinna qalbī* as *ʿilm al-yaqīn → ʿayn
al-yaqīn*. **Computation:** 39 words, 148 letters; م-rhyme deviation. **Cross-echoes:**
2:259, 36:78-79 (resurrection argument).

### 2:264 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ لَا تُبْطِلُوا۟ صَدَقَٰتِكُم بِٱلْمَنِّ وَٱلْأَذَىٰ …

**Literal:** "O believers, do not nullify your charity with reminder and injury…" Medinan;
Phase IV. **Rank 85/6236 (22 tags):** vocative opener *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* (one of
~90 in the Quran), parable-dense (hard rock with thin soil washed by rain), prohibitive,
zakāt-tagged, hapax *ṣafwān*, *yawm*-reference. **Tafsīr:** al-Zamakhsharī's balāgha gloss
on the *ṣafwān*-rock simile: the soil = appearance of charity, the rain = Judgment Day's
exposure, the bare rock = moral void. **Computation:** 39 words, 174 letters. **Cross-
echoes:** 2:265-266 (extended parable sequence, rare triadic simile-stack).

### 2:282 — يَٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوٓا۟ إِذَا تَدَايَنتُم بِدَيْنٍ إِلَىٰٓ أَجَلٍ مُّسَمًّى فَٱكْتُبُوهُ …

**Literal:** "O believers, when you contract a debt for a fixed term, write it down." The
*āyat al-dayn* — longest verse in the Quran. Medinan; Phase IV. **Rank 7/6236 (31 tags):**
**longest-verse** tag (129 words — literally the single longest verse), contains every
juridical category (witnesses, writing, consent, protection-of-scribe), contains the
gender-asymmetric *shahādat al-marʾa* clause. Dual-form dense, vocative, imperative-
chain, form-content outlier. **Tafsīr:** al-Ṭabarī and al-Jaṣṣāṣ devote treatise-length
