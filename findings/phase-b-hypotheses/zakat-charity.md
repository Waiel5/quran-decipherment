Notes.
* The **technical noun zakāh** (`zakaw`t`, 32 tokens) is exactly the figure traditionally cited; the root in its entirety reaches 59 because the Quran preserves the earlier *ethical* senses (`tazakkā` "purify oneself", `zakiyy` "pure"), most of which are Meccan.
* The **noun `ṣadaqa`/`ṣadaqāt`** (alms specifically, not the abstract verbal or truth-senses of the root) has **13 tokens, all in Medinan suwar**: 2:196, 2:263, 2:264, 2:271, 2:276, 4:114, 9:58, 9:60, 9:79, 9:103, 9:104, 58:12, 58:13. This is the structural pivot of task 4 below.
* **`ʾanfaqa`/`infāq`**: 86 % of the 111 tokens of root `nfq` are Medinan; only 15 Meccan. The Quran's vocabulary of "spending for God" is overwhelmingly a Madīnah idiom.
* Root `rqb` carries both "neck" (→ slave, 9 tokens) and "watch" (→ `Raqīb`, divine epithet, 5 tokens). The semantic pivot is identical to Hebrew *qōbel* / *rākab*: the neck is the site of both supervision and subjugation.

## 2. "Ṣalāh + Zakāh" — The Most Common Quranic Pair-Command

Of the 32 occurrences of the noun `zakaw`t` (zakāh), **28 co-occur in the same verse with `ṣalaw`t` (ṣalāh)**. The four exceptions are 7:156 (ethical phrase "*wa-raḥmatī wasiʿat kulla shayʾin fa-sa-aktubuhā li-lladhīna yattaqūna wa-yuʾtūna z-zakāta*"), 18:81 (ethical `zakāt-an wa-aqraba ruḥman`), 19:13 (Yaḥyā given "zakāt"), and 41:7. Among pair-commands — dual imperatives / dual relative clauses in which two religious acts are syntactically joined — the collocation

  **"aqīmū ṣ-ṣalāta wa-ʾātū z-zakāta"**

is the single most recurrent formula in the Quran. The 28 shared verses are:

2:43, 2:83, 2:110, 2:177, 2:277, 4:77, 4:162, 5:12, 5:55, 9:5, 9:11, 9:18, 9:71, 9:103, 19:31, 19:55, 21:73, 22:41, 22:78, 24:37, 24:56, 27:3, 31:4, 33:33, 35:18, 58:13, 73:20, 98:5.

Observations. (a) The command is distributed across **the full chronological span**: Meccan (19:31 to Jesus, 19:55 to Ishmael, 21:73 to Abraham/Lot/Isaac/Jacob, 27:3, 31:4, 73:20, 98:5) and Medinan (almost all of sūrat al-Baqara and al-Tawba instances). The pair is therefore projected *backward* onto earlier prophets — Jesus announces it in his cradle-speech (19:31), Ishmael is remembered as commanding his family to perform it (19:55), and the Abrahamic dhurrīya received it as waḥy (21:73). (b) Of the 5 "unshared" Meccan instances where *zakāh* stands without `ṣalāh`, three (18:81; 19:13; 23:4; 7:156) concentrate the more archaic *purity*-sense, consistent with the chronology below.

## 3. The Eight Categories of Recipients (Q 9:60)

The morphology of 9:60 segments cleanly into the eight canonical aṣnāf. Word-by-word (Buckwalter → Arabic → sense):

1. `al-fuqarāʾ` (ROOT fqr) — the poor.
2. `al-masākīn` (ROOT skn) — the indigent / destitute.
3. `al-ʿāmilīn ʿalayhā` (ROOT ʿml + prep `ʿalā`) — **administrators/collectors** appointed over it.
4. `al-muʾallafa qulūbuhum` (ROOT ʾlf form II passive PCPL, + `qalb` "heart") — "those whose hearts are to be reconciled".
5. `fī r-riqāb` (ROOT rqb, prep `fī`) — **"in (the cause of) necks"**, i.e. the enslaved.
6. `al-ghārimīn` (ROOT grm) — the debt-burdened.
7. `fī sabīli llāh` (ROOT sbl, prep `fī`) — "in the path of Allāh".
8. `ibn as-sabīl` (ROOT bny + sbl) — **"son of the road"**, the stranded traveller.

The verse closes with `farīḍatan mina llāhi wa-llāhu ʿalīmun ḥakīm` — "an obligation from Allāh; and Allāh is Knowing, Wise." The word **`farīḍa`** (ROOT frḍ) occurs only a handful of times in the Quran and here marks the juridical *faraḍa* (fixed, imposed duty), signalling a shift from voluntary to obligatory almsgiving. Two of the eight categories are preceded by **`fī`** (5, 7): "in the matter of". This is the Qurʾanic syntax for "earmarked funding", as opposed to direct hand-to-recipient transfer (categories 1-4, 6, 8 use `li-` or parataxis).

## 4. Meccan Ṣadaqa vs. Medinan Zakāh — Chronology of Obligation

Three converging statistical facts argue for a genuine chronological shift from voluntary to obligatory almsgiving:

**(a) The technical noun `ṣadaqa(t)/ṣadaqāt` is 13/13 Medinan.** Not a single occurrence falls in a Meccan sūra. The Meccan use of root `Sdq` is dominated by the *truth-senses*: `ṣādiq` (truthful, 40 Meccan vs 19 Medinan), `ṣidq` (11 vs 3), `ṣiddīq` (3 vs 2). The specifically *transactional* noun appears only after the Hijra.

**(b) Root `nfq` is 86 % Medinan** (96 of 111 tokens). `Anfaqa` "to spend" as a quasi-technical verb of charitable giving is a Madinan creation on top of the older Meccan verbs of *giving* (`āta`, `aṭʿama`, `ṣaddaqa`). The feminine pl. participle `mutaṣaddiqāt` (58:12 / 33:35) and the causative `taṣaddaqa` intensify in Medinan.

**(c) The noun `zakāh` is evenly split (27 Meccan, 32 Medinan) but the *inflectional paradigm* is different.** In Meccan usage the root appears most often in form V (`tazakkā`, 8 tokens, "to purify oneself", e.g. 20:76; 35:18; 79:18; 80:3; 80:7; 87:14; 92:18; 35:18 again) or form IV (`ʾazkā` "purer", 4 tokens). The noun `zakaw`t` occurs in Meccan texts but its semantics are often still ethical-spiritual — 18:81 couples it with `ruḥm` (compassion) and 19:13 makes it a *gift* (Yaḥyā's trait, not a tax). By contrast every Medinan `zakaw`t` is paired with `aqīmū / yuqīmūna ṣ-ṣalāta`, framing it as a legislated duty.

**(d) The explicit command to collect comes only at Medina.** Q 9:103 — `xudh min amwālihim ṣadaqatan tuṭahhiruhum wa-tuzakkīhim bihā wa-ṣalli ʿalayhim` — is addressed to the Prophet as *collector*, and the verb pair `tuṭahhiru / tuzakkī` (root `ṭhr` + root `zkw`) ties the *zakāh* back to its Meccan etymology of "purification" at the very moment it becomes a state function. The farīḍa-marker of 9:60 ("an obligation from Allāh") is the juridical seal.

Thus the chronology is not lexical replacement (*ṣadaqa* → *zakāh*) but **semantic narrowing and juridical specification**: Meccan revelation uses a broad vocabulary of *purification* (`tazakkā`, `ʾazkā`), *truth-alms* (`taṣaddaqa`), and *feeding* (`ʾaṭʿama`, 76:8); Medinan revelation retains all three but adds a fixed-share `farīḍa` whose administrative machinery (category 3: `al-ʿāmilīn ʿalayhā`) appears nowhere in Meccan sūras.

## 5. Riqāb in the Eight Categories — Slave-Manumission as Fifth Recipient

The noun `raqaba(t)` ("neck → slave"), lemma of root `rqb`, occurs 9 times. Every occurrence is in a Medinan sūra (4:92, 5:89, 9:60, 47:4, 58:3, 90:13, and three within the expiation verse 4:92 itself):

| Passage | Context | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 2:177 (`fī r-riqāb`) | birr-verse: "and spends... on the [freeing of] necks" | voluntary |
| 4:92 (×3) | kaffāra for accidental killing | expiation |
| 5:89 | kaffāra for broken oath | expiation |
| 9:60 (`fī r-riqāb`) | zakāh recipient category | **obligatory fund** |
| 47:4 | war captives — `fa-immā mannan baʿdu wa-immā fidāʾ` | policy |
| 58:3 | ẓihār expiation | expiation |
| 90:13 (`fakku raqaba`) | "the freeing of a neck" — defining *al-ʿaqaba* | moral exaltation |

The Q 9:60 phrasing **`wa fī r-riqāb`** (with the preposition `fī`, "in/for") parallels `fī sabīli llāh` — both are *earmark* constructions: a slave is not personally gifted money; rather the zakāh fund contracts to *redeem* the slave from the master. Classical jurists (Abū Ḥanīfa, Mālik, Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad) agree this is a purchasing expenditure made from the collected alms. The grammatical twin with `fī sabīli llāh` in the same verse is therefore load-bearing: the two "earmark" categories flank the administrative category (`al-ʿāmilīn`), sandwiching logistics between two mission-funds.

Structurally 2:177, 9:60, and 90:13 together construct a **triangle**: 2:177 makes slave-redemption part of *birr* (voluntary righteousness); 9:60 makes it part of *farīḍa* (obligatory alms); 90:13 makes it the archetypal act of *steep ascent* (al-ʿaqaba). The root `rqb` — "neck" — also yields the divine title `Raqīb` (5 tokens), "the Watcher over necks": the same noun denotes both the subjugated neck and the divine gaze that watches every neck. The doctrinal inference is obvious: God "watches" and mankind "redeems". 

## 6. Infāq "In the Way of God" — Q 2:261-264 (Grain-of-Wheat Parable)

Q 2:261 opens with the explicit parable-marker `mathalu` (→ `mithl` "likeness"). The verse's arithmetical skeleton reads:

  `mathalu lladhīna yunfiqūna amwālahum fī sabīli llāhi ka-mathali ḥabbatin anbatat sabʿa sanābila fī kulli sunbulatin miʾatu ḥabbah`

"The likeness of those who *spend* (`yunfiqū`, root nfq) their wealth in the way of God is like the likeness of a grain that put forth seven ears, in every ear a hundred grains." Morphology: `ḥabba(t)` (Hbb) "grain"; `anbatat` (nbt, form IV impf. fem.) "caused to grow"; `sabʿa sanābila` (sbl → sanābil pl. of sunbula) "seven ears"; `miʾatu ḥabba` (mAy + Hbb) "a hundred grains". The multiplier sits on a literal ×700 (7 × 100), capped by the hyperbolic **`wa-llāhu yuḍāʿifu li-man yashāʾu`** (`yuḍāʿifu`, root ḍEf, form III impf.) — "and Allāh multiplies for whom He wills". The same doubling-verb `yuḍāʿifu` will recur in 64:17 (below), linking the two parables lexically.

**2:262** extends the parable into *ethics of giving*: `lā yutbiʿūna mā anfaqū mannan wa lā adhan` — "they do not follow up what they have spent with *reminder* (`mann`, root mnn) or *injury* (`adhā`, root A*y)". The two negatives name the two classical *vices* of charity.

**2:263** crystallises them: `qawlun maʿrūfun wa-maghfiratun xayrun min ṣadaqatin yatbaʿuhā adhā` — "a kind word and forgiveness are *better* (`xayr`, root xyr) than a charity followed by injury". Here `xayr` (the good) from our root-family is placed in explicit *axiological comparison* with `ṣadaqa`: a bad-faith alms is outranked by a good word.

**2:264** seals the parable with its counter-image: `ka-mathali ṣafwānin ʿalayhi turābun fa-aṣābahu wābilun fa-tarakahu ṣaldan` — "like the likeness of a smooth rock with dust on it; a rainstorm struck it and left it bare." The two parables of 2:261 and 2:264 form a matched pair — fertile earth vs. barren rock, 700-fold vs. 0-fold — with the ethical hinge in 2:262-263. This is a ring-structure (A-X-Y-Aʹ) around the axis `xayrun min ṣadaqatin`: *value inheres not in the alms but in the state of the heart that gives*.

Note that 2:264 opens with `lā tubṭilū` (root bṭl), "do not nullify", making *riʾāʾ* (ostentation, `riʾāʾa n-nās`) — not the alms itself — the destroyer of merit. The parable's final clause `wa-llāhu lā yahdī l-qawma l-kāfirīn` then identifies the ostentatious giver with *kufr* — a strong rhetorical escalation. The pedagogical arc is: (i) infāq is exponential; (ii) mann and adhā void it; (iii) a kind word outranks a wounded alms; (iv) ostentation is kufr. Four verses, full ethic of giving.

## 7. Q 64:16-17 — "A Good Loan to Allāh"

Q 64:16 issues the triplet command `wa-smaʿū wa-aṭīʿū wa-anfiqū ***xayran li-anfusikum***` — "listen, obey, and *spend good for yourselves*". The adverbial accusative `xayrā` (ROOT xyr) of *infāq* is the rhetorical hinge: you are not giving to the beneficiary, you are giving *to your own selves*. The verse closes with `wa-man yūqa shuḥḥa nafsihi fa-ulāʾika humu l-mufliḥūn` — "whoever is spared the greed (`shuḥḥ`, root $HH) of his own self, those are the successful." `shuḥḥ` here is the interior vice that `ʾīthār` (task 8) externally overcomes; in 59:9 the same Ansār are said to prefer (`yuʾthirūna`) others `ʿalā anfusihim wa-law kāna bihim khaṣāṣa`, over their own selves "even if they had need", and are then *also* sealed with `wa-man yūqa shuḥḥa nafsihi fa-ulāʾika humu l-mufliḥūn`. The formula is a **cross-sūra hook** — 59:9 and 64:16 share the identical closing clause.

Q 64:17 then reframes infāq as a *loan*: `in tuqriḍū llāha qarḍan ḥasanan yuḍāʿifhu lakum wa-yaghfir lakum` — "if you *loan* (ROOT qrḍ form IV) Allāh a beautiful loan, He will multiply (`yuḍāʿifhu`, ḍEf) it for you and forgive you". The verb `yuḍāʿifu` is identical to 2:261's multiplication-verb — the two passages are lexically stitched: 2:261 models the multiplication through a *parable* (grain → 700), 64:17 models it through a *commercial metaphor* (loan → forgiveness + doubling). The divine names at the end of 64:17 — `shakūr ḥalīm` ("the Grateful, the Forbearing") — are unique: elsewhere `shakūr` collocates with `ʿalīm` or `ḥalīm` only here with `ḥalīm`, emphasising that God *thanks* the giver and *forbears* his lapses.

The same "good loan" formula (`qarḍ ḥasan`) recurs at 2:245, 5:12, 57:11, 57:18, 73:20 — exactly six verses. Five of the six are explicitly Medinan; 73:20 is Medinan by almost universal tradition despite being appended to a Meccan sūra. The "loan" metaphor is therefore, like `anfiqū`, a Madinan idiom of charitable mobilisation.

## 8. Miqdārayn of Mal-Spending — The Two Measures

"Miqdārayn" (two measures) is the classical Qurʾanic figure for the *bounded* character of right spending. The locus is Q 25:67:

  `wa-lladhīna idhā anfaqū lam yusrifū wa-lam yaqturū wa-kāna bayna dhālika qawāmā`

"And those who, when they *spend* (`anfaqū`, nfq IV), neither *squander* (`yusrifū`, srf IV → ʾisrāf) nor *withhold* (`yaqturū`, qtr) — and it [their spending] stands between that in *balance* (`qawām`, qwm)." The two prohibited extremes are thus **ʾisrāf** (prodigality, root srf) and **qatar** (miserliness, root qtr). `Qawām` is the Qurʾanic word for *equilibrium* — a morphology-level twin of Aristotelian μεσότης.

The parallel locus is Q 17:29, which gives the figure in bodily metaphor:

  `wa-lā tajʿal yadaka maghlūlatan ilā ʿunuqika wa-lā tabsuṭhā kulla l-basṭi fa-taqʿuda malūman maḥsūrā`

"Do not make your hand *chained* (`maghlūla`, gll) to your neck, nor *stretch it* (`tabsuṭhā`, bsT) to every outstretching, lest you sit *blamed and desolate*." The "fettered hand" mirrors *qatar* (miserliness) — the giver who will not give — while the fully-unfurled hand mirrors *ʾisrāf* (prodigality) — the giver who spends until ruined. Note the ring with Q 17:26 three verses earlier, which commands `wa-ātī dhā l-qurbā ḥaqqahu wa-l-miskīna wa-bna s-sabīli wa-lā tubadhdhir tabdhīrā` — "give the kinsman his right, and the indigent, and the wayfarer, but do not squander (`tubadhdhir`, b*r, form II) wastefully". Root `b*r` (tabdhīr) is thus the specifically Meccan vocabulary for what becomes `ʾisrāf` in Medinan discourse.

The "two measures" are therefore:

* **Upper bound:** `ʾisrāf` / `tabdhīr` — extravagance, waste. The Qurʾanic name for this sin is *tabdhīr*, which 17:27 defines: `ʾinna l-mubadhdhirīna kānū ikhwāna sh-shayāṭīn` — "the squanderers are the brothers of Satans". The prodigal violates the *maṣlaḥa* of the gift: he strips himself so as to need charity in turn.
* **Lower bound:** `qatar` / `shuḥḥ` / *the fettered hand* — miserliness. 4:37 names the vice: `al-ladhīna yabkhalūna wa-yaʾmurūna n-nāsa bi-l-buxl` — the *bakhīl* is named by a root (bxl, 12 tokens) that in the Qurʾan is always morally condemned.

The two bounds define **qawām** (25:67) — the upright mean. Structurally, 17:26-29 and 25:67 function as *twin micro-codes* on charitable economics; they sit alongside the "ʾīthār" micro-code at 59:9 (give preference to others even in one's own need) and the "xayr-for-self" micro-code at 64:16 (spend as a good for your own soul). Together these four micro-codes articulate a complete doxa of spending: bounded (25:67), poised (17:29), self-sacrificial when the community is in need (59:9), and self-profiting in eschatological accounting (64:16-17; 2:261).

## Cross-Synthesis

A single conceptual thread ties all eight tasks: **zakāh is the *juridification of tazakkī***. The Meccan Quran teaches self-purification (`tazakkā`, form V, reflexive) through ʾinfāq bounded between ʾisrāf and qatar (17:26-29; 25:67); the Medinan Quran appoints an administered fund (9:60) with eight fixed aṣnāf, two of which (`fī r-riqāb`, `fī sabīli llāh`) are earmark-projects, and promises a multiplication factor of up to ×700 or more (2:261), figured as a loan that Allāh Himself repays (64:17). The pair-command `aqīmū ṣ-ṣalāta wa-ātū z-zakāta`, occurring 28 times, binds vertical worship (ṣalāh) to horizontal redistribution (zakāh); and the figure of *ʾīthār* (59:9) supplies the spiritual ceiling above the farīḍa floor. The morphology corpus makes each of these claims testable and exact.
