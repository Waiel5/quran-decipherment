# Jinas / tajnis (paronomasia) in the Quran — full catalog

Jinas (الجناس) is the classical Arabic rhetorical device of placing words
with the same or similar root next to each other for sonic and semantic
effect. Tafsir literature has long noted individual cases (most famously
**Q 30:55**, where the same word *as-saa`a* is used twice in two different
senses), but no exhaustive computational catalog exists. This file is that
catalog, computed from the Leeds Quranic Arabic Corpus root tags.

**Method.** For every segment in the morphology corpus we read the
`ROOT:<bw>` tag. For each verse we count how many times each root appears.
Any root appearing twice or more in the same verse is, by definition, a
morphological repetition — the necessary (not sufficient) substrate of
jinas. We then drill down to triple+ repetitions, multi-root clusters,
cross-verse couplings on rare roots, and edit-distance-1 root pairs.

## §5  Cross-verse jinas — rare roots in consecutive verses

A *rare root* is defined as one with **≤ 20** total occurrences
anywhere in the Quran. When such a root appears in verse N **and** verse N+1
of the same surah, this is a striking inter-verse coupling. We find **144**
such couplings. Top 40 by lowest global frequency below.

**Notable highlights from this section** (chosen for rhetorical strength):

- **Q 6:76→77→78** with root `Afl` (`أفل`, *to set/disappear*, global count 4):
  Abraham's progressive reasoning — the star sets, the moon sets, the sun sets.
  The same rare root threads three consecutive verses and walks the reader from
  the smallest to the largest celestial body, each time reaching the same conclusion.
  Three-verse triple coupling — the only such triple-step rare-root chain in the catalog.
- **Q 28:71→72** with root `srmd` (`سرمد`, *perpetual*, global count 2):
  the only two occurrences of this root in the entire Quran, in directly adjacent
  verses, used in the perfectly mirrored 'if Allah made the night perpetual' /
  'if Allah made the day perpetual' parallel rhetorical questions. Pure structural jinas.
- **Q 96:15→16** with root `nSy` (`نصي`, *forelock*, global count 4):
  'We will surely drag him by the forelock — a lying, sinning forelock.'
  The rare anatomical root recurs across the verse boundary as the threat is named.
- **Q 9:108→109** with root `Ass` (*to found, foundation*, global count 3):
  the contrasted images of a mosque founded on righteousness vs. a building
  founded on a crumbling brink — the same root frames both halves of the parable.
- **Q 84:17→18** with root `wsq` (*to envelop / become full*, global count 2):
  the night that *envelops* and the moon that *waxes-full* — the same root both
  times, on its only two occurrences in the Quran, in adjacent verses of the oath.

### §7a  Meccan vs Medinan finding

- **Meccan mean density:** 0.1222  (n=86)
- **Medinan mean density:** 0.2366  (n=28)
- **Medinan/Meccan ratio:** 1.936×

Of the **top 15 most jinas-dense surahs**, **13 are Medinan** and **2 are Meccan** — a striking concentration given that Medinan surahs are only 28 of the 114.

This is a substantive observation: the rhetorical figure of root-repetition
is strongly *Medinan-coded*. Meccan surahs (especially the late short ones)
favour rhyme and assonance over root repetition; Medinan surahs are dominated
by long legal-moral discourses where contracts, witness, inheritance, kinship,
and disbelief are *named* over and over by their root-words. The single Meccan
outlier in the top 10 is **Al-Kafirun (109)**, whose entire surah is a
six-verse polemic structurally built around the repetition of `Ebd` (worship).

**Pearson r (surah length in verses, jinas density):** +0.165

Note that not every famous case is *root-level* repetition: 30:55's *as-saa`a*
uses the same root **swE** twice, which we verify positively. But 16:127 (Hzn vs Dyq)
is a *semantic* near-jinas, not a root one — the morphology won't catch it.
Similarly 17:14 (qara'a / kafa) plays on alliteration not root identity.
So a ❌ here means *the root-level test does not see jinas*; the verse may still
be a classical jinas at the lexical or sonic level.

## §10  Eleven most rhetorically beautiful detections

Hand-picked from §3 / §4 / §2b detections for maximum semantic-play yield.
All are root-level confirmations from the morphology corpus.


### 1. Q 13:28 — alladhina amanu wa-tatma'innu qulubuhum bi-dhikri llah — ala bi-dhikri llahi tatma'innu l-qulub — 'those who believed and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of Allah; verily, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest'

**Arabic:** الذين آمنوا وتطمئن قلوبهم بذكر الله ۗ ألا بذكر الله تطمئن القلوب

**Sahih:** Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured."

**Root:** `*kr` (ذكر) — appears **2** time(s) in this verse (global Quran count: 292)

**Wordplay note:** The most jinas-dense verse in the Quran by length-normalised density (8 of 9 root-bearing stems participate in some repetition, density 0.889). Four roots — *Tmn* (rest/assurance), *qlb* (heart), *dkr* (remembrance), *Alh* (Allah) — each appear exactly twice in a near-perfect chiastic mirror: {Tmn qlb dkr Alh | dkr Alh Tmn qlb}. The verse is a ring-composition palindrome compressed into a single line; its root-pattern is its meaning, the words *literally circling* the way a heart finds rest by returning to remembrance.

### 2. Q 30:55 — as-saa`a / saa`a — 'the Hour' vs 'an hour'

**Arabic:** ويوم تقوم الساعة يقسم المجرمون ما لبثوا غير ساعة ۚ كذلك كانوا يؤفكون

**Sahih:** And the Day the Hour appears the criminals will swear they had remained but an hour. Thus they were deluded.

**Root:** `swE` (سوع) — appears **2** time(s) in this verse (global Quran count: 49)

**Wordplay note:** The eschatological Hour and a fleeting hour collide in the same root. On the day the (capital-H) Hour rises, the criminals swear they only stayed an (lowercase) hour. The same word in two scales of time at the moment time ends — the most often-cited jinas in classical Quranic balagha.

### 3. Q 24:35 — Allahu nuru s-samawati wa-l-ardi … nurun `ala nur — 'Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth … light upon light'

**Arabic:** ۞ الله نور السماوات والأرض ۚ مثل نوره كمشكاة فيها مصباح ۖ المصباح في زجاجة ۖ الزجاجة كأنها كوكب دري يوقد من شجرة مباركة زيتونة لا شرقية ولا غربية يكاد زيتها يضيء ولو لم تمسسه نار ۚ نور على نور ۗ يهدي الله لنوره من يشاء ۚ ويضرب الله الأمثال للناس ۗ والله بكل شيء عليم

**Sahih:** Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. The example of His light is like a niche within which is a lamp, the lamp is within glass, the glass as if it were a pearly [white] star lit from [the oil of] a blessed olive tree, neither of the east nor of the west, whose oil would almost glow even if untouched by fire. Light upon light. Allah guides to His light whom He wills. And Allah presents examples for the people, and Allah is Knowing of all things.

**Root:** `nwr` (نور) — appears **6** time(s) in this verse (global Quran count: 194)

**Wordplay note:** n-w-r six times across the Light Verse, including the climactic *nurun `ala nur*. The verse doesn't just describe radiance — its sound *is* radiant, the root tolling like a bell across every clause.

## Honest statistics and caveats

- **Repetition ≠ jinas.** Many of the high-density verses use the same root
  three or more times for plain syntactic reasons (e.g. *qaala ... qaala*,
  *kaana ... kaana*) rather than rhetorical paronomasia. The catalog reports
  the morphological substrate; rhetorical labelling is a secondary judgement.
- **Over-counted roots.** Some segments tag both a derived noun and its
  Lemma to the same triliteral, so what looks like jinas is sometimes just
  a definite article + its noun's expected internal echo. The CSV preserves
  raw counts so any reader can re-filter.
- **Edit-distance-1 noise.** Many edit-distance-1 root pairs are accidental
  (e.g. `qwl` ↔ `qbl`) and not heard as jinas. We report them so a future
  agent can rank them by phonetic distance instead of orthographic.
- **Cross-verse coupling rarity.** A rare root re-appearing in the very next
  verse is striking, but the prior probability that *any* root drawn from
  verse N also lives in verse N+1 is non-trivial; we did not run a
  permutation null model in this run. Treat §5 as descriptive.
- The **Sahih translation** is included for context only; we did not use it
  to count anything.
