# Tawḥīd Rhetoric — How the Qurʾān Positively Argues for Monotheism

Most scholarly and polemical attention to Quranic monotheism focuses on its **negative** mode: refutation of *shirk* (association), the relentless critique of idols, the destruction-narratives around mushrikūn communities. This finding collects the **positive** rhetorical mode: the verses that do not merely deny other gods but *build affirmative arguments* for the One. Six rhetorical devices dominate the positive register, and they interlock.

## 1. The Six Positive Tawḥīd Devices — Overview

| # | Device | Function | Key locus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Signs-in-horizons / signs-in-selves** | Epistemic: evidence-from-cosmos-and-psyche | Q 41:53; Q 51:20-21 |
| 2 | **Creation-signs-rhetoric** (*inna fī dhālika la-āyāt*) | Inductive: catalogue of cosmic phenomena → God | Q 2:164; 3:190; 10:5-6; 13:2-4; 16:10-17; 30:20-27; 45:3-5 |
| 3 | **"Could there be another god?"** rhetorical-question family | Reductio ad absurdum: multiple-gods is incoherent | Q 17:42; 21:22; 23:91 |
| 4 | **The Q 27:60-64 cascade** | Five consecutive *a-ilāhun maʿa llāh* Q-marks | unique in corpus |
| 5 | **Self-reflection verses** | Interior evidence, embodied epistemology | Q 51:20-21; 30:8; 41:53 |
| 6 | **The four tawḥīd-masterpiece passages** | Distilled dogmatic statements (each a different mode) | Al-Fātiḥa, Āyat al-Kursī, Khawātim al-Ḥashr, Al-Ikhlāṣ |

And one structural spine running through all six: the formula **lā ilāha illā (huwa / Allāh / anā / anta)** appears in **35 verses** across 23 surahs. That is the verbal signature of the whole system.

## 2. "Signs in the horizons and in themselves" — Q 41:53

> سَنُرِيهِمْ آيَاتِنَا فِي الْآفَاقِ وَفِي أَنفُسِهِمْ حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَهُمْ أَنَّهُ الْحَقُّ أَوَلَمْ يَكْفِ بِرَبِّكَ أَنَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ شَهِيدٌ

> "We will show them Our signs in the horizons and within themselves, until it becomes clear to them that He is the truth. Is it not sufficient that your Lord is over all things a witness?" (Q 41:53)

This verse is the **pivot of Quranic epistemology**. It locates the evidence for God in two loci:

- **Fī al-āfāq** ("in the horizons") — the outer/cosmic/empirical
- **Fī anfusihim** ("in themselves") — the inner/introspective/psychosomatic

Structurally, the verse is unique. The exact phrase *fī al-āfāq wa-fī anfusihim* ("in the horizons and in themselves") appears only here. The phrase *fī anfusikum* ("in yourselves") as an epistemic locus — i.e. not "in yourselves" in a psychological-state sense but in the *sign*-sense — appears only at Q 51:21 and Q 41:53, making the Q 41:53 / Q 51:21 pair a two-verse micro-corpus for "interior evidence." (The 13 other `fī anfusihim/fī anfusikum` verses in the Quran are about internal dispositions, guilt, secrecy, hypocrisy — not epistemic signs.)

The second half of Q 41:53 does something else remarkable: "**Is it not enough that your Lord is over all things a witness?**" The argument moves from external evidence (signs), to interior evidence (self), and then steps up to *God as His own witness*. This is a three-layer epistemology: cosmic → psychic → theonomic. The fallback from empirical evidence to divine self-witnessing is not scepticism about the empirical; it is the claim that God's *shāhid*-hood is the substrate underneath all sign-reading.

## 3. Creation-signs-rhetoric — the *inna fī dhālika la-āyāt* refrain

The Qurʾān has a standardised inductive argument-form. It lists a cosmic phenomenon (rain, sun-cycle, fruit diversity, ships at sea, livestock, stars-as-compass) and then closes with a sign-refrain:

- **inna fī dhālika la-āyāt li-qawm yaʿqilūn / yatafakkarūn / yaʿlamūn / yasmaʿūn / yūqinūn**
  ("truly in that are signs for a people who reason/reflect/know/listen/are certain")

Corpus counts:

| Refrain | Count |
|---|---:|
| *la-āyāt li-qawm [verb]* | **29** verses |
| *la-āya li-qawm [verb]* (singular) | 6 verses |
| *inna fī dhālika la-āyāt* | 24 verses |
| *inna fī dhālika la-āya* | 20 verses |
| *afalā taʿqilūn / yaʿqilūn* | 13 verses |
| *min āyātihi* ("of His signs") | 12 verses (7 in Sūra 30 alone) |

### Case studies (the seven passages the task specifies)

**Q 2:164 — the "single verse" creation argument.** One verse stacks SEVEN signs: creation of heavens and earth, alternation of night and day, ships on the sea, rainfall reviving dead earth, living creatures spread abroad, winds, clouds suspended between heaven and earth. It closes *la-āyāt li-qawm yaʿqilūn*. This is the densest pack of natural signs in a single Quranic verse. It is also the longest "creation-signs" verse in the Quran.

**Q 3:190-191 — signs + embodied response.** The signs (heavens/earth alternation/day/night) lead explicitly to a HUMAN response in 191: the people-of-understanding *yadhkurūna Llāha qiyāman wa-quʿūdan wa-ʿalā junūbihim wa-yatafakkarūna fī khalqi l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* — "remember God standing, sitting, lying on their sides, and reflect on the creation." The sign-catalogue couples with a prescription for what to *do* with the signs. This is the only creation-signs passage in the Qurʾān that explicitly includes a remembrance-posture rubric (standing/sitting/reclining). Sign-reading is given a bodily protocol.

**Q 10:5-6 — astronomy + calendar.** "He made the sun a radiance and the moon a light and determined for it stations so you may know the number of years and the calculation [of time]." The monotheistic argument here is **utilitarian**: look at the sun-and-moon; they are *for* something (navigation, reckoning). Purposiveness entails Purposer. Closes *yufaṣṣilu l-āyāti li-qawm yaʿlamūn* (He details the signs for a people who know). Then v6 adds the alternation-of-night-and-day + the sky-and-earth-creation + a sign-refrain with a *different* verbal descriptor: *li-qawm yattaqūn* (a people who are God-conscious). The two refrains differ — one for cognition, one for piety. Signs work differently for different audiences.

**Q 13:2-4 — heavens-without-pillars + geography.** "Allah is the One who raised the heavens without pillars that you see." Then Throne-imagery, sun-and-moon subjugation, mountains, rivers, paired fruits, irrigation of adjacent fields by *a single water* yet producing preferred tastes. Closes with TWO sign-refrains (one per theme-cluster). The argument in v4 is particularly striking: *yusqā bi-māʾin wāḥidin wa-nufaḍḍilu baʿḍahā ʿalā baʿḍin fī l-ukul* — "irrigated with one water yet We make some superior to others in taste." Same substrate, differentiated outcomes: the Qurʾān pulls a *design* argument from the non-uniformity that emerges from uniform inputs. This is cognate with Q 16:10-17.

**Q 16:10-17 — the long creation-signs arc.** Eight consecutive verses chain signs across: rain → vegetation → grains/olives/dates/grapes → night+day+sun+moon+stars → color-diversity of livestock → sea (fish, ornaments, ships, bounty) → mountains-as-anchors → rivers → roads → landmarks (*ʿalāmāt*) → stars (*bi-l-najmi hum yahtadūn* — "by the stars they are guided"). Then v17 closes with a *rhetorical* pivot: **"Is the one who creates like the one who does not create? Will you not then remember?"** — *a-faman yakhluqu ka-man lā yakhluq a-fa-lā tadhakkarūn*. The entire sign-catalogue is retrospectively re-framed as the premise of a single rhetorical question: the creator-god and the non-creator-god are not equal — therefore only the Creator is God. This is a two-step argument: 8 verses of inductive data → 1 verse of deductive form.

**Q 30:20-27 — the *min āyātihi* cascade.** Seven consecutive verses open with "and of His signs is that…" (*wa-min āyātihi*) and catalogue: (20) your creation from dust → human propagation; (21) spouses from your own selves, put between you affection and mercy; (22) creation of heavens and earth, diversity of languages and colours; (23) sleep by night, seeking provision by day; (24) lightning (fear and hope) + rain; (25) heavens-and-earth standing by His command; and then (26) closes with a meta-statement "and to Him belong whoever is in the heavens and earth, all are devoutly obedient." Verse 27: "He is the One who initiates creation then restores it." **Sūra 30 (al-Rūm) holds 7 of the Quran's 12 *wa-min āyātihi* occurrences** — nearly 60% concentrated in one surah. This is a **local genre**: Sūrat al-Rūm is the Quran's dedicated "signs-of-God" catalogue.

**Q 45:3-5 — the compressed creation-signs.** Three verses deliver the same argument in ~40 words. Heavens and earth are signs for believers; *your creation and the creatures spread abroad* are signs for those certain in faith; alternation of night and day, provision from the sky, winds — signs for the reasoning. The interesting feature: each sign-cluster is indexed to a **different cognitive class** (*li-l-muʾminīn* → *li-qawm yūqinūn* → *li-qawm yaʿqilūn*). This is one of the tightest three-beat sign-audiences patterns in the corpus.

### The macro-pattern

Taken together, the creation-signs-rhetoric operates as **inductive monotheism**: nature is read as a signed text. The argument is not "look at the design → infer a designer" in the narrow Paley sense; it is "look at these phenomena, none of which you or any rival deity can *produce*, and ask who provides." The refrain *li-qawm yaʿqilūn* ("a people who reason") recurs 13+ times; *yatafakkarūn* (reflect) 11×; *yadhkurūn* (remember) 11×; *yaʿlamūn* (know) dozens. **The Qurʾān does not posit monotheism as a dogma accepted on authority; it posits it as the reasonable inference from unprocurable phenomena**, and names the cognitive faculty expected to make the inference each time.

## 4. "Could there be another god?" — the reductio ad absurdum family

Three verses deploy a *reductio* against polytheism:

- **Q 17:42**: *qul law kāna maʿahu āliha kamā yaqūlūn idhan la-btaghaw ilā dhī l-ʿarshi sabīlā* — "If there were with Him gods as they say, then they would have sought a way to the Lord of the Throne." (Multiple gods would jostle for position; they would seek a path to the Supreme. Since reality shows no such cosmic contest, the premise fails.)

- **Q 21:22**: *law kāna fīhimā ālihatun illā llāhu la-fasadatā fa-subḥāna llāhi rabbi l-ʿarshi ʿammā yaṣifūn* — "If there were in them [the heavens and earth] gods other than Allah, they would both be corrupted. Glory to Allah, Lord of the Throne, above what they describe." (The stability of the cosmos proves singular sovereignty; dual-sovereignty implies collapse.)

- **Q 23:91**: *mā-ttakhadha llāhu min waladin wa-mā kāna maʿahu min ilāhin idhan la-dhahaba kullu ilāhin bimā khalaqa wa-la-ʿalā baʿḍuhum ʿalā baʿḍ sub ḥāna llāhi ʿammā yaṣifūn* — "Had there been with Him any god, then each god would have taken what he created, and some would have overcome others." (Polytheism → partitioning of creation by gods + hierarchical contest; neither is seen, so the premise is false.)

The three share a move: **assume the adversary premise, derive an absurdity, conclude with *subḥān Allāh ʿammā...***. This is the Quran's *tanzīh* dialectic — the same formula that closes several of the Q 27:60-64 cascade-verses.

Note also the surface-feature: **three of these four "reductio" verses end with the phrase *dhī / rabbi l-ʿarsh***. The Throne becomes the figure for the unity being argued: a Throne does not host two occupants. *Subḥāna rabbi l-ʿarshi ʿammā yaṣifūn* is the liturgical coda of the reductio.

## 5. The Q 27:60-64 Cascade — Five Consecutive *a-ilāhun maʿa llāh*

This passage is one of this project's flagged cryptographic-signature cases.

| v | Opening | Content | Closing refrain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | *a-man khalaqa l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa* | creation of heavens/earth + rain + gardens | **a-ilāhun maʿa llāh**, bal hum qawmun yaʿdilūn |
| 61 | *a-man jaʿala l-arḍa qarāran* | earth as dwelling, rivers, mountains, barrier-between-seas | **a-ilāhun maʿa llāh**, bal aktharuhum lā yaʿlamūn |
| 62 | *a-man yujību l-muḍṭarra* | the responder to the distressed, the one who makes you successors | **a-ilāhun maʿa llāh**, qalīlan mā tadhakkarūn |
| 63 | *a-man yahdīkum fī ẓulumāti l-barri wa-l-baḥr* | guidance in land/sea darknesses, winds of mercy | **a-ilāhun maʿa llāh**, taʿālā llāhu ʿammā yushrikūn |
| 64 | *a-man yabdaʾu l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduhu* | origination + restoration of creation, provision | **a-ilāhun maʿa llāh**, qul hātū burhānakum in kuntum ṣādiqīn |

**The key finding.** The phrase *a-ilāhun maʿa llāh* ("is there a god WITH Allah?") occurs in **exactly five verses in the entire Qurʾān — all five here, consecutive, Q 27:60-64**. I ran the scan on the full 6,236-verse corpus (see `journal/tawhid-run-1.md` for methodology): zero occurrences outside this block. This is a zero-background, five-hit consecutive run. It is structurally identical to the *huwa llāhu lladhī lā ilāha illā huwa* twin-opener pattern at Q 59:22-23 and Q 2:149-150 — the Quran's rhetorical spotlighting device.

**Internal choreography.** The five *a-man…* openings (each a rhetorical question "who is it that…?") rotate through five different divine actions:

1. **Cosmogony** (heavens/earth + rain + gardens)
2. **Terraforming** (dwelling/rivers/mountains/barrier-between-seas)
3. **Responsive deity** (answerer-of-the-distressed)
4. **Guidance** (through darkness)
5. **Eschatology** (origination + restoration, the *badaʾa/yuʿīdu* pair)

The five panels map cleanly onto: **cosmological → geological → pastoral → navigational → eschatological**. And the five closing tails rotate through five different *responses-to-disbelief*:

1. "they are a people who deviate"
2. "most of them do not know"
3. "little do you remember"
4. "exalted is Allah above what they associate"
5. "produce your proof if you are truthful"

So the cascade is a five-by-five matrix: five divine actions × five rebukes. And the *badaʾa l-khalqa thumma yuʿīduhu* at v64 is one of the Quran's most loaded phrases — it appears in only 6 verses (Q 10:4, 10:34, 27:64, 29:19, 30:11, 30:27), functioning as the Quran's compressed proof-from-eschatology ("the one who starts it can remake it"). Anchoring the cascade's final beat on this phrase ties the last panel to the resurrection-argument.

**Numerical shape.** Letter counts across the five verses: 104 / 89 / 70 / 82 / 78. Word counts: 27 / 21 / 16 / 20 / 19. Total: 103 words, 423 letters. The middle verse (62) is the shortest — a classical *taqṣīr* (compression) at ring-center. The first and last are the longest, framing.

**Verdict.** The Q 27:60-64 cascade is a signature-level case. Five-times-zero-elsewhere is as close to a hard rhetorical-signature as this corpus offers, on par with the Ḥashr twin-opener and the Q 2:149-150 qibla-pair. Classical rhetoric names this device *tikrār* (strategic repetition) combined with *taqsīm* (division into panels). The Qurʾān uses this device exactly once for a-ilāhun maʿa llāh, exactly once for huwa-llāhu-lladhī-lā-ilāha-illā-huwa (Ḥashr), and exactly once for wa-min-ḥaythu-kharajta (qibla, Baqara). These three ring-locking devices mark the three densest tawḥīd-passages in the Qurʾān.

## 6. Self-Reflection Verses — Q 51:20-21 and the Interior Turn

> وَفِي الْأَرْضِ آيَاتٌ لِّلْمُوقِنِينَ / وَفِي أَنفُسِكُمْ ۚ أَفَلَا تُبْصِرُونَ (Q 51:20-21)

> "And on the earth are signs for those of certainty, and in yourselves; will you not then see?"

This two-verse pair is the Qurʾān's most compressed statement of interior-evidence-for-God. Nothing else matches its economy: five Arabic words for "and in yourselves; will you not see?" Compare Q 41:53 (longer, more discursive): the two verses form a planetary pair, both invoking *fī anfus-* as epistemic locus.

Related self-reflection verses:

- **Q 30:8**: *a-wa-lam yatafakkarū fī anfusihim mā khalaqa llāhu l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa wa-mā baynahumā illā bi-l-ḥaqq* — "Have they not reflected within themselves [that] Allah did not create the heavens and the earth and all between them except in truth…?" Here *fī anfusihim* functions as the locus of *reflection itself*, not only of evidence. Introspection is the *site* where the creation-truth becomes visible.

- **Q 57:22** (not a tawḥīd verse per se but using the same locus-grammar): *mā aṣāba min muṣībatin fī l-arḍi wa-lā fī anfusikum* — misfortunes struck in the earth or in yourselves. The earth/self pairing here is calamity-locus rather than sign-locus, but the *grammatical template* is the same: Quranic Arabic regularly treats the self as a parallel ontological site to the earth.

This is where Quranic epistemology diverges sharply from Greek natural-theology. The Quran's *āfāq / anfus* division is structurally identical to the later Islamic philosophical division of *āfāqī* (objective) vs *anfusī* (subjective) evidence — and that distinction is post-Quranic, drawing directly on Q 41:53 as the proof-text. The *anfusī* mode is not a mystical add-on to objective nature-theology; it is co-original with it in the Quran's sign-epistemology.

## 7. The Four Tawḥīd-Masterpiece Passages — Fourfold Distillation

Four passages in the Qurʾān are singled out by classical tradition as apex tawḥīd-statements. Each operates in a different rhetorical mode.

### (a) Al-Fātiḥa (Q 1) — **liturgical-relational tawḥīd**

29 words, 7 verses. Invokes Allāh, al-Raḥmān, al-Raḥīm, Mālik (Owner of the Day of Recompense), then pivots at v5 to *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn* ("You alone do we worship; from You alone do we seek help"). The tawḥīd is enacted in the *iyyāka*-construction — the grammatical exclusive. This is **worship-tawḥīd** (*tawḥīd al-ulūhiyya* in later theological terminology): monotheism as the restriction of the worship-object to one.

### (b) Āyat al-Kursī (Q 2:255) — **ontological-negative-positive tawḥīd**

50 words, 189 letters (= 3³ × 7). Ten *jumal* in ring-composition with a rhetorical question at the center. Braids **apophasis** (no drowsiness, no sleep, no fatigue) with **kataphasis** (al-Ḥayy, al-Qayyūm, al-ʿAlī, al-ʿAẓīm). This is **being-tawḥīd** (*tawḥīd al-rubūbiyya* — lordship): monotheism as the statement of what God *is* (Living, Self-Subsisting, Most-High) with the *lā*-clauses blocking idolatrous encroachment. Uses the formula *Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa* — one of only 8 verses.

### (c) Khawātim al-Ḥashr (Q 59:22-24) — **name-listing positive tawḥīd**

49 words (= 7²) in vv 22-24, 216 letters (= 6³), 15 divine names (8 of which appear nowhere else in the Qurʾān). Uses *huwa llāhu lladhī lā ilāha illā huwa* twin-opener technique (only two such pairs in the entire Quran). This is **names-tawḥīd** (*tawḥīd al-asmāʾ wa-l-ṣifāt* — names-and-attributes): monotheism as the concentration of all perfection-names in a single referent. Ends with the meta-statement *lahu l-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā* ("to Him belong the Most Beautiful Names") — one of only 4 verses.

### (d) Al-Ikhlāṣ (Q 112) — **definitional apophatic tawḥīd**

15 words, 61 letters, 4 verses. The most compressed tawḥīd-passage. The first verse is pure identity (*qul huwa llāhu aḥad* — "He is God, One"); the second is a unique divine-name hapax (*al-ṣamad*, "the Eternal Refuge," found only here); the third and fourth are apophatic triplet (*lam yalid wa-lam yūlad wa-lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad* — "He begets not, nor was He begotten, nor is anything comparable to Him"). This is **definitional tawḥīd**: monotheism as the denial of *all* possible analogies (progenitor, progeny, equal). Classical hadith: it is worth one-third of the Quran (Bukhari 5013, Muslim 811).

### The fourfold as a system

The four passages are NOT redundant. Each operates at a different rhetorical-theological scale:

| Passage | Mode | Formal signature |
|---|---|---|
| Al-Fātiḥa | Relational (worship-directed) | *iyyāka* grammatical exclusive |
| Āyat al-Kursī | Ontological (being-directed) | Rhetorical Q at ring-center + apophasis/kataphasis braid |
| Khawātim al-Ḥashr | Nominal (name-directed) | Name-pile + twin-opener + asmāʾ-ḥusnā meta |
| Al-Ikhlāṣ | Definitional (essence-directed) | Triplet-apophasis + hapax (*al-ṣamad*) |

They form a **four-corners system** that covers (1) the worshipper's posture, (2) God's being, (3) God's names, (4) God's essence. Classical tradition ranks each "greatest" for a different reason. The tradition is not confused; it has correctly identified four different apex-passages that do four different things.

**Cross-link.** All four use the *lā ilāha illā* / *aḥad* negation-of-rivals signature in some form:

- Fātiḥa: *iyyāka naʿbud* (positive exclusive)
- Kursī: *Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa*
- Ḥashr: *huwa llāhu lladhī lā ilāha illā huwa* ×2
- Ikhlāṣ: *wa-lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad*

All four are "grammatical exclusives" — whether by *iyyā*, by *lā... illā*, or by the *aḥad/kufuw* pairing of Sūra 112.

## 8. The *lā ilāha illā* Formula — 35 Verses, 23 Surahs

The phrase *lā ilāha illā* + pronoun/noun appears in **35 verses**:

| Variant | Count |
|---|---:|
| *lā ilāha illā huwa* | 29 |
| *lā ilāha illā Allāh* | 2 (Q 37:35, 47:19) |
| *lā ilāha illā anā* (first-person, divine voice) | 3 (Q 16:2, 20:14, 21:25) |
| *lā ilāha illā anta* (second-person, prayer) | 1 (Q 21:87, the prayer of Jonah) |
| **total** | **35** |

The 35 instances distribute across the corpus with clusters in Sūras 3 (Āl ʿImrān, 3×), 20 (Taha, 3×), 40 (Ghāfir, 3×), and pairs in Sūras 2, 6, 9, 21, 28, 59. The Medinan / Meccan split is roughly balanced. 35 = 5 × 7.

The **pronominal variation** is theologically substantive:
- *huwa* (third-person) is the narrator-voice declaration: God is being *described* by the text.
- *Allāh* (explicit subject) is the community-voice declaration: God is being *named*.
- *anā* (first-person) is the divine-voice declaration: God is being *self-quoted*.
- *anta* (second-person) is the prayer-voice declaration: God is being *addressed*.

All four subject-positions of Arabic grammar are represented. The Qurʾān cycles through every grammatical position for the one formula. **Tawḥīd is declared in every deictic voice** — narrator, community, deity, worshipper.

### Sub-formulas

- *Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa* (8×): Q 2:255, 3:2, 4:87, 9:129, 20:8, 27:26, 28:70, 64:13.
- *lladhī lā ilāha illā huwa* (3×): Q 20:98, 59:22, 59:23. (Two of the three cluster in Khawātim al-Ḥashr.)
- *ilāhun wāḥid* ("one god") phrasing: Q 2:163, 4:171, 5:73, 14:52, 16:22, 16:51, 21:108, 22:34, 29:46, 41:6, 18:110 — another ~11 instances, often with the "your god is one god" declaration-formula.

If we combine the *lā ilāha illā* family (35) with the *ilāhun wāḥid* family (~11) and the reductio-family (3), plus the Q 27:60-64 cascade (5 unique *a-ilāhun maʿa llāh*), the Qurʾān carries **~54 distinct explicit tawḥīd-declarations** across its surface, in addition to the ~100+ implicit arguments via creation-signs-rhetoric. Tawḥīd is not concentrated in the masterpiece-passages — it is textual infrastructure.

## 9. Synthesis — What the Quran's Positive Tawḥīd Does

1. **It argues from data**, not dogma. Creation-signs-rhetoric (inductive), self-reflection verses (introspective), reductio-arguments (deductive) and eschatology-arguments (predictive) are deployed together.

2. **It indexes cognitive class.** Each sign-refrain names the faculty expected to make the inference (*yaʿqilūn* / *yatafakkarūn* / *yaʿlamūn* / *yūqinūn* / *yadhkurūn* / *yasmaʿūn*). The Qurʾān is self-conscious that different audiences have different epistemic access.

3. **It operates at four ontological registers.** Relational (Fātiḥa), ontological (Kursī), nominal (Ḥashr), essential (Ikhlāṣ). The masterpiece-passages distribute the theological load; none is alone.

4. **It engineers its most-loaded passages as structurally unique.** The Q 27:60-64 cascade, the Q 59:22-23 twin-opener, and the Q 2:255 ring-composition are not accidental rhetoric. Each is rhetorically unique-in-corpus. This is the formal side of the tawḥīd-claim: if God is One, the claim is expressed with one-shot-per-device signatures.

5. **It uses all four grammatical persons.** The *lā ilāha illā huwa/Allāh/anā/anta* family cycles through third-person, named-subject, first-person, and second-person in 35 verses. The grammatical totality mirrors the theological totality: there is no deictic refuge from the declaration.

6. **It grounds monotheism in sign-reading, not authority.** The Quran does not say "I am God; believe it." It says: *look*, *reflect*, *remember*, *reason*, *see*. Authority comes after sign-reading, not before. This is the Quran's deepest contrast with mythological monotheisms: the God it declares is presented as already *evidently* the God, and the verses arguing this are the evidence-presenting layer.

## 10. Honest Caveats

- The "grammatical exclusive" move in §7 is partly conventional exegesis, not a hard structural claim.
- The four-corners theological schema maps onto the later Ibn Taymiyyan classification of *ulūhiyya / rubūbiyya / asmāʾ-wa-ṣifāt* — this is readable from the Quran but is also an imposed interpretive lens.
- "35 verses" is the strict match of the *lā ilāha illā* formula on no-tashkeel orthography; fuzzy matches (e.g., *mā min ilāhin illā*, Q 5:73) add a few more under looser rules.
- The Q 27:60-64 cascade being zero-background elsewhere is a hard corpus fact; the claim that it is a "cryptographic signature" is a framework claim from our signature-hunt methodology, not a corpus-theoretic result.
- The cognate *a-faman yakhluqu ka-man lā yakhluq* at Q 16:17 is a rhetorical-question-tawḥīd in a *different* register (comparison-rhetoric rather than possibility-rhetoric); I placed it in §3 rather than §4 but it could belong to either family.
