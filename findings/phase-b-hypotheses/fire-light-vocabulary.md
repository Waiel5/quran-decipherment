# Fire and Light Vocabulary in the Qurʾān

**Phase B hypothesis — run-2. Source: Leeds Quranic Arabic Corpus (QAC) morphology v0.4.
Inventories are extracted by ROOT and LEM features; all token counts are segment-level unless otherwise noted.**

Counts reported here are per-segment from the QAC morphology file. One orthographic word can yield several morphological segments, but for the "core" lemmas inventoried below every segment is its own token (no proclitics attached to the head), so segments equal tokens in all the tables that follow.

Two headline observations already fall out:

1. **The fire/light ratio inside a single root.** The root ن و ر splits into *nār* 145 and *nūr* + *munīr* 49. The text's single most productive root for this domain is the one that sustains both polarities — destructive fire and illuminating light — in one lexeme. Arabic rarely pairs antonyms from one triliteral; *nār* and *nūr* are the paradigmatic exception, and the Qurʾān leans on it ~194 times.
2. **Hapax and near-hapax concentrations.** *Ramād* (14:18) is an absolute hapax; *qabas* (20:10, 27:7, 57:13) is a thrice-attested cross-link; *iqtabasa* occurs only in 57:13; *istawqada* only in 2:17. Three of the rarest words in the fire/light field are exactly the words that tie the Mosaic theophany to the hypocrite parable to the eschatological partition of light.

## 2. The nār / nūr semantic split

Lemma-level counts are exact: **naAr 145, nuwr 43, muniyr 6**. Distributionally these two lemmas almost never interfere — *nār* is virtually always a punitive referent (hell, destruction, fire as test), and *nūr* is virtually always a divine, guiding, or revelational referent. The shared root is the hinge.

**nār as punishment / test / earthly fire.** Of the 145 occurrences of *nār*, the overwhelming majority collocate with *aṣḥāb al-nār* ("companions of the Fire"), *khālidīn fī l-nār* ("abiding in the Fire"), *ʿadhāb al-nār* ("punishment of the Fire"), or *dakhala l-nār* ("enters the Fire"). The two largest concentrations are in al-Baqarah (18 tokens) and Āl ʿImrān (12) — precisely the surahs that argue most about covenant breaking and disbelief. Earthly uses of *nār* cluster around (a) the fire Moses sees on the mountain, (b) the fire Abraham is thrown into (Q 21:69 *yā nāru kūnī bardan*), (c) metallurgy and combustion as sign (13:17; 36:80 "from the green tree"), and (d) fire-starting parables (2:17, 5:64, 14:18).

**nūr as divine light / guidance / revelation.** 43 *nūr* tokens span 33 distinct verses. The cluster that dominates the concordance is the formula *min al-ẓulumāt ilā al-nūr* — "from the darknesses to the light" — which recurs at 2:257, 5:16, 14:1, 14:5, 33:43, 57:9, 65:11. In every case it describes God's (or the Prophet's, or the Book's) extractive action: rescuing from plural darkness into singular light. The lexical asymmetry *ẓulumāt* (plural) vs *nūr* (singular) is stable across all seven instances — darkness is many, light is one.

**munīr as epithet.** The adjective *munīr* ("light-giving, luminous") occurs 6× and attaches to three referents: the Prophet (33:46 — *sirājan munīran*), the moon (25:61 — *qamaran munīran*), and the Book (3:184; 22:8; 31:20; 35:25 — *al-kitāb al-munīr*). Notice the substitution pattern: moon, book, prophet — all derivative luminaries that reflect a primary light, never the sun. The sun is consistently *sirāj* / *ḍiyāʾ* (original emitters); the moon is *nūr* / *munīr* (reflective). See 10:5 and 71:16 below.

**The Q 2:257 hinge verse.** "Allāh is the walī of those who believe; He brings them out of the darknesses into the light. And those who disbelieve — their walīs are the ṭāghūt: they bring them out of the light into the darknesses. Those are the companions of the Fire; therein they abide." The verse stacks both lemmas from the same root in a single sentence: *al-nūr* is what the believer is led into; *al-nār* is what the disbeliever is committed to. The typology is lexical, not just semantic: one root, two vowelings, two eschatologies.

## 3. Surah 24 al-Nūr and the Light Verse (Q 24:35)

Surah 24 is the only surah named for a lemma in this field. Its root-distribution inside the surah is:

- *nūr* 7 tokens (24:35 ×5, 24:40 ×2)
- *nār* 2 tokens (24:35, 24:57)
- *miṣbāḥ* 2 tokens (24:35 both)
- *awqada → yūqad* 1 token (24:35)
- *aḍāʾa → yuḍīʾu* 1 token (24:35)

Thirteen of the 14 fire/light tokens in Surah 24 are packed into verse 35 alone. No other verse in the Qurʾān comes close to this density for the field.

**Anatomy of Q 24:35 ("The Light Verse").** The verse is a nested simile:

1. Opening predication: *Allāhu nūru l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* — "God is the light of the heavens and the earth."
2. First comparison: *mathalu nūrihi ka-mishkātin fīhā miṣbāḥ* — "the likeness of His light is as a niche in which there is a lamp"
3. Second nesting: *al-miṣbāḥu fī zujājah* — "the lamp is in a glass"
4. Third nesting: *al-zujājatu ka-annahā kawkabun durriyy* — "the glass is as if it were a pearly star"
5. Source of fuel: *yūqadu min shajaratin mubārakatin zaytūnatin* — "lit from a blessed tree, an olive" (uses the wqd root)
6. Neither-nor clause: *lā sharqiyyatin wa-lā gharbiyyah* — oil's autonomy from east/west
7. Property of oil: *yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾu wa-law lam tamsashu nār* — "its oil would almost glow though fire had not touched it" (the only co-occurrence of *nār* inside the Light Verse; it is explicitly *absent* — light without fire)
8. Climactic triplication: *nūrun ʿalā nūr* — "light upon light"
9. Closing: *yahdī llāhu li-nūrihi man yashāʾu* — "God guides to His light whom He wills"

The rhetorical engine of the verse is the claim that the highest light is light without fire. *Yakādu zaytuhā yuḍīʾu wa-law lam tamsashu nār* is the only place in the Qurʾān where *ḍawʾ* and *nār* stand in direct negation of one another. Every other fire/light co-occurrence in the Qurʾān treats the two as causally linked (fire produces light; no fire, no light); here alone the oil is so pure it would glow autonomously. The pattern *nūr ʿalā nūr* is the grammatical peak — no predicate, no simile, just lexical doubling of the headword, matching the prior triple simile-nesting with a triple iteration of the same word. The structure is 3×simile → 3×nūr.

**Q 24:40 as the negative image.** Verse 40, five verses later, furnishes the contrast: *ẓulumātun baʿḍuhā fawqa baʿḍ* — "darknesses, one above another." The threefold nesting of lamp → glass → star is inverted to the threefold darkness of wave → wave → cloud, and the closing line *wa-man lam yajʿali llāhu lahu nūran fa-mā lahu min nūr* closes the ring: no light for whom God has not made light. The surah's lexical frame is: the Light Verse as theophanic assertion, then the darkness-verse as existential diagnosis, then 24:57's closing *wa-maʾwāhumu l-nār* — hell's fire as the terminal option for those outside the nūr.

## 4. Q 2:17 — the hypocrite fire-starter

The opening simile of the hypocrites (Q 2:17) is lexically dense:

*mathaluhum ka-mathali lladhī **istawqada nāran**, fa-lammā **aḍāʾat** mā ḥawlahu, **dhahaba llāhu bi-nūrihim** wa-tarakahum fī **ẓulumātin** lā yubṣirūn.*

"Their parable is as the parable of one who **kindled a fire**, and when it **illuminated** what was around him, **God took their light away** and left them in **darknesses** — they see not."

Four of the ten fire/light roots are fired in one verse: **wqd** (*istawqada*, hapax form), **nwr** (*nār*, then *nūr*), **DwA** (*aḍāʾat*), and the implicit contrast-set **ẓulumāt**. The narrative move is brutal. The hypocrites do not receive light as a gift (contrast 57:28 — *yajʿal lakum nūran tamshūna bih*, "He will make for you a light by which you walk"); they **ignite** it themselves (*istawqada*, the reflexive/middle of *awqada*). Then, at the moment of illumination, God does not take the fire — He takes the **light** (*bi-nūrihim*). The hypocrite-parable thus encodes exactly the split diagnosed in §2: fire and light, though from a single root, are ontologically separable. The hypocrite-actor can manufacture *nār* but cannot retain *nūr*. The verse executes the theological claim *at the morphological level*: take two vowel-patterns of the same root, give one to man and the other to God, remove God's gift, and the man is left with the darkness his fire was supposed to dispel.

**Parallel:** Q 5:64 uses the same active *awqadū nāran* for the enemies of the Prophet — "every time they kindle a fire for war, God extinguishes it." The pattern is Qurʾānic: human-kindled fire is put out by God; God-given light is the one that is not.

## 5. Moses seeking fire, finding God: Q 20:10, 27:7, 28:29

The Mosaic theophany at the tree is told three times, and the fire-light vocabulary increases in density each retelling.

- **Q 20:10** (Ṭā-Hā): *innī **ānastu nāran**, laʿallī ātīkum minhā **bi-qabasin** aw ajidu ʿalā l-nāri hudan.* — "I have perceived a fire; perhaps I will bring you from it a firebrand (*qabas*) or find guidance at the fire."
- **Q 27:7** (al-Naml): *innī ānastu nāran, sa-ātīkum minhā bi-khabarin aw ātīkum **bi-shihābin qabasin** laʿallakum taṣṭalūn.* — "…or I will bring you a flaming brand (*shihāb qabas*) that you may warm yourselves."
- **Q 28:29** (al-Qaṣaṣ): *ānasa min jānibi l-ṭūri nāran … laʿallī ātīkum minhā bi-khabarin aw **jadhwatin min al-nār** laʿallakum taṣṭalūn.* — "…or a glowing ember (*jadhwah*) from the fire that you may warm yourselves."

Three tellings, three different specific terms for the hand-carried flame: *qabas*, *shihāb qabas*, *jadhwah*. The verb *ānasa* ("perceived, sensed, felt friendliness toward") is shared by all three. Moses approaches the *nār* looking to extract warmth and illumination from it — *laʿallakum taṣṭalūn*, "that you may warm yourselves," is literally a utility request. What he receives is theophany (*fa-lammā atāhā nūdiya*). The fire was for heating; the Voice was the gift.

The **qbs** root, rare throughout (three tokens), anchors the pattern. *Qabas* = a portable piece of a larger fire. Moses's domestic use of the word — taking a little fire home to his family — becomes the lexical bridge to 57:13, where the same root surfaces as *iqtabasa*: "the hypocrites say 'wait for us, **let us take from your light**' (nqtabis min nūrikum)." In 57:13, the object of the request is no longer *nār* (as for Moses) but *nūr* — and the request is refused. The qbs root thus spans the entire theological arc: Moses's permitted portable fire → the hypocrites' denied portable light. The Leeds morphology confirms the lemma link (both *qabas* and *naqotabiso* are tagged under ROOT:qbs).

Two further linkages worth registering:

- Q 27:7 pairs **qabas** and **shihāb** in a construct: *shihāb qabas* — the very word that elsewhere names anti-jinn missiles (see §6). In the Moses story the *shihāb* is benign, domestic. In the jinn surahs it is a defensive weapon. The lexeme's semantic range (from Moses's kitchen firebrand to heaven's anti-eavesdropping ordnance) is fixed by cotext, not by the word itself.
- Q 21:48 awards Moses and Aaron *al-furqān wa-ḍiyāʾ wa-dhikr* — "the criterion, a light (*ḍiyāʾ*), and a reminder." The *ḍiyāʾ* in Moses's hand is the revelation; the *nār* on the mountain is the summons. The two are distinguished lexically.

## 6. Shihāb: the anti-eavesdropping missiles

The root **ش ه ب** is used 5 times, across 4 verses:

| Location | Form | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 15:18 | *shihāb mubīn* | "a clear burning brand" follows the one who steals a hearing |
| 27:7 | *shihāb qabas* | Moses — benign firebrand (domestic) |
| 37:10 | *shihāb thāqib* | "piercing burning brand" pursues the one who snatches a snatch |
| 72:8 | *shuhub* (pl.) | the jinn find the sky "filled with stern guards and shooting stars" |
| 72:9 | *shihāb raṣad* | "a burning brand in wait" for any would-be eavesdropper |

Four of the five tokens (15:18, 37:10, 72:8, 72:9) describe the same cosmological scene: the lower heavens are now guarded; jinn who used to sit there to steal scraps of divine speech are now shot down. Only 27:7 deploys the term outside this frame, in the Moses narrative, and there the word is disambiguated by the attached noun *qabas* ("firebrand *for-lighting*"). Contextually: bare *shihāb* + attribute of piercing/burning = missile; *shihāb + qabas* = household firebrand.

The surahs that carry the scene are clustered toward the end of the Meccan sequence (15, 37, 72), with the al-Jinn surah (72) offering both the jinn's own first-person report ("we used to sit there") and the aetiology of the shooting-star phenomenon. Q 72:9's *raṣad* ("in ambush / lying in wait") is the key adjective: the *shihāb* is not random celestial debris but targeted interdiction. The Qurʾānic cosmology repurposes a shooting-star — a public, observable phenomenon — as the trace of an eavesdropper-interception event.

## 7. The horn — nafkh al-ṣūr

Leeds tags 10 tokens of the noun *ṣūr* (horn) in the form *S~uwri*, distributed across 10 verses: **6:73, 18:99, 20:102, 23:101, 27:87, 36:51, 39:68 (×2), 50:20, 69:13, 78:18**. Every occurrence pairs with the verb **نفخ** (nfkh), and every occurrence is eschatological: the blast of the horn signals the cessation of the present order and the commencement of resurrection and judgment.

**Morphological pattern.** The verb forms used with the horn are almost always passive: *yunfakhu fī l-ṣūr* ("the horn is blown"; 6:73, 18:99, 20:102, 23:101, 27:87, 36:51, 39:68 ×2, 50:20, 78:18) or *nufikha fī l-ṣūr* (perfective passive). The agent is never named — the horn blows itself, or is blown by an unnamed celestial. Only Q 74:8 uses a different locution (*nuqira fī l-nāqūr* — "when the trumpet is sounded"), which is not nfkh+ṣūr but qnr+nāqūr, a lexical variant.

**The two blasts of Q 39:68.** Z. 39:68 is the only verse that stages **both** blasts consecutively:

*wa **nufikha fī l-ṣūri** fa-ṣaʿiqa man fī l-samāwāti wa-man fī l-arḍi illā man shāʾa llāh; **thumma nufikha fīhi ukhrā** fa-idhā hum qiyāmun yanẓurūn.*

"The horn is blown, and whoever is in the heavens and the earth is struck senseless, except whom God wills. Then it is blown again, and behold, they are standing, looking on."

Two blasts (*marratān* / *ukhrā*), two effects: (a) universal ṣaʿaq ("thunder-stunning, death-faint"), then (b) universal upright standing. Q 69:13 — *fa-idhā nufikha fī l-ṣūri **nafkhatan wāḥidah*** ("a single blast") — is sometimes read as the first of the two (the destructive blast), though commentators disagree. The word *nafkhah* (action-noun, marked form) is itself a hapax in the Qurʾān and appears only here, within the opening of al-Ḥāqqah (69:13), precisely where the "Reality" surah is staging the cosmic rupture.

**The dual usage of nafakha.** The root نفخ is used 20 times across the Qurʾān, and its application divides cleanly into three theological uses:

- **Blowing the horn** (10 tokens — see above).
- **Breathing the spirit into Adam** (four tokens: 15:29, 32:9, 38:72 for Adam; see also 21:91 / 66:12 for the Virgin Mary, who receives *nafakhnā fīhā min rūḥinā*). The formula is always *nafakha fīhi/fīhā min rūḥī / rūḥinā / rūḥihi* — the possessor of the breathed spirit is God.
- **Jesus breathing into the clay bird** (3:49, 5:110 — *fa-anfukhu fīhi fa-yakūnu ṭayran bi-idhni llāh*). Jesus is the only human who takes over the verb as its subject; even then the act is qualified *bi-idhni llāh*, "by God's permission."

So one root, نفخ, carries three theological acts: creation (Adam, Mary), miracle (Jesus's bird), and judgment (the horn). In all three cases the breath is either God's directly or delegated. The bookends of the human story — breath-in at Adam's forming, breath-in at the resurrection horn — use the same verb. Genesis and apocalypse share a lexical root, and the implication is that the same creator-breath that animated the first human will sound the trumpet that ends and re-raises the species.

## Cross-connections

A small number of triple-link verses tie the field together:

- **Q 57:13** (*naqtabis min nūrikum*) quotes the hypocrites of 2:17 lexically (they want light), morally (they failed to obtain it), and via root qbs (Moses's word for portable fire). Late-Medinan audit of the early-Medinan parable, using Moses's word for Moses's fire.
- **Q 24:35** stacks five roots (nwr, SbH, wqd, DwA, nwr again) into a single verse, making it the densest fire/light verse in the Qurʾān and the only verse where *ḍawʾ* is affirmed **independent of** *nār*.
- **Q 27:7** uses *shihāb qabas* in the Mosaic narrative, the one non-weaponised use of *shihāb*. Same lexeme, different genre; the jinn-missile word is naturalised by Moses's domestic use.
- **Q 10:5 + 71:16** both pair sun-as-sirāj/ḍiyāʾ with moon-as-nūr/munīr. Emitter vs reflector is stable across surahs.
- **Q 33:46** gives the Prophet the epithet *sirājan munīran* — the same noun the sun holds in 71:16. The Prophet is iconographically **solar** in vocabulary, not lunar.

- The root جذو (jadhwah, Q 28:29) was not inventoried; it appears to be a hapax and deserves a hapax-only check.
- For *nūr* vs *nār* totals I report lemma counts (43 / 145 / 6 munīr = 194 root segments). The often-cited "49 nūr" figure in devotional literature counts orthographic occurrences of the string including pronoun-suffixed forms (*nūrinā*, *nūrihim*, etc.) at the word level; Leeds tags the head noun once per segment, so 43 + 6 munīr = 49 exactly when one adds the epithet adjective. Both counting conventions converge on the classical total.
