# Earth-Sciences & Life-Sciences Claims in the Qur'an: An Honest Audit

**Phase B hypothesis audit · Run 1 · 2026-04-12**

This document works through twelve verse-clusters routinely cited in the "scientific miracles of the Qur'an" literature (i'jāz 'ilmī). For each, I give the verse(s), a classical tafsir gloss (principally al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr), the modern apologetic reading, the relevant science, and a verdict. The project's commitment is honesty: remarkable observations should not be denied, but neither should apologetic overreach be endorsed. The usual failure mode is to read modern findings *into* a verse whose classical interpreters understood it in perfectly mundane terms.

---

## 1. Mountains as Pegs (awtād / rawāsiya)

**Verses.** Q 16:15 "wa alqā fī-l-arḍi rawāsiya an tamīda bikum" ("He cast into the earth firmly-set mountains lest it sway with you"). Q 78:6-7 "alam najʿali-l-arḍa mihādā · wa-l-jibāla awtādā" ("Have We not made the earth a cradle, and the mountains as pegs?"). Q 79:32 "wa-l-jibāla arsāhā" ("and the mountains He anchored them").

**Classical tafsir.** Ṭabarī glosses *awtād* as *awtād al-bayt*, tent-pegs, because they fix the earth so it does not move its inhabitants. Rāzī in Mafātīḥ al-ghayb argues mountains prevent the earth from "tilting" (*al-mayl*) and adduces this as an argument for divine design. Qurṭubī cites the hadith tradition in which the earth was unstable until God "nailed it with mountains." None of these commentators conceive of a root extending below the mountain; the image is uniformly the tent-peg driven into a surface.

**Modern apologetic reading.** Popularised by Maurice Bucaille and later by the Zakir Naik circuit, the claim is that *awtād* anticipates Airy–Heiskanen isostasy: that mountains have crustal roots 5–7× their visible height, and that these roots stabilise continental crust against lateral motion.

**The science.** Isostasy (Pratt 1855; Airy 1855) does describe buoyant crustal roots beneath mountain belts, typically 30–70 km thick under Himalaya and Andes vs. ~35 km average continental crust. But: (a) roots do not *prevent* earthquakes — orogens like the Himalaya are the *most* seismic regions on earth; (b) mountains do not stabilise plates — they are consequences of plate collision, not causes of plate stability; (c) the earth would not "sway" without them in any physical sense; (d) the tent-peg geometry is upside-down relative to isostatic roots (a peg is narrow going down; a crustal root is broad and blunt).

**Verdict: apologetic overreach.** The tent-peg metaphor is shared with Psalm 104:5, Job 9:6, and pre-Islamic Arabic poetry (e.g. al-Nābigha; Imruʾ al-Qays uses *awtād* for tent-posts). The classical tafsir tradition reads it exactly that way. The isostatic reading requires projecting a 19th-century geodetic discovery onto a metaphor whose source domain is Bedouin tent-pitching. The verse makes a claim about earth stability that is, strictly taken, false (earth does quake; plates do drift). It is better read as an argument-from-design addressed to an audience that experienced mountains as monumental fixtures — which they are, on human timescales.

## 2. The Barrier Between Two Seas (barzakh)

**Verses.** Q 25:53 "marajal-baḥrayni hādhā ʿadhbun furātun wa-hādhā milḥun ujāj, wa-jaʿala baynahumā barzakhan wa-ḥijran maḥjūrā" ("He released the two seas: this one sweet and fresh, that one salty and bitter; and He set between them a barrier and a forbidden partition"). Q 55:19-20 "marajal-baḥrayni yaltaqiyān, baynahumā barzakhun lā yabghiyān" ("…between them a barrier they do not transgress"). Q 27:61 echoes.

**Classical tafsir.** Ṭabarī, Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr understand the *baḥrayn* as the ordinary fresh-and-salt contrast — rivers and the ocean. Some commentators (including Muqātil b. Sulaymān) suggest the "barrier" is God's decree keeping rivers from salting and seas from freshening. Rāzī entertains the possibility of an eschatological reading (two cosmic seas) but settles on the naturalistic one. *Barzakh* here is explicitly theological-legal ("they do not transgress"), not oceanographic.

**Modern apologetic reading.** Jacques Cousteau (the apocryphal conversion story is uncorroborated) is invoked to claim the Qur'an anticipates the halocline — the sharp salinity gradient at estuarine mixing zones or between adjacent sea basins (Strait of Gibraltar, Bab al-Mandeb). The further claim extends to pycnoclines between Mediterranean outflow and Atlantic waters.

**The science.** Haloclines and estuarine plumes are real. Fresh river water, being less dense, rides atop salt water for tens of kilometres (e.g. Amazon, Mississippi). Mediterranean–Atlantic exchange at Gibraltar shows a well-defined density interface. None of this, however, was unknown to ancient Near-Eastern observers. The Shatt al-ʿArab, the Nile delta, the Tigris-Euphrates confluence, and the Persian Gulf coast all present the fresh-meets-salt phenomenon visibly. Pliny the Elder (Natural History 2.166) and Aristotle (Meteorologica) both discuss it.

**Verdict: banal observation, apologetically inflated.** The verse states what any sailor from Basra knew. The halocline reading requires treating "barrier" as a physical density interface; the classical reading treats it as divine decree. Neither is impressive as science. Honest rating: a poetic/theological figure drawing on common observation.

## 3. Darkness in the Deep Sea

**Verse.** Q 24:40 "aw ka-ẓulumātin fī baḥrin lujjiyyin, yaghshāhu mawjun min fawqihi mawjun min fawqihi saḥāb — ẓulumātun baʿḍuhā fawqa baʿḍ, idhā akhraja yadahu lam yakad yarāhā" ("Or like darknesses in a deep sea, covered by a wave, above which is a wave, above which are clouds — layers of darkness one above another: when he stretches out his hand he can scarcely see it").

**Classical tafsir.** The verse is a simile for the state of the disbeliever's heart (contrast Q 24:35, the Light Verse). Ṭabarī reads the three darknesses as (i) the sea's depth, (ii) the wave, (iii) the cloud overhead. Rāzī explicitly treats the image as a rhetorical intensifier for obscurity, not an oceanographic datum. No classical commentator proposes internal waves or the photic-zone/aphotic-zone distinction.

**Modern apologetic reading.** The verse is read as describing (i) attenuation of sunlight with depth (the photic/dysphotic/aphotic zones), (ii) internal waves at pycnoclines (the "wave above which a wave"), and (iii) cloud cover above — together producing progressive darkness.

**The science.** Light does attenuate exponentially with depth (Beer-Lambert); red wavelengths are absorbed within ~15 m, blue penetrates to ~200 m, the aphotic zone begins near 1000 m. Internal waves at density interfaces are real but were identified by Ekman (1904) following Nansen's Fram observations. The overlay "wave above a wave" *could* be read this way — but it equally describes, and more naturally describes, surface chop riding on swell, which any fisherman sees.

**Verdict: evocative simile, not a scientific disclosure.** Deep-sea diving experience was limited to breath-hold (~30 m maximum) in the 7th century; no one below that depth returned to report. But the verse does not need to describe deep-sea physics — it needs to evoke compounded obscurity, which it does masterfully. Reading "above which are clouds" as atmospheric optics *and* the two waves as surface + internal is a post hoc harmonisation. Best read as a storm-at-sea image. Not misleading, but not prescient either.

## 4. The Water Cycle

**Verses.** Q 30:48 (wind drives clouds, God spreads them, rain issues from their midst). Q 24:43 (clouds piled, rain from their *khilāl*/interstices, hail from mountains-in-the-sky). Q 7:57 (winds as harbingers; heavy clouds driven to dead land; water revives it). Also Q 23:18, 39:21, 15:22 (winds as *lawāqiḥ*, fertilising/pollinating).

**Classical tafsir.** Commentators read these as descriptions of the familiar meteorology: wind carries clouds, clouds drop rain, rain becomes springs and rivers. *Lawāqiḥ* ("fertilising winds") was understood either as clouds becoming pregnant with water or as winds pollinating plants — both meanings are attested in pre-Islamic lexicography.

**Modern apologetic reading.** The Qur'an anticipates the hydrological cycle as formalised by Perrault (1674) and Halley (1687) — evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff.

**The science.** The water cycle is real and was partially understood in antiquity (Anaximenes, Anaxagoras, Aristotle's Meteorologica, Vitruvius). Greek and Indian sources describe evaporation → cloud → rain. The Qur'an's account is meteorologically accurate at the observable level: winds drive clouds, clouds yield rain, rain revives land.

**Verdict: accurate but not novel.** Every agricultural and pastoralist civilisation has some version of this. The verses are striking for rhetorical concentration and for linking the cycle theologically to resurrection (*kadhālika-l-khurūj* — "thus the emergence") — not for disclosing unknown physics. Honest rating: observational theology.

## 5. Iron "Sent Down"

**Verse.** Q 57:25 "wa anzalnā-l-ḥadīda fīhi baʾsun shadīdun wa manāfiʿu lil-nās" ("We sent down iron; in it is great might and benefits for mankind").

**Classical tafsir.** Ṭabarī records two views: (1) *anzalnā* means "we sent down from heaven" in a literal sense — an allusion to Adam bringing iron from paradise, or to iron falling as meteorite (some narrations mention the Black Stone-like origin); (2) *anzalnā* is idiomatic for "we bestowed / brought into existence" — the same verb is used in Q 39:6 for cattle (*anzala lakum min al-anʿām thamāniyata azwāj*, "sent down to you of livestock eight pairs") and Q 7:26 for clothing. The second reading is the majority position.

**Modern apologetic reading.** All iron in the universe originates in stellar nucleosynthesis — specifically the silicon-burning stage and Type II supernovae (actually more recently attributed in large part to neutron-star mergers for the heavy r-process elements, but iron peaks are produced in core-collapse). Therefore iron on earth is truly "sent down" from space.

**The science.** The statement about stellar origins is correct. All elements heavier than helium (and in bulk, heavier than lithium) were forged in stars. Terrestrial iron came to earth via accretion from the protoplanetary disk, which was seeded by earlier-generation supernovae. The iron in human hemoglobin does, in the most literal sense, have a stellar origin. Meteoritic iron, specifically, was the *only* iron available to pre-smelting cultures (Tutankhamun's dagger, Inuit Cape York iron), so there is a narrower pre-Islamic sense in which iron was literally "sent down."

**Verdict: classical apologetic overreach; narrower claim defensible.** The cosmogonic reading (supernova nucleosynthesis) is not recoverable from the verse on any principled philological reading — because *anzalnā* is used for livestock and garments, which were not "sent down" astronomically. The parallelism in the Qur'an itself defeats the cosmological reading: God "sends down" whatever He bestows. However, the meteoritic-iron reading is historically plausible and consistent with pre-Islamic Arabic usage. The verse, at best, gestures at iron's remarkable provenance. The "supernova miracle" is an artefact of selective translation.

## 6. Mountains Stabilising the Earth

**Verses.** Q 16:15, 21:31, 31:10, 77:27, 79:32 — all variants on *rawāsiya an tamīda bikum* ("firm mountains lest it sway with you").

**Classical tafsir.** Uniformly: God placed mountains so the earth would not move under its inhabitants. The reason given (Rāzī, Qurṭubī) is the earth's natural tendency to spin or tilt under forces applied to it; mountains act as ballast. This was coherent within geocentric Ptolemaic cosmology, where earth's stability was a matter of balance around a centre.

**Modern apologetic reading.** Two variants: (i) isostasy (as §1); (ii) that mountains slow plate motion or damp tectonic oscillation.

**The science.** Plate tectonics (Wegener 1912; Hess/Dietz 1960s) teaches the opposite: mountains *arise from* plate collisions. Plates move relative to each other at ~1-10 cm/year regardless of mountain presence; mountains are the wake of motion, not brakes on it. The earth's actual rotational stability is due to its oblate-spheroid shape and angular momentum, not mountain distribution. The moon's tidal torque is the dominant rotational perturbation. Earthquakes occur *because* of the tectonics that builds mountains.

**Verdict: scientifically incorrect on the strict reading; rescuable only as metaphor.** If one insists the verse is making a geophysical claim, it is wrong — mountains do not prevent earth from swaying. If one reads it poetically (mountains as monumental, unshakeable features on human timescales, and as symbols of stability), it is apt. The Qur'an's own usage elsewhere (mountains that "pass like clouds," Q 27:88; mountains "crushed to powder" at resurrection, Q 56:5) suggests the Qur'an itself treats mountain-permanence as contingent, not essential. Apologists typically pick isostasy; the verse, on its most obvious reading, claims what tectonics denies.

## 7. Milk "From Between Feces and Blood"

**Verse.** Q 16:66 "wa inna lakum fī-l-anʿāmi la-ʿibrah, nusqīkum mimmā fī buṭūnihi min bayni farthin wa-damin labanan khāliṣan sāʾighan lil-shāribīn" ("In livestock is a sign for you: We give you drink from what is in their bellies, from between chyme and blood, pure milk, palatable to drinkers").

**Classical tafsir.** Ṭabarī, Qurṭubī: the verse describes the digestive process — food becomes *farth* (chyme/partly-digested fodder) in the rumen, nutrients pass into blood, and from the blood milk is synthesised in the udder. Ibn Kathīr notes the Arabs recognised that blood and milk share a common source because lactating animals' blood volume is altered. Classical commentators do not claim anatomical novelty; they treat this as known.

**Modern apologetic reading.** The verse anticipates the physiology of lactation: nutrients from digested food cross the gut wall into portal circulation, reach the mammary gland, and are extracted by lactocytes to synthesise milk fats, lactose, and caseins.

**The science.** Modern physiology confirms that milk components derive (a) from blood-borne precursors (glucose → lactose; amino acids → caseins; free fatty acids → triglycerides) and (b) from de novo synthesis in the mammary epithelium. The gut-blood-udder pathway is real.

**Verdict: remarkable observation, not prescient anatomy.** Herders dissected animals routinely. The juxtaposition of rumen contents, mesenteric vasculature, and the udder would be observable in any slaughtered ruminant. The phrase "from between chyme and blood" is a rhetorical anatomical compression, not a declaration of the lactose-synthesis pathway. But it is strikingly accurate in structure — the milk does come from between the gut contents and the blood. Honest rating: plausibly the best of the "anatomical" verses for suggestiveness, but explicable by herder observation without special revelation.

## 8. Bees, Revelation, and Honey

**Verses.** Q 16:68-69 "wa awḥā rabbuka ilā-l-naḥli ani-ttakhidhī min al-jibāli buyūtan wa min al-shajari wa mimmā yaʿrishūn · thumma kulī min kulli-l-thamarāt fa-sluki subula rabbiki dhululan, yakhruju min buṭūnihā sharābun mukhtalifun alwānuhu fīhi shifāʾun lil-nās" ("Your Lord inspired the bee: take houses in mountains, trees, and what they build; then eat of every fruit and follow the paths of your Lord made easy; from their bellies emerges a drink of varying colours in which is healing for people").

**Classical tafsir.** *Waḥy* ("inspiration") to the bee is understood as instinct (*ilhām gharīzī*) — not the prophetic *waḥy*. The feminine imperative ("*ittakhidhī … kulī … sluki*") is noted as significant: the worker bees are all female. Classical commentators (Qurṭubī especially) cite this as evidence of Qur'anic precision — though they did not know modern entomology. *Shifāʾ* ("healing") has sustained a whole genre of prophetic medicine literature.

**Modern apologetic reading.** Two claims: (i) the feminine imperatives anticipate that workers and foragers are female (drones don't forage); (ii) "from their bellies" matches the honey-stomach (*ingluvies*) biology; (iii) honey has genuine antimicrobial properties.

**The science.** Honeybee colonies are female-biased: the queen and all workers are female; drones (males) are seasonal and do not forage or produce honey. Bees forage nectar, store in the crop, regurgitate, process enzymatically (invertase, glucose oxidase), and deposit in wax combs. Honey is antibacterial (osmotic pressure, hydrogen peroxide from glucose oxidase, low pH) — this has medical literature behind it (manuka honey and wound care).

**Verdict: some real strength, some apologetic inflation.** The feminine-imperative observation is genuine; Arabic grammatical gender tracks the biology here. However: (a) beekeeping was ancient (Egyptian apiculture from the Old Kingdom, extensively documented); (b) that workers are female was probably not known in 7th-century Arabia in those terms, but the feminine verb could simply reflect *naḥl* as a feminine collective (*ism jins*) defaulting to feminine agreement — which weakens the "anatomical precision" argument. Honey's medicinal properties were universally recognised in antiquity (Hippocrates, Dioscorides, ancient Egyptian medical papyri). Honest rating: aesthetically elegant verse; the strongest apologetic point (feminine imperatives) is grammatically ambiguous.

## 9. Mary's Date Palm

**Verses.** Q 19:23-25 "…wa-huzzī ilayki bi-jidhʿi-l-nakhlati tusāqiṭ ʿalayki ruṭaban janiyyā" ("Shake toward you the trunk of the date palm; it will drop on you ripe fresh dates").

**Classical tafsir.** The miracle is the fresh *ruṭab* appearing for Mary in travail. Commentators focus on divine provision; the botanical detail (a dry trunk fruiting) is itself the miracle. Some commentators (Qurṭubī) note that *ruṭab* is particularly appropriate for parturient women — echoing ancient Near-Eastern folk dietetics.

**Modern apologetic reading.** *Ruṭab* is nutritionally ideal post-partum (easy sugars, minerals, fibre), and oxytocin-like effects of dates are claimed in some contemporary obstetric literature.

**The science.** There is *some* peer-reviewed literature (Al-Kuran et al. 2011, Razali et al. 2017) suggesting date consumption in late pregnancy correlates with reduced need for labour induction and augmentation. Mechanisms proposed: saturated fatty acids as prostaglandin precursors; natural sugars; fibre. The evidence base is modest (small RCTs), and the effect sizes are marginal.

**Verdict: interesting folk wisdom, tentative modern support.** The verse does not claim medical efficacy; the miracle is provision. The apologetic reading adds a layer the verse does not assert. The nutritional claim is plausible but not established. Honest rating: cultural-medical intuition rather than a scientific disclosure.

## 10. The Ant Verse

**Verse.** Q 27:18 "ḥattā idhā atū ʿalā wādi-l-namli, qālat namlatun: yā ayyuhā-l-namlu-dkhulū masākinakum, lā yaḥṭimannakum Sulaymānu wa junūduhu wa hum lā yashʿurūn" ("…until when they came upon the Valley of the Ants, one ant said: 'O ants, enter your dwellings lest Sulaymān and his hosts crush you unawares'").

**Classical tafsir.** This is part of the Sulaymān (Solomon) narrative, featuring prophetic command of speech with animals. The *namla* speaking is treated as miraculous — part of Solomon's gift (Q 27:16, *ʿullimnā manṭiqa-l-ṭayr*). Commentators do not propose it as a biology claim.

**Modern apologetic reading.** The verse anticipates ant social communication (pheromone trails, alarm signals via formic acid and other cuticular compounds — first characterised mid-20th century by E. O. Wilson and colleagues).

**The science.** Ants communicate via pheromones (alarm, trail, recruitment, identification). They have sophisticated caste organisation and collective behaviour. The verse, however, says nothing about chemical signalling: it attributes *articulate speech* to an ant, within a miracle narrative.

**Verdict: category error.** The verse is narratively miraculous, not entomological. Reading it as prefiguring pheromone communication confuses speech with chemical signalling and strips the miracle of its theological role. If ants "really" have language, the point of Solomon's gift dissolves. Honest rating: apologetic overreach; the verse is not trying to teach biology.

## 11. The Spider's "Weakest House"

**Verse.** Q 29:41 "mathalu-lladhīna-ttakhadhū min dūni-llāhi awliyāʾa ka-mathali-l-ʿankabūti-ttakhadhat baytan, wa inna awhana-l-buyūti la-baytu-l-ʿankabūt, law kānū yaʿlamūn" ("Those who take protectors besides God are like the spider that takes a house; verily the weakest of houses is the spider's house — if they only knew").

**Classical tafsir.** A classical *mathal* (parable): reliance on any other than God is as fragile as a spider's web. Commentators (Ṭabarī, Rāzī) do not dispute the web's literal fragility; they treat it as a visual truism.

**Modern apologetic reading.** Two lines: (i) spider silk has tensile strength comparable to steel per unit cross-section — so the web is not *materially* weak, and the verse must be referring to the *social* fragility of the spider's household (cannibalism, prey-trap impermanence, no emotional bonds); (ii) alternatively, the "weakness" is in the web's incompleteness as a shelter (no walls, no roof in the ordinary sense).

**The science.** Spider dragline silk (major ampullate) has tensile strength ~1-1.5 GPa, toughness exceeding Kevlar. However, a web as a *house* — considered as a shelter from weather, predators, wind — is trivially inadequate; it is a prey-capture device, not a refuge. Additionally, spider social life in most species is antagonistic: female cannibalism of males, juvenile cannibalism, the "widow" motif.

**Verdict: parable works; apologetic rescue unneeded.** The verse does not make a material-science claim, so the Kevlar comparison does not falsify it. The phrase "weakest of houses" is straightforwardly true *as a dwelling*: a web offers no protection. The "social fragility" reading, while cute, is speculative over-interpretation. Honest rating: a successful parable; apologetic attempts to extract biology from it are unnecessary and often weaken the verse's rhetorical force.

## 12. Every Living Thing From Water

**Verses.** Q 21:30 "awa lam yara-lladhīna kafarū anna-l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa kānatā ratqan fa-fataqnāhumā, wa jaʿalnā mina-l-māʾi kulla shayʾin ḥayy" ("Have those who disbelieved not seen that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, then We separated them, and from water We made every living thing?"). Q 11:7 "wa kāna ʿarshuhu ʿalā-l-māʾ" ("His throne was upon the water"). Q 24:45 "wa Allāhu khalaqa kulla dābbatin min māʾ" ("God created every creature from water").

**Classical tafsir.** The *ratq/fatq* ("joined, then separated") is read variably: (a) the heavens were one, then layered; (b) the heavens and earth were joined, then split; (c) the heavens were closed (no rain) then opened, and earth was closed (no vegetation) then opened. Ṭabarī prefers (c), Rāzī favours (a)/(b). "Every living thing from water" is understood either as literal (water in all flesh) or as water being essential to life (no creature survives without drink).

**Modern apologetic reading.** (i) The *ratq-fatq* prefigures Big Bang cosmology or primordial nebular separation; (ii) "from water" prefigures the aquatic origin of life (abiogenesis in archaic oceans, ~3.5-4 Ga), or simply the biochemical centrality of water.

**The science.** Life's aqueous origin is scientifically robust: the first self-replicating chemistry evolved in water; extant cells are 60-90% water by mass; water is the universal solvent for biochemistry. Cosmology: the universe expanded from a hot, dense state — not exactly a "joined" heaven-and-earth, but the idea of a separation has a loose analogy. However, the *ratq-fatq* motif is widespread in ancient Near-Eastern cosmogony: Enuma Elish (Tiamat split by Marduk), Hesiod's Theogony (Ouranos and Gaia separated), Genesis 1:6-8 (waters above and below divided by firmament), Rig Veda 10.129. The primordial-water motif is likewise common: Egyptian Nun, Mesopotamian Apsu, Genesis 1:2 ("Spirit of God hovered over the waters").

**Verdict: shared mythological topos, selectively accurate.** "Every living thing from water" is a striking line and maps onto real biochemistry. But the motif is inherited from the wider ancient Near Eastern cosmogonic vocabulary, not invented by the Qur'an, and the classical tafsir tradition does not read it as abiogenesis. The cosmology reading of *ratq-fatq* as Big Bang is anachronistic and not supported by classical exegesis, which knows nothing of cosmic expansion. Honest rating: theologically rich, culturally embedded in ancient mythic vocabulary, partially suggestive of modern findings but not uniquely so.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

**1. The apologetic method typically requires three moves simultaneously:** (a) strip the verse from its classical exegetical reception; (b) calibrate the translation to a target modern finding; (c) suppress parallel occurrences of the same vocabulary that would undercut the reading (as with *anzalnā* for iron vs. livestock). When any one of these moves is resisted, the "miracle" thins.

**2. Distinguish three strength-tiers:**
- **Tier A — Accurate but not prescient.** Water cycle, milk physiology, bees, deep-sea darkness. These describe real phenomena that any attentive pre-modern observer could note.
- **Tier B — Metaphorical, mishandled as science.** Mountains-as-pegs, mountain stabilisation, spider web, ant verse. Reading metaphors as physics produces either overreach or falsifiable claims.
- **Tier C — Embedded in prior mythological vocabulary.** Primordial water, *ratq-fatq*. The Qur'an participates in a shared Near-Eastern cosmological idiom.

**3. The strongest honest claim** the text can sustain is not *prescient science* but *observational theology with unusual rhetorical concentration*. The water cycle verses, the livestock-to-milk verse, and the cloud-layering verse are genuinely elegant summaries of what an attentive desert dweller could know. The *i'jāz 'ilmī* literature weakens this honest strength by overreaching.

**4. On the project's honesty commitment.** The claim "the Qur'an anticipated 20th-century science" is, for the twelve verses audited here, not sustainable without selective reading. The claim "the Qur'an contains striking naturalistic observations integrated into theology" is sustainable. These are different claims; the project should assert the second and decline the first.

**5. Suggested further audits:** embryology (Q 23:12-14, the *nuṭfa-ʿalaqa-muḍgha* sequence — a separate and larger dossier deserving its own run); astronomy (celestial "orbits," Q 36:40, 21:33); the *sabʿa samāwāt* cosmology; and the meteorological verses on hail and "mountains in the sky" (Q 24:43).
