# Innamā (إنما) — The Restrictive-Emphatic Formula

**Phase B hypothesis map.** Run-1. Date: 2026-04-12.
**Data:** Quranic Arabic Corpus morphology v0.4 (Dukes); Tanzil Uthmani text.
**Method:** In the Dukes morphology, **innamā** is segmented into two tokens: the emphatic accusative particle `<in~a` (POS=ACC) immediately followed by the "preventive" mā, `maA` (POS=PREV, ḥarf kāff). Classical grammarians (Sībawayh, Ibn Hishām, Zamakhsharī) analyze the compound identically: inna's accusative-governing force is "prevented" (*kuffat*) by the appended *mā*, and the particle is repurposed as a two-word restrictive cliticized unit — *ḥarf al-ḥaṣr* ("particle of restriction"). This segmentation lets us count every innamā mechanically.

---

## 1. Corpus count

- **Total innamā tokens: 143** (Dukes segmentation: ACC `<in~a` + PREV `maA` adjacent).
- **Unique verses containing at least one innamā: 134.**
- **Verses containing two innamā's: 9** (see §9 below).
- Related constructions in the ACC+PREV family:
  - **annamā** (أنما, the subordinated "that-only"): 11 occurrences.
  - **ka-annamā** (كأنما, "as if it were only," a counter-factual simile particle): 6 occurrences.
  - Total ACC+PREV compounds: **160**.
- Reference benchmark: the *other* restrictive device, the paired *mā...illā* (excepting with `إلّا`/`<il~aA`, POS=RES), occurs **558** times. Innamā is roughly **one-quarter** as frequent as mā-illā, suggesting they operate in partial complementary distribution rather than perfect synonymy.

The scholar's estimate of "~150" is accurate: 143 is the morphologically-segmented figure for innamā proper, with 160 if we pool annamā and ka-annamā.

## 2. Meccan vs Medinan density

| Period | Innamā tokens | Verses in period | Rate per 1000 verses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meccan | 86 | 4,613 | **18.6** |
| Medinan | 57 | 1,623 | **35.1** |

Medinan density is **1.88× Meccan density**. This is a striking asymmetry. The Meccan corpus favors open rhetorical questions, oaths, and cosmic imagery; the Medinan corpus — which must legislate, define membership, and adjudicate — reaches for innamā as a precision instrument. The Medinan preference aligns with innamā's grammatical function: *ḥaṣr* narrows a category ("X is only Y, not Z"), and legal/communal definition requires exactly that narrowing.

## 4. The "innamā al-muʾminūn" community-defining formula

The scholar identified five verses; morphology confirms that **only three of the five** actually open with the innamā al-muʾminūn formula, while two (9:71 and 23:1) praise believers using *different* constructions:

| Cited | Opening | Has innamā? |
|---|---|---|
| **8:2** | *innamā al-muʾminūna alladhīna idhā dhukira llāhu wajilat qulūbuhum* | **YES** — defines believers by heart-trembling at God's mention |
| 9:71 | *wa-l-muʾminūna wa-l-muʾminātu baʿḍuhum awliyāʾu baʿḍ* | **NO** — simple conjunction; no restriction particle |
| 23:1 | *qad aflaḥa l-muʾminūn* | **NO** — emphatic particle *qad* + perfect verb, different rhetoric |
| **49:10** | *innamā al-muʾminūna ikhwatun fa-aṣliḥū bayna akhawaykum* | **YES** — defines believers as brothers, command to reconcile |
| **49:15** | *innamā al-muʾminūna alladhīna āmanū bi-llāhi wa-rasūlihi thumma lam yartābū* | **YES** — defines believers by firm faith + striving |

A fourth innamā al-muʾminūn exists that the scholar did not cite: **24:62** — *innamā al-muʾminūna alladhīna āmanū bi-llāhi wa-rasūlihi wa-idhā kānū maʿahu ʿalā amrin jāmiʿ lam yadhhabū ḥattā yastaʾdhinūh* ("only those are true believers who do not depart without the Prophet's leave"). This is a community-discipline definition (Medinan, procedural). So the innamā al-muʾminūn formula has **four canonical instances: 8:2, 24:62, 49:10, 49:15** — all Medinan, all defining the believing community by successively higher thresholds (inner trembling → procedural respect → fraternal ethics → unwavering jihād). One might read the quartet as a graded catechism of qualifying attributes.

## 5. Innamā amruhu — the divine creative command (kun fa-yakūn)

Of the **8 Qurʾānic occurrences** of the "kun fa-yakūn" creative command (2:117, 3:47, 3:59, 6:73, 16:40, 19:35, 36:82, 40:68), **6 are introduced by innamā** or its conjunctive form fa-innamā (confirmed by morphological scan: 6 innamā verses contain `kun fa-yakūn` as substring):

| Verse | Clause |
|---|---|
| 2:117 | *fa-innamā yaqūlu lahu kun fa-yakūn* |
| 3:47 | *fa-innamā yaqūlu lahu kun fa-yakūn* |
| 16:40 | *innamā qawlunā li-shayʾin idhā aradnāhu an naqūla lahu kun fa-yakūn* |
| 19:35 | *fa-innamā yaqūlu lahu kun fa-yakūn* |
| **36:82** | *innamā amruhu idhā arāda shayʾan an yaqūla lahu kun fa-yakūn* |
| 40:68 | *fa-innamā yaqūlu lahu kun fa-yakūn* |

The two that lack innamā (3:59, 6:73) are contextual — 3:59 frames it as narrative analogy ("thumma qāla lahu kun fa-yakūn"), 6:73 as a trailing apposition ("wa-yawma yaqūlu kun fa-yakūn"). Where the formula stands as an *independent theological assertion* about the mode of God's creative will, innamā appears. **Q 36:82 is the most emphatic version** — it is the *only* instance that uses "amruhu" (His command-matter) rather than qawluhu/yaqūl, and the only one with the dyad *innamā + idhā arāda* (restriction + volition). The syntactic packing is maximal: restriction of mode, temporal subordination of volition, subjunctive purpose clause, imperative, and habitual indicative — five clause types fused into one creative utterance. This matches Yāsīn's rhetorical crescendo (the surah closes immediately after).

## 6. Innamā yakhshā llāha min ʿibādihi al-ʿulamāʾ (Q 35:28)

Only two innamā verses contain the verb *khashya* (fearful reverence): 35:28 and 79:45 (*innamā anta mundhiru man yakhshāhā* — "you are only a warner to whoever fears Her [the Hour]"). In 79:45 the object of fear is the Hour, a feminine pronoun. **Q 35:28 is therefore the sole innamā verse in the Qurʾān where the object of khashya is Allāh himself.** The verse's tafsīr-significance is amplified by its structural isolation: 134 innamā verses, one with yakhshā. The restriction is doubly sharp: (a) restriction of the subject of yakhshā to al-ʿulamāʾ ("only the knowledgeable [truly] fear Allāh from among His servants"), and (b) a rarified verbal choice — *khashya* denotes reverential fear with knowledge (contrast with *khawf*, mere fright). The classical commentators (Rāzī ad loc., Ibn Kathīr) specifically note the syntactic trick of fronting the object *Allāh* before the subject *al-ʿulamāʾ*; innamā amplifies the effect by making the restriction explicit rather than merely implicational. The verse is embedded in a chromatic-biological passage (28a: varied colors of mountains, fruits, humans, animals) — the diversity of creation is the epistemic ground for learned fear.

## 7. Innamā yurīdu — divine will verses

Four innamā-verses use the verb *yurīd* (to will). They form a stark contrastive pairing:

| Verse | Subject | Object of will |
|---|---|---|
| **5:91** | *al-shayṭān* | sowing enmity through wine & gambling |
| **9:55** | *Allāh* | punishing hypocrites through their wealth/children in this life |
| **9:85** | *Allāh* | punishing them + taking their souls as unbelievers |
| **33:33** | *Allāh* | *li-yudhhiba ʿankum al-rijsa ahla l-bayt wa-yuṭahhirakum taṭhīrā* — removing defilement from the House-People and purifying them |

The inversion is theologically pointed: three of four divine-will innamā verses are negative (Shayṭān's corrupting will, God's chastising will against hypocrites), and **only 33:33 is positively purgative**. The very rarity of positive-purification innamā-yurīd sharpens its semantic weight. The verse's disputed referent (the "People of the House": prophetic wives in Sunnī reading [context of v. 28–34 addresses azwāj al-nabī], vs. the five-of-the-cloak — Fāṭima, ʿAlī, Ḥasan, Ḥusayn + Prophet — in Shīʿī reading) turns on (i) the shift from feminine 2FP plural in the surrounding verses to masculine 2MP plural at *ʿankum* and *yuṭahhirakum*, and (ii) whether innamā here restricts the subject (Allāh and no other), the object (ahla l-bayt and no others), or the predicate (taṭhīr and nothing less). Classical ḥaṣr can operate on any of these constituents; context-dependent resolution is genuinely underdetermined by the particle alone — which is why the verse became a tafsīr-battleground. A morphological finding cannot adjudicate the dispute, but it does confirm how much restrictive weight rests on a single particle.

## 8. Innamā mathalu / parable formula

Only **one** innamā verse opens with the *mathal*-parable formula:
- **10:24** — *innamā mathalu l-ḥayāti l-dunyā ka-māʾin anzalnāhu min al-samāʾ...* ("the parable of worldly life is only like water We sent down from heaven...").

The paucity is itself interesting: parables are one of the Qurʾān's most frequent rhetorical devices, yet innamā rarely introduces them. The cognate "ka-annamā" ("as if only") appears **6 times** and these *do* function parabolically:

| Verse | Simile |
|---|---|
| 5:32 | whoever kills one soul, *ka-annamā* killed all humanity; whoever saves, *ka-annamā* saved all |
| 6:125 | the constricted chest of the misguided, *ka-annamā* he climbs into the sky |
| 8:6 | they dispute, *ka-annamā* they are driven to death with eyes open |
| 10:27 | their faces covered *ka-annamā* with pieces of dark night |
| 22:31 | whoever associates, *ka-annamā* fell from the sky and was snatched by birds |

So the *restrictive* innamā rarely opens similes; the *counter-factual* ka-annamā does. Grammar encodes the distinction: innamā asserts reality-with-restriction, ka-annamā asserts appearance-like-restriction.

## 9. Double-innamā verses — parallelism as rhetoric

Nine verses contain **two innamā** each, almost always in structural parallelism:

| Verse | Structure |
|---|---|
| 4:171 | *innamā l-masīḥu ʿīsā...* / *innamā llāhu ilāhun wāḥid* — Christology bracketed by two restrictions |
| 7:187 | *innamā ʿilmuhā ʿinda rabbī...* (eschatological knowledge) / *innamā ʿilmuhā ʿinda llāh* |
| 10:108 | *fa-innamā yahtadī li-nafsihi* / *fa-innamā yaḍillu ʿalayhā* — guidance/misguidance chiasm |
| 17:15 | same chiastic pair as 10:108 |
| 27:92 | same chiastic pair + *innamā anā min al-mundharīn* |
| 29:50 | *innamā l-āyātu ʿinda llāh* / *innamā anā nadhīrun mubīn* — signs/messenger split |
| 35:18 | *innamā tundhiru lladhīna yakhshawna...* / ... |
| 48:10 | *innamā yubāyiʿūna llāh* / *fa-innamā yankuthu ʿalā nafsih* — bayʿa + naksh |
| 67:26 | *innamā l-ʿilmu ʿinda llāh* / *innamā anā nadhīrun mubīn* |

The most common double-innamā is the **guidance/misguidance chiasm** (10:108, 17:15, 27:92: *fa-man ihtadā fa-innamā yahtadī li-nafsih, wa-man ḍalla fa-innamā yaḍillu ʿalayhā*) — restrictive particle used in both arms of the antithesis to ration moral causality to its agent alone. Parallel double-innamā is a **deliberate rhetorical marker of theological antithesis**.

Notably, 18:110 and 41:6 pair innamā with the embedded annamā ("qul innamā anā basharun mithlukum yūḥā ilayya **annamā** ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥid") — the outer innamā restricts the messenger's nature (I am only human), the inner annamā restricts the content of revelation (only: God is one). Two levels of restriction nested as a single credo.

The heavy concentration in **messenger-role self-identification** (8 verses, all beginning "qul innamā anā..." or "innamā anā...") reveals innamā as the grammatical signature of *prophetic humility*: each time the Prophet is instructed to deny extra-human claims, the restrictor innamā polices the boundary. These are overwhelmingly Meccan/late-Meccan — the period when opponents demanded miracles.

The **kun fa-yakūn** cluster (6 verses) and the **messenger-role** cluster (8 verses) together account for 10% of all innamā occurrences. Both are doctrinal definitions of asymmetry: God alone commands "be," the Prophet is only a warner. Innamā polices the ontological gap between the two.

## 11. Classical grammatical distinction: innamā vs. mā…illā

Both structures mean "only X" but differ in:

| Feature | innamā (إنما) | mā ... illā (ما ... إلّا) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | single fused particle + predicate | negation + exceptive |
| Semantic operation | positive restriction ("it is only X") | exceptive restriction ("nothing but X") |
| Emphatic force | inherits *inna*'s emphasis; the *mā* kāffah *prevents* case-assignment but retains emphatic overtone | emphasis derives from negation polarity |
| Typical use | theological assertion, community definition, restriction of mode | contrastive correction, limiting a presupposed alternative |
| Frequency | 143 | 558 tokens of RES (≈ exceptive illā; *mā...illā* is a subset) |

Zamakhsharī (*al-Kashshāf* on 2:11): innamā is used when the addressee *already believes X is Y* and is being restricted *against* admitting Z; mā...illā is used when alternatives are *presumed possible* and being denied. This pragmatic distinction predicts the data: innamā dominates theological-restrictive contexts (God's unity, prophetic role, believer-identity), where doctrinal *boundary-policing* matters; mā...illā dominates argumentative contexts where one alternative is being excluded in favor of another (see e.g. 2:9 *mā yakhdaʿūna illā anfusahum*).

The ACC segmentation of innamā in the Dukes corpus preserves the grammatical intuition: innamā retains inna's emphatic nominal-governing imprint even though the *kāffah* mā has neutralized case assignment. It is, morphologically, an emphatic particle that *was* a governor and has been grammaticalized into a restrictor — a rare trajectory and distinctively Arabic.

## 12. Hypotheses for further work

1. **H1 — Medinan legal drift.** The 1.88× Medinan density ratio predicts that innamā is statistically over-represented in Medinan legal pericopes (dietary, marital, judicial). Test: segment Medinan verses by legal vs narrative content and check rates.
2. **H2 — Messenger-humility cluster.** "Qul innamā anā..." formulaic phrases (11 instances) should cluster chronologically in Meccan middle-late period when prophetic claims were contested. Test against Nöldeke chronology.
3. **H3 — Innamā + khashya pair at 35:28 is a hapax pairing.** Confirmed in §6; worth cross-referencing with root frequencies in Rāzī/Ṭabarī commentary to see if classical exegetes note the isolation.
4. **H4 — Purification-innamā (33:33) is semantically isolated.** Among 143 innamā verses, only 33:33 combines *innamā + yurīd Allāh + positive purgation (li-yudhhib + yuṭahhir)*. This explains why the verse became structurally load-bearing in Shīʿī theology — there is no other verse with this exact particle-verb-purpose triad.
5. **H5 — Double-innamā as chiastic marker.** The 9 double-innamā verses concentrate in a narrow function: antithetical moral parallelism (10:108, 17:15, 27:92, 48:10). Not a stylistic tic but a semantic signal that the verse contains a deliberate pair-structure. Test: do these verses also rank high in chiasm-detection runs?
6. **H6 — Innamā as boundary-marker for an asymmetric ontology.** The distribution across kun-fa-yakūn (divine creativity), messenger-role (prophetic limitation), divine unity, and believer-definition forms a system: innamā polices exactly the asymmetries between God / prophet / community / outsider. The absence of innamā in ḥadīth qudsī style first-person divine utterances (where such restriction would be redundant) would be worth checking.

## Appendix A — Method notes and reproducibility

- Extraction: `awk` pass over `data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt` identifying adjacent (same word-index) pairs of POS=ACC (form `<in~a`) followed immediately by POS=PREV (form `maA` or `maA^`). 143 hits.
- Cross-checked against Tanzil text (`quran-text/quran-min-tashkeel.json`) for all target verses.
- Surah typology (Meccan/Medinan) as carried in the Tanzil JSON `type` field.
- Three ACC forms precede PREV *mā* in the corpus: `<in~a` (143 innamā), `>an~a` (11 annamā), `ka>an~a` (6 ka-annamā). Plus two outliers with preceding `N` or `P` tag — `rubbamā` (15:2, PREV preceded by noun *rubba*) and `mimmā` (71:25, PREV preceded by preposition *min*) — not innamā-family.
- 558 RES (restrictive illā) tokens provide the comparative baseline.
