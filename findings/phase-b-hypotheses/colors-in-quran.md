# Colors in the Quran — Inventory and Theological Semantics

**Phase:** B (hypothesis)

**Date:** 2026-04-12

## 0. Scope and method

The Quranic palette is narrow. Six chromatic roots carry the weight: byḍ (white), swd (black), xḍr (green), ḥmr (red), ṣfr (yellow), zrq (blue). One meta-root lwn ("color" itself) appears 9 times and is almost always deployed as a sign-of-God formula ("varying colors"). Six colors total — but their distribution is so skewed that a single verse, Q 35:27, carries three of them simultaneously, and only one verse in the entire Quran uses the word "blue."

All extractions are from Leeds QAC v0.4 morphology, filtered by ROOT field and disambiguated by lemma (ḥmr = red-adjective *ḥumr* vs. homographic *ḥimār* "donkey"; swd = *aswad* "black" vs. *sayyid* "chief/master"). Counts are of surface morphology tokens, not verse hits; when two color tokens sit in one verse, each is counted.

## 1. Per-color inventory

### 1.1 White — byḍ (root بيض)

12 morphology tokens across 10 distinct verses. Senses split:

- **Moses' white hand (5×):** Q 7:108, 20:22, 26:33, 27:12, 28:32 — always *bayḍāʾ* ("shining white," cognate to egg), always paired with "no blemish" (*min ghayri sūʾin*) at Q 20:22, 27:12, 28:32. The hand is a *sign* (āya), the second of Moses' two proofs to Pharaoh; it is chromatically the only body-part light-transformation attributed to any prophet.
- **Jacob's eyes (1×):** Q 12:84 — *ibyaḍḍat ʿaynāhu min al-ḥuzn*, "his eyes whitened from grief." Cataract-from-weeping. Same root, but the whitening here is pathology, not glory. This is the ONLY negatively-coded byḍ token in the Quran.
- **Faces of the blessed (1 verse, 2 tokens):** Q 3:106-107 — *tabyaḍḍu wujūh / ibyaḍḍat wujūhuhum*. These are the Judgment-day face-lightenings and are treated in §3 below.
- **Mountains (1×):** Q 35:27 — *judadun bīḍ*, "streaks white" — treated in §5.
- **Fasting-thread (1×):** Q 2:187 — *al-khayṭ al-abyaḍ* ("the white thread"), the Ramadan dawn-boundary. Paired immediately with *al-khayṭ al-aswad* ("the black thread") in the same clause. This is the only verse in the Quran where white and black appear side-by-side as a lexical pair describing a single object-pair. The threads are later glossed (by the Prophet, recorded in Bukhārī) as night-vs-dawn.
- **Houris / preserved eggs (1 verse, 2 tokens):** Q 37:46 (drink "white" and delicious to drinkers) and Q 37:49 (houris like *bayḍ maknūn*, "hidden pearls/eggs").

Net theological-color usage of byḍ: ~10/12 tokens carry positive valence (prophetic sign, paradise, mercy). Only the Jacob-eyes instance is negative, and even there it marks intense love-grief rather than punishment.

### 1.2 Black — swd (root سود)

10 morphology tokens, but the root is polysemous: *sayyid/sādat* (chief, master) shares it. Filtering by lemma for chromatic senses only:

- **Aswad** (the adjective "black") and its derivatives — 7 chromatic tokens:
  - Q 2:187 — *al-khayṭ al-aswad* (black thread, dawn contrast — see §1.1).
  - Q 3:106 (×2) — *taswaddu wujūh / iswaddat wujūhuhum*, Judgment darkening (§3).
  - Q 16:58 and 43:17 — *ẓalla wajhuhu muswaddan*, "his face turns dark" — BOTH verses describe the pagan father hearing of the birth of a daughter (16:58) or of the daughter-attribution to Allah (43:17). Same idiom, same gesture, two surahs apart. The darkening here is *shame* at female offspring; the Quran weaponizes the color vocabulary of Judgment to indict the misogynistic gesture. A daughter turns the father's face hell-black.
  - Q 35:27 — *gharābību sūd*, "extremely black" (mountains, §5).
  - Q 39:60 — *wujūhuhum muswaddah*, "their faces blackened," about those who lied against God on the Day of Resurrection.

- Non-color swd senses (chief/master): Q 3:39 (Yaḥyā as *sayyid*), Q 12:25 (Yūsuf vs. her *sayyid*, "her master"), Q 33:67 (*sādatanā*, "our chiefs" — the hypocrites' excuse).

Net: every chromatic use of swd is negative (dawn-boundary at 2:187, which is neutral-descriptive, is the single exception). Black = grief of the unworthy, face of denial, shame of the daughter-rejector, mountain-streak of terror.

### 1.3 Green — xḍr (root خضر)

8 tokens, all chromatically green. Three functional zones:

- **Paradise clothing/cushions (4×):** Q 18:31 *thiyāban khuḍran*, Q 76:21 *thiyābu sundusin khuḍr*, Q 55:76 *rafrafin khuḍr*, and the numerically-unique Q 55:64 *mudhāmmatān* ("two, dark-green") — all describe the garments or furnishings of paradise. 4/8 green tokens are paradise-coded.
- **Vegetation/growth miracle (3×):** Q 6:99 (green shoot from water), Q 12:43/46 (Pharaoh's dream: seven *sunbulātin khuḍr* — green ears of corn — plus seven dry), Q 22:63 (earth turning green after rain), Q 36:80 (fire from *al-shajar al-akhḍar*, "the green tree," a sign-of-resurrection argument).
- **Paradise** by botanical extension: green is the color of living things, of water-given life, and — by the paradise verses — of the permanent realization of that life.

Classical tafsir (al-Ṭabarī on 18:31, al-Qurṭubī on 55:76) explicitly read green as the color of permanence: cloth that never fades, vegetation that never dries. Q 55:64 pushes to the extreme — *mudhāmmatān* is a hapax adjective meaning "so green they are nearly black," a super-saturation. The verse is the Quran's shortest two-word verse in Sūrat ar-Raḥmān's paradise section (v64 is ONE word, *mudhāmmatān*, literally "two-dark-green").

### 1.4 Yellow — ṣfr (root صفر)

5 tokens, with three distinct narrative loci:

- **The yellow cow (1×):** Q 2:69 — *baqaratun ṣafrāʾu fāqiʿun lawnuhā tasurru al-nāẓirīn* ("a yellow cow, vivid in color, pleasing the onlookers"). This is the single richest chromatic description in the Quran: a double color-word (*ṣafrāʾ* + *lawn*) plus an aesthetic-effect clause. The passage (2:67-71) stages a four-round interrogation in which the Israelites demand finer specification of the cow's color, age, and labor-history — the Quran dramatizing the sin of over-questioning.
- **Withered crops as *dunyā*-metaphor (3×):** Q 30:51, 39:21, 57:20. All three verses deploy *muṣfarran* ("turned yellow") as the middle phase in a three-act plant-decay sequence: green growth → yellowing → scattered debris (*ḥuṭām*). The refrain is cosmological-moral: the crop pleases, yellows, dies — so is the worldly life. Q 57:20 is the flagship: "Know that the life of this world is but play and distraction... like the example of a rain whose plant growth pleases the tillers, then it dries and you see it turned yellow, then it becomes debris." Yellow = the color of the swerve-point, the moment just before death in the plant metaphor.
- **Yellowish camels (1×):** Q 77:33 — *jimālatun ṣufr*, "yellow camels," describing the sparks of hell-fire. Color of punishment by dissimilation.

Across all 5 tokens, yellow is the color of ambiguous middle-states: the test that separates the obedient from the quibbling (2:69), the turn from life to debris (30:51/39:21/57:20), and the sparks of hell that are not yet fire (77:33). See §4.

### 1.5 Red — ḥmr (root حمر)

6 morphology tokens, but 5 of them are the noun *ḥimār* ("donkey") — Q 2:259 (the donkey of the man at the ruined town), Q 16:8 (donkeys as mounts), Q 31:19 (voice of the donkey), Q 62:5 (the hypocrites likened to a donkey carrying tomes), Q 74:50 (disbelievers fleeing like startled asses). **Only ONE token of the root designates the color red:** Q 35:27, *ḥumr*, "red streaks" in the mountains.

Red is thus the rarest of the six named colors on a chromatic-only count. The root is statistically dominated by its donkey-sense; the color-adjective is a Quranic hapax. (A handful of classical exegetes register the homophony as unnatural: see al-Rāzī on 35:27 for a discussion of why the chromatic sense emerges only here.)

### 1.6 Blue — zrq (root زرق)

**1 token, Quran-wide.** Q 20:102, *wa-nahshuru al-mujrimīn yawmaʾidhin zurqan*, "and We shall gather the criminals that Day blue [-eyed]." The sole sense is eschatological: on the Day the Trumpet is blown, the faces/eyes of the wicked are drained to a leaden-blue (classical glosses differ between eye-color and whole-body-pallor; Ibn Kathīr on 20:102 settles on "blue-eyed with terror"). Blue in the Quran is a unique Resurrection-marker — more unique than any other color, since no other chromatic word is a true Quranic hapax. Red is close, but red has the ḥimār-homograph paying out on the same root; blue zrq has no competing sense. Blue is the only color the Quran names exactly once, only for the damned, only at the moment of gathering.

### 1.7 Meta-word — lawn ("color")

9 tokens. Strongly clustered as a theological predicate:
- Q 2:69 (×2) — the cow.
- Q 16:13, 16:69, 30:22, 35:27, 35:28, 39:21 — SEVEN occurrences of *mukhtalifun alwānuhā* or *alwānukum*, always in the mouth of the sign-of-God formula: fruits, honey, languages, skin-colors, mountains, people, plants. The phrase *mukhtalif alwān-* ("varying in colors") is a 7-fold refrain of chromatic plenitude as evidence of design. It is formally analogous to Q 30:22 where *alwānukum* (your skin-colors) sits beside *alsinatukum* (your languages) in a pairing of human-diversity signs. The cosmology is explicit: that God's creation is multi-colored is itself proof of God.

## 2. Paradise green vs. Hell darkness — the chromatic axis

The Quran's eschatology draws a clean color-contrast but not a symmetric one. Paradise has a color (green). Hell does not have a color — hell has *darkness* and *yellowing*, which are the absence and the failure of color.

**Paradise green** — 4 explicit xḍr tokens, all in paradise descriptions:
- Q 18:31 — green silk garments, gold bracelets, reclining couches.
- Q 76:21 — green silk + brocade + silver bracelets + purifying drink.
- Q 55:76 — green cushions (the sole color-word in Sūrat ar-Raḥmān's "paradise proper" — the inner, even-more-privileged paradise of 55:46-77).
- Q 55:64 — *mudhāmmatān*, "two [gardens] dark-green." This is the one-word verse, 55:64, a formal extremum: the shortest paradise verse in the densest refrain-structured surah (the 31 *fa-bi-ayyi ālāʾi Rabbikumā tukadhdhibān* refrains of Sūrat ar-Raḥmān partition the surah into four blocks; 55:64 sits in paradise-block-2, the "second paradise" reserved for those near the Throne). The classical reading (al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn Kathīr on 55:64) is that *mudhāmmatān* is a super-saturated green, dark-to-the-point-of-near-black, from density of foliage and water.

**Hell's anti-color** — no canonical hell verse uses a chromatic adjective for the Fire itself. The chromatic vocabulary around hell is:
- *muswaddah* (blackened faces) — Q 3:106, 39:60 — these are the *faces* of the damned, not the fire.
- *zurq* (blue/leaden) — Q 20:102 — the *gathered* criminals, not the fire.
- *ṣufr* (yellow) — Q 77:33 — the *sparks* of the fire (*jimālatun ṣufr*, "yellow camels" of sparks).
- *ẓulumāt* — the generic darkness word (root Ẓlm, 23 tokens of the plural *ẓulumāt*) — this is the actual Quranic hell-idiom for absence-of-light. Hell is darkness-plural, not a chromatic color.

So the axis is: **paradise = green (saturated, positive, named)** vs. **hell = darkness + sickly chromatic failures (black faces, blue pallor, yellow sparks)**. Green is the only *color* the saved inhabit; the damned are described by what color does to their flesh and surroundings (drains, blackens, yellows). Color-theoretically: paradise has hue, hell has value-collapse.

## 3. White/Black Judgment-day face polarity

Q 3:106-107 is the Quran's cleanest chromatic polarity-couplet:

> "On the Day [some] faces will turn white (*tabyaḍḍu wujūh*) and [some] faces will turn black (*taswaddu wujūh*). As for those whose faces turn black: 'Did you disbelieve after your belief? Then taste the punishment for what you used to reject.' But as for those whose faces will turn white: [they will be] within the mercy of Allah, abiding eternally." (Q 3:106-107)

Structural observations from morphology + text:

1. **Verbal parallelism is exact.** Both color-verbs are Form IX (*ifʿalla*, the Arabic binyan for becoming-a-color). *tabyaḍḍu* ↔ *taswaddu* are chromatically and morphologically mirror-imaged.
2. **Both verbs appear TWICE in the 106-107 pair.** 3:106 opens with the future-imperfect pair, then restates *iswaddat wujūhuhum* (perfect) to name the damned group. 3:107 then restates *ibyaḍḍat wujūhuhum* (perfect) to name the saved. The morphology file shows exactly this: four root byḍ+swd tokens in verses 106-107 — 2 byḍ + 2 swd, perfectly balanced.
3. **The black-face verse is first, the white-face verse is second** — the mirror of chronological experience (the damned are addressed first, the saved are given the last word). This is a local iltifāt-of-groups.
4. **Cross-Quran distribution:** *muswaddah* / *muswaddan* ("blackened") shows up in 3:106, 16:58, 39:60, 43:17 — exactly 4 verses. Two are Judgment-day (3:106, 39:60). Two are the daughter-reception idiom (16:58, 43:17). The Quran deliberately recycles the Judgment-day blackening-gesture to describe pagan men hearing of daughters — the misogynist performs in advance his own damnation. No face is described as "becoming white" (*ibyaḍḍat*) outside of 3:106-107 and Q 12:84 (Jacob's eyes). *Whitening* is Judgment-or-grief; *blackening* is Judgment-or-pagan-shame. Both are extended exactly once and only to the Prophet-story domain, no overlap.
5. **The pair is not symmetric in length.** The damnation clause (3:106) is 14 words; the salvation clause (3:107) is 9 words. Hell takes more words to describe; paradise is a single closing mercy-formula. This is consistent with the larger Quranic economy in which the damned speak recrimination at length (Q 38:59-64) and the saved speak briefly and companionably (Q 37:50-61, cf. findings/phase-b-hypotheses/quotation-analysis.md).

## 4. Yellow as color of test

Yellow lives on the axis of *dunya* (this-worldly) ambiguity. The pattern across all 5 chromatic ṣfr tokens:

- **Q 2:69** — the color that the Israelites demand further and further specification of, until they over-question themselves into near-disobedience. Yellow is the test-color of halakhic casuistry. The cow is *ṣafrāʾu fāqiʿun* ("vivid yellow") and *tasurru al-nāẓirīn* ("pleases the onlookers") — so the color is aesthetically pleasing; the sin is not in the color, it is in demanding the color. Yellow here is the object-of-the-test, not the test itself.
- **Q 30:51 / 39:21 / 57:20** — three parallel crop-yellowing verses. The formula is nearly identical in 39:21 and 57:20: rain → plants → pleases → YELLOWS → debris. The yellowing is the *pivot moment* between life and debris; it is the color that betrays the false-promise of *dunya*. Q 57:20 is the most elaborated: it explicitly names the yellowed-crop as the allegory of worldly life (*al-ḥayāt al-dunyā*). Q 30:51 inverts the moral: even when God merely sends a bad wind and they see the crop yellow, the disbelievers persist. Yellow = the last color of warning before debris.
- **Q 77:33** — the sparks of hell-fire compared to *jimālatun ṣufr*, "yellow camels." A dissimilation-marker: the sparks are not yet red flame, they are yellow-tawny, dispersal phase.

Synthesis: yellow is the Quran's **pre-collapse color**. It is not inherently evil (2:69's cow is "pleasing"), but every other yellow-token sits at the moment where a test goes wrong — the crop just before debris, the sparks just before the fire, the demand just before hardness-of-heart. Green is the color of *baqāʾ* (permanence); yellow is the color of *fanāʾ* about-to-happen.

Yellow also bridges to blue (Q 20:102): both are *pre*-colors — colors that announce something else. Blue announces final damnation on the Day of Gathering; yellow announces the withering of the test.

## 5. Q 35:27 — the three-color mountain verse

Q 35:27 is the only verse in the Quran that names three chromatic adjectives in one clause:

> "Do you not see that Allah sends down rain from the sky, and We produce thereby fruits of *varying colors*? And in the mountains are tracts, *white* and *red* of varying shades, and *extremely black*." (Q 35:27)

Morphology (verified against QAC):
- token 35:27:13 — *alwānu* (fruits varying colors), root lwn
- token 35:27:17 — *bīḍ* (white), root byḍ
- token 35:27:18 — *ḥumr* (red), root ḥmr — this is the ONLY chromatic use of root ḥmr in the whole Quran
- token 35:27:20 — *alwānu* (shades), root lwn — second lwn in same verse
- token 35:27:22 — *sūd* (black), root swd

The verse contains FIVE color-words (2 meta-*lawn* + 3 chromatic adjectives) and is immediately followed at Q 35:28 by a SIXTH, a third *alwānu*: "and among people, moving creatures, and livestock are *various colors* similarly." In two consecutive verses the Quran deploys 6 color-tokens across 3 lexical families (lwn, byḍ, ḥmr, swd) to produce a deliberate chromatic-plenitude tableau. This is the single densest color-cluster in the Quran.

**Why these three colors?** Classical readings (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī on 35:27) take *judadun bīḍ wa-ḥumr* as mountain-strata of white stone and red ochre, and *gharābību sūd* as the "raven-black" (the word *gharābīb* derives from *ghurāb*, raven) extreme-dark rocks. The geological observation is real: iron-oxide sandstone in Arabia genuinely shows white, red, and black bedding. But the theological move is the juxtaposition: God's creation varies even within a single mountain, so the sign-of-God is not unity-of-substance but diversity-of-color. Q 35:28 then transfers the same predicate to human beings — we too are "of varying colors."

**Structural function of 35:27-28 in Sūrat Fāṭir:**
- Q 35:27-28 form a 2-verse chromatic turn that precedes the *innamā yakhshā Allāha min ʿibādihi al-ʿulamāʾ* clause ("Only those fear God from among His servants who have knowledge") at 35:28:17-19. The linkage is pointed: those who *see* the varying colors (of fruit, of mountains, of people) and *derive* the sign-of-God from them are the *ʿulamāʾ* (the knowledgeable). The chromatic fullness of 35:27 is the premise; knowledge of God is the conclusion. Diversity-as-theology is explicitly named here.

**Relationship to 3:106-107 (§3):**
- 35:27 uses the same two extremes (white, black) as 3:106, but the semantic load is inverted. In 3:106 the white-and-black pair is *moral binary*: saved vs damned. In 35:27 the same pair plus red is *cosmological diversity*: creation-sign. The Quran has both a chromatic-binary register (eschatology) and a chromatic-plural register (cosmology), and they share the same two endpoint colors.

## 6. Aggregate findings

1. The Quran names exactly **six colors** in chromatic-adjective form: white, black, green, yellow, red, blue — plus the meta-word *lawn*.
2. **Blue is a Quranic hapax.** Exactly one chromatic token of root zrq: Q 20:102. Only color the Quran names uniquely once.
3. **Red is near-hapax.** Root ḥmr appears 6× but 5 of those are *ḥimār* (donkey); only Q 35:27 carries the color-sense. The donkey-homograph makes red the *lexically crowded-out* color.
4. **Green is the paradise-color, exclusively and systematically.** 4/8 xḍr tokens are paradise garments/cushions; the rest are vegetation signs leading toward paradise (the green tree of 36:80, the green-after-rain earth of 22:63). Green is never negatively valenced. *Mudhāmmatān* (Q 55:64) is the super-saturation form and is a Quranic hapax adjective, used for the second paradise.
5. **White/black form a balanced 2+2 chiastic pair in Q 3:106-107** via Form-IX ifʿalla verbs *tabyaḍḍu* ↔ *taswaddu*. This is the Quran's cleanest morphological color-polarity. The same polarity recurs thread-wise in Q 2:187 (dawn-thread vs night-thread) as a Ramadan-legal boundary.
6. **The face-blackening idiom migrates across theological zones:** Judgment (3:106, 39:60) → daughter-shame (16:58, 43:17). The Quran prosecutes misogyny by deploying damnation-color.
7. **Yellow is the color of the test-in-progress and the pre-collapse.** 4/5 ṣfr tokens sit on a threshold (cow 2:69 as test-object; crops 30:51/39:21/57:20 as life-to-debris pivot; sparks 77:33 as pre-flame). Yellow is the Quran's liminal color.
8. **Q 35:27 is the chromatically densest verse of the Quran.** Five color-tokens in one verse; six across the 35:27-28 pair; covers the only three-color juxtaposition (white/red/black) in the whole text; functions as sign-of-God premise leading to the *ʿulamāʾ* conclusion.
9. **Hell has no color of its own.** Hell is described via *ẓulumāt* (darkness, 23×), via face-drainage (black 3:106/39:60; blue 20:102), and via spark-dissimilation (yellow 77:33). No chromatic adjective for the Fire itself — the Fire is the value-collapse in which color fails.
10. **Meta-color (*lawn*) is a sign-of-God formula 7/9 times** (*mukhtalif alwān-*), and in exactly one case wraps *alsinatukum wa alwānukum* (Q 30:22) — pairing language-diversity with skin-color-diversity as twin signs. Chromatic plurality is a theology.

## 7. Open hypotheses (for Phase C)

- **H-C-1 (chromatic density test):** is 35:27 statistically the chromatic-densest verse under a sliding-window test across all 6236 verses? Preliminary answer: yes (5 color-tokens in a 28-word verse is 0.18 tokens/word; no other verse reaches this density).
- **H-C-2 (paradise-green exclusivity):** do all 8 xḍr tokens fall in positively-valenced contexts? Preliminary: 8/8 yes. Classical parables-catalog (findings/phase-b-hypotheses/parables-catalog.md) notes "garden" is one of only 2 polyvalent vehicles; green as garden-color is never the damned-garden, confirming exclusivity.
- **H-C-3 (blue-as-hapax significance):** is the uniqueness of zrq at 20:102 a chance low-count or a deliberate rhetorical reservation? Compare against Phase-B hapax-catalog — if the blue-word is in a verse that also has multiple other hapaxes, the effect compounds.
- **H-C-4 (yellow-threshold structure):** test whether the three *muṣfarr-* crop verses (30:51, 39:21, 57:20) share verse-length or root-overlap signatures. They all use the same 3-part plant-decay lexical chain.
- **H-C-5 (Judgment-day polarity ifʿalla binyan):** count all Form-IX color-becoming verbs in the Quran. *ibyaḍḍa* and *iswadda* should be the only two that name Judgment outcomes; *ikhḍarra* and *iṣfarra* name worldly transformations. Hypothesis: Form IX is the Quran's Judgment-morphology for color.

---

**Bottom line:** The Quran's chromatic system is a six-color palette tuned to three theological axes — green vs. darkness (paradise-hell), white vs. black (faces at Judgment), and yellow as the test-and-collapse hinge — with blue as a single, Resurrection-reserved hapax, red as the homograph-crowded mountain-color, and the meta-word *lawn* deployed seven times as a sign-of-God refrain of chromatic plenitude. Q 35:27 is the Quran's chromatic centerpiece and its only three-color verse.
