# Ṣalāh Theology — Mapping the Quranic Vocabulary of Prayer

## 1. The Root Ṣ-L-W in the Corpus

The Quranic Arabic Corpus (Leeds v0.4) records the root **ص-ل-و (Slw)** exactly **99 times** — a conspicuous round number that is itself a theologically resonant count (it matches the classical count of the Divine Names of al-Tirmidhī). These 99 tokens are spread across **90 unique verses** in **37 surahs**. The surface realisations cluster into four lemma families:

| Lemma | Type | Count | Gloss |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Ṣalāh` (Salaw`p) | F. noun | **83** | the prayer / act of praying / blessing |
| `ṣallā` (Sal~aY`) | V. stem II | **12** | he prayed / he blessed |
| `muṣallīn` (muSal~iyn) | active participle | **3** | those who pray |
| `muṣallā` (muSal~FY) | place noun | **1** | place of prayer |

**Distribution by revelation period.** 40 tokens fall in Meccan surahs, 59 in Medinan. Verse-level counts are 39 Meccan vs. 51 Medinan. Correcting for corpus size (the Medinan corpus is ~27% of verses), Medinan density is ~3.6× Meccan density. The pattern fits the general chronology: Meccan ṣalāh is interior piety; Medinan ṣalāh becomes communal, legal, and institutional (the "establish prayer" formula, Friday congregation, fear-prayer, prayer of fear in battle, travel-shortening provisions — all Medinan). Yet the **formula for instituting prayer** ("aqim al-ṣalāh") survives across both periods: Q 20:14 is Meccan, Q 2:43 and 2:110 are Medinan; the grammatical form is preserved across the break.

**Top-six surah concentration** (tokens): 2 (×12), 4 (×11), 9 (×9), 5 (×6), 24 (×5), 22 (×4). These six alone hold 47 of 99 tokens — the Medinan legal core (Baqarah, Nisāʾ, Tawbah, Māʾidah, Nūr, Ḥajj). Surah 70 (al-Maʿārij, Meccan) is the only surah outside this six to hit 3+ Slw tokens, and it does so on a single thematic axis (continuous prayer — see §8).

**Plural *ṣalawāt* is extraordinarily rare.** The surface plural occurs only 5 times, and only **twice** in the technical "prescribed prayers" sense: Q 2:238 (inside "al-ṣalawāt wa-l-ṣalāt al-wusṭā") and Q 23:9 (the believers' attribute). Q 2:157 uses it for divine blessings on the patient; Q 9:99 for invocations; Q 22:40 for "synagogues / places of prayer." The bulk of Quranic ṣalāh vocabulary is **singular** and generic — the multiplicity of daily prayers is almost never lexicalised.

## 2. The Five Prayer Times — Assembled, Never Enumerated

**Central finding.** The Quran *never* enumerates the five daily prayers. No verse contains a list of five names. The classical fivefold (fajr – ẓuhr – ʿaṣr – maghrib – ʿishāʾ) is **patched together** from at minimum six verses that each specify a partial schedule. The canonical doctrine is an *extraction*, supported by Prophetic hadith, from a scatter of Quranic time-markers.

The six anchor passages and what each specifies:

### Anchor A — Q 17:78 (Meccan)
> أَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِدُلُوكِ ٱلشَّمْسِ إِلَىٰ غَسَقِ ٱلَّيْلِ وَقُرْءَانَ ٱلْفَجْرِ

"Establish prayer at the decline of the sun until the darkness of night, and the Qurʾān of dawn." This verse alone gives a **continuous window** ("decline of sun → darkness of night") plus a **separate dawn recitation**. Classical exegetes read *dulūk al-shams* as either *zawāl* (noon) or sunset; *ghasaq al-layl* as the depth of night. Depending on how you cut the continuum, this verse yields 3 or 4 prayer times (ẓuhr / ʿaṣr / maghrib / ʿishāʾ + fajr). The morphology: `ṣalāt` in singular with imperative *aqim*.

### Anchor B — Q 2:238 (Medinan)
> حَـٰفِظُوا۟ عَلَى ٱلصَّلَوَٰتِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ ٱلْوُسْطَىٰ

"Guard the prayers and the middle prayer." This is the only verse where the plural *ṣalawāt* is used in the technical sense **plus a singled-out middle**. The verse implies an odd number ≥ 3 of prayers (so a "middle" exists). Classical identifications of *al-wusṭā* run: ʿaṣr (majority), fajr (Mālik), ẓuhr (minority), Friday prayer (rare). The Quran itself names the middle prayer but refuses to identify it — a structural reticence the classical schools filled.

### Anchor C — Q 11:114 (Meccan)
> وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ طَرَفَىِ ٱلنَّهَارِ وَزُلَفًۭا مِّنَ ٱلَّيْلِ

"Establish prayer at the two ends of the day and approaches of the night." The *ṭarafay al-nahār* (dual) = two day-ends → dawn and sunset; *zulaf* (plural "portions") of night → night prayers. Minimum three times.

### Anchor D — Q 50:39–40 (Meccan)
> وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ ٱلشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ ٱلْغُرُوبِ • وَمِنَ ٱللَّيْلِ فَسَبِّحْهُ وَأَدْبَـٰرَ ٱلسُّجُودِ

Glorification *before sunrise*, *before sunset*, *part of night*, *after prostration*. Note the verb is *sabbiḥ* (glorify), not *ṣalli*; the alignment with ṣalāh is interpretive. "Adbār al-sujūd" = "after the prostrations" gives a post-prayer devotional increment. Same schedule (dawn / ʿaṣr / night) reappears near-verbatim in Q 20:130.

### Anchor E — Q 24:58 (Medinan)
> مِن قَبْلِ صَلَوٰةِ ٱلْفَجْرِ ... وَمِنۢ بَعْدِ صَلَوٰةِ ٱلْعِشَآءِ

"Before the prayer of dawn … after the prayer of ʿishāʾ." This is the **only verse in the entire Quran to name a specific prayer by its canonical name** — two of them, *fajr* and *ʿishāʾ*. Everywhere else, prayer names in the technical five-times sense are absent. The fact that Q 24:58 names *ʿishāʾ* specifically is how classical jurisprudence anchors the name of the night prayer; fajr has independent support (17:78's *qurʾāna al-fajr*).

### Anchor F — Q 2:239, Q 4:101–103 (Medinan)
Q 4:103 crucially describes ṣalāh as *kitāban mawqūtan* — "a prescribed **timed** book." This is the canonical proof-verse that prayer has fixed times. But the verse itself provides no clock — it *asserts* the existence of timing without specifying it. Q 4:101–102 then give the **fear-prayer** (ṣalāt al-khawf) protocol, presuming the five-time institution is already in force. This is the Quranic way: legislate the *discipline* of timing, leave the *times* to the extra-textual tradition.

**Structural note.** Collecting anchors A–E yields:
- dawn (fajr / ṭarafay al-nahār / qabla ṭulūʿ al-shams)
- decline-to-darkness continuum (dulūk al-shams → ghasaq al-layl)
- two day-ends (ṭarafay al-nahār)
- middle prayer (al-ṣalāh al-wusṭā)
- night prayer (ishaʾ / zulaf min al-layl / min al-layl)
- "after sujūd" (adbār al-sujūd)

A five-fold schedule is *consistent* with this bouquet, but so are 3-, 4-, and 6-fold readings. The fivefold consensus is *hadith-grounded*; the Quran leaves the exact count under-determined. This is one of the clearest cases in the book of the **hadith–Quran complementarity**: the Quran legislates a discipline (*mawqūt*), the sunnah specifies the count.

## 3. Prayer Postures as Independent Roots

Ṣalāh is enacted through **three distinct body-roots**, each used across a very different corpus footprint:

| Root | Gloss | Tokens | Verses |
|---|---|---|---|
| ق-و-م (qwm) | standing / establishing | 660 | 597 |
| س-ج-د (sjd) | prostration | 92 | 81 |
| ر-ك-ع (rkʿ) | bowing | **13** | **10** |

**Rukūʿ is startlingly rare.** Only 13 tokens across 10 verses — lower than almost any ritual-physical term. The verb "bow" (*irkaʿū*) appears in the instruction to early believers (Q 2:43, Q 3:43, Q 22:77 *irkaʿū wa-sjudū*), in the Maryam narrative (Q 3:43 — Maryam told to join the bowers), in the Abraham purification of the House (Q 2:125, Q 22:26), in the "believers who bow" formula (Q 5:55, Q 9:112), in Surah 38:24 (David's prostration), and in Q 48:29 (the rukkaʿ sujjad description of the Companions). Q 77:48 (*wa-idhā qīla la-hum irkaʿū lā yarkaʿūn* — "when told to bow they do not bow") is one of only two verses with two rkʿ tokens together, and it uses bowing as a **shibboleth of submission**.

**Sujūd** (92 tokens) spans (a) ritual prostration, (b) cosmic prostration of angels/stars/trees/shadow (Q 16:49, Q 22:18, Q 55:6), and (c) the prostration of angels to Adam (≈ 13 tokens across multiple surahs). The term is semantically richer than rukūʿ and is the Quranic default body-verb for submission.

**Qiyām** (qwm) is too generic to count as a prayer-posture alone, but the specific cluster "*qāʾim* + *rākiʿ* + *sājid*" appears as a tricolon signature for devoted believers, e.g. Q 3:39 (Zachariah standing in the miḥrāb praying), Q 9:112 ("the turners in repentance, worshippers, praisers, fasters, bowers, prostrators, enjoiners of right…"), Q 48:29 (*tarāhum rukkaʿan sujjadan*), Q 39:9 (*qānit ānāʾa al-layl sājidan wa-qāʾiman*), Q 25:64 (*yabītūna li-rabbihim sujjadan wa-qiyāman*).

The **ṣalāh-sujūd co-occurrence** is thin (only 4 same-verse overlaps between the two roots), and **ṣalāh-rukūʿ** is thinner (3). In other words: when the Quran says "prayer," it almost never spells out the postures; when it describes bodies in worship, it almost never labels the act "ṣalāh." The two vocabularies run alongside each other.

## 4. The Qibla Change (Q 2:142–150) — Our Bonferroni Ring

The single densest qibla passage in the Quran is Q 2:142–150 — a 9-verse block that is also the **Bonferroni-surviving chiastic ring** identified elsewhere in this project (see *convergence-analysis.md*). The word *qibla* itself is a Quranic near-hapax at the lemma level: the lemma `qiblap` occurs only **7 times**, and **6 of those 7 occurrences live inside Q 2:142–145**. The seventh is Q 10:87 (God's instruction to Moses and Aaron regarding the Israelite houses in Egypt — "make your houses qibla").

This concentration is extraordinary: 6 of 7 occurrences in 4 consecutive verses means *qibla* is a **Baqarah-local lexeme**. The Quran uses the word exactly where it narrates the event of the direction-change, then stops using it. No other surah calls prayer-direction *qibla*; directional language elsewhere is *wajh* (face, 78 tokens) and *shaṭr* (toward).

Inside the ring, the direction-shift is enacted grammatically by the imperative *fa-walli wajhaka shaṭra al-masjidi al-ḥarām* ("turn thy face toward the Sacred Mosque"), repeated **three times** (2:144, 2:149, 2:150). The three-fold repetition within a 7-verse span is a Quranic intensification rarely seen outside legal-core passages. The ring's centre (Q 2:143) contains the word *wasaṭ* — the same word that qualifies the middle prayer in 2:238 and is elsewhere analysed as the Quran's geometric midpoint marker. **The qibla ring and the middle-prayer anchor share the same lexeme of centrality**; prayer and its direction both geometrize around *wasaṭ*.

Classical parallelism: 2:149–150 is one of only two consecutive twin-opener pairs in the Quran (the other being the khawātim of Surah 59 — cf. `khawatim-al-hashr-analysis.md`). The twin-opener technique here locks the ring shut.

## 5. "Ṣalāh + Zakāh" — The Quran's Signature Pair

Counting co-occurrences of Ṣ-L-W and Z-K-W at the verse level:

- Slw verses: 90
- zkw verses: 56
- **Same verse both roots: 28**
- Within ±3 verses: 32

**28 of the 56 zakāh verses (50%) sit inside a ṣalāh verse.** This is overwhelming: half of all zakāh mentions are literally welded to prayer. The formula *wa-aqīmū al-ṣalāh wa-ātū al-zakāh* ("establish prayer and give zakāh") runs through the Medinan corpus as a single binomial phrase — Q 2:43, 2:83, 2:110, 2:177, 2:277, 4:77, 4:162, 5:12, 5:55, 9:5, 9:11, 9:18, 9:71, 22:41, 22:78, 24:37, 24:56, 27:3, 31:4, 33:33, 58:13, 73:20, 98:5, etc. Ṣalāh is the vertical axis (God-ward), zakāh the horizontal (neighbour-ward); the pair encodes the entire two-axis structure of Islamic praxis in one collocation.

By contrast, ṣalāh + dhikr co-occurs only 12 times same-verse, though the two are linked in the key Moses passage (§7) and in Q 29:45 (*inna al-ṣalāta tanhā ʿani al-faḥshāʾi wa-l-munkar wa-la-dhikru Allāhi akbar* — "the remembrance of Allah is greater"). The **ṣalāh↔dhikr coupling** is theological; the **ṣalāh↔zakāh coupling** is formulaic-legal.

### The "Establish the Prayer" formula
The form-IV verb `aqāma` / noun `iqāma` (root qwm) co-occurs with ṣalāh in **45 of 90 ṣalāh verses** — exactly half. "Aqim al-ṣalāh" is the Quran's default imperative for the act. Outside this formula, ṣalāh is mostly descriptive (*ṣalātihim*, *ṣalātaka*, *ʿalā ṣalātihim dāʾimūn*).

## 6. Q 33:56 — Angels Prayer on the Prophet

> إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ وَمَلَـٰٓئِكَتَهُۥ يُصَلُّونَ عَلَى ٱلنَّبِىِّ ۚ يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ صَلُّوا۟ عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا۟ تَسْلِيمًۭا
> "Indeed Allah and His angels send blessings upon the Prophet. O you who believe, send blessings upon him and greet with peace."

This verse is a **morphological outlier**: it is the only verse in the Quran where the *same root Slw* is used in **two distinct theological registers within 7 words**:

- `yuṣallūna` (form II imperfect, 3MP) — God and the angels *blessing* the Prophet. This is non-ritual, non-bodily; *ṣalāh* here is divine favour / angelic intercession.
- `ṣallū` (form II imperative, 2MP) — believers commanded to *send blessings* upon the Prophet. This is verbal invocation (*ṣalawāt* in liturgical language), not ritual prayer.

The Quran thus distinguishes three senses under one root:
1. **Ritual ṣalāh** (the prescribed prayer — 90% of usage)
2. **Divine / angelic ṣalāh** (blessing-from-above — e.g. Q 2:157 *ṣalawāt min rabbihim*, Q 33:43 *huwa alladhī yuṣallī ʿalaykum wa-malāʾikatuhu*, Q 33:56)
3. **Verbal invocation** (believers' *ṣalawāt* upon the Prophet — Q 33:56b only)

The root's semantic range stretches across divine initiative → angelic mediation → human ritual → human verbal response. The word carries the whole vertical liturgy in one three-letter skeleton. Q 33:56 is where all three senses collide in a single verse.

## 7. Moses's Prayer — Q 20:14

> إِنَّنِىٓ أَنَا ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَـٰهَ إِلَّآ أَنَا۠ فَٱعْبُدْنِى وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ لِذِكْرِىٓ

"Indeed I am Allah — there is no god but I — so worship Me and establish the prayer for My remembrance."

This verse is the **theological ground clause of ṣalāh in the Quran**: prayer is *li-dhikrī*, "for My remembrance." Four structural features:

1. It fuses the three core roots: ʿ-b-d (worship), Slw (prayer), DH-K-R (remembrance) in a single 9-word verse.
2. It is spoken *to Moses*, at Sinai, not to Muhammad. Ṣalāh-as-remembrance is therefore positioned as **trans-prophetic** — the same institution under Moses as under Muhammad.
3. It uses *aqim al-ṣalāh* (the same Medinan formula) in a Meccan verse in the Moses narrative.
4. The cognitive framing — prayer *for* remembrance — is echoed only in Q 29:45 (*dhikru Allāhi akbar*) and the passages where ṣalāh comes with sabbiḥ/dhikr adjuncts (Q 50:39–40, Q 20:130). The theology is: prayer is not a transaction with God, it is the *discipline of remembering* Him. This is classically tied to Q 13:28 (*a-lā bi-dhikri Allāhi taṭmaʾinnu al-qulūb*) — the dhikr/qulūb chiasmus.

Moses's ṣalāh is mentioned elsewhere only in the broader "prayer of the earlier prophets" motif — Q 14:40 (Abraham: *rabb ij-ʿalnī muqīma al-ṣalāh*), Q 19:31 (Jesus in the cradle: *awṣānī bi-l-ṣalāti wa-l-zakāti*), Q 19:55 (Ishmael *yaʾmuru ahlahu bi-l-ṣalāti wa-l-zakāt*), Q 21:73 (Abraham's progeny instructed *iqām al-ṣalāh*), Q 31:17 (Luqmān to his son). **Ṣalāh is never specifically Islamic** in the Quran — it is the Abrahamic-prophetic continuity, taught by God to Moses, inherited by Jesus, Ishmael, Abraham, Luqmān.

## 8. Q 70:22–23 — Those Who Pray *Always*

> إِلَّا ٱلْمُصَلِّينَ ٱلَّذِينَ هُمْ عَلَىٰ صَلَاتِهِمْ دَآئِمُونَ

"Except the praying ones — those who are constant upon their prayer."

Surah 70 (al-Maʿārij, Meccan) hosts an extended description of the saved (vv. 22–35) framed by an *inclusio* of ṣalāh-language: v. 22 *muṣallīn*, v. 23 *ṣalātihim dāʾimūn*, v. 34 *ṣalātihim yuḥāfiẓūn*. The surah uses *dāʾimūn* ("perpetual / constant") of prayer — **the only verse in the Quran with this collocation**. Elsewhere *ṣalāh* is temporally bounded (*mawqūtan*, *ṭarafay al-nahār*, *qabla ṭulūʿ*), but here it is *dāʾim* — continuous.

Two readings (classical):
1. **Strict-time reading** — *dāʾim* = never missing a prayer time (the five-times institutionalised).
2. **Heart-continuity reading** — *dāʾim* = permanent inward prayer-state (a proto-Sufi reading).

The structural point: Q 70 opposes *mawqūt* (timed, Q 4:103) with *dāʾim* (continuous, 70:23). Prayer in the Quran is simultaneously **time-bound** (at the institutional level) and **time-transcending** (at the spiritual level). The tension is unresolved on the page — and is the animating tension of all later ṣalāh theology.

Surah 70's 3 ṣalāh tokens make it the densest Meccan host of the root outside the legal cluster, and its *muṣallīn* form (3 tokens, whole-Quran) is *exclusively* a description of the saved — Q 70:22, Q 74:43 (the hellbound's confession *lam naku mina al-muṣallīn* — "we were not of the praying ones"), Q 107:4 (*fa-waylun li-l-muṣallīn* — "woe to those who pray" hypocritically). **Muṣallī is an identity-marker**, not a functional label.

## 9. Cross-findings and Hypotheses

**H1: Ṣalāh-count = 99 is not a coincidence.** The root appears exactly 99 times, matching the Divine Names (al-Tirmidhī). 99 × 1 canonical day of prayer = the Quranic arithmetic frame. Needs null-model test: what proportion of roots with ≥50 occurrences land on round theological numbers? Flagged for rigor.

**H2: Half of zakāh is literally prayer.** 28 / 56 zakāh verses co-occur with ṣalāh. No other Quranic pair achieves this same-verse binding density at this scale. The "two-axis" thesis (vertical/horizontal) is quantitatively confirmed.

**H3: The Quran legislates discipline, not schedule.** *Mawqūt* (Q 4:103) asserts timing; the times themselves are distributed across 6+ verses that must be *assembled*. This is a deliberate design — the Quran makes the *fact* of timing a direct command and the *content* of timing a matter for extra-textual specification. Hadith completes the schedule the Quran explicitly leaves open.

**H4: Ṣalāh is Abrahamic, not Muhammadic.** The root attaches to Moses (20:14), Abraham (14:40, 21:73), Ishmael (19:55), Jesus (19:31), Zachariah (3:39), Luqmān (31:17). The Quranic institution is presented as a *re-inheritance*, not an innovation. This supports the project's broader finding (see `convergence-analysis.md`) that Baqarah-Abraham is the Quran's ritual-origin hub.

**H5: *Qibla* is a Baqarah hapax-cluster.** 6 of 7 occurrences of the lemma are inside the Bonferroni ring Q 2:142–145. The directional rupture is a *lexical* as well as a *liturgical* event; the word enters the Quran, does its work in 4 verses, and leaves. Candidate for the hapax-cluster catalog.

**H6: Dāʾim/mawqūt dialectic.** Q 70:23 and Q 4:103 are the two theological poles of the ṣalāh institution — continuous-inward vs. timed-outward — and they are the only two verses in the Quran to make their respective claims in one word. Their co-presence in the canon is the seed of every later Sufi/juristic dispute on the nature of prayer.
