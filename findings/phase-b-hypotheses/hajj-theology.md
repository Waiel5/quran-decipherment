# Pilgrimage (Ḥajj) Theology in the Quran

## 0. Thesis

The Quran treats Ḥajj as a *reactivation*, not an invention. Its
legal-ritual skeleton is deposited almost entirely in a single Medinan
pericope (Q 2:196–203), with a parallel abstract-liturgical block in
Sūrat al-Ḥajj (Q 22:26–37). The cultic vocabulary is tight,
asymmetric, and studded with hapaxes: ʿArafāt, al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām,
al-Ṣafā, al-Marwa each appear exactly once. The pilgrimage is framed
not as new legislation but as the rehabilitation of an Abrahamic
rite long corrupted by Jāhilī praxis (idols, *nasī*ʾ intercalation,
taboo-inversion). The Quran's theology of Ḥajj is therefore
*archaeological*: every named station is dug out of Abrahamic memory
and reset into monotheistic practice.

This document maps (1) the vocabulary, (2) Q 22's paradoxical near-
silence on pilgrimage mechanics despite the surah's name, (3) Q
2:196–203 as the legal spine, (4) the *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ* formula,
(5) the historical pivots (Ḥudaybiyya Q 48; Farewell Pilgrimage Q 5:3),
(6) Abrahamic origin (Q 22:26–27; Q 2:124–129; Q 14:37), and (7)
pilgrimage-month timing.

## 1. Full vocabulary catalog

Counts verified against `data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`.

### 1.1 Root `Hjj` — the pilgrimage root itself

The root `Hjj` carries two distinct semantic streams in the Quran:
**(a) the noun `Haj~` = pilgrimage**, and **(b) the verb `HaA^j~a` /
noun `Huj~ap` = to argue / proof / dispute**. The same consonantal
frame designates both the *going-to-witness* and the *adducing-
evidence*; classical philology treats these as cognate senses around
"setting out purposefully, bringing forward a weighty matter."

Nominal `Haj~` occurrences (pilgrimage sense):
- **2:158** *Haj~a al-bayt* — "makes pilgrimage to the House" (infinitival verb)
- **2:189** *mawāqītu li-l-nās wa-l-Haj~* — "times of measurement for people and for Ḥajj"
- **2:196** *wa-atimmū l-Haj~a wa-l-ʿumrata li-llāh* — opens the legal pericope; three occurrences in this verse alone
- **2:197** *al-Haj~u ashhurun maʿlūmāt* — "Ḥajj is well-known months"; three more occurrences
- **3:97** *wa-li-llāhi ʿalā l-nāsi Hij~u l-bayti* — "people owe to God pilgrimage of the House"
- **9:3** *yawma l-Haj~i l-akbari* — "the day of the greater pilgrimage"
- **22:27** *wa-adhdhin fī l-nāsi bi-l-Haj~i* — "proclaim the Ḥajj to the people"

Total nominal-pilgrimage occurrences: ~10, densely clustered in Q 2 and Q 22. The root appears in the dispute sense far more often (e.g. 2:76, 2:139, 3:20, 3:61, 3:65, 6:80, 40:47, 42:16, 45:25), but the **pilgrimage sense is the restricted, marked subsense** and appears only in surahs treating cultic law or Abrahamic origin.

A related form `Hijaj` (plural) at **28:27** in the Moses-Shuʿayb marriage-contract (*thamāniya ḥijaj* — "eight pilgrimages" as a unit of time) shows that *ḥijja* was an archaic calendrical word meaning "year by its pilgrimage." Ḥajj is thus, in old Semitic idiom, a *season marker*.

### 1.2 Root `Emr` — ʿumra

The *minor pilgrimage* vocabulary:
- `Eumorap` (ʿumra) — noun, 2:196 (×2: accusative and genitive)
- `{Eotamara` (VIII stem verb) — "performed ʿumra" — 2:158
- Root `Emr` also covers *lifespan*, *maintenance of a mosque* (9:17–19), and *inhabited/frequented* (`al-Bayt al-Maʿmūr` 52:4). The semantic center is "to populate, to make frequent, to maintain alive." ʿUmra is the *visiting that keeps a place alive*.

ʿUmra appears only **three times** as a cultic term (2:158 verb; 2:196 ×2). It is legislatively coupled to Ḥajj at 2:196 (*wa-atimmū l-Haj~a wa-l-ʿumrata*) but otherwise under-specified. Its distinctive cultic content in the Quran is: (a) it may be joined to Ḥajj (*tamattuʿ*) with an atoning offering; (b) it triggers the Ṣafā/Marwa saʿy (2:158).

### 1.3 Root `Twf` — ṭawāf / circumambulation

The `Twf` root branches widely (wandering, encompassing, *ṭāʾifa* =
faction). The ritual-circumambulation subsense appears in four
verses only:
- **2:125** *Tahhirā baytī li-l-Ṭā'ifīn* — "purify My House for the circumambulators" (addressed to Abraham and Ishmael)
- **2:158** *an yaṭṭawwafa bi-himā* — "to go around (Ṣafā and Marwa)"
- **22:26** *li-l-Ṭā'ifīn* — "for the circumambulators" (again to Abraham, parallel phrasing)
- **22:29** *wa-l-yaṭṭawwafū bi-l-bayti l-ʿatīq* — "let them circumambulate the Ancient House"

The pattern is exact: **every ritual `Twf` form is tied either to Abraham's commission (2:125, 22:26) or to the resulting pilgrimage action (2:158 around Ṣafā/Marwa; 22:29 around the House).** No ṭawāf verse exists outside the Abrahamic framework. This is a theologically sealed vocabulary loop: the act of circumambulation is *structurally* traced back to Abraham's purification-order.

### 1.4 Root `sEy` — saʿy

The root primarily denotes "striving, running, pursuing one's business" (17:19, 18:104, 79:35, 92:4). In ritual sense it appears **only once** as the pilgrimage action between Ṣafā and Marwa — and even there obliquely: 2:158 uses *yaṭṭawwafa* (from `Twf`) rather than *yasʿā* for the act itself. The *technical* word "saʿy" for the Ṣafā-Marwa run is **post-Quranic, a hadith-fiqh development**. The Quran only says "no blame in going around them." This is a striking data-point: the famous ritual *saʿy* is named in the Quran by the Twf-verb, not the sEy-verb.

### 1.5 Root `HSr` — iḥṣār, being prevented

- `>uHoSiro` 2:196 — "if you are prevented (besieged)" — the *muḥṣar* provision that permits substitute offering and exit from iḥrām. The same root governs 48:25 (Ḥudaybiyya: the sacrifice "prevented from reaching its place") and 9:5 (the polytheists' "besieging"). Thus `HSr` in ritual contexts is bound to **Ḥudaybiyya's blocked pilgrimage** — the historical trigger that generated the *muḥṣar* rule.

### 1.6 Root `jnH` — junāḥ / blame

`junāḥ` occurs 25+ times in the Quran, nearly always in the negative *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ* ("no blame upon you") formula. Four of these appearances are **ritual-pilgrimage** contexts: 2:158 (Ṣafā-Marwa), 2:198 (seeking bounty during Ḥajj), 2:203 (hastening or delaying departure from Minā), 5:93 (formerly-prohibited foods for ritual participants). The remaining occurrences are domestic law (marriage, inheritance, privacy: Q 2:229–240, 4:23–24, 24:58–61, 33:5). The pilgrimage-junāḥ cluster is thus a *sub-family* within a widely-used Medinan civil-law formula. See §4.

### 1.7 Root `hdy` — hady (sacrificial animal)

Same tri-literal root as *hidāya* (guidance), but in the specific
sub-lemma `hadoy` = "that which is led forward, brought as
offering." Occurrences:
- **2:196** ×3 — opens and closes the iḥṣār / tamattuʿ rules
- **5:2** — don't violate the sacrificial marking
- **5:95** — ransom for breach of iḥrām hunting
- **5:97** — Kaʿba + hady + qalāʾid as signs of God
- **48:25** — Ḥudaybiyya: "the hady was prevented from reaching its *maḥill*"

Hady appears only **seven times**, all in contexts of obstruction, substitution, or marking. The Quranic hady is always *directed* — it is an animal being *led* to the House. The same semantic root that elsewhere means "guidance" here names the *animal being guided to the sanctuary*: a hidden cognate echo — the pilgrim is guided, and the pilgrim's offering is itself a "guided one."

### 1.8 Proper-noun stations (see also `sacred-geography.md` §1)

| al-Ṣafā | الصفا | 1 | 2:158 |

**The naming pattern.** Every pilgrimage station that has a proper name appears *exactly once* (ʿArafāt, Mashʿar, Ṣafā, Marwa) — except al-Bayt (many), al-Kaʿba (2), al-Bayt al-Ḥarām (2), al-Bayt al-ʿAtīq (2), Maqām Ibrāhīm (2), al-Masjid al-Ḥarām (15). The **central node** (the House) is repeatedly named; the **peripheral stations** are named exactly once. Minā and Muzdalifa — two of the most ritually important locations — are *never* named by proper noun. This is a radically centered geography: the House is everywhere, the stations are each a hapax, and two whole stations are nameless.

### 1.9 Other cultic terms

- **iḥrām** (the sacralized state): root `Hrm`; the verbal form `Huromun` appears at 5:1 and 5:95, 5:96. The *state* is never named by a nominal *iḥrām* in the Quran — only by the adjective "you being *ḥurum*" (sacralized). The technical term *iḥrām* is, like *saʿy*, post-Quranic.
- **shaʿāʾir Allāh** (signs/rites of God): 2:158, 5:2, 22:32, 22:36. Generic name for the sacralized objects of pilgrimage (the stations themselves; the sacrificial animals).
- **rafath, fusūq, jidāl** (Q 2:197): the three prohibitions during Ḥajj — sexual conduct, transgression, quarreling — nowhere else grouped this way.
- **al-ashhur al-ḥurum** (the sacred months): 2:194, 2:217, 5:2, 5:97, 9:5, 9:36.

## 2. Sūrat al-Ḥajj (Q 22) — the named surah with almost no mechanics

Q 22 is the only surah named *Ḥajj*, yet it contains almost none of the legislative-ritual detail present in Q 2:196–203. Its 78 verses fall into several blocks, only one of which is ḥajj-specific:

- **22:1–24:** the Hour, the Resurrection, disputation about God, reward/punishment — a cosmological-eschatological opening.
- **22:25:** the bridge verse — those who obstruct "the path of Allah and al-Masjid al-Ḥarām which We made for the people, equal for the resident and the visitor" — introduces the sanctuary.
- **22:26–33:** the pilgrimage pericope — Abraham's commission, the proclamation of Ḥajj, ṭawāf, sacrifice, shaʿāʾir.
- **22:34–37:** continuation on *manāsik* (rites) and offerings: "for every nation We have appointed a *mansak*," the "camels we appointed among the shaʿāʾir," "their meat reaches not God, nor their blood, but the piety reaches Him" (22:37 — the theological climax).
- **22:38–78:** permission to fight (the Jihād verse 22:39, often held to be the first fighting-permission), victims' theodicy, lists of denied prophets, creation signs, prostration imperative, final call to *strive in God* and to "the religion of your father Abraham" (22:78).

The ḥajj-specific block (26–37) is only 12 verses out of 78 (~15%). **What the surah contains about Ḥajj is primarily theological, not procedural.** It says:

- *Who* established it (Abraham: 22:26);
- *Why* people come (to witness benefits, to mention God's name over offerings: 22:28);
- *What* they do (circumambulate the Ancient House; fulfill their vows; remove their untidiness: 22:29);
- *How* to regard the sacrifices (they are signs; piety reaches God, not meat/blood: 22:37);
- *The principle* of sanctity (whoever honors the sacred ordinances, the shaʿāʾir, does well: 22:30, 32).

But it does not tell you *when* the Ḥajj is, *where* to stand, *what* the sequence is, *how long* it lasts, *what* not to do. Q 2:196–203 supplies those.

**Why is the surah named Ḥajj?** Three likely reasons:
1. **The Abrahamic proclamation.** 22:27 contains the *cosmic call*: *wa-adhdhin fī l-nāsi bi-l-Haj~i* — "proclaim the Ḥajj to all people." This is the *foundational moment* of the rite. The surah bears the name of what Abraham was commanded to announce.
2. **The theology of sacrifice.** 22:36–37 contains the Quran's sharpest statement on the spiritual meaning of the Ḥajj offerings: the animals themselves are *shaʿāʾir* (signs), not mechanical efficacy — piety (*taqwā*) is what reaches God. This is the *hermeneutic center* of what Ḥajj *means*, as opposed to the *legal specifics* of what it *requires*.
3. **The surah's universality.** Unlike Q 2:196–203, which addresses a concrete pilgrim community with concrete rules, Q 22 addresses *al-nās* — humanity as such. Abraham's proclamation is universal. The surah's name gestures at the archetype.

Classical exegesis (al-Rāzī, *Tafsīr al-Kabīr* ad 22:1; al-Zamakhsharī *Kashshāf*) notes this precise asymmetry: Sūrat al-Ḥajj is the *theology* of pilgrimage, Sūrat al-Baqara's 196–203 pericope is the *law*.

## 3. Q 2:196–203 — the legal corpus

This 8-verse block is the single most legislatively dense pilgrimage passage in the Quran. Verse by verse:

- **2:196** — **The mother-verse.** "Complete the Ḥajj and ʿumra for Allah. If you are *prevented* (*uḥṣirtum*), then what is easy of the hady-offering; do not shave your heads until the hady reaches its place. Whoever is ill or has a head-ailment: a ransom of fasting, charity, or offering. When you are secure, whoever enjoys ʿumra-to-Ḥajj (*tamattuʿ*): what is easy of the hady. Whoever cannot find it: three days' fast during Ḥajj and seven after return — ten complete. This is for him whose family is not at al-Masjid al-Ḥarām." Contains: (i) the intention/completion command; (ii) the iḥṣār provision; (iii) the head-shaving rule; (iv) the sick-person ransom triad; (v) the *mutamattiʿ* rules; (vi) the fasting substitute; (vii) a residency exclusion.

- **2:197** — **The ethical frame.** *al-Ḥajju ashhurun maʿlūmāt* — "Ḥajj is well-known months." Prohibits *rafath, fusūq, jidāl* (sex, sin, dispute) during Ḥajj. Commands provision-taking: "and the best provision is *taqwā*." This verse turns the ritual into an ethical discipline: sanctity is behavioral, not just spatial.

- **2:198** — **The bounty-dispensation and the stations.** *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an tabtaghū faḍlan min rabbikum* — "no blame upon you for seeking bounty from your Lord" (commerce during pilgrimage is permitted). Then: "when you pour forth (*afaḍtum*) from ʿArafāt, remember Allah at al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām." The verse names **both ʿArafāt and the Mashʿar al-Ḥarām** — each a Quranic hapax. It is the *only* verse in the entire Quran that names these stations.

- **2:199** — "Then pour forth from where the people pour forth, and seek Allah's forgiveness." This is a correction-verse: classical exegesis (Ṭabarī ad loc.) holds that Quraysh had privileged *ḥums* standing at Muzdalifa and not going down to ʿArafāt; the verse commands them to pour forth *from where the people pour forth* — egalitarianizing the pilgrimage.

- **2:200** — "When you complete your rites (*manāsik*), remember Allah as you remember your fathers — or more intensely." Condemns those whose prayer is only for *dunyā*.

- **2:201** — The famous *rabbanā ātinā fī l-dunyā ḥasana* prayer: "Our Lord, give us good in this world and good in the hereafter" — the *pilgrimage-prayer archetype*.

- **2:202** — "Those will have a share of what they have earned. Allah is swift in account."

- **2:203** — **The departure rule.** "Remember Allah during numbered days (*ayyām maʿdūdāt*). Whoever hastens in two — no sin; whoever delays — no sin: for the one who fears Allah." This codifies the *ayyām al-tashrīq* at Minā without naming Minā.

### 3.1 The pericope's structural logic

The block moves from (a) the **macro** legal frame (complete the pilgrimage, 196–197) through (b) the **stations** and their prayers (198–200) to (c) the **prayer itself** (201–202) to (d) the **exit rule** (203). It is a descending funnel from law to prayer to departure.

The two named stations (ʿArafāt, Mashʿar al-Ḥarām) occur in a single verse (198), surrounded by formulae of "no blame." This structural placement makes 198 the *geographic hinge* of the entire legal pericope.

## 4. The *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ* formula

Q 2:198 opens with the most distinctive legal formula of Medinan ritual law: *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an tabtaghū faḍlan min rabbikum* — "There is no blame upon you for seeking bounty from your Lord (during the Ḥajj)." The formula is a *permissive clause* in a register of prohibitions; it appears in the Quran more than 20 times, always in the same syntactic shape: (negation of sin) + (subjunctive clause naming the permitted act).

### 4.1 Ritual-pilgrimage junāḥ-verses

Four verses position the formula in the cultic domain:
- **2:158** *fa-lā junāḥa ʿalayhi an yaṭṭawwafa bihimā* — no blame for going around Ṣafā and Marwa. *Reason:* pre-Islamic idols had stood on these hills; some believers hesitated. The formula resolves the hesitation.
- **2:198** *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥun an tabtaghū faḍlan* — no blame for trading during Ḥajj.
- **2:203** ×2 *fa-lā ithma ʿalayh* — no sin for hastening or delaying departure from Minā. (Uses *ithm* rather than *junāḥ*, a close synonym in this register.)
- **5:93** *laysa ʿalā l-ladhīna āmanū … junāḥun fī-mā ṭaʿimū* — no blame on the believers for what they used to eat (before prohibition).

### 4.2 Why the formula matters

The formula is a *dispensation idiom*. Its function is psychological and halakhic simultaneously: it **reassures communities carrying pre-Islamic anxieties** that their ritual practices can continue, reinterpreted. The *saʿy* between Ṣafā and Marwa had pagan overtones (idols); commerce during Ḥajj seemed profaning; hastening or delaying at Minā were both felt to need sanction. The Quran says: *no blame*. This is the voice of *permission into an existing practice*, not invention.

Contrast this with the *ḥurrimat ʿalaykum* ("made unlawful to you") formula that legislates prohibitions. Where *ḥurrimat* inaugurates, *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ* confirms. The pilgrimage pericope mixes both: prohibitions (rafath, fusūq, jidāl; hunting in iḥrām) alongside explicit permissions (trade; saʿy; variable departure). The result is a ritual framework that preserves Arab pilgrimage-custom while monotheizing its referents.

### 4.3 The formula's Quranic reach

Beyond the pilgrimage cluster, *junāḥ* attaches to: marriage negotiations (2:235, 4:24), divorce (2:229, 2:230, 2:236, 2:240), widow-remarriage (2:234), orphan care (4:128), shortening prayer in danger (4:101), stepchildren (33:5), privacy rules (24:58, 24:60, 24:61). The formula is therefore the *Medinan civil-dispensation voice* par excellence. Its appearance in the Ḥajj passage is not unusual — it is the same legislative register that runs through Q 2 and Q 24.

What *is* unusual is how the formula clusters at precisely the *named-station* moments: Ṣafā/Marwa and ʿArafāt/Mashʿar. The most Arabia-specific ritual locations receive the permission-voice. This is one more evidence that Ḥajj in the Quran is reformed-continuation, not new-founding.

## 5. Historical context — Ḥudaybiyya and the Farewell Pilgrimage

### 5.1 Ḥudaybiyya (Q 48)

Sūrat al-Fatḥ narrates the 6 AH expedition: the Prophet and ~1400 Companions set out from Medina for *ʿumra*, were blocked at Ḥudaybiyya, and — after negotiation — signed a 10-year treaty including the provision that the Muslims would return the next year for ʿumra but not enter Mecca in 6 AH. Key verses:

- **48:10** — *bayʿat al-riḍwān* under the tree: the pledge of those willing to die.
- **48:18** — Allah's pleasure with the tree-pledge.
- **48:24** — *wa-huwa l-ladhī kaffa aydiyahum ʿankum … bi-baṭn makka* — "He withheld their hands from you within Mecca" (the *only* occurrence of the name *Makka* in the Quran — see `sacred-geography.md` §5).
- **48:25** — *hum alladhīna kafarū wa-ṣaddūkum ʿan al-masjid al-ḥarām wa-l-hadya maʿkūfan an yabluġha maḥillahu* — "they are those who disbelieved and obstructed you from the Sacred Mosque and the hady, held back from reaching its place of sacrifice." This verse is the *exegetical root* of the *iḥṣār* (prevention) rule at 2:196. Ḥudaybiyya is the **historical event** that generated the **legal provision**.
- **48:27** — *la-tadkhulunna l-masjid al-ḥarām inshāʾa llāh āminīna muḥalliqīna ruʾūsakum wa-muqaṣṣirīn* — "you will certainly enter the Sacred Mosque, if Allah wills, in safety, heads shaved or shortened, fearing nothing." Refers to ʿumrat al-qaḍāʾ of 7 AH and looks further to the Conquest.

Ḥudaybiyya produced three legal outcomes embedded in the Quran:
1. *muḥṣar* substitute-offering (2:196 + 48:25);
2. permitted head-shaving outside of Mecca (48:27);
3. the theological lesson that *being prevented* from the sanctuary is itself a trial of faith.

### 5.2 The Farewell Pilgrimage and Q 5:3

Classical tradition places the revelation of Q 5:3b — *al-yawma akmaltu lakum dīnakum wa-atmamtu ʿalaykum niʿmatī wa-raḍītu lakum al-islāma dīnan* — at ʿArafāt during the 10 AH Ḥajj. It is the **completion-of-religion verse**. Its placement within Sūrat al-Māʾida means the pilgrimage-law surah (al-Māʾida has extensive iḥrām and hady rules in 5:1–2, 5:94–97) terminates at the moment of completion.

Three observations:
1. **Q 5:3's first half** is the major kosher-law prohibition list. The *completion* clause is embedded *mid-verse* after a prohibition and before an exception. This extraordinary placement — the most theologically weighty sentence in the Quran, sandwiched between dietary law — mirrors the logic of ritual law as the crown of the covenant.
2. **Q 5:1–2 and 5:94–97** form a chiastic ring around the rest of al-Māʾida's legislation. The surah opens with pilgrimage law (*awfū bi-l-ʿuqūd … ghayra muḥillī l-ṣayd wa-antum ḥurum*, 5:1; *lā tuḥillū shaʿāʾir allāh*, 5:2) and closes its primary legal block with pilgrimage law (*lā taqtulū l-ṣayda wa-antum ḥurum*, 5:95; *uḥilla lakum ṣaydu l-baḥr*, 5:96; *jaʿala llāhu l-kaʿbata l-bayta l-ḥarāma qiyāman li-l-nās*, 5:97). Pilgrimage law is the **structural frame** of al-Māʾida — as Ḥudaybiyya-then-Conquest is the frame of al-Fatḥ.
3. **Q 5:97** — *jaʿala llāhu l-kaʿbata l-bayta l-ḥarāma qiyāman li-l-nās* — "Allah made the Kaʿba, the Sacred House, a pillar (qiyām) for humankind." The verb *jaʿala* places the Kaʿba in the list of divinely-instituted universal realities (night, day, sun, moon, pairs), elevating the sanctuary from Arabian local shrine to cosmic *qiyām*.

### 5.3 Ḥajj and the post-Conquest order (Q 9)

The Ḥudaybiyya treaty was abrogated by the 8 AH Conquest of Mecca, and Q 9:1–28 sets the new pilgrimage regime:
- **9:3** names the *yawm al-Ḥajj al-akbar* — "day of the greatest pilgrimage" — as the day of proclamation of disavowal from the polytheists.
- **9:19** critiques those who thought providing water to pilgrims (*siqāyat al-ḥājj*) and maintaining the Sacred Mosque (*ʿimārat al-masjid al-ḥarām*) equaled believing and striving.
- **9:28** — "the polytheists are *najas*, so let them not approach the Sacred Mosque after this year of theirs" — the verse that closed the Kaʿba to non-Muslims from 9 AH onward.
- **9:36** — "the number of months with Allah is twelve, of which four are sacred" — locks the lunar calendar and its sacred months.
- **9:37** — *al-nasīʾ* (intercalation) is *an increase in disbelief*; abolishes the pre-Islamic practice of shifting sacred months.

Thus the Quranic pilgrimage legislation unfolds in three historical layers:
- **Pre-Hijra / early-Medinan:** the basic permission to face and honor the House (Q 2:144, 2:158).
- **Mid-Medinan:** the core legal pericope (Q 2:196–203) and the Abrahamic theology (Q 2:124–129; Q 22:26–37) — mostly 2–6 AH.
- **Late-Medinan:** Ḥudaybiyya (Q 48, 6 AH), al-Māʾida's completion (Q 5:3, 10 AH), and the post-Conquest purge (Q 9, 9 AH).

Ḥajj theology in the Quran therefore tracks a *historical arc*: from exile-anchor-point (the qibla turn, Q 2:144), through restricted-access (Ḥudaybiyya), through reform of the rite (Q 2/Q 22), to exclusive sanctuary (Q 9), to completion (Q 5:3).

## 6. The Abrahamic origin of Ḥajj

### 6.1 Q 2:124–129 — the foundation pericope

These six verses in Sūrat al-Baqara construct the **origin story** of the Ḥajj sanctuary:

- **2:124:** Abraham is tried with *kalimāt* (commands/words) and fulfills them. God makes him *imām li-l-nās*.
- **2:125:** The House is made *mathābatan li-l-nās wa-amnan* ("a recurring meeting place and a sanctuary"). Maqām Ibrāhīm is designated a prayer-place. Abraham and Ishmael receive the *ṭahhirā baytī* command — purify My House for the ṭāʾifīn, the ʿākifīn, and the rukkaʿ sujjūd.
- **2:126:** Abraham prays for the land's security and provision.
- **2:127:** Abraham and Ishmael *raise the foundations* of the House.
- **2:128:** Their prayer: *rabbanā wa-jʿalnā muslimayni laka wa-min dhurriyyatinā ummatan muslimatan laka wa-arinā manāsikanā* — "show us our rites (*manāsik*)." The verb *arinā* is a revelation-request: Abraham asks God to *show* the proper rite. This makes the Ḥajj ritually transmitted, not invented.
- **2:129:** Their prayer for a messenger from among the descendants who will recite, teach the Book, and purify — read in tradition as Muhammad's prefiguration.

### 6.2 Q 22:26–27 — the proclamation

- **22:26:** *idh bawwa'nā li-Ibrāhīma makāna l-bayt an lā tushrik bī shay'an wa-ṭahhir baytiya li-l-ṭā'ifīn* — "when We designated for Abraham the site of the House: 'Associate nothing with Me, and purify My House for the circumambulators, the upright, the bowing, the prostrating.'"
- **22:27:** *wa-adhdhin fī l-nāsi bi-l-Haj~i* — "proclaim the Ḥajj to all people; they will come to you on foot and on every lean camel, coming from every deep pass." This is the **inaugural imperative**, a prophetic *adhān* issued through Abraham to the whole earth. In classical homiletic (al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabarī ad loc.), every pilgrim who comes is responding to that primordial call.

### 6.3 Q 14:37 — Abraham's settling of Ishmael and Hagar

*rabbanā innī askantu min dhurriyyatī bi-wādin ghayri dhī zarʿin ʿinda baytika l-muḥarrami* — "Our Lord, I have settled some of my descendants in an uncultivated valley by Your sacred House." This verse locates the Abrahamic settlement at Mecca (implied: *ʿinda baytika*) and grounds the Hagar-Ishmael pericope that classical tradition attaches to the Zamzam well and the Ṣafā-Marwa running.

### 6.4 The cumulative picture

Five elements of the Ḥajj are Abrahamicly grounded in the text:
1. **The House itself** — raised by Abraham and Ishmael (2:127; 22:26).
2. **The proclamation** — commanded to Abraham (22:27).
3. **Purification** (*ṭahhir baytī*) — spoken to Abraham twice (2:125, 22:26).
4. **The *manāsik*** — "show us our rites" (2:128) — the ritual content itself is *received*, not authored.
5. **The valley-and-House** topography — Abraham's settling of the family (14:37) grounds Ṣafā-Marwa and Zamzam in the received tradition, though the Quran does not expound these.

No pre-Abrahamic origin is given (Adam-stories about the House are extra-Quranic). No *post*-Abrahamic innovation is introduced. Muhammad's role in the Ḥajj is therefore *restoration* — the removal of the 360 idols the Quraysh had accumulated, the end of *nasīʾ*, the closing of the sanctuary to polytheists. The ritual itself is Abrahamic.

## 7. Pilgrimage-month timing

Q 2:197 opens with *al-Ḥajju ashhurun maʿlūmāt* — "Ḥajj is [during] well-known months." The plural *ashhur* is grammatically a *jamʿ qillah* (plural of three to ten), taken by classical fiqh as designating Shawwāl, Dhū al-Qaʿda, and the first ten of Dhū al-Ḥijja.

Related verses:
- **2:189** — *yas'alūnaka ʿan al-ahillati qul hiya mawāqītu li-l-nāsi wa-l-Ḥajj* — "they ask about the new moons: say, 'they are measurements of time for people and for Ḥajj.'" This places the crescent observation in direct service of pilgrimage timing, linking lunar astronomy to ritual.
- **9:36** — *inna ʿiddata l-shuhūri ʿinda llāhi ithnā ʿashara shahran … minhā arbaʿatun ḥurum* — "the count of months with Allah is twelve, of which four are sacred." The four are classically Dhū al-Qaʿda, Dhū al-Ḥijja, Muḥarram, Rajab — three contiguous plus one isolated. Ḥajj occurs within the three contiguous sacred months.
- **9:37** — *al-nasīʾ ziyādatun fī l-kufr* — "postponement is an increase in disbelief." Abolishes the pre-Islamic intercalation by which priests would shift a sacred month forward to allow warfare or desired seasons. **This verse locks the Ḥajj to the true lunar calendar.**

The result: Ḥajj cycles through the solar year (~11 days earlier each year), is never tied to a fixed solar season, and is therefore universal in climate-conditions. The Abrahamic "from every deep pass" (22:27) assumes pilgrims drawn from every direction; the un-intercalated lunar year ensures no region perennially faces the hardest-climate arrival.

### 7.1 The calendrical theology

The pairing of 2:189 (*"crescents are for Ḥajj"*) with 9:36–37 (*"twelve months, four sacred, no intercalation"*) constructs a theology in which:
- **astronomical observation** (the crescent);
- **calendrical fixity** (twelve lunar months);
- **ritual anchoring** (Ḥajj months);
- **moral injunction** (no nasīʾ manipulation)

form an interlocking system. The Ḥajj is *when the moon says it is*, and that is ultimately *when God says it is*. Pilgrims converging from "every deep pass" converge not by solar season but by shared submission to the lunar sign. This is perhaps the strongest Quranic argument for the lunar calendar's universality.

### 7.2 *Yawm al-Ḥajj al-akbar* (Q 9:3)

The "day of the greater pilgrimage" is traditionally the 10th of Dhū al-Ḥijja — the Day of Sacrifice (*yawm al-naḥr*). The epithet *al-akbar* implies a *lesser* pilgrimage (*al-aṣghar*), taken as ʿumra. The contrast ḥajj-akbar / ḥajj-aṣghar emerges from this verse; it is not elsewhere made explicit.

## 8. Synthesis

Quranic Ḥajj theology rests on four structural pillars:

1. **Abrahamic precedence.** Ḥajj is not new — it is *restored*. Abraham received the commission (22:26–27), built the House (2:127), asked to be shown the rites (2:128), and proclaimed the pilgrimage to humanity. Muhammad's role is purification of a sanctuary long profaned.
2. **Historical consolidation.** The legal pericope (Q 2:196–203), the Abrahamic reframe (Q 22:26–37), and the calendar-purge (Q 9:36–37) unfold across the Medinan decade, responding to three concrete events: the qibla turn, Ḥudaybiyya, and the Conquest.
3. **Named and unnamed stations.** ʿArafāt, al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām, al-Ṣafā, al-Marwa each appear exactly once. Minā and Muzdalifa are never named. The House is named ~15+ times. The geography is theologically asymmetric: peripheral stations are marked by *uniqueness*, the center by *abundance*.
4. **Permission-register.** The *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ* formula domesticates pre-Islamic ritual anxieties. Saʿy is permitted, trading is permitted, hastening and delaying are permitted. The voice is not inventor-of-law but reformer-of-received-practice.

The theological climax is Q 22:37: *lan yanāla llāha luḥūmuhā wa-lā dimāuhā wa-lākin yanāluhu l-taqwā minkum* — "Neither their meat nor their blood reaches Allah; it is piety from you that reaches Him." The Quran's pilgrimage is a ritual framework whose highest yield is interior. The outer geography (House, stations, months, offerings) is in the service of the inner disposition (taqwā). That verse, positioned at the heart of the surah named for the pilgrimage, is the Quran's own gloss on its own ritual.

## 9. Open questions

- Why are Minā and Muzdalifa never named when ʿArafāt and Mashʿar are named? (Hypothesis: the two un-named sites were continuous pagan ritual-spaces whose names carried too much residue, whereas the named pair had been attached to idolatrous tribes that the Quran chooses to *rename-by-monotheizing* rather than elide.)
- Why is the **technical term *saʿy*** never used in the Quran for Ṣafā/Marwa, only the Twf-verb? (Hypothesis: `sEy` in Quranic diction is eschatological/moral — "striving for the Hereafter" — and has been reserved for that register; ritual circumambulation is kept under `Twf`.)
- The asymmetry between Q 22 (theology, universal audience) and Q 2:196–203 (law, community audience) — is this a genre-encoded distinction native to the Quran's self-differentiation between *dhikr* and *ḥukm*?
- The embedded *al-yawma akmaltu* clause at 5:3 — is its placement *between* prohibition and exception a deliberate structural signal that *completion of religion* occurs precisely at the threshold of permission/prohibition? If so, that reads the Ḥajj as the emblem of the completed law.
