# [[h-new-44-muqattaat-combinatorial-closure|H-NEW-44]] — Muqaṭṭaʿāt Combinatorial Closure

## Fixed fact

29 surahs open with *muqaṭṭaʿāt* (disconnected letters). The distinct opening multisets form exactly **14 unique patterns** drawn from a pool of exactly **14 letters** = {أ, ح, ر, س, ص, ط, ع, ق, ك, ل, م, ن, ه, ي}. That's half the 28-letter alphabet. This is the universally agreed count (al-Zarkashī, al-Suyūṭī, modern Nöldeke/Welch).

The 14 subsets, with their cardinalities:
- Size 1: {ص}, {ق}, {ن} — 3 singletons
- Size 2: {ط,ه}, {ي,س}, {ط,س}, {ح,م} — 4 pairs
- Size 3: {أ,ل,م}, {أ,ل,ر}, {ط,س,م} — 3 triples
- Size 4: {أ,ل,م,ص}, {أ,ل,م,ر} — 2 quadruples
- Size 5: {ك,ه,ي,ع,ص}, {ح,م,ع,س,ق} — 2 quintuples

Total = 14 distinct sets covering 29 surahs (some sets open multiple surahs).

## Question

Let F = {S₁, ..., S₁₄} be the family of 14 opening subsets. The three questions:

**Q1 (CLOSURE)**: Is F closed under some simple combinatorial operation (union, intersection, symmetric difference, deletion) relative to a parent alphabet? Specifically:
- Are all 14 subsets of a common 14-letter universe? **Yes, by construction.**
- Is F closed under intersection? (if A,B∈F then A∩B∈F)
- Is F an antichain? (no S_i⊂S_j)
- Does F form a matroid or simplicial complex?

**Q2 (INFORMATION-CONTENT)**: Treating the 14-letter universe as bits, each subset is a 14-bit vector. Compute the rank of the 14×14 incidence matrix (rows = subsets, columns = letters). If rank < 14 there is linear dependence — meaning some subsets are derivable from others. Also compute Boolean rank (minimum #subsets whose unions generate F).

**Q3 (ENRICHMENT-VS-RANDOM)**: Under a null where 14 subsets of size-distribution [3,4,3,2,2] (matching observed cardinalities) are drawn uniformly from the 14-letter powerset, how many closure properties of F are expected by chance vs observed? Bonferroni k=6 properties × uniform-null.

## Null model

10,000 uniform-random samples of 14 subsets from 2^14 = 16,384 possible subsets, conditioned on cardinality distribution [3,4,3,2,2]. For each sample, check:

(a) Is the family an antichain?
(b) Is it intersection-closed?
(c) Is the incidence-matrix rank full (14)?
(d) Is there a common parent 14-letter universe (always true for the Quran; we check how often a uniform sample has union = exactly 14 letters)
(e) Do all size-k subsets form an antichain among themselves (automatic) — not interesting
(f) Does the poset have width = 14 (all incomparable)?

Observed (Quran) vs null (random). α = 0.05 / 6 = 0.0083 per property.

## Positive control (MW-5)

Construct a known-non-random family: 14 subsets generated by closing {singletons of ص, ق, ن} under adding pairs. This family has KNOWN closure properties. Verify the pipeline recovers them at α=0.0083. If not, pipeline broken → NULL-BROKEN.

## Pre-committed verdict table

| Outcome | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ≥3 of 6 properties significant at α=0.0083 | STRONG-PASS — F has non-random combinatorial structure |
| 1-2 properties significant | EXPLORATORY-hit |
| 0 properties significant | NULL — F is combinatorially generic conditional on cardinality |
| Positive control fails | NULL-BROKEN |

## Second layer (pre-registered, one-shot)

**Letter-frequency correlation:** For the 14 letters used in muqaṭṭaʿāt, do their Quran-wide frequencies in non-opening text show a distinctive pattern (vs the 14 NOT used)? Observed fact from prior scholarship (Nöldeke, Welch): the 14 used letters cover the most-frequent Arabic letters. Test: Spearman ρ between "is-muqaṭṭaʿa-letter" indicator and "Quran-wide letter frequency rank" — compare to binomial expectation.

## Mechanism interpretation

- STRONG-PASS + full rank: muqaṭṭaʿāt subsets are combinatorially "engineered" — a non-obvious signal about the 14-selection mechanism.
- STRONG-PASS + rank-deficient: some subsets ARE derivable from others, hinting at an internal generative rule.
- NULL: the observed 14 subsets are cardinality-distribution-generic; the muqaṭṭaʿāt mystery is not combinatorial but semantic/phonetic.
