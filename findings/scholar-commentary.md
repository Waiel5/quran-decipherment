# The Quran Decipherment Project — Scholar's Commentary

*Synthesis author: the project's synthesis scholar. Drafted 2026-04-12 during Phase B. Cross-referenced to the live inventory in [`docs/master-index.md`](../docs/master-index.md).*

---

## Preface

This document is the running commentary for a project that set out to do something embarrassingly simple and — it turns out — almost entirely undone: read the Qur'ān as one text of 114 *suwar* and 6,236 *āyāt*, count what is actually there under disclosed rules, and compare the result to what the literature claims is there. The project is not a refutation of the Quran or an apology for it. It is not an "edition" study; it treats the Quran as a single work whose small presentation differences (tashkeel variants, scribal conventions, verse-numbering traditions) are rendering choices to be made explicit, not separate books. And it is not an attempt to uncover a "hidden code". The working posture is the posture of a McKay after the Bible Codes: the right question is never "is there a pattern?" but "does this particular pattern survive a pre-specified test that could in principle have failed?"

Work proceeds in three phases. **Phase A** replicates the entire published catalogue of Quranic numerological and structural claims — every Khalifa Code-19 entry, every Al-Kaheel / Nawfal word-pair parity, every Farrin or Cuypers ring — against the canonical text under the rule tuples specified in [`docs/methodology.md`](../docs/methodology.md). **Phase B** hunts for novel statistical structure (gematric distributions, palindromes, *jinās* clustering, chronological drift, information-theoretic signatures), with every novel finding required to clear at least two null models from different branches of the [statistical-rigor protocol](../docs/statistical-rigor-protocol.md). **Phase C** maps the wording *schema* itself — ring composition, root cartography, surah fingerprints — where the question is no longer "does the number match?" but "does the text hang together in a structurally describable way?"

The invariants are few and inflexible. The Quran is one text. Every numerical claim carries a rules tuple. Every novel finding passes at least two nulls. Every claim records its prior-art search honestly. **Failures are reported with the same prominence as successes.** This last invariant is the project's moral spine: in a field where the loudest voices have historically published only the numbers that worked, an honest ledger of what does *not* work is itself a scholarly contribution.

A word about the novelty-versus-replication-versus-honest-negative triangle. Much of the best work in this project is negative: Khalifa's specific ALM letter counts do not reproduce; Al-Kaheel's bahr/barr parity does not reproduce; Cuypers's Al-Ma'ida ring does not reproduce lexically; Farrin's macro-ring of 114 surahs is anti-correlated. But the project is not a debunking exercise. Several replications *do* survive (*raḥmān* = 57, *qaf* in Surah 50 = 57, the verse-index wasat coincidence at Q 2:143, the Medinan verse-length climb, the muqaṭṭaʿāt letter-density anomaly). And a small but real set of novel findings has cleared the statistical bar — the *raḥma*-114 lemma anchor, the Ash-Shams seven-verse palindrome, the one-verse chiastic root palindrome at Q 13:28, the Bonferroni-surviving ring at Al-Baqarah 131–144, the Yusuf / Kahf surah-fingerprint coincidences, the post-Hijra monopoly on the proper name *Muḥammad*. The project's scholarly character depends on keeping these three classes — negative, replicated, novel-confirmed — visible at the same time. A reader who takes away only the negatives has missed the signal; a reader who takes away only the positives has missed the discipline that licensed them.

## Part I — Foundations

Before any analysis could be credited, the raw text had to be pinned down. The starting dataset was `amrayn/quran-text`: seven files purporting to encode the same 114-surah, 6,236-verse canonical Hafs Qur'ān in structured JSON and flat UTF-8, with and without diacritics. The first finding of the project was unwelcome. The three flat-text files — the ones a counting script would reach for by default — disagreed on whitespace-tokenised word count by roughly 18,000 tokens. Because tashkeel cannot affect whitespace counts, something else had to be wrong. A focused investigation ([journal/text-shape-investigation.md](../journal/text-shape-investigation.md)) tracked the discrepancy to its origin: two of the three flat files were `GROUP_CONCAT` dumps from MySQL that had silently hit the server's 1 MiB string-length ceiling and been truncated mid-verse (`quran-flat-min-tashkeel.txt` at Q 61:5, `quran-flat-full-tashkeel.txt` at Q 40:40), each beginning with a literal SQL header line. Only `quran-flat-no-tashkeel.txt` was intact, and even it was byte-equal to its JSON sibling rather than an independent source. The three JSON files were intact; the flat truncations were data-distribution artefacts, not textual variants.

This is the methodological warning the project needed to begin with. Had we trusted the flat files, every letter count and word count in every downstream analysis would have been silently wrong by surah 40-onwards. The lesson is embedded in the master index as a permanent convention: the primary corpus is `quran-no-tashkeel.json`; the full-tashkeel and min-tashkeel JSONs are used only when shadda-sensitivity or source-paper orthography requires; the flat files are deprecated. Every counting tool in `analysis/tools/` is pinned to a set of **locked anchor values** in [`docs/methodology.md §8`](../docs/methodology.md): 114 surahs, 6,236 verses, 77,797 real-word tokens, 330,709 letter graphemes, 19 letters / 4 words in the *basmala*, and the exact adjustments that convert between the three basmala-policy conventions (`counted-only-in-surah-1`, `counted-in-surah`, `always-separator`). A tool that cannot reproduce these values is broken; a claim whose numbers cannot be mapped to these anchors is not yet a claim.

The canonical corpus choice is a choice about what kind of question is being asked. By working from `quran-no-tashkeel.json` and citing morphology from the Quranic Arabic Corpus (Dukes, 2009–2011), we are asking a *lemma-and-grapheme* question, not a *pronunciation* question. Claims that only resolve under full-tashkeel orthography — notably Khalifa's ALM letter counts, which sit between our no- and full-tashkeel counts in a way no single scribal tradition recognises — are flagged as orthography-dependent and reported under every variant. The `rules` tuple on every finding makes this traceable. One tuple, one text, one reproducible number.

## Part II — What the computational audit shows about published claims

### The Khalifa Code-19 family

Rashad Khalifa's 1974–1989 program is the most famous and most assertive numerological claim ever made about the Quran. The full audit is in [`findings/phase-a-replications/code19-khalifa-full-audit.md`](phase-a-replications/code19-khalifa-full-audit.md); the short verdict is that five claims survive honest counting and thirteen fail. The survivors are:

1. **Bismillāh = 19 letters.** Trivially true under any reasonable orthography of the canonical phrase. This is the anchor, not a derivation.
2. **114 surahs = 19 × 6.** Arithmetic. Small-integer coincidence.
3. **Surah 96 is the 19th surah from the end.** Arithmetic, derived from 114.
4. **al-Raḥmān = 57 occurrences in the Quran.** Holds cleanly at the QAC lemma level. Consistent with chance for a single hit out of four basmala-word tests.
5. **Qāf appears 57 times in Surah 50 (Qāf) and 57 times in Surah 42 (Ash-Shūrā), so 57 + 57 = 114 = the surah count.** This is the single non-trivial Khalifa survivor and it is striking.

Everything else fails. The most damning failure is also the most diagnostic. Khalifa published exact alif / lām / mīm counts for six ALM-initialled surahs in *Quran: The Final Testament* Appendix 1. For every one of those six, his alif count sits *between* our no-tashkeel and full-tashkeel counts. His lām and mīm counts almost match no-tashkeel. The alif-floating pattern is consistent with one and only one explanation: he was counting some *alif maddah* / *alif khanjariyya* instances and skipping others, inconsistently, per surah, to reach a pre-chosen total. The Quran Talk Blog's defensive response to this discrepancy is openly unfalsifiable — "the totals were divinely revealed to Rashad, the individual numbers were his typos that God corrected as he typed" — and is cited verbatim in the audit as a textbook example of the Bible-Codes-style immunisation move. This is the exact structural failure McKay et al. 1999 diagnosed in Witztum–Rips–Rosenberg: the degree of freedom available in selecting the counting convention is exactly the size of the desired effect, and the convention is never pre-registered.

Two further Khalifa claims — `Allah` = 2,698 and the *grand total* formula equalling 346,199 = 19² × 959 — pass if and only if Q 9:128–129 are deleted from the text. Every extant manuscript (Ṣanʿāʾ, Topkapı, Birmingham, every printed Mushaf) contains those two verses. Khalifa's declaration that they are "later interpolations" is precisely the off-by-one patch the numbers require, and it is accepted by neither Sunni nor Shīʿī nor academic scholarship. Under the canonical text the claims fail.

The single weaker-but-real signal inside the Code-19 family is *not* Khalifa's divisibility-by-19 rule. It is the **muqaṭṭaʿāt density effect**: the letters that open a *ḥurūf muqaṭṭaʿāt* surah are statistically over-represented inside that surah's body, well above the letter's Quran-wide rate. Two independent agents confirmed this under two independent null models (see [`muqattaat-analysis.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/muqattaat-analysis.md) and the audit above). Chi-squared on the 78 (surah, letter) pairs gives 228.78 on 78 degrees of freedom, p < 10⁻¹⁵. Under a 3-gram letter-Markov surrogate that preserves local bigram and trigram statistics, the Stouffer combined Z across the 29 surahs is +4.48, one-tailed p ≈ 3.8 × 10⁻⁶. The signal is not uniform: it is driven predominantly by Surah 50 (qāf, z = +4.68), Surah 2 (alif-lām-mīm, z = +3.43), and Surah 29 (alif-lām-mīm, z = +2.74); several muqaṭṭaʿāt surahs are individually weak or even anti-enriched. And a tempering fact: nine of the fourteen "luminous letters" (*al-ḥurūf al-nūrāniyya*) are among the fourteen most frequent letters of Arabic, which depresses the bar for enrichment. The honest verdict is that classical observers (Welch in the *Encyclopaedia of Islam*, and earlier) were right to notice a phonological echo between a muqaṭṭaʿāt prefix and its host surah; the effect is real; it is not Khalifa's miraculous divisibility rule. Khalifa found one genuine local signal (the qāf in Surah 50) and built a global edifice on top of it that the numbers do not support. The honest McKay analogy is exact: a statistician applying the same standard to Witztum–Rips–Rosenberg's 1994 rabbi paper arrived at the same verdict in 1999. This project is, as far as we can determine, the first formal McKay-style audit of Code-19.

### The Al-Kaheel / Nawfal word-pair symmetry family

The second great body of popular numerology is the claim, associated with Abd al-Dāʾim al-Kaheel (building on Abdul-Razzāq Nawfal's 1959 *al-Iʿjāz al-ʿadadī*), that the Quran is perforated with exact word-count parities: *yawm / layl*, *rajul / imraʾa*, *baḥr / barr*, *ḥayāh / mawt*, *al-dunyā / al-ākhira*, *malāʾika / shayāṭīn*, *Ādam / ʿĪsā*. The full replication is in [`word-pair-symmetry.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/word-pair-symmetry.md) and [`root-cartography.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/root-cartography.md). The verdict is that this tradition is textbook selective counting.

Under QAC lemmas, *yawm* = 405 and *layl* = 92; no natural filter recovers the famous 365/365 pairing. *Baḥr* and *barr* do not produce the 32/13 ratio (actual roots: 42 / 32). *Ḥayāh* = 76 and *mawt* = 50; neither matches the claimed 145. *Al-dunyā* and *al-ākhira* produce no clean 115/115 at any granularity. *Ādam* and *ʿĪsā* do both equal 25, which is true but essentially free: both are proper nouns with zero inflection, and matching-count proper-noun pairs are abundant. *Malāʾika* / *shayāṭīn* is the single clean survivor — lemma `malak` = 88 and lemma `shayṭān` = 88 — and it holds. One replication out of seven.

The crucial methodological contribution here is the **McKay denominator**: the number of root pairs (A, B) in the QAC with identical occurrence counts, at a floor of ≥ 10 occurrences per root, is **2,817**. This is the baseline any claim of "miraculous balance" has to clear. If you are willing to go scanning the Quran for pairs of roots that appear an equal number of times, you will find thousands. The prior art's method is to select the handful that happen to be thematically paired and publish those as "miracles". Our method is to print the whole list and ask why these pairs are more special than the 2,816 others. The answer, in every case we have traced, is that the authors do not know — they never constructed the denominator. The catalog is in the root-cartography file; we commend it to anyone writing a future claim as a mandatory reference.

### The middle-ayah / chiastic / ring-composition family

Ring composition is the most interesting of the traditional claims because, unlike numerology, it has a serious modern scholarly literature (Mir, Zahniser, Cuypers, Farrin, Neuwirth, Sinai) and because some rings are actually there. The master test is in [`chiastic-audit.md`](phase-c-structures/chiastic-audit.md), which computes paired root-set Jaccard scores between the *i*-th and (*N* + 1 – *i*)-th verses of every surah and every contiguous sub-window of length 5 to 15, with 200 within-surah shuffle trials per surah and 50 per sub-window (57,996 sub-windows in total).

Three results stand out. The first is strongly positive. **Al-Baqarah 131–144, the Abraham-to-qibla pericope, is the single strongest ring in the Quran**, with z = +9.69 over a 57,996-window family. This survives Bonferroni at α = 0.05 by a wide margin. The ring was identified by Mathias Zahniser (1991) and Raymond Farrin (2014, ch. 2) on literary grounds; our algorithm rediscovers it without being told where to look. The pairs that drive the z include *slm* (submit), *ḥnf* (incline), *millat*, *dīn*, *rbb*, and the proper names *Ibrāhīm*, *Ismāʿīl*, *Yaʿqūb*. The rhetorical pivot is at v137–v138, at the boundary between Abraham's legacy and the qibla change: *"And thus we have made you a just community* (*ummatan wasaṭan*) *that you will be witnesses over the people"* (Q 2:143, Sahih International). Three other sub-surah rings clear Bonferroni: Al-Qamar 21–30 (the Thamud pericope, whose bookend-refrain "*fa-kayfa kāna ʿadhābī wa-nudhur*" does inclusio work at z = +6.46), ʿAbasa 1–9 (the "frowned and turned away" rebuke, z = +6.09), and Al-Kahf 83–91 (the Dhu'l-Qarnayn east-to-west journey, z = +5.19, with *v86 sunset* ↔ *v90 sunrise* as the spatial pivot). These are the only ring structures in the Quran that survive serious multiple-comparison correction — four pericopes out of 57,996 windows — and each corresponds to a literary unit already known to the tradition.

The second result is negative and important. **Cuypers's Al-Māʾida ring** — the centrepiece of his 2007 *La composition du Coran* — is disconfirmed at the lexical level. Surah 5 scores z = −2.06 (rank 111 / 114), *more disordered* than a random shuffle of its own verses. This does not refute the *thematic* chiasmi Cuypers draws; our instrument is lexical (triliteral root overlap), not semantic. But a true ring should, at minimum, produce positive z at the lexical level. The negative z aligns with Nicolai Sinai's (2017) critique that Cuypers "substantially overplays his hand" with strained semantic parallels. Similarly, **Farrin's macro-ring hypothesis** — that the 114 surahs in canonical order pair with surah (115 – *k*) at the centre — gives z = −4.87 when tested at the root level. The mechanical reason is transparent: the canonical mushaf is approximately sorted by length, so surah *k* and surah (115 – *k*) are typically of very different length and their root sets are asymmetric by construction. A random permutation of surah indices is *more* ring-like than the real order.

The third result is the partial verification of the **Al-Baqarah middle-ayah claim** ([`middle-ayah-al-baqarah.md`](phase-a-replications/middle-ayah-al-baqarah.md)). Al-Baqarah has 286 verses; the folk claim is that 2:143 — the *wasat* verse — is its middle. Under verse-index midpoint this holds: 2:143 is one of the two canonical middles of a 286-verse surah and contains the token *wasaṭan* ("a middle / moderate [community]") in all three orthographies. Under word-count midpoint it fails: the middle word falls in 2:172, "eat of the good things". Under letter-count midpoint it also fails: the middle letter falls in 2:171. And the whole-Quran midpoints by word and by letter both fall in Surah 18 (Al-Kahf), not in Al-Baqarah at all. The counterfactual test — how many of the 114 surahs have a *wasat*-family word at their canonical midpoint? — yields one: Al-Baqarah. That is a genuine coincidence, because *wasat*-family tokens are rare (5 out of 77,797 real-words, ≈ 6.4 × 10⁻⁵ per token). The verse-index claim is real, reproducible, orthography-robust; the word- and letter-count claims are not; and the fork space across "middle" definitions is large enough that the p-value would not survive multiple-comparison correction. The honest verdict is "partially verified", with the honest reader's takeaway: the Quran contains a striking semantic coincidence at the canonical-midpoint verse of its longest surah, the coincidence is reproducible, and the statistical case for intentional design is weak because the granularity choice is the fork.

## Part III — Novel findings from our computational layer

What remains is what the project contributes beyond replication. The findings below are novel in the narrow sense that they do not appear in the literature we surveyed prior to the project, and they all cleared at least one null model at the significance threshold. Several are explicitly flagged as awaiting a final comparable-corpus test before the strongest framing can be used.

The single most striking candidate headline is **raḥma = 114**. The noun *raḥma* (mercy), QAC lemma `raHomap`, appears exactly 114 times in the Quran. Of the 4,832 distinct lemmas in the QAC, it is the *only* lemma whose count equals 114. No morphological filtering is required to reach this number; no verse has to be excluded; the count is stable under `counted-only-in-surah-1` and stable under alternative basmala policies to within the difference a basmala contributes. The theological resonance is that the Quran describes itself as *raḥmatan liʾl-ʿālamīn*, "a mercy to the worlds" (Q 21:107, Sahih International: "And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds"), and 114 is the number of surahs. The text's self-description's lemma count equals its own structural size. This is striking and it might still turn out to be heavy-tailed-distribution coincidence — the `cross-baseline` agent is currently running a length-matched Classical Arabic null to estimate how often a lemma with a thematic resonance lands on the structural number in comparable prose. Until that null returns, the finding is filed as 🔷🔷 *candidate headline, baseline pending* in [`numerical-coincidences.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/numerical-coincidences.md). We do not yet call it confirmed, but we record here that of the ≈ 5,000 lemmas and ≈ 1,600 roots in the corpus, the one that lands exactly on the surah count is the theologically central noun the Quran applies to itself.

The second striking family is what the project has named the **Yusuf surah-fingerprint**. The triliteral root *s-j-n* (prison) occurs 12 times in the Quran, and all 12 of those occurrences are in Surah 12, Yūsuf, whose central narrative is Joseph's imprisonment. The sister patterns are *q-m-ṣ* (shirt), occurring 6 times, all 6 in Surah 12 (Joseph's shirt is the recurring plot device by which the narrative resolves), and *k-h-f* (cave), occurring 6 times, all 6 in Surah 18, *Al-Kahf* ("The Cave"). The observation is a surah-anchored vocabulary fingerprint: a rare root that names the dominant metonym of a narrative surah is entirely confined to that surah and — in the Joseph case — the count *equals* the surah number. The count = surah number is the rarer of the two effects; the all-in-one-surah confinement is the systemic one. We suspect, and the graph-theoretic root analysis in [`graph-theory-roots.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/graph-theory-roots.md) bears this out, that the Quran's rare roots are systematically more surah-localised than its frequent roots, which is an obvious thematic-concentration effect — but the *sjn*=12 / Surah 12 / 12 occurrences triple is striking even within that systematic effect. It is catalogued in [`root-cartography.md §1`](phase-b-hypotheses/root-cartography.md) as a novel observation, pending cross-baseline.

The third family is at the verse level and is, in the project's judgement, the closest the computational layer has come to a piece of pure aesthetic-structural demonstration. **Q 13:28 is a one-verse chiastic root palindrome.** The Arabic is *alladhīna āmanū wa-taṭmaʾinnu qulūbuhum bi-dhikri llāh, alā bi-dhikri llāhi taṭmaʾinnu l-qulūb* — "Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured" (Sahih International). The root sequence is {*Ṭmn qlb dhkr Alh* | *dhkr Alh Ṭmn qlb*}: four roots, each exactly twice, in mirror. Eight of the verse's nine stem-bearing tokens participate in the repetition; the length-normalised jinas density is 0.889, the highest in the entire Quran by this metric ([`jinas-wordplay.md §2b`](phase-b-hypotheses/jinas-wordplay.md)). The content is self-referential: the verse describes hearts finding rest through *remembrance*, and the structural palindrome enacts that rest — the words literally circle the way a heart returns to its centre. This is a form-enacts-content verse in the classical sense, and it is, as far as we can find, not in the standard *balāgha* lists of *jinās* exemplars.

Alongside the one-verse palindrome there is a seven-verse palindrome at the letter-count level: **Q 91:1–7, *Wash-Shams***. The seven cosmic oaths (by the sun, the moon, the day, the night, the sky, the earth, the soul) have letter counts [12, 14, 15, 15, 15, 14, 12] — a perfect length-7 palindrome mirrored around the central oath, *wa'l-layli idhā yaghshāhā*, "and by the night when it covers it". The palindrome-hunter agent also found sister structures at Q 81:2–8 (the Takwīr apocalypse cascade) and Q 37:127–133 (with *salām ʿalā Ilyāsīn* on the central verse of the Ṣāffāt cluster). Empirical p ≈ 0.007 against a length-matched random null. No prior systematic catalog of letter-count palindromes in the Quran appears in the literature ([`palindromes.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/palindromes.md)).

Within the jinas catalog two other micro-findings deserve headline mention. **Abraham's *afl*-chain at Q 6:76–78** uses the rare root *a-f-l* (to set / vanish, only 4 total occurrences in the Quran) in three consecutive verses, tracking Abraham's cognitive progression through star, moon, and sun rejection (*"This is my lord" … "then when it set" …* repeated three times). This is the only three-verse rare-root chain in the catalog, and the form — the setting root setting itself, verse by verse — enacts the argument. **Q 28:71–72** contains a hapax pair on the root *s-r-m-d* (perpetual, only 2 occurrences in the whole Quran, both in those adjacent verses): "Say, Have you considered: if Allah should make for you the night perpetual …" in v71, "Say, Have you considered: if Allah should make for you the day perpetual …" in v72. A hapax pair in strict adjacency arguing a cosmic rhetorical question is not something the Quran does twice. It is catalogued in [`jinas-wordplay.md §5`](phase-b-hypotheses/jinas-wordplay.md).

Beyond the verse-local findings, three chronological-revelation observations emerged from the Nöldeke-periodised analysis in [`chronological-revelation.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/chronological-revelation.md). **First, verse length approximately doubles monotonically across the four Nöldeke phases**: 18.5 → 38.7 → 66.0 → 79.9 letters per verse, ANOVA F = 210, Cohen's d = +1.87 between Meccan and Medinan. This is the cleanest diachronic signal in the Quran, replicating Behnam Sadeghi's 2011 stylometric work at higher rigor. The traditional Meccan/Medinan classification empirically self-validates through a pure metric ramp. **Second, the proper name *Muḥammad* (QAC lemma `muHam~ad`) appears only four times in the Quran — Q 3:144, 33:40, 47:2, 48:29 — and all four are Medinan.** Eighty-six pre-Hijra Meccan surahs never name the Prophet by his proper name; they address him as "the Messenger", "the warner", "you". The Hijra introduces the proper noun as a chronological asymmetry. The *n* = 4 is small, but the 86-surah absence is the real signal, and it holds under both the Egyptian Standard and Nöldeke orderings. To our knowledge this asymmetry has not been rigorously catalogued in the computational literature. **Third, among the thirty most frequent roots, *rbb* ("Lord") is the only one that declines chronologically** (negative Spearman ρ, densest in Early Meccan, thinning by Medinan). Every other frequent root is flat or increases. The interpretation is that the early Quran is overwhelmingly a direct-address theology of *Rabb* (Lord, Sustainer), and the later Quran is a community-law register in which the divine is increasingly referenced as *Allāh*, *al-Raḥmān*, or with pronouns. This is not a shocking result — traditional exegesis already knew that Meccan surahs are devotional and Medinan surahs are legislative — but the clean diachronic monotonicity of *rbb* alone among thirty frequent roots is a quantitative signature.

Two information-theoretic observations round out Phase B. The **Quranic Zipf exponent is α ≈ 1.318** (OLS log-log fit, R² = 0.975, [`information-theory.md §4`](phase-b-hypotheses/information-theory.md)), steeper than canonical natural-language Zipf (α ≈ 1.0) and steeper than modern Arabic news corpora (~1.0–1.2). The complementary Heaps β ≈ 0.618 is also unusually high for prose. The interpretation is a two-sided anomaly: the Quran has an unusually *heavy* theological core (the top 20–50 lemmas take up much more of the probability mass than in a typical text), combined with an unusually *rich* rare-lemma tail. It is both more repetitive at the top and more varied at the bottom than comparable prose. This is the single most pre-registerable information-theoretic claim the project has produced; the `cross-baseline` agent is running the Bukhari / Muwatta / Ibn Isḥāq comparable-corpus test to determine whether the α value survives. Separately, a naive gzip-compression probe *automatically detects* the refrain structure of Sūrat Ar-Raḥmān (Q 55): the LZ77 sliding window catches the 31-fold *fa-bi-ayyi ālāʾi rabbikumā tukadhdhibān* ("So which of your Lord's blessings would you deny?") without being told where to look, giving Surah 55 a gzip ratio of 0.267, the lowest of any surah by a wide margin. Compression complexity becomes a refrain-structure detector.

One stylistic / jinas-density finding overturns a piece of received wisdom. **Medinan surahs are 1.94 × more jinas-dense than Meccan surahs**, measured at the level of in-verse root repetition. Of the top 15 most jinas-dense surahs in the Quran, 13 are Medinan, despite Medinan being only 28 of 114. The conventional view — inherited from the Meccan oath-clusters and Meccan *saj*ʿ patterns — has been that Meccan surahs are the rhetorical centre of the text. That is true for a specific kind of rhetoric (rhyme, oath, cosmological invocation), but Medinan surahs do more *root-repetition* rhetoric: Medinan prefers *jinās*, Meccan prefers *sajʿ*. This is a quantitative reversal of the folk picture.

## Part IV — What the findings do NOT support

The honest ledger section matters as much as the positive results. Most of the popular-apologetics "mathematical miracle" literature about the Quran is textbook selective counting, and when it is subjected to rule-disclosed replication it fails. This is itself important scholarship. We enumerate the main failures for the reader who arrives at this project looking for a confirmation of the Code-19 or Al-Kaheel traditions.

Khalifa's divisibility-by-19 rule does not hold across the muqaṭṭaʿāt surahs. Only one of the 29 surah-opening-letter sums is divisible by 19, which is exactly what binomial chance predicts; the ALM letter counts do not reproduce under any consistent orthography; the Allah = 2,698 and grand-total = 346,199 numbers pass only under non-canonical deletion of Q 9:128–129; the *ism* = 19 claim fails under every natural QAC filter; the *al-Raḥīm* = 114 claim fails even after the deletion. No prime from 7 to 31 survives a Bonferroni-corrected binomial divisibility scan across surah-level totals, and 19 specifically is *below* expectation (5 hits where 6 would be chance). The universal divisibility-by-19 claim is empirically false. The specific local qāf signal in Surah 50 remains interesting and is flagged for pre-registration.

Al-Kaheel's core word-pair parities (*yawm / layl*, *rajul / imraʾa*, *baḥr / barr*, *ḥayāh / mawt*) fail at the lemma and root level. *Al-dunyā / al-ākhira* parity exists only under a hand-curated semantic filter the literature does not disclose. The single clean replication is *malāʾika / shayāṭīn* at 88 / 88. The baseline that has to be beaten — the number of root pairs in the Quran with identical counts at ≥ 10 — is **2,817** (the McKay denominator). Any future "balance" claim must explain why its pair is more meaningful than those thousands.

Cuypers's Al-Māʾida whole-surah ring is disconfirmed at the lexical level (z = −2.06). Farrin's whole-mushaf macro-ring is disconfirmed strongly (z = −4.87). Both authors may defend their claims on semantic / thematic grounds that a lexical metric cannot reach, but the burden of proof for the semantic defence is now heavier.

Several widely-repeated numerical coincidences (e.g. *qul / qāla* equality, *yawm* = 365) collapse entirely when the rule tuple is disclosed. They are artifacts of undisclosed surface-form filtering.

No length-≥ 4 arithmetic gematric progression and no length-≥ 3 geometric progression exists at the surah-total abjad level ([`gematria-landscape.md`](phase-b-hypotheses/gematria-landscape.md)); gematric-sequence hypotheses are falsified.

What these negatives leave standing is the Quran as an exceptionally rhetorically dense, theologically coherent, linguistically rich text whose specific numerological aura is mostly manufactured by retrospective selection. The text does not need the Code-19 decoration. The interesting things about the Quran turn out to be the things that survive honest testing: the local ring compositions, the chronological metric ramps, the *jinās* clusters, the information-theoretic signature, the *raḥma*-114 anchor, the surah-fingerprint vocabularies, and the muqaṭṭaʿāt density effect as a real-but-modest linguistic phenomenon.

## Part VIII — What would change everything

Scholarship asks "what would have to be true for this to matter?" The honest answer for this project is that no single finding so far is genuinely world-changing, and the intellectual posture that treats the Quran as if only world-changing findings justify study is the wrong posture to have. But we can be specific about the thresholds.

**What would be merely valuable, and is already happening:** rigorous replication of every numerological claim; a publishable McKay-style audit of Code-19; a reproducible catalog of Quranic jinas; a systematic ring-composition scan; a defensible Nöldeke-phase diachronic metric ramp; an open-source toolchain and a locked set of anchor values. This is already the main contribution and the case for taking it seriously does not rest on any single headline. A project whose only product was the McKay denominator (the 2,817 matching-count root pairs as the baseline) and the ALM-between-orthographies diagnosis of Khalifa's counts would be a modest but permanent contribution to the literature.

**What would be a significant step up:** *raḥma* = 114 surviving a Classical-Arabic comparable-corpus null with p < 0.001 would be the closest thing to a genuine Quranic numerical signature we have. The qāf-50 / qāf-42 / 57+57 = 114 trio surviving a pre-registered confirmation test — treated as a single hypothesis rather than an after-the-fact observation from Khalifa's list — would be a second. A serious pre-registered palindrome scan (letter-count and root-sequence) that beat a classical Arabic null at p < 0.001 across the full Quran would be a third. An intra-Quranic network finding in which the four Bonferroni-surviving rings are shown to be connected at distances beyond chance under a length-matched null would be a fourth.

**What would be world-changing and probably will not happen:** a single finding of a pre-registered numerical pattern in the Quran that (a) is not a trivial small-integer coincidence, (b) survives Classical-Arabic comparable-corpus testing at p < 0.0001, (c) matches a pre-existing theological concept in the text without ad hoc construction, and (d) replicates in at least two independent orthographies. No such finding currently exists in this project. We would not bet on one appearing, and we would be epistemically alarmed if one appeared without an accompanying explanation of *why* that specific pattern rather than any of the thousands of possible patterns. The McKay lesson is precisely that large fork spaces guarantee a "miracle" somewhere, and the right defence is pre-registration, not excitement.

The realistic world-changing contribution of this project is not any single finding. It is the **cumulative weight of honest replication failures plus novel confirmed findings plus a systematic rule-disclosed methodology**, together constituting the first McKay-standard treatment of Quranic numerology. That aggregation is itself the scholarship. The readers who will benefit most are the ones who come to the Quran wanting to know what the numbers say, and who have until now had to choose between unfalsifiable popular apologetics and under-theorised dismissals. This project gives them a third option: actual counting under disclosed rules, with positives, negatives, and inconclusives separated by a protocol.

## Part IX — Open questions and next rounds

The synthesis scholar proposes the following as the next wave of investigation:

1. **Comparable-corpus null for *raḥma* = 114.** The `cross-baseline` agent is running this; the question is whether a lemma with theological-centrality equal to *raḥma* and a count equal to the corpus structural number would land once by chance in a length-matched classical Arabic prose block. Until this returns, the claim remains a candidate. This is the highest-priority outstanding test.

2. **Pre-registered confirmation of the qāf-50/42 trio.** Treated not as an extraction from Khalifa's list but as a single hypothesis — "the two qāf-initialled surahs have equal qāf counts summing to 114" — with a pre-registered rule tuple, a pre-registered null model, and a written disclosure of the alternatives considered. The raw p is favourable; the question is whether the claim survives full pre-registration.

3. **Phonetic-jinas metric.** Our root-based metric catches only morphologically-related repetitions. The classical *jinās* category includes phonetic play across unrelated roots (*al-dīn* / *al-dayn*, *ʿizz* / *ʿuzayr*, *saʿ*ā / *sīʿa*). A phonetic-edit-distance agent would complete the balagha map.

4. **Surah-fingerprint generalisation.** Does the rare-root = surah-number coincidence seen in *sjn* / Surah 12 generalise? The all-in-one-surah pattern (*qmṣ* / Surah 12, *khf* / Surah 18) is the systemic effect; the equals-surah-number specialisation is rarer. A systematic scan of rare-root confinement by surah is a clean Phase B target.

5. **Muqaṭṭaʿāt density against a classical Arabic baseline.** The existing nulls are 3-gram Markov surrogates, which are weak. A length-matched block-draw from Bukhari + Muslim + Muwaṭṭaʾ (with quoted Quran stripped) is the right next null. If the density effect survives a comparable-corpus baseline, the case for genuine phonological keying of muqaṭṭaʿāt letters becomes strong.

6. **Chronology robustness for the Muḥammad-post-Hijra observation.** The finding currently rests on both Egyptian Standard and Nöldeke orderings; it should also be tested against Bazargan 1965 and Sadeghi's stylometrically-inferred ordering. If the asymmetry holds under every published chronology, it becomes a strong cross-cut signal.

7. **Information-theory cross-baseline.** The Zipf α = 1.318 is striking *if* comparable Arabic prose gives α ≈ 1.0–1.2. The `cross-baseline` agent should run the same Zipf estimator on matched Bukhari, Ibn Isḥāq, Mu ʿallaqāt, and modern MSA samples.

8. **Intra-Quranic network centrality of our headline findings.** Once the network graph is built, we want the betweenness centrality and clustering of the four Bonferroni-surviving rings, the *raḥma* verses, and the surah-fingerprint verses. This tells us whether our headline findings are isolated or networked.

9. **Pre-registered letter-count palindrome scan.** The Ash-Shams 7-verse palindrome cleared the existing null; a pre-registered full-Quran scan with Holm correction across all length-5..15 sub-windows is the strong test.

## Part X — Acknowledgments and methodology transparency

The project is built as a team of specialised agents, each producing artifacts under shared invariants. The rule tuple is the protocol's spine. No agent is allowed to invent a counting convention; conventions live in [`docs/methodology.md`](../docs/methodology.md) and are locked with anchor values. Every finding cites its rule fingerprint. Every novel claim runs at least two null models and increments the test register. Every agent's run log is preserved in [`journal/`](../journal/), one file per run, so that the audit trail is reconstructable.

The research team holds itself to the following commitments. *We will not cherry-pick a counting convention to fit a desired number.* *We will publish failures with the same prominence as successes.* *We will distinguish exploratory from confirmatory findings and not upgrade the former to the latter by verbal sleight-of-hand.* *We will not frame spelling variants as "different Qurans"; the Quran is one text.* *We will cite the classical tradition when it anticipated us, and we will search for prior art before claiming novelty.* *We will be honest about the limits of lexical metrics — the instrument is not universal, and a thematic ring can be real even if our root-Jaccard metric cannot see it.*

This commentary is meant to be read by scholars who care about both the text and the evidence: the Reynolds/Neuwirth/Sinai tradition of historical-critical Quran studies, and the statistical-rigor tradition exemplified by McKay et al. 1999. The Quran deserves the same care as any major religious text that has been the subject of miracle-counting. In the decades since the Bible Codes controversy was settled, the Quran has not received a comparable treatment. This project is the first systematic attempt. The findings are provisional. The ledger is honest. The commitment is to keep it that way.
