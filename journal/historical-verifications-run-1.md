# Journal — historical-verifications (run 1)

## Task scope

Phase B world-history audit. Twenty-five distinct items, each referenced in the Qurʾān, each checked against the current (2026) archaeological and historical-critical record. For each item: Arabic verse, status, graded verdict.

Grading schema defined (not importable, defined in-output):
- CONFIRMED — independent archaeological or epigraphic evidence backs the core claim.
- PARTIAL — the actor or event is attested but Qurʾānic specifics go beyond the record.
- CONTESTED — scholarly opinion divided; evidence is ambiguous or candidates plural.
- TRADITION-ONLY — attested in earlier Near Eastern literary tradition but not archaeologically recoverable.
- UNRESOLVED — no clear identification; candidates do not converge.

## Method

Working from the item list supplied by the orchestrator. For each:

1. Quote the most load-bearing verse in Arabic with a working translation.
2. State the current archaeological / historical-critical status — who excavated, what inscriptions, what primary sources, who the key revisionists are.
3. Note where the Qurʾānic detail goes beyond or diverges from the Hebrew-Bible parallel (where relevant), and where it preserves independent or older traditions (Syriac, rabbinic, apocryphal).
4. Issue a graded verdict.

Cross-referenced prior Phase B files in findings/phase-b-hypotheses/ (especially historical-details.md and sacred-geography.md) to avoid duplicating work and to keep terminology aligned with the rest of the Phase B corpus.

## Key calls made along the way

**Iram dhāt al-ʿimād (item 1).** Shiṣr / Ubar is a genuine late frankincense-caravan site but the Clapp/Zarins 1992 "Atlantis of the Sands" identification with Iram is not accepted by the field (Retsö, Kitchen). Flagged CONTESTED, not CONFIRMED.

**Thamūd (item 2).** Independent attestation in Sargon II annals (c. 715 BCE) plus Greco-Roman sources plus the rock-cut tomb complex at Ḥegra put this firmly in CONFIRMED. Among the strongest-supported items in the list.

**Sabaʾ / Marʾib dam (item 4).** CIH 541 (Abraha 548 CE) and RES 4069 (Sharaḥbīl Yaʿfur c. 450 CE) directly document dam-repair campaigns — the Qurʾān's "sayl al-ʿarim" sits in a real late-antique engineering history. CONFIRMED.

**Pharaoh of Exodus (item 5).** Downgraded from apologist CONFIRMED. The Bucaille mummy-drowning argument is not sustained by Egyptologists; the Exodus itself has thin archaeological support at the scale Scripture describes. CONTESTED / TRADITION-ONLY.

**David (item 7).** Tel Dan "byt dwd" (c. mid-9th c. BCE) ends the mythicist reading — dynasty CONFIRMED. Biographical details (Goliath combat) remain PARTIAL.

**Roman–Persian war (item 8).** The full 602–628 sequence (Jerusalem 614, Nineveh 627, True Cross return 630) is independently documented in Byzantine, Armenian, Syriac chronicles. Q 30:1–6 is Meccan (c. 615–620), fits the gap between Persian victory and Byzantine reversal. CONFIRMED.

**Abraha (item 9).** Abraha himself: CONFIRMED (Marʾib dam inscription, Murayghān inscription, Byzantine sources). A specific Meccan expedition as distinct from the attested northern campaigns: PARTIAL. Dating (570 vs. 552): CONTESTED per Kister, Conrad.

**Dhū al-Qarnayn (item 14).** Treated seriously. Van Bladel's Alexander-legend argument (2008) is the strongest literary case — but the chronology of the Neṣḥānā (c. 629–630 in Reinink's dating) vs. Meccan dating of Q 18 remains genuinely contested. Did not collapse to "obviously Alexander" or "obviously Cyrus." UNRESOLVED.

**Hāmān (item 20).** Kept as CONTESTED. The Geiger / Esther-conflation reading is the majority critical view; the Bucaille ḥm-nṯr defense has real methodological problems (ḥm-nṯr is a title not a name; Qurʾānic Hāmān is named and political, not priestly) but is not zero. Did not collapse either way.

**Sodom / Tall el-Ḥammām (item 21).** The 2021 Bunch et al. airburst paper has been seriously challenged by Boslough and others post-2022. Held to CONTESTED — real Middle Bronze destruction at the site, contested mechanism, contested Sodom identification.

**Jūdī (item 22).** The Peshitta qardu + Targum Onqelos + Berossus + Josephus convergence on Corduene (not Ararat) is striking. The Qurʾānic Jūdī aligns with the older Near Eastern tradition, not with the later Armenian-Christian Ararat identification. CONFIRMED as aligning with the older tradition — the event itself remains TRADITION-ONLY.

**Joseph in Egypt (item 18).** Noted the malik / firʿawn distinction in Q 12 vs. the Mosaic narrative — "Pharaoh" (prʿʿ, "great house") becomes a personal royal title only from the late New Kingdom (c. 1200 BCE), so calling an earlier Egyptian monarch "al-Malik" rather than "Firʿawn" is arguably historically correct for a Hyksos-era or Second-Intermediate-Period context. Marked as a notable Qurʾānic precision even within a PARTIAL verdict.

## Limitations and open threads

1. I did not consult live epigraphic databases (DASI, OCIANA) for this pass. An upgrade would cross-reference item 2 (Thamūd) and item 4 (Sabaʾ) against the latest Ancient-North-Arabian and Ancient-South-Arabian corpora as of 2026.
2. Mecca archaeology (item 25) remains a structural gap not in my audit but in the field — modern construction has foreclosed most direct excavation.
3. The Alexander-legend / Qurʾān 18 chronological question (item 14) is a live research area; the 2024–2026 literature may have shifted positions I have not captured.
4. Hāmān (item 20) deserves a dedicated Phase C deep-dive — the Bucaille ḥm-nṯr argument has never had a proper Egyptological rebuttal in the academic literature.
5. The Night Journey item (23) touches on Shoemaker-style revisionism that I gave a sentence to; a full audit would need to engage Shoemaker 2018 and successors.
