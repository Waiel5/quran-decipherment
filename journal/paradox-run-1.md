# Paradox Verses — Run 1 Journal

**Date:** 2026-04-12
**Agent phase:** Phase B
**Task:** Map 15 theologically challenging verses the Quran deploys rhetorically; provide Arabic, classical (Rāzī / Zamakhsharī / Qurṭubī) and modern treatment, with honest tension discussion.

## Method

1. Treat each verse as a *triplet*: (a) textual surface, (b) classical kalām/tafsīr resolution, (c) the residual tension that resolution leaves.
2. Where the Quran is self-consciously polemical (anti-rabbinic at 50:38; anti-Christian at 3:55 / 4:157–158), preserve the intertextual edge rather than flatten it.
3. Where the tension is internal (predestination vs free will; 4:3 vs 4:129), resolve via the classical harmonisation but flag the *force* of the surface contradiction.
4. Do not smooth — Rāzī, Zamakhsharī, and Qurṭubī often *disagree with each other*; preserve that stratigraphy.

## Working notes

- 50:38 (lughūb = "weariness / fatigue / lassitude") — rare root, three occurrences in Quran all anti-fatigue of God. Rāzī explicit that this is directed *against* Genesis 2:2 via the Jewish-Arabian interlocutor.
- 3:55 *mutawaffīka* — the lexical force of *tawaffā* in the Quran is overwhelmingly "to cause to die." Only exegetes struggling with 4:157–158 force it to mean "fully receive / take in sleep-like state." Rāzī lists six classical readings and admits multiplicity.
- 74:30 (nineteen) — Khalifa 1974 uses this as the numerological anchor. Classical tafsīr: 19 = number of hell's angelic guards. The *purpose clause* in 74:31 ("so that those of the Book may be certain") is the key — the number is a *sign* aimed at prior scripturaries.
- 22:52 (Satanic verses / *gharānīq*) — al-Ṭabarī's *Jāmiʿ* reports multiple isnāds; Ibn Kathīr and Qāḍī ʿIyāḍ reject on *ʿiṣma* grounds. Shahab Ahmed (2017) *Before Orthodoxy* reconstructs pre-9th c. Muslim acceptance of the tradition — a genuine *diachronic* shift in Islamic orthodoxy.
- 20:121 Adam — the *ʿiṣma al-anbiyāʾ* doctrine is *post-Qurʾānic*; the text itself says *ʿaṣā* ("disobeyed") plainly. Classical tafsīr (especially Ashʿarī) reads *ʿaṣā* as *tark al-awlā* ("abandoning the preferable").
- 4:3 vs 4:129 — the "inner contradiction" framing is Muḥammad ʿAbduh's and Rashīd Riḍā's; they read 4:129 as *de facto* legislating monogamy. Classical (pre-modern) fiqh does not read it that way.
- 66:1 (Maria al-Qibṭiyya) — asbāb al-nuzūl is contested; Ṭabarī gives two parallel accounts (honey incident vs Maria incident). The *rhetorical* force (God chiding the Prophet) is the theologically awkward edge.
