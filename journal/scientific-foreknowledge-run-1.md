## Brief

Rigorous audit of the 12 most-cited *iʿjāz ʿilmi* ("scientific miracle of the
Quran") claims. For each: what the Quran says, what 7th-century Arabia /
surrounding Hellenistic-Syriac-Persian world already knew, what modern
science says, and an honest verdict on a six-level scale
(ACCURATE / PARTIALLY ACCURATE / AMBIGUOUS / INACCURATE / PRE-EXISTING
KNOWLEDGE / APOLOGETIC OVERREACH).

Framing from user: "what if something stated it existed but never existed
back then, or if it told the future." These are the load-bearing apologetic
claims; the project's credibility requires distinguishing signal from stretch.

## Method

1. For each of the 12 claims, extract the Quranic Arabic verbatim.
2. State the apologetic reading in its strongest form (steelman).
3. State the 7th-century background: what Galen, Aristotle, the Talmud,
   Syriac Christian writers, and pre-Islamic Arabic poetry already
   contained in the region. The Quran was revealed into a late-antique
   Near East saturated with Hellenistic, Jewish, Christian, Zoroastrian
   and Arabic oral knowledge — not into a vacuum.
4. State what modern science actually says (where claimed).
5. Apply red-flags: post-hoc rule selection, match-tolerance stretch,
   cherry-picked modern concept, alternative-tafsir suppression.
6. Rate on the six-level scale.

## Key calibrating observations

- **Background knowledge of 7th-century Mecca/Medina.** Mecca was a trade
  hub connecting Yemen, Syria, and Sassanid Iraq. Syriac Christianity was
  present in the Najrān and Ḥīra. Jewish tribes in Medina (Banū
  Qurayẓa, Naḍīr, Qaynuqāʿ) had rabbinic learning. Hellenistic medicine
  (Galen, 2nd c.) and Aristotelian natural philosophy circulated in
  Syriac translation through Jundishapur and Alexandria. Claims that
  "the 7th-century Arab could not have known X" must be checked against
  this Syriac-Jewish-Hellenistic background, not against a fictional
  isolated desert.
- **Provenance-language rule.** The verb *anzala* ("to send down") is
  used in the Quran for rain, iron, garments, cattle (Q 39:6 uses it
  for livestock), the scales (Q 57:25), the Kaʿba (in tafsir), and
  revelation itself. It does *not* specifically encode cosmic
  provenance; it's a stock-divine-gift verb. This rule falsifies two
  separate apologetic claims (iron, also cattle).
- **Survivor bias.** The famous claims are the ones that look good
  *after* modern science caught up. The Quran also contains statements
  that look scientifically odd by modern lights (sperm from between the
  backbone and ribs, Q 86:6-7; sun setting in a muddy spring,
  Q 18:86; seven heavens as a layered structure, Q 67:3; stars as
  missiles against jinn, Q 67:5; a worm destroying Solomon's staff,
  Q 34:14). An honest audit reports *both* sides. The apologetic
  literature is cherry-picked.
- **Bucaille's methodology.** Maurice Bucaille (*The Bible, The Quran
  and Science*, 1976) explicitly practised concordism: pre-select a
  modern scientific fact, scan the scripture for any verse that can
  be read as saying it, stretch the semantics until a match appears,
  celebrate. This is the same method McKay et al. 1999 formally
  refuted for the Bible Codes: unbounded degrees of freedom produce
  unbounded "miracles."

## Per-claim notes (condensed)

1. **Embryology.** The stage-sequence nuṭfa→ʿalaqa→muḍgha is *Galen's*
   stage-sequence with Arabic names. Galen's *De Semine* (2nd c. CE)
   describes: seminal fluid → clotted blood-like stage → fleshy stage
   → formed limbs. The Talmud (Niddah 25a, Yevamot 69b) discusses
   similar stages. The word *ʿalaqa* from root ʿ-l-q covers
   "clinging/leech-like/clot" — the "leech" match the 1980s
   apologists celebrated requires one specific polysemy choice; "clot"
   was the dominant classical tafsir reading (Ibn Kathīr, Ṭabarī).
   Modern embryology: bones and muscle develop *concurrently* from the
   same mesenchymal tissue, not sequentially as the Quran's
   "then flesh covered the bones" implies. VERDICT: PRE-EXISTING
   KNOWLEDGE + partial inaccuracy.
2. **Cosmic expansion.** *mūsiʿūn* is an active participle from w-s-ʿ
   ("to be vast, to encompass"). Classical tafsir (Ibn ʿAbbās,
   Ṭabarī, Qurṭubī) read it as "We are its extender" → "We are vast
   in power / make it vast." The Hubble reading requires forcing an
   ongoing-progressive sense. Lisān al-ʿArab records both senses.
   VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS; apologetic reading is a retro-fit that is
   linguistically *possible* but was not the pre-modern sense.
3. **Big Bang / ratq-fatq.** Classical tafsir reads this as sky
   being sealed → rain released → earth sealed → vegetation emerged.
   Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿIkrima all say "they were sealed and we
   unsealed them with rain." The cosmological reading appeared only
   post-1970s. VERDICT: APOLOGETIC OVERREACH.
4. **Orbits / falak.** The word *falak* denoted the celestial sphere
   in pre-Islamic Arabic (cf. Lisān al-ʿArab; pre-Islamic poetry).
   "Swimming in a falak" matches the Ptolemaic celestial-sphere
   cosmology, not Keplerian orbits. Aristarchus's heliocentrism was
   3rd c. BCE; the spherical heavens were standard Hellenistic
   astronomy. VERDICT: PRE-EXISTING KNOWLEDGE.
5. **Mountains as pegs.** Pre-Islamic Arabic poetic trope; Psalm 104:5
   ("earth shall not be moved"), Job 26:11 ("pillars of heaven"),
   1 Samuel 2:8 ("pillars of the earth"). Isostasy requires depth; the
   Quran says mountains prevent the earth from *shaking*/swaying
   (*an tamīd*), which is a seismic-stability claim that is
   geologically wrong — mountains *cause* earthquakes via tectonic
   activity, not prevent them. VERDICT: PRE-EXISTING METAPHOR +
   modern-geology mismatch.
6. **Two seas.** Pre-Islamic Arab sailors in the Persian Gulf and
   Red Sea regularly observed river-mouth mixing and sweet/salt
   boundaries. The Shatt al-Arab (Tigris-Euphrates mouth) is the
   paradigm. *Barzakh* is a general "barrier" word, used elsewhere
   for the interval between death and resurrection (Q 23:100).
   VERDICT: PRE-EXISTING OBSERVATIONAL KNOWLEDGE.
7. **Deep-sea darkness.** Q 24:40 is explicitly a simile (*aw
   ka-ẓulumātin*, "or like darknesses") for the spiritual state of
   the disbeliever. The "waves upon waves" matches *storm-on-the-
   surface darkness*, observable to any Red Sea sailor, not abyssal
   photic-zone oceanography. VERDICT: PRE-EXISTING + literary.
8. **Iron from sky.** Already rejected in
   `hadid-deep-dive.md` §1. The verb *anzalnā* in Q 57:25 is the
   same verb used for cattle in Q 39:6 ("and He sent down for you
   from livestock eight pairs"). No one claims cattle were
   meteoric. VERDICT: APOLOGETIC OVERREACH.
9. **Fingerprints.** Q 75:4 says God can *reshape* (*nusawwī*) the
   fingertips at resurrection. The rhetorical force is
   "even the smallest bones — we can rebuild them perfectly,"
   not "each fingerprint is unique." Unique-fingerprint
   identification was known to the Chinese in ancient times and
   documented by Quintilian (1st c. CE); the apologetic claim also
   requires the anachronistic reading. VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS but
   leaning APOLOGETIC OVERREACH.
10. **Pharaoh's body.** Q 10:92 says *your body* will be
    preserved as a sign. All Egyptian pharaohs were mummified.
    The verse does not specify Ramesses II vs Merneptah vs any
    other. Bucaille's claim depends on an Exodus-Ramesses II
    identification that is itself contested in modern
    Egyptology (Merneptah stele, Thutmose III, etc. all
    candidates). VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS.
11. **Atom/dharra.** *Dharra* in pre-Islamic Arabic = "ant-mite,
    speck of dust visible in sunbeam." Q 99:7-8 uses it for
    *moral* accountability (good/evil of *dharra*-weight). Q 10:61
    simply says "nothing even smaller than a *dharra* is hidden
    from your Lord" — a theological claim about divine
    omniscience, not physics. VERDICT: APOLOGETIC OVERREACH.
12. **Milk formation.** A herder could observe that milk comes
    from the same animal whose belly contains dung and whose
    veins contain blood. The "between feces and blood" is vivid
    anatomy, not a vascular-absorption claim. Aristotle
    (*De Partibus Animalium*) and Galen both discuss milk-blood
    conversion. VERDICT: PRE-EXISTING OBSERVATIONAL KNOWLEDGE.

## Counter-evidence the audit must also document

Cherry-picking defence: the following Quranic statements, if
submitted to the *same* concordist method, would score as scientifically
inaccurate by current understanding:

- Q 86:6-7: sperm from between the backbone and ribs. Anatomically
  wrong (sperm is produced in testes).
- Q 18:86: Dhū al-Qarnayn finds sun setting in a muddy spring.
  Literal reading is geographically wrong; metaphorical reading is
  defensible but then symmetric with apologists' refusal to read
  Q 51:47 metaphorically.
- Q 67:5: stars as *rujūm* (missiles) used to pelt the jinn.
- Q 15:16-18: shooting stars as meteors targeting eavesdropping
  jinn.
- Q 13:2: heavens raised without pillars "that you can see" —
  cosmological picture is a *domed* sky.
- Q 65:12: seven heavens and a matching seven earths. Flat /
  stratified cosmology.

Any honest iʿjāz audit must disclose these. They are absent from
Bucaille, Harun Yahya, and Naggar.

## Decisions

- Write the finding as a mixed-verdict catalog. No claim rated
  ACCURATE. One claim (embryology) is PARTIALLY ACCURATE *and*
  PRE-EXISTING. The rest split between AMBIGUOUS, PRE-EXISTING
  KNOWLEDGE, and APOLOGETIC OVERREACH.
- Include the counter-evidence section in the finding itself,
  not just the journal — this is load-bearing for the audit's
  honesty.
- Do not soften the verdicts to please either apologists or
  polemicists. Stats-rigor protocol §3.4 ("no retrofitting")
  applies to interpretation too: the red-flag for post-hoc
  rule selection is exactly what concordism does.
