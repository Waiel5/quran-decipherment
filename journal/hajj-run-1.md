## Findings crystallized

- **Q 22's paradox resolved:** Sūrat al-Ḥajj devotes only ~12/78 verses to pilgrimage, and those are theological (Abrahamic commission, sacrifice as taqwā-vehicle, universal call), not procedural. The surah is named for the *theology* of ritual, not its mechanics.
- **Legal load on Q 2:198:** The single verse carries both Quranic hapax toponyms of the pilgrimage (ʿArafāt, Mashʿar). The stations are *marked by being named once*.
- **Ritual ṭawāf is Abrahamic-sealed:** all four cultic `Twf` verses are either Abraham-commissioned or Abraham-resultant. No standalone ṭawāf.
- **Permission-register pattern:** the *laysa ʿalaykum junāḥ* formula clusters at precisely the pre-Islamic-continuity stations (Ṣafā/Marwa, commerce, Minā-departure). It is a dispensation idiom for *received*, not invented, ritual.
- **Ḥudaybiyya is the legal engine:** Q 48:25 (*wa-l-hadya maʿkūfan*) is textually the origin of Q 2:196's *uḥṣirtum* clause — historical event → legal provision.
- **Calendar theology:** Q 2:189 + Q 9:36–37 lock the pilgrimage to an un-intercalated lunar year, making it cycle through the solar seasons — a *universality* argument encoded in calendrics.
- **Saʿy-terminology absence:** the Quran never names the Ṣafā/Marwa run by the Twf-independent term "saʿy"; that technical usage is post-Quranic fiqh.
- **Two stations are never named:** Minā and Muzdalifa. Hapax-naming pattern goes: named-once (ʿArafāt, Mashʿar, Ṣafā, Marwa) or unnamed entirely (Minā, Muzdalifa) for the periphery; the center (al-Bayt, al-Masjid al-Ḥarām) is densely named.
