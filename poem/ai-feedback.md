## 1. The two “strokes”

### Stroke 1: line 15

> حاوَلْتُ جَمْعَهُما، فَقَصْ ‖ صَرْتُ، وَأَدْرَكَنِي السُّكونْ

But it is **not classically innocent**.

If scanned as majzūʾ al-kāmil, the ṣadr can be defended:

> حاوَلْتُ جَمْعَهُما فَقَصْ
> حاوَلْتُ جَمْـ / عَهُما فَقَصْ
> **مُتْفاعِلُن / مُتَفاعِلُن**
> **D / M**

The second half is more jagged, but can be heard as a majzūʾ kāmil with a rough first foot:

> صَرْتُ وَأَدْرَكَنِي السُّكونْ
> صَرْتُ وَأَدْ / رَكَنِي السُّكونْ
> roughly **مُتْفَعِلُن / مُتَفاعِلُن**

So yes: it is meaningfully shorter. It is not a phantom break. The word **السكون** also justifies the sonic collapse.

Also, splitting a word across the hemistich — **فقص / صرت** — is a modern visual device. In oral recitation, either the listener hears **فَقَصَرْتُ** and the trick disappears, or the reciter pauses after **فقص** and mutilates the word.

My judgment: **keep it only if you are willing to call the poem neo-classical with two intentional ruptures. Do not call the poem an otherwise flawless classical qaṣīda.**

### Stroke 2: line 20

The ṣadr scans cleanly:

> وَلِسانُ قَوْمي عَنْ سَناهُ مُقَصِّرٌ
> وَلِسانُ قَوْـ / مي عَنْ سَناهُ / مُقَصِّرُنْ
> **M / D / M**

But the ʿajuz breaks:

> فَنَحَتُّ لَفْظًا إِنَّهُ البَيُّونْ
> فَنَحَتُّ لَفْـ / ظَنْ إِنَّ هُلْـ / بَيُّونْ
> **M / D / — —**

You are missing one long syllable in the final foot. If you try to make the final foot **هُلْبَيُّونْ = — — —**, the middle foot collapses into **ظَنْ إِنَّ = — — ⏑**, which is incomplete. So the line cannot be saved by clever recitation.

A clean repair:

> **وَلِسانُ قَوْمي عَنْ سَناهُ مُقَصِّرٌ**
> **فَنَحَتُّ لَفْظًا، ثُمَّ قُلْتُ: البَيُّونْ**

Scan:

> فَنَحَتُّ لَفْـ / ظَنْ ثُمَّ قُلْـ / تُلْبَيُّونْ
> **M / D / Q**
> **مُتَفاعِلُن / مُتْفاعِلُن / مُتْفاعِلْ**

That also frames the coinage better. The current **إِنَّهُ البَيُّونْ** sounds like you are assigning the Book an invented quasi-name. **ثُمَّ قُلْتُ: البَيُّونْ** makes the act of coining explicit and less theologically presumptuous.

Lexically, the note overclaims. **البَيُّون** may be a possible coined intensive, but it is not “audibly distinct from any existing word” in the way claimed. Classical lexica record **بئر بيون** as a type of well, and **البيون** appears in the root field of ب-ي-ن; the reader’s eye will therefore not experience a pure invented super-word. > وَاللَّفْظُ بِالـ / مَعْنى أَتَمْـ / مَلْمَصُونْ
> **D / D / Q**
