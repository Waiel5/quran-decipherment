# 09 — IʿJĀZ SCAFFOLDING
### The classical theory of inimitability, the lafẓ/maʿnā war, and the topos of the poet who confesses he cannot match the sublime
*Intellectual reference for a qaṣīda whose central conceit is: the poet tries to match the Qurʾān, fails (meaning and music cannot both be maximal — the human tax), and offers his own ʿAJZ (incapacity) as living proof of the Qurʾān's IʿJĀZ (inimitability). This file is concepts, terms, and real cited quotes — NOT a draft.*

> **Sourcing note.** Every Arabic passage below was fetched from the open web and is cited at the point of use and in the SOURCES block. Primary-text quotations come from: al-Jurjānī's *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz* (IslamWeb's digitized text, book 239), al-Jāḥiẓ's *al-Ḥayawān* (via almerja, with the standard vol. 3 p. 131–132 reference), al-Rummānī's *al-Nukat fī Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*, al-Nafarī's *al-Mawāqif*, al-Būṣīrī's *Burda* (al-Kawākib al-durriyya), Kaʿb b. Zuhayr's *Bānat Suʿād* (aldiwan), and al-Mutanabbī (cross-referenced to `02-power-toolkit.md`). Where a wording varies between transmissions, that is flagged. Tashkīl is preserved as the sources give it.

> **The load-bearing pun the whole poem turns on:** **إِعْجاز** *(iʿjāz,* the Qurʾān's inimitability*)* and **عَجْز** *(ʿajz,* the poet's incapacity*)* are **the same root** — ع-ج-ز, "to be unable." *Iʿjāz* is literally the **Form-IV causative**: "the act of **rendering-incapable**." So the classical name for the Qurʾān's miracle already contains the poet's defeat inside it: the Book is *muʿjiz* = "the incapacitator," and the poet is *ʿājiz* = "the incapacitated." The poem's thesis is etymologically pre-installed: **to demonstrate one's own ʿajz IS to demonstrate the Book's iʿjāz.** They are one word, conjugated for two subjects. (And note the bonus: عَجُز *ʿajuz* = "the second hemistich of a line of verse" — so *ʿajz* even names the very place in a bayt where the poet's craft must close. The failure has a prosodic address.)

## 1. AL-JURJĀNĪ'S NAẒM THEORY — *why meaning and form cannot be prised apart*
### ʿAbd al-Qāhir al-Jurjānī (d. 471/1078), *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz*

This is the **classical theory of the project's "anti-twin"** (`r = −0.86`: meaning ⊥ music, both at maximum). Al-Jurjānī's whole life-work is the argument that **iʿjāz/balāgha does not live in words, and does not live in meanings — it lives in the *binding* of the two: in *naẓm*, the syntactic construction.** You cannot improve the sound without disturbing the sense, or the sense without disturbing the sound, because in true *naẓm* they are *the same act*. That inseparability is exactly what the poem wants to claim no human hand can sustain.

### 1a. The core doctrine — *naẓm is not arranging words; it is realizing the meanings of grammar*

Al-Jurjānī's signature move is to **redefine *naẓm*** away from "putting nice words in a row" (the old badīʿ view) and toward **the construction of syntactic-semantic relations** (*maʿānī al-naḥw*). The famous definitional sentence, from the chapter titled *al-Naẓm huwa tawakhkhī maʿānī al-iʿrāb* ("Naẓm is the pursuit of the meanings of grammar"):

> **«لَا نَظْمَ فِي الكَلِمِ وَلَا تَرْتِيبَ، حَتَّى يُعَلَّقَ بَعْضُهَا بِبَعْضٍ، وَيُبْنَى بَعْضُهَا عَلَى بَعْضٍ.»**
> *"There is no naẓm in words, and no ordering, until some of them are **hung upon** others, and some are **built upon** others."*
> — al-Jurjānī, *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz* (IslamWeb 239/7)

And the famous definition proper, from the chapter *al-Qawl fī naẓm al-kalām wa-makān al-naḥw minhu*:

> **«اِعْلَمْ أَنَّ لَيْسَ النَّظْمُ إِلَّا أَنْ تَضَعَ كَلَامَكَ الوَضْعَ الَّذِي يَقْتَضِيهِ عِلْمُ النَّحْوِ.»**
> *"Know that naẓm is nothing but that you place your speech in the placement that the science of grammar requires."*
> — *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz* (IslamWeb 239/12)

The radical consequence (this is the line a poet can detonate): **the word, in isolation, has no virtue at all.** Excellence is not a property of *alfāẓ*; it is a property of *relations between* alfāẓ:

> **«إِنَّ الفَصاحَةَ لَا تَظْهَرُ فِي أَفْرَادِ الكَلِمَاتِ، وَإِنَّمَا تَظْهَرُ بِالضَّمِّ عَلَى طَرِيقَةٍ مَخْصُوصَةٍ.»**
> *"Eloquence does not appear in single/isolated words; it appears only through **joining** them in a particular manner."*
> — *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz* (IslamWeb 239/73, the chapter *bayān anna l-fikra lā yataʿallaq bi-maʿānī l-kalimi mujarradatan min maʿānī l-naḥw* — "that thought does not attach to the meanings of words **stripped** of the meanings of grammar")

### 1b. The smith / builder — *naẓm as a craft whose excellence is in the making, not the material*

Al-Jurjānī repeatedly figures the composer as a **craftsman** (the move the project loves: the poet as *ṣāʾigh*/*nāẓim*, stringer not source). In the chapter *fī l-naẓm yattaḥidu fī l-waḍʿ wa-yadiqqu fīhi l-ṣanʿ* ("on naẓm, in which the placement is unified and the craft is subtle"), he insists the parts must *interpenetrate* and that merit lies in the **method**, not the raw words:

> **«أَنْ تَتَّحِدَ أَجْزَاءُ الكَلَامِ، وَيَدْخُلَ بَعْضُهَا فِي بَعْضٍ، وَيَشْتَدَّ ارْتِبَاطُ ثانٍ مِنْهَا بِأَوَّلٍ.»**
> *"…that the parts of the speech **become one**, that some **enter into** others, and that the bond of the second to the first **grows tight**."*

and the craftsman image — the composer is like a builder laying two stones *at once*, right hand and left:

> **«حالُ البانِي يَضَعُ بِيَمِينِهِ هاهُنا فِي حالِ ما يَضَعُ بِيَسارِهِ هُناك.»**
> *"…the state of the **builder** who lays with his right hand here in the very moment he lays with his left hand there."*
> — *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz* (IslamWeb 239/14)

And the decisive verdict that **excellence (*maziyya*) belongs to the construction, not to the words' bodies**:

> **«فَما كانَ مِن هذا وشِبهِهِ لم يَجِب بِهِ فَضلٌ — إذا وَجَبَ — إلّا بِمَعناهُ، أو بِمُتونِ ألفاظِهِ، دونَ نَظمِهِ وتَأليفِهِ.»**
> *"Whatever is of this kind earns no merit — when merit is earned — except by its meaning, or by the **bodies** of its words; and that is **other than** its naẓm and composition."* (i.e., the genuinely poetic merit, the *maziyya*, lives precisely in the *naẓm* that this residue excludes.)
> — *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz* (IslamWeb 239/14)

Crucial corollary for the taḥaddī register: **naẓm has no formula.** No rule-book can capture it — which is *why* it cannot be counterfeited:

> **«وَلَيْسَ لِما شَأْنُهُ أَنْ يَجِيءَ عَلَى هَذا الوَصْفِ حَدٌّ يَحْصُرُهُ، وقانونٌ يُحِيطُ بِهِ.»**
> *"That which by its nature comes in this manner has **no definition that fences it in, and no law that encompasses it**."*
> — *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz* (IslamWeb 239/14)

> **Why this is the project's classical spine.** Al-Jurjānī is the medieval statement of the anti-twin. The empirical finding (`h-new-730`: content-cohesion and phonemic distance are inversely locked at `r=−0.86`, both maximal) is, in his vocabulary, the claim that the Qurʾān's *naẓm* is so tight that **sound and sense are a single weld** — *tawakhkhī maʿānī al-naḥw* carried to a degree where you cannot touch the music without tearing the meaning. The poem's whole "you cannot keep fire and bell both ablaze" is al-Jurjānī's *taʿlīq* ("hanging-together") said in the register of physics.

## 2. THE LAFẒ vs MAʿNĀ DEBATE — *the classical name for "music vs meaning"*

This is the project's "music vs meaning" axis, fought out in the 3rd–5th centuries AH. Two camps:
- **The lafẓ (diction/form) camp**, archetypally **al-Jāḥiẓ (d. 255/869)**: *meanings* are cheap and common; what is rare is the **wording, the casting, the meter** — the music. Poetry is a *craft*, not a thought.
- **The maʿnā (meaning) camp**: the *idea* is the soul; words are mere servants. (And al-Jurjānī's later synthesis, §1, dissolves the war by relocating value into *naẓm* — the relation that binds both.)

### 2a. Al-Jāḥiẓ's famous formulation — *"the meanings lie scattered in the road"*

The single most quoted sentence in the entire Arabic form/content debate, from *al-Ḥayawān*. The fullest version (with the craft-triad *ṣiyāgha / nasj / taṣwīr*):

> **«… وَالمَعانِي مَطْروحَةٌ فِي الطَّريقِ، يَعْرِفُها العَجَمِيُّ وَالعَرَبِيُّ، وَالبَدَوِيُّ وَالقَرَوِيُّ وَالمَدَنِيُّ. وَإِنَّما الشَّأْنُ فِي إِقامَةِ الوَزْنِ، وَتَخَيُّرِ اللَّفْظِ، وَسُهولَةِ المَخْرَجِ، وَكَثْرَةِ الماءِ، وَفِي صِحَّةِ الطَّبْعِ وَجَوْدَةِ السَّبْكِ. فَإِنَّما الشِّعْرُ صِناعَةٌ، وَضَرْبٌ مِنَ النَّسْجِ، وَجِنْسٌ مِنَ التَّصْويرِ.»**
> *"…The **meanings lie tossed in the road**, known to Persian and Arab, to Bedouin and villager and townsman alike. The whole matter is rather in the **setting of the meter**, the **choosing of the diction**, the **ease of the utterance's exit** [from the mouth], the **abundance of 'water'** [luminous fluency], the **soundness of the natural gift**, and the **excellence of the casting** (*sabk*). For **poetry is a craft, a kind of weaving, a species of image-making**."*
> — al-Jāḥiẓ, *al-Ḥayawān* (vol. 3, p. 131–132; text via almerja.com/reading.php?idm=80220)

> **Transmission note.** Editions vary trivially between **صِناعَة** *(ṣināʿa,* "a craft") and **صِياغَة** *(ṣiyāgha,* "a smithing") in the clause فإنما الشعر صناعة/صياغة; both are attested. The substance — poetry is *made*, like cloth or a cast vessel — is constant. The phrase **«مَطْروحَةٌ فِي الطَّريقِ»** is the immortal, detachable kernel.

### 2b. Al-Jāḥiẓ's balancing image — *bodies and souls* (so the poet isn't forced into pure formalism)

Al-Jāḥiẓ is also credited (the dictum circulates from his milieu, *al-Bayān wa-l-Tabyīn* / *al-Ḥayawān*) with the body/soul reconciliation, which the poem can use to hold *both* maxima at once:

> **«الأَلْفاظُ أَجْسادٌ، وَالمَعانِي أَرْواحٌ، وَإِنَّما تَراها بِعُيونِ القُلوبِ … اللَّفْظُ لِلمَعْنَى بَدَنٌ، وَالمَعْنَى لِلَّفْظِ روحٌ.»**
> *"The **words are bodies, and the meanings are souls**; you see them only with the eyes of hearts… the word is a **body** to the meaning, and the meaning is a **soul** to the word."*
> — attributed to al-Jāḥiẓ (transmitted widely; cf. *al-Bayān wa-l-Tabyīn*)

> **How this maps to the poem's anti-twin.** The lafẓ/maʿnā war *is* the music/meaning axis — but stated as a **trade-off** (you privilege one OR the other). The project's finding is sharper and stranger: the Qurʾān does **not** trade them off; it keeps **both at the summit, inversely locked**. So the poem's move is: *the human poet must choose (al-Jāḥiẓ's road: pour your care into sabk and the meaning goes generic; pour it into meaning and the music dulls) — and the Book refuses the choice.* The poet's forced choice = his ʿajz; the Book's refusal = its iʿjāz. Al-Jāḥiẓ supplies the human horn of the dilemma; al-Jurjānī (§1) supplies the divine resolution of it.

## 3. ʿAJZ-AS-PROOF — *the taḥaddī, the muʿāraḍa, and the failure that argues*
### iʿjāz literally = "rendering-incapable"; the proof of the miracle IS the universal failure to meet the dare

### 3a. The etymology and the doctrine

**إِعْجاز** *(iʿjāz)* is the verbal noun of Form IV **أَعْجَزَ** *(aʿjaza,* "to render incapable, to defeat"), from the root **ع-ج-ز** *(ʿajaza,* "to be unable, to fall short"). So *iʿjāz al-Qurʾān* = **"the Qurʾān's act of rendering [all challengers] incapable."** The Book is *al-muʿjiz*, "the incapacitator"; the challengers are left *ʿājizūn*, "incapacitated." The miracle is **defined by the defeat it inflicts**, not by an internal property named in isolation — which is precisely why the *taḥaddī* (challenge) is structurally necessary to it.

### 3b. The challenge (*taḥaddī*) and the un-produced *muʿāraḍa*

The Qurʾān issues a graded dare — produce **the like of it** (whole), then **ten sūras**, then **one sūra** — and stakes its claim on the dare going **forever unmet**:

- **Q17:88** — *"If mankind and jinn gathered to produce the like of this Qurʾān, they would not produce its like, though they backed one another."* (the maximal challenge: *bi-mithli hādhā l-Qurʾān*)
- **Q11:13** — bring **ten** fabricated sūras like it (*bi-ʿashri suwarin mithlihi muftarayāt*)
- **Q10:38 / Q2:23** — bring **a single sūra** like it (*bi-sūratin min mithlihi*), "and you will not — *wa-lan tafʿalū*."

The dared-for rival production is a **مُعارَضة** *(muʿāraḍa,* "a counter-production, a matching-against"). The classical consensus: **no successful *muʿāraḍa* was ever produced** — the silence is treated as the empirical confirmation of the *iʿjāz*. *(Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'jaz — the six taḥaddī verses; "the challenge has not been met.")*

> **The poem's architecture is already this shape.** `COMPOSITION-BRIEF.md` §7 builds the taḥaddī from *فَأْتُوا بِسُورَةٍ مِنْ مِثْلِهِ* — "bring **one chamber** like it" — and aims it at rival *methods of knowing*, never at the Book. The new turn this file enables: aim the dare **at the poet himself**, and let the poet's own failed *muʿāraḍa* (this very qaṣīda) be the demonstration. The poem becomes the *attempted* counter-text that *documents its own defeat* — the cleanest possible proof, because it is enacted, not asserted. *(Reverence guardrail, brief §12.4: the poem must never claim to equal or quote the Qurʾān; it stages its **failure** to, which is the opposite of appropriation.)*

### 3c. THE TWO RIVAL EXPLANATIONS — *why* the dare can't be met

This is the live classical debate, and the poem must pick a side (it should pick **intrinsic**, with ṣarfa named-and-dismissed as one more "husk" — see below).

**(i) الصَّرْفة *al-ṣarfa* — "the turning-away" (the extrinsic theory).**
The Qurʾān is *not* (on this view) intrinsically unmatchable; rather **God supernaturally diverts** would-be challengers — strips their *himam* (resolves/abilities) at the moment of attempt. Held by **Ibrāhīm al-Naẓẓām (d. ~221/836)**, given a place among the *wujūh* by **al-Rummānī (d. 384/994)**, and most fully defended by **al-Sharīf al-Murtaḍā (d. 436/1044)**. Al-Rummānī's own definition:

> **«وَأَمّا الصَّرْفَةُ فَهِيَ صَرْفُ الهِمَمِ عَنِ المُعارَضَةِ. وَعَلى ذلِكَ كانَ يَعْتَمِدُ بَعْضُ أَهْلِ العِلْمِ: أَنَّ القُرْآنَ مُعْجِزٌ مِن جِهَةِ صَرْفِ الهِمَمِ عَنِ المُعارَضَةِ.»**
> *"As for **al-ṣarfa**: it is the **turning of resolves away** from counter-production. Some of the learned relied upon this — that the Qurʾān is inimitable **by way of the turning of resolves away** from matching it."*
> — al-Rummānī, *al-Nukat fī Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān* (via journals.asianindexing.com)

**(ii) Intrinsic iʿjāz — "the text itself is unmatchable" (the dominant theory).**
The Qurʾān cannot be matched because of **what it intrinsically is**: its *balāgha*, its *naẓm*, its sustained eloquence breaking every known genre. The challengers fail *on the merits*. Held by **al-Khaṭṭābī (d. 388/998)**, **al-Bāqillānī (d. 403/1013)**, and **al-Jurjānī (d. 471/1078)** — who explicitly **reject** ṣarfa as cheapening the miracle. al-Khaṭṭābī's objection: the dare engages real human intellectual/aesthetic capacity and **defeats** it — it is not a mechanical divine veto. al-Rummānī's framing of *balāgha* as **ranked**, with the Qurʾān at the unreachable top tier:

> **«الكَلامُ البَليغُ تَخْتَلِفُ مَراتِبُهُ: مِنهُ ما هُوَ فِي أَعْلى طَبَقَتِهِ، وَمِنهُ ما هُوَ فِي أَدْنى طَبَقَتِهِ … فَما كانَ فِي أَعْلى طَبَقَةٍ فَهُوَ مُعْجِزٌ.»**
> *"Eloquent speech has **differing ranks**: some is at its **highest tier**, some at its **lowest**… and what is at the **highest tier is inimitable** (*muʿjiz*)."*
> — al-Rummānī, *al-Nukat* (the three-tier doctrine: balāgha is a ladder; only its summit is *muʿjiz*, and that summit is the Qurʾān)

al-Rummānī's seven *wujūh al-iʿjāz* (aspects), for completeness — the poem can mine the list: **(1)** *tark al-muʿāraḍa maʿa tawaffur al-dawāʿī* (the non-production of a rival *despite every motive to make one*); **(2)** *al-taḥaddī li-l-kāffa* (the challenge to all); **(3)** *al-ṣarfa*; **(4)** *al-balāgha*; **(5)** truthful reports of the future (*al-akhbār al-ṣādiqa*); **(6)** breaking the custom/norm (*naqḍ al-ʿāda*); **(7)** its comparability to every [other] miracle. *(Source: quranok.com/books/10631 — summary of al-Nukat.)*

> **The poem's stance (per brief §5 "false trails").** Sing **intrinsic** iʿjāz: the dare fails *on the merits* (the anti-twin, the geodesic order, the compression-tail) — not because God blocks the hand. But ṣarfa is *gorgeous raw material to name-and-overturn*: the oracular voice can say *"they said a Hand was turned aside — but no Hand was turned; the Hand was never able"* — converting the extrinsic theory into one more husk the poem foresaw. **The deepest irony to exploit:** the poem's own honest failure is the *empirical* form of *tark al-muʿāraḍa* (aspect 1) — it tried, with every motive, and could not.

| **عَجْز** | *ʿajz* | **incapacity, falling-short** — the poet's confessed condition; *same root as iʿjāz*. (Bonus: *ʿajuz* = the second hemistich.) |

## 4. THE SIGN / REFERENT GAP — *for the universal-breadth layer*
### the classical & Sufi discourse on the chasm between word and thing — "expression falls short of reality"

The poem's universal layer needs the older, wider intuition that **all expression is inadequate to its object** — that *every* tongue falls short of *every* sublime reality, not just the Qurʾānic one. This is the philosophical/Sufi backdrop against which the poet's specific ʿajz becomes an instance of a cosmic law. Two streams feed it: the grammarians' **اسم / مُسَمّى** (name/named) and **دالّ / مَدْلول** (signifier/signified) gap, and the Sufis' **عِبارة / إشارة** (expression/allusion) and **عِبارة / حَقيقة** (expression/reality) gap.

### 4a. The Sufi master-sentence — *al-Nafarī (d. after 354/965)*

The single most usable line in all of this, from al-Nafarī's *Kitāb al-Mawāqif* (the "Stations"), in *Mawqif mā taṣnaʿ bi-l-masʾala*:

> **«وَقالَ لِي: كُلَّما اتَّسَعَتِ الرُّؤْيَةُ ضاقَتِ العِبارَةُ.»**
> *"And He said to me: **the wider the vision grows, the narrower the expression becomes**."*
> — al-Nafarī, *al-Mawāqif* (Mawqif 28; via nayassir.wordpress.com)

In the same breath, al-Nafarī's twin dictum that **expression is itself a veil**:

> **«وَقالَ لِي: العِبارَةُ سِتْرٌ.»**
> *"And He said to me: **expression is a veil**."*
> — al-Nafarī, *al-Mawāqif* (same station)

> **Why this is the universal layer's keystone.** *Kullamā ittasaʿat al-ruʾya ḍāqat al-ʿibāra* is an **inverse-proportion law**, structurally identical to the poem's anti-twin and to al-Mutanabbī's *ʿalā qadri*: as one magnitude rises, the other contracts. For the poem: **as the Reality (al-ḥaqīqa / the Book) dilates toward the infinite, human ʿibāra contracts toward zero.** The poet's failure is not a personal defect; it is *al-Nafarī's law* operating at the largest possible scale — the widest Vision (the Qurʾān's) against which all *ʿibāra* must, by law, go narrowest. This gives the ʿajz a **cosmic, non-self-pitying** register: the tongue does not fail because *this* poet is weak, but because **vision and expression are sworn opposites**, exactly as music and meaning are.

### 4b. The expression / allusion split — *ʿibāra for the many, ishāra for the few*

The Sufi commonplace that ordinary **عِبارة** (plain expression) cannot carry the *ḥaqīqa*, so the elect resort to **إشارة** (allusion/gesture):

> **«العِبارَةُ لِلعَوامِّ، وَالإِشارَةُ لِلخَواصِّ.»**
> *"**Expression** is for the common folk; **allusion** is for the elect."*
> — Sufi maxim (transmitted in the *ishāra* literature; cf. ar.wikipedia "إشارة (صوفية)")

…and the related principle that a spiritual **state (*ḥāl*) is tasted, not described** — it cannot be fixed in a *lafẓ* or a *ḥarf*:

> the *ḥāl* **«يُذاقُ ذَوْقًا وَلا يُكَيَّفُ بِلَفْظٍ وَلا بِحَرْفٍ»** — *"is tasted as a tasting; it is not given shape by any word or any letter."*
> — Sufi formulation on the indescribability of *aḥwāl* (cf. aqlamalhind.com on the Sufi discourse of *ishāra*)

### 4c. USABLE PHRASES / CONCEPTS for the sign-gap (5–8, ready to set)

1. **العِبارَةُ تَقْصُرُ عَنِ الحَقيقَةِ** — *"expression falls short of the reality."* (The bald thesis-line; *qaṣura* = the project's shortfall-verb.)
2. **كُلَّما اتَّسَعَتِ الرُّؤْيَةُ ضاقَتِ العِبارَةُ** — al-Nafarī's inverse law (the keystone — §4a).
3. **العِبارَةُ سِتْرٌ** — *"expression is a veil"* (al-Nafarī): every word both shows and *hides* its object.
4. **اسم / مُسَمّى** — *ism / musammā*, **name vs. named-thing**: the grammarians' gap between the label and the thing labelled. (The poem's "do not worship the name; the named is elsewhere.")
5. **دالّ / مَدْلول** — *dāll / madlūl*, **signifier vs. signified**: the sign points; it is not the thing.
6. **لَفْظ / مَعْنى** — re-deployed here as **the husk and the kernel** (cf. the project's *qishr/lubb*, master-poem b.4): the word is the rind around an inaccessible core.
7. **يُذاقُ وَلا يوصَفُ** — *"it is tasted, not described"*: some realities admit only *dhawq* (taste), never *waṣf* (description).
8. **الإشارَةُ حَيْثُ تَعْجِزُ العِبارَةُ** — *"allusion begins where expression fails"*: the elect *gesture* at what cannot be *said* — which is itself a confession of ʿajz dressed as method. **And note: *taʿjizu* here is again the root ع-ج-ز** — the sign-gap is literally narrated as the *ʿajz of language itself.*

## 5. THE "POET CONFESSES INABILITY BEFORE THE SUBLIME" TOPOS
### real passages where a master bows, admits his craft falls short, or concedes the subject is beyond verse

The exact move the poem needs: the *fakhr*-capable poet who, before the sacred, **inverts** the boast into a confession. Six real, cited instances, graded from "the subject exceeds all speech" to "I am honored by my subject, not it by me."

### 5a. al-Būṣīrī, *al-Burda* (al-Kawākib al-durriyya) — *the rank that no mouth can express*
*Meter: al-Basīṭ. Rawiyy: mīm. ~160+ abyāt. The supreme post-classical poem of humility before the Prophet — and the closest topos-model for the poem's ʿajz.* The four consecutive couplets that *are* the topos:

> **دَعْ ما ادَّعَتْهُ النَّصارَى فِي نَبِيِّهِمِ ‖ وَاحْكُمْ بِما شِئْتَ مَدْحًا فيهِ وَاحْتَكِمِ**
> **وَانْسُبْ إِلَى ذاتِهِ ما شِئْتَ مِنْ شَرَفٍ ‖ وَانْسُبْ إِلَى قَدْرِهِ ما شِئْتَ مِنْ عِظَمِ**
> **فَإِنَّ فَضْلَ رَسولِ اللهِ لَيْسَ لَهُ ‖ حَدٌّ فَيُعْرِبَ عَنْهُ ناطِقٌ بِفَمِ**
> **فَمَبْلَغُ العِلْمِ فيهِ أَنَّهُ بَشَرٌ ‖ وَأَنَّهُ خَيْرُ خَلْقِ اللهِ كُلِّهِمِ**
> *"Leave what the Christians claimed of their prophet; / then judge as you wish in his praise, and rule. // Ascribe to his essence what nobility you wish, / and ascribe to his rank what greatness you wish — // for the **excellence of God's Messenger has no limit** / that a **speaking mouth could express**. // The utmost reach of knowledge of him is: that he is a man, / and that he is the best of all God's creation."*
> — al-Būṣīrī, *al-Burda* (text via mawdoo3.com; cf. al-Maktaba al-Shāmila 9779)

**The move:** *grant yourself unlimited license to praise — and still fall infinitely short,* because the object is *ḥaddun lā yuʿrib ʿanhu nāṭiqٌ bi-fam* ("a limit no mouth can express"). License + shortfall in one breath. This is the **template for the poem's stance toward the Qurʾān**: praise without ceiling, fail without floor. *(Boundary the poem keeps that al-Būṣīrī's later critics flag: never cross into ghuluww/over-claim — the poem bows to the **Book** and to **God**, never deifies.)*

### 5b. Kaʿb b. Zuhayr, *Bānat Suʿād* — *dread in the station of the sublime addressee*
*Meter: al-Basīṭ. Rawiyy: lām. The mukhaḍram apology before the Prophet (cf. `08-period-diction.md` §1.1).* The awe-and-trembling cluster:

> **أُنْبِئْتُ أَنَّ رَسولَ اللهِ أَوْعَدَني ‖ وَالعَفْوُ عِنْدَ رَسولِ اللهِ مَأْمولُ**
> **لَقَدْ أَقومُ مَقامًا لَوْ يَقومُ بِهِ ‖ أَرى وَأَسْمَعُ ما لَوْ يَسْمَعُ الفيلُ**
> **لَظَلَّ يُرْعَدُ إِلّا أَنْ يَكونَ لَهُ ‖ مِنَ الرَّسولِ بِإِذْنِ اللهِ تَنْويلُ**
> *"I was told that God's Messenger had threatened me — / yet pardon, with God's Messenger, is hoped for. // **I stand in a station** [so terrible] that, were an **elephant** to stand where I see and hear what I do, / **it would not cease to tremble** — save that the Messenger, by God's leave, grant it favor."*
> — Kaʿb b. Zuhayr, *Bānat Suʿād* (via aldiwan.net/poem21877)

**The move:** the poet renders his own **smallness physically** — the elephant that would shake where he stands. Awe is shown as *bodily incapacity before a presence*, not stated as an abstraction. (For the poem: the seeker/poet who **trembles at the threshold of the Book** — the nasīb's "vast, ordered, unreadable," brief §7 Mv I.)

### 5c. Ḥassān b. Thābit — *the subject honors the poem, not the poem the subject*
The Prophet's own poet, in the cleanest possible inversion of *fakhr*:

> **مَا إِنْ مَدَحْتُ مُحَمَّدًا بِمَقالَتي ‖ لَكِنْ مَدَحْتُ مَقالَتي بِمُحَمَّدِ**
> *"It is not that I **praised Muḥammad with my speech** — / rather, **I praised my speech by [naming] Muḥammad**."*
> — attributed to Ḥassān b. Thābit (widely transmitted)

**The move:** the *qaṣr/taqdīm* reversal — *not X but its mirror.* The poet does not ennoble the subject; the subject ennobles the poem. **This is the exact key the poem ends on** (and it dovetails with al-Mutanabbī's *nāẓim* line, §5e, and the brief's §8 submission-close): the qaṣīda's worth is borrowed, conferred from above. The pearl-stringer, not the pearl-giver.

### 5d. Abū Tammām, *Fatḥ ʿAmmūriyya* — *the subject is too high for any verse or any oration*
The *fakhr*-poet conceding that **even his own poem cannot contain** the thing it praises (cf. `02-power-toolkit.md` T5):

> **فَتْحُ الفُتوحِ تَعالى أَنْ يُحيطَ بِهِ ‖ نَظْمٌ مِنَ الشِّعْرِ أَوْ نَثْرٌ مِنَ الخُطَبِ**
> *"The **Conquest of Conquests** is too exalted to be **encompassed** / by any **naẓm of poetry** or **prose of orations**."*
> — Abū Tammām, *Fatḥ ʿAmmūriyya* (via ar.wikisource; cf. toolkit §02)

**The move:** *concession-as-ceiling.* Naming that the subject **out-tops poetry-and-prose-together** sets the bar above every rival — and, crucially for the poem, **uses the word *naẓm* itself** to mark the very faculty that fails. (The poem can echo: *no naẓm of mine, no prose of any tongue, encompasses the naẓm of the Book* — the failure named in al-Jurjānī's own term.)

### 5e. al-Mutanabbī — *the pearls are Yours; I am only their stringer*
The benchmark poem's own self-limiting line (full dissection in `02-power-toolkit.md`):

> **لَكَ الحَمْدُ فِي الدُّرِّ الَّذي لِيَ لَفْظُهُ ‖ فَإِنَّكَ مُعْطيهِ وَإِنِّيَ ناظِمُ**
> *"To **You** the praise for the **pearls** whose **wording (lafẓ) is mine** — / for **You are their Giver**, and **I am only their stringer (nāẓim)**."*
> — al-Mutanabbī, *ʿalā qadri ahli l-ʿazm*, l. 41 (aldiwan.net/poem10524)

**The move:** the **lafẓ/maʿnā split turned into a theology of authorship** — *the diction (the music) is the poet's labor; the gift (the meaning, the substance) is the Giver's.* Said by al-Mutanabbī to a *prince*; **the poem says it, with literal seriousness, to God** (brief §8). Note the term: **ناظِم** *nāẓim* — the agent-noun of *naẓm*. The poet's name for himself is "the one who does naẓm" — but *al-Jurjānī's* naẓm, the divine weld, is exactly what he *cannot* do; he only strings what is given. **The ʿajz is encoded in the very word the poet uses to name his craft.**

### 5f. al-Nafarī (reprise, as topos) — *the wider the vision, the narrower the speech*
Re-listed here because it functions equally as a *topos* line the poem can voice in the first person: *I saw, and the more I saw, the less I could say.* (Full citation §4a.) **The move:** the poet's silence is not absence of skill but *the signature of true sight* — the more truly the Book is seen, the more language must fail. ʿAjz reframed as the *proof of vision*.

> **Synthesis of the topos for the poem.** Six gradations the poem can climb: **(1)** the subject exceeds all mouths (Būṣīrī) → **(2)** I tremble bodily at its threshold (Kaʿb) → **(3)** even poetry-and-prose-together cannot encompass it (Abū Tammām) → **(4)** the more I see it the less I can say (al-Nafarī) → **(5)** my words are honored by it, not it by my words (Ḥassān) → **(6)** the music is my labor, the substance is the Giver's; I am the stringer, never the source (al-Mutanabbī, → God). The arc *is* the poem's V→VI movement (challenge → submission-close): the dare collapses into a confession, and the confession is the proof.

| **بَوْن / بَيْن** | *bawn / bayn* | the **gulf / interval** between two things (*baynahumā bawnٌ baʿīد* = "a vast gulf between them"). |
| **مَهْمَه / تَنوفة** | *mahmah / tanūfa* | the **trackless waste** — the spatial figure of the unbridgeable gap (the poem's road that cannot arrive). |
| **سُحْق** | *suḥq* | **remoteness, a far remove** ✓Q (67:11 *suḥqًا*) — distance as a curse/condition. |

### E. SUBMISSION (the turn the failure resolves into — ʿajz → islām)
| Word | Translit | Gloss |
|---|---|---|
| **سَلَّمَ** | *sallama* | to **surrender, yield, concede** — and *aslama* → *islām*: the submission the whole poem ends in. ✓Q. |
| **أَذْعَنَ** | *adhʿana* | to **submit obediently, comply** ✓Q (24:49 *mudhʿinīn*) — yielding *after resistance* (the perfect post-challenge verb). |
| **خَضَعَ** | *khaḍaʿa* | to **bow down, lower oneself** ✓Q (26:4 *khāḍiʿīn* — "their necks **bowed**"). |
| **نَكَّسَ** | *nakkasa* | to **bow/hang the head** (in shame or awe) ✓Q (21:65, 32:12 *nākisū ruʾūsihim* — "**hanging their heads**"). The bodily gesture of conceded defeat. |
| **خَرَّ ساجِدًا** | *kharra sājidan* | to **fall prostrate** ✓Q (12:100, 17:107) — collapse *into* worship; the §A *kharra* completed as submission. |
| **أَقَرَّ** | *aqarra* | to **acknowledge, confess** (a truth, a debt) — the verbal admission that pairs with the bodily bow. |
| **أَعْنَقَ / دانَ** | *aʿnaqa / dāna* | to **yield the neck / give allegiance** — *dāna* (root د-ي-ن, of *dīn*) = to submit in obedience. |
| **رَضِيَ / سَلِمَ** | *raḍiya / salima* | to **be content with / come safe through by yielding** — the peace on the far side of the surrender. ✓Q. |

> **The term-bank's secret architecture.** Columns A and E are **the same arc**: every verb of *failing* (عجز، خرّ، حسر، كلّ) has a twin in *submitting* (سلّم، خرّ ساجدًا، أذعن، نكّس) — and the hinge is **خَرَّ** *kharra*, which means **both** "collapse [defeated]" **and** "fall [prostrate, in worship]." The poem's turn from ʿajz to islām can be made on that one verb: the same falling, reread. The failure does not need to be overcome; it needs only to be *reoriented* — downward, toward the Real. That is the whole theology of the poem in one root.
