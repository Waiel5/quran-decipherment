# 08 — PERIOD-DICTION TOOLKIT (re-dictioning *al-Nūniyya al-Kubrā* into ~7th-c. mukhaḍram register)

**Purpose.** *master-v2.md* reads too modern: a sacred-architectural subject dragged in modern geometric/numeric vocabulary (المَدار، المَرْكَز، النُّقْطَة، الرَّقْم، الزَّيْف) over a smooth MSA surface. This file is the **reference toolkit** — model texts, a bayt-by-bayt purge-list, a concept-lexicon in desert realia, gharīb injectors, and a register checklist — from which the poet re-dictions by hand. **It does not rewrite the poem.**

**Target register.** A *mukhaḍram* poet (lived across jāhiliyya → Islam) singing a sacred subject in full archaic desert register: the texture of Kaʿb b. Zuhayr's *Bānat Suʿād*, Labīd, Zuhayr, al-Nābigha, Ḥassān — Qurʾanic diction admitted where it is itself archaic.

**Sources are cited inline.** All Arabic verses below were fetched verbatim from al-Diwan (aldiwan.net), Arabic Wikisource (ar.wikisource.org), Arabic Wikipedia (ar.wikipedia.org), and al-Maʿrifa (marefa.org); Qurʾanic attestations by sūra:āya. Where a transmission varies, the variant is flagged.

## PART 1 — MODEL TEXTS (real, cited) + what makes them read ANCIENT

### 1.1 Kaʿb b. Zuhayr, *Bānat Suʿād* (*Qaṣīdat al-Burda*) — THE key model
*Mukhaḍram praise of the Prophet. Meter: al-Basīṭ. Rawiyy: lām (‑ūl). ~57–59 bayts. The single closest analogue to the poem's task: archaic desert texture carrying a sacred subject.*
Sources: [al-Diwan](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem21877.html); [Wikisource](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/قصيدة_بانت_سعاد); [al-Maʿrifa](https://www.marefa.org/قصيدة_البردة_(كعب_بن_زهير)).

**Nasīb (the beloved departs):**
> بانَتْ سُعادُ فَقَلْبي اليَوْمَ مَتْبولُ ‖ مُتَيَّمٌ إثْرَها لَمْ يُفْدَ مَكْبولُ
> وَما سُعادُ غَداةَ البَيْنِ إِذْ رَحَلوا ‖ إِلّا أَغَنُّ غَضيضُ الطَّرْفِ مَكْحولُ
> هَيْفاءُ مُقْبِلَةً عَجْزاءُ مُدْبِرَةً ‖ لا يُشْتَكى قِصَرٌ مِنها وَلا طولُ

**The mirage / desert-waste (camel-journey realia):**
> تَنْفِي الرِّياحُ القَذى عَنْهُ وَأَفْرَطَهُ ‖ مِنْ صَوْبِ سارِيَةٍ بِيضٌ يَعاليلُ

**The gnomic turn (mortality — ḥikma in the middle of praise):**
> كُلُّ ابْنِ أُنْثى وَإِنْ طالَتْ سَلامَتُهُ ‖ يَوْماً عَلى آلَةٍ حَدْباءَ مَحْمولُ
> ("Every son of woman, however long he is spared, is one day borne upon a humped bier." — *ālah ḥadbāʾ* = the bier; pure archaic kenning for death.)

**The prophet-praise cluster (sacred subject, ancient idiom):**
> أُنْبِئْتُ أَنَّ رَسولَ اللهِ أَوْعَدَني ‖ وَالعَفْوُ عِنْدَ رَسولِ اللهِ مَأْمولُ
> لَقَدْ أَقومُ مَقاماً لَوْ يَقومُ بِهِ ‖ أَرى وَأَسْمَعُ ما لَوْ يَسْمَعُ الفيلُ
> إِنَّ الرَّسولَ لَسَيْفٌ يُسْتَضاءُ بِهِ ‖ مُهَنَّدٌ مِنْ سُيوفِ اللهِ مَسْلولُ
> في عُصْبَةٍ مِنْ قُرَيْشٍ قالَ قائِلُهُمْ ‖ بِبَطْنِ مَكَّةَ لَمّا أَسْلَموا: زولوا

> **Transmission note.** The 4th verse circulates in two readings: **لَسَيْفٌ** ("a *sword* by which light is sought") — the reading in al-Diwan and Wikisource — and **لَنورٌ** ("a *light*"). The *sayf* reading is the dominant/older transmission; *nūr* is the popular variant. If you echo this line, **لَسَيْفٌ ... مُهَنَّدٌ مِنْ سُيوفِ اللهِ مَسْلولُ** is the more authentically-attested.

**What makes it read ANCIENT (extract):**
- **The tripartite qaṣīda spine intact:** nasīb (Suʿād) → raḥīl (the she-camel, dozens of lines of pure desert realia) → madīḥ (the Prophet). The poem's own nasīb→road→close arc is correct; *Bānat Suʿād* proves you may carry a sacred climax on a jāhilī chassis.
- **Kennings for abstractions, never the abstraction.** Death is not *al-mawt* but *ālah ḥadbāʾ* (a humped bier). This is the master-move the poem needs for "math."
- **Gharīb realia studding:** *yaʿālīl* (swelling white rain-clouds/bubbles), *muhannad* (Indian-steel blade), the tick that slides off the camel's hide — concrete, tribal, technical-of-the-desert.
- **Asseverative + nominal openings:** إِنَّ الرَّسولَ ل... ، أُنْبِئْتُ أَنَّ... ، لَقَدْ... — heavy use of إِنّ / لَ / قَدْ as line-openers (the "sworn-testimony" texture).
- **Fronted predicate, verbal sentences:** بانَتْ سُعادُ (verb-first), تَنْفِي الرِّياحُ القَذى (verb-subject-object).

### 1.2 Labīd b. Rabīʿa, *al-Muʿallaqa* — the mukhaḍram who became Muslim
*Meter: **al-Kāmil**. Rawiyy: **mīm**. **This is the poem's own meter** (al-Kāmil), so Labīd is the prosodic-texture model par excellence.* 88 bayts. Source: [al-Diwan](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem21196.html); cf. [al-Zawzanī, Sharḥ al-Muʿallaqāt](https://shamela.ws/book/11253/183).

> عَفَتِ الدِّيارُ مَحَلُّها فَمُقامُها ‖ بِمِنىً تَأَبَّدَ غَوْلُها فَرِجامُها
> فَمَدافِعُ الرَّيّانِ عُرِّيَ رَسْمُها ‖ خَلَقاً كَما ضَمِنَ الوُحِيَّ سِلامُها
> دِمَنٌ تَجَرَّمَ بَعْدَ عَهْدِ أَنيسِها ‖ حِجَجٌ خَلَوْنَ حَلالُها وَحَرامُها
> رُزِقَتْ مَرابيعَ النُّجومِ وَصابَها ‖ وَدْقُ الرَّواعِدِ جَوْدُها فَرِهامُها
> وَجَلا السُّيولُ عَنِ الطُّلولِ كَأَنَّها ‖ زُبُرٌ تُجِدُّ مُتونَها أَقْلامُها
> أَوْ رَجْعُ واشِمَةٍ أُسِفَّ نَؤورُها ‖ كِفَفاً تَعَرَّضَ فَوْقَهُنَّ وِشامُها
> فَوَقَفْتُ أَسْأَلُها وَكَيْفَ سُؤالُنا ‖ صُمّاً خَوالِدَ ما يُبينُ كَلامُها

**What makes it read ANCIENT (extract):**
- **Toponyms as load-bearing nouns:** Minā, al-Rayyān, al-Ghawl, al-Rijām — places named, not described. A jāhilī signature: the map is the emotion.
- **The writing-simile that the poem already wants:** *the floods bared the traces كَأَنَّها زُبُرٌ ... أَقْلامُها* — "as though they were **scriptures** whose lines the **reed-pens** refresh," and *rajʿu wāshimah* — the tattooer's re-inked lines. The aṭlāl read as **a re-inscribed text**: precisely the poem's "fresh ruin of revelation" image (bayt 1–2), done in 600 CE idiom. **Steal the mechanism: ruins = re-traced writing.**
- **Gharīb density:** *dimanun tajarrama, ḥijaj, wadqu r-rawāʿid, jawduhā fa-rihāmuhā* (downpour vs. drizzle, named), *ṣummā khawālid* (deaf, abiding stones). Rain and stones have *technical* names.
- **‑hā suffixes threading the rawiyy** (maḥalluhā, muqāmuhā, ghawluhā, rijāmuhā…): a cohesion device — the land's features declined in series, each landing the rhyme. Directly transferable to al-Kāmil/nūniyya.

### 1.3 Zuhayr b. Abī Sulmā, *al-Muʿallaqa* — the ḥikma register (gnomic close)
*Meter: al-Ṭawīl. Rawiyy: mīm. Praises Harim b. Sinān + al-Ḥārith for ending the War of Dāḥis. Its closing ḥikma cluster is the model for the poem's sentential/aphoristic lines (bayts 15–16, 18, 28).* Source: [al-Diwan](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem25065.html); cf. [al-Zawzanī](https://shamela.ws/book/11253/143).

> سَئِمْتُ تَكاليفَ الحَياةِ وَمَنْ يَعِشْ ‖ ثَمانينَ حَوْلاً لا أَبا لَكَ يَسْأَمِ
> رَأَيْتُ المَنايا خَبْطَ عَشْواءَ مَنْ تُصِبْ ‖ تُمِتْهُ وَمَنْ تُخْطِئْ يُعَمَّرْ فَيَهْرَمِ
> وَمَنْ لا يُصانِعْ في أُمورٍ كَثيرَةٍ ‖ يُضَرَّسْ بِأَنْيابٍ وَيوطَأْ بِمَنْسِمِ
> وَمَنْ هابَ أَسْبابَ المَنِيَّةِ يَلْقَها ‖ وَلَوْ رامَ أَسْبابَ السَّماءِ بِسُلَّمِ
> وَمَهْما تَكُنْ عِنْدَ امْرِئٍ مِنْ خَليقَةٍ ‖ وَإِنْ خالَها تَخْفى عَلى الناسِ تُعْلَمِ
> وَمَنْ يَجْعَلِ المَعْروفَ مِنْ دونِ عِرْضِهِ ‖ يَفِرْهُ وَمَنْ لا يَتَّقِ الشَّتْمَ يُشْتَمِ

**What makes it read ANCIENT (extract):**
- **Conditional-gnomic engine:** مَنْ يَفْعَلْ ... يَلْقَ ... (jussive apodosis). The aphorism is built on شرط/جزاء, not on abstract nouns. The poem's *kullu l-ḥurūfi makānuhā, lā tantaziʿ ḥarfan* (b.28) is exactly this mode — keep it; extend it.
- **Death as a blind she-camel** (*khabṭa ʿashwāʾ*) trampling at random: again, the abstraction (Fate) rendered as a desert animal. The kenning, not the concept.
- **Realia of violence:** *yuḍarras bi-anyāb, yūṭaʾ bi-mansim* (ground under molars, trodden by a camel's foot-pad). No Latinate abstraction survives near this.
- **The ladder to heaven** (*sullam*) as the limit-image for the unreachable: a concrete object standing for the impossible — model for rendering "infinity / the unattainable like."

### 1.4 al-Nābigha al-Dhubyānī, *Iʿtidhāriyya* to al-Nuʿmān — the apology/awe register
*Meter: al-Ṭawīl. Rawiyy: bāʾ. The supreme jāhilī apology — model for the poem's submission/awe before a transcendent addressee (bayts 20, 29).* Source: [al-Diwan](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem2015.html).

> أَتاني — أَبَيْتَ اللَّعْنَ — أَنَّكَ لُمْتَني ‖ وَتِلْكَ الَّتي أُهْتَمُّ مِنْها وَأَنْصَبُ
> فَإِنَّكَ شَمْسٌ وَالمُلوكُ كَواكِبٌ ‖ إِذا طَلَعَتْ لَمْ يَبْدُ مِنْهُنَّ كَوْكَبُ
> فَلا تَتْرُكَنّي بِالوَعيدِ كَأَنَّني ‖ إِلى النّاسِ مَطْلِيٌّ بِهِ القارُ أَجْرَبُ

*(Famous companion line, widely transmitted in the same poem — the inescapable-night simile, the model for "the night wheels on Him," bayt 15):*
> فَإِنَّكَ كَاللَّيْلِ الَّذي هُوَ مُدْرِكي ‖ وَإِنْ خِلْتُ أَنَّ المُنْتَأى عَنْكَ واسِعُ

**What makes it read ANCIENT (extract):**
- **The vocative oath-formula أَبَيْتَ اللَّعْنَ** ("mayst thou avoid the curse") — a court-archaism with no modern equivalent; pure 6th-c. address.
- **Cosmic single-image hyperbole done plainly:** *you are the sun, kings are stars* — the SUN/STARS hierarchy as a flat assertion (فَإِنَّكَ شَمْسٌ), no simile-machinery. Model for the poem's tawḥīd center.
- **The inescapable night** as the figure of an overwhelming presence — the very image the poem reaches for in "the blessed night has wheeled on Him."
- **Concrete abjection:** *as though I were a scabby camel smeared with tar (qār)* — humiliation rendered as veterinary realia, never as an abstraction.

### 1.5 Ḥassān b. Thābit — Islamic praise in archaic form (+ his jāhilī fakhr)
*Poet of the Prophet; a mukhaḍram. Two registers worth modelling: (a) the famous praise; (b) his pre-Islamic Ghassānid lāmiyya, to feel the un-Islamicized desert base he is working from.*

**(a) Praise of the Prophet** (widely transmitted; not in all six recensions of the Dīwān, per editors — flag as popular-canonical):
Source: [al-Diwan](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem21530.html).
> وَأَحْسَنُ مِنْكَ لَمْ تَرَ قَطُّ عَيْني ‖ وَأَجْمَلُ مِنْكَ لَمْ تَلِدِ النِّساءُ
> خُلِقْتَ مُبَرَّأً مِنْ كُلِّ عَيْبٍ ‖ كَأَنَّكَ قَدْ خُلِقْتَ كَما تَشاءُ

**(b) Jāhilī/Ghassānid lāmiyya** (the desert-court base register):
Source: [al-Diwan](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem21361.html).
> لِلّهِ دَرُّ عِصابَةٍ نادَمْتُهُمْ ‖ يَوْماً بِجِلَّقَ في الزَّمانِ الأَوَّلِ
> يُغْشَوْنَ حَتّى ما تَهِرُّ كِلابُهُمْ ‖ لا يَسْأَلونَ عَنِ السَّوادِ المُقْبِلِ

**What makes it read ANCIENT (extract):**
- **لِلّهِ دَرُّ ...** — the admiration-formula ("God's bounty on…!"), a fossil expression, instant period-marker.
- **في الزَّمانِ الأَوَّلِ** — "in the first age": the elegiac time-frame, not a numbered epoch (contrast the poem's *alf ḥaqība*, below).
- **Hospitality realia as virtue-proof:** *their dogs do not even growl* (so constant are the guests); *they ask not who the approaching dark mass is* (they feast friend and stranger alike). Virtue shown by a single concrete desert scene.
- Even the praise of the Prophet stays in *plain superlative + كَأَنَّ* — no abstraction; the body ("no eye has seen fairer") carries the theology.

### 1.6 Cross-model synthesis — the FIVE ancient-texture engines to install
1. **Kenning over concept.** Death = *ālah ḥadbāʾ*; Fate = *khabṭ ʿashwāʾ*; the unreachable = *sullam ilā s-samāʾ*. → Render "math/number/order" only as desert realia (Part 3).
2. **Toponyms & named realia.** Minā, al-Rayyān, Jillaq; *muhannad, qār, mansim, yaʿālīl*. → Replace generic nouns with specific archaic ones (Parts 3–4).
3. **Asseverative openings.** إِنَّ … لَ… ، قَدْ ، أُنْبِئْتُ ، لِلّهِ دَرُّ ، أَبَيْتَ اللَّعْنَ. → Open lines with oath/testimony particles.
4. **Verb-first / fronted-predicate syntax.** بانَتْ سُعادُ ؛ عَفَتِ الدِّيارُ ؛ رَأَيْتُ المَنايا. → Prefer the verbal sentence; front the predicate in nominal ones.
5. **Conditional-gnomic apodosis** for any sententious line. مَنْ … يَلْقَ … → Build aphorisms on شرط/جزاء, not on ‑iyya abstractions.

## PART 2 — PURGE-LIST for *master-v2* (bayt by bayt)

Format: **bayt#** → flagged item — *why it reads modern / wrong* → **period replacements** (2–3).
"OK/keep" notes mark what is already authentic, so you don't over-correct.

**1.** رَسْمٍ مِنَ الوَحْيِ — *rasm* (trace) is correctly archaic (cf. *ʿuriya rasmuhā*, Labīd). **Keep.** • مَكْنونْ is good Qurʾanic (*lūʾluʾun maknūn*, 52:24; 56:23). **Keep.** — No change; strong opening in register.

**2.** **أَلْفَ حَقيبَةٍ** ("a thousand saddlebags" = a thousand epochs) — *the worst single anachronism of texture*: **ḥaqība as a unit of *time*** is a **modern coinage** (MSA "حقبة زمنية / حِقَب" = era). A *ḥaqība* to a 7th-c. ear is the **leather saddle-bag**, not an epoch; "a thousand saddlebags of time" reads as a calque. → **أَحْقاباً** (Qurʾanic plural of *ḥuqb*, "long ages": *lābithīna fīhā aḥqābā*, 78:23) — but as **حُقْباً / أَحْقابا**, the *age*, never *ḥaqība*; or **دَهْراً فَدَهْراً** (age upon age); or **كَرَّ الجَديدَيْنِ** / **مَرَّ الفَرْقَدَيْنِ** (the wheeling of day-and-night / the two stars — time by celestial cycle). *Best:* drop the "thousand-container" conceit; let *al-dahru marra ʿalayhi* govern an **archaic age-word**.

**3.** بَحْرٌ تَغوصُ … العُبابِ — *ʿubāb* (the swell) is excellent archaic. **Keep.** • تُحيطُ بِقاعِهِ — *qāʿ* (sea-floor) fine. The whole bayt is in register. **Keep.**

**4.** قِشْرٍ … اللُّبُّ — husk/kernel: authentic. **Keep.** • مَضْنونْ (withheld, from *ḍ-n-n*) — good (cf. classical *maḍnūn bihi*). **Keep.**

**5.** قائِفٍ … يَدْعو الحُروفَ — *qāʾif* (track-reader) is a superb period word. **Keep.** • **خَرَّ الزَّمانَ بِبابِهِ** — "fell [for] the age at its gate": the adverbial accusative *al-zamāna* is strained; reads as compression, not idiom. → recast with **حِجَجاً** ("for [long] years," cf. Labīd *ḥijaj*) or **دَهْرَهُ** (his lifetime) or **حَوْلاً فَحَوْلاً** (year on year). • مَفْتونْ good (*fitna*). **Keep.**

**6.** سَيْرَ رَواحِلٍ ، اللَّيْلُ داجٍ ، الدَّليلُ مَأْمونْ — flawless caravan register. **Keep all.** (*dājin* = pitch-dark; *dalīl* = guide; *raḥā'il* = mounts.)

**7.** مَنْزِلْ فَمَنْزِلْ كالنُّجومِ — *manzil* doubles perfectly as way-station **and** lunar mansion (*manāzil al-qamar*). **Keep.** • صِدْقِ النَّجْمِ … مَقْرونْ — fine.

**8.** زاغَ نَجْمٌ … تَبَدَّلَ مَنْزِلٌ … المَشْحونْ — *zāgha* (Qurʾanic, *mā zāgha l-baṣar* 53:17) and *al-mashḥūn* (the laden ship, 26:119; 36:41) are both Qurʾanic. **Keep.** Strong.

**9.** الفَيافي تَنْطَوي ، صَدوقِ السَّيْرِ ، وِرْدٌ مَرْهونْ — *fayāfī* (waterless wastes), *wird* (the watering), *marhūn* (pledged): excellent. **Keep.**

**10.** بُرْدِ **البَيانِ** — **البَيان in the modern "rhetoric/eloquence" sense is anachronistic.** To the period ear *al-bayān* is plain "making-clear / declaration" (Qurʾanic *ʿallamahu l-bayān*, 55:4 — admissible) but as an aesthetic-rhetorical abstraction ("the cloak of *eloquence*") it is a *balāgha*-era technical sense (post-Jāḥiẓ). → **بُرْدِ القَوافي** (cloak of the rhymes), **بُرْدِ المَقالِ** / **القَوْلِ** (of the utterance), or concretely **بُرْدِ الوَحْيِ** (of the revelation) — name the cloth's *content*, not an abstraction. • نَسْجُهُ مَوْضونْ — *mawḍūn* (close-woven, Qurʾanic *sururun mawḍūnah* 56:15) is superb; **keep**. • خَيْطانِ تَعانَقا — fine.

**11.** يَنْأى المَعاني … جَرْسُها … مَسْجونْ — *jars* (the ringing of a sound) is authentically archaic; *masjūn* fine. The line is abstract in *idea* but its **lexis** is period. **Keep** (a "cold-strike" line). Watch only: *al-maʿānī* as grammatical-plural subject of singular verb is fine classically.

**12.** نارٌ وَغَيْثٌ … مَرْصونْ — *ghayth* (the rain that revives), *marṣūn* (welded/closely-set, *bunyānun marṣūṣ* 61:4 → here *marṣūn*) good. **Keep.** "مَوْطِنٍ" fine.

**13.** ذَرْوانِ … البَرْقُ … الغَيْثُ السَّخِيُّ مَطْعونْ — *dharwān* (two summits/peaks), *barq*, *ghayth*: register-perfect. **Keep.**

**14.** تيهِ السُّرى — *suran* (night-journey) + *tīh* (trackless waste): excellent (Part 3). **Keep.** • **فَإِذا المَدارُ مَوْطونْ** — **المَدار ("the orbit") is a modern astronomical noun.** No jāhilī attestation in the celestial-orbit sense. → **القُطْب** (the pole-star, *al-Jady*, the fixed pivot of the turning sky) — "fa-idhā l-quṭbu mawṭūnu"; or **الرَّحى** (the millstone whose turning has a fixed eye); or **مَدارَ الرَّحى** only if you keep the *millstone* frame explicit. *Best:* replace with **القُطْب** — the authentic "fixed point the heavens wheel on" (see bayt 15, 25).

**15.** أَوَّلٌ … آخِرٌ … واحِدٌ صَمَدٌ — pure Qurʾanic tawḥīd (57:3; 112:1–2). **Keep.** • عَلَيْهِ اللَّيْلُ دارَ المَيْمونْ — the night wheeling: this is exactly al-Nābigha's inescapable-night. **Keep.** *(If you want the pivot literal here, قُطْب slots in upstream, not this line.)*

**16.** لا كُفْءَ ، لا نِدَّ … الأَحَدْ … المَوْزونْ — *kufʾ* (112:4), *nidd*, *al-Aḥad*: Qurʾanic. *al-mawzūn* (the weighed/measured) — Qurʾanic *kulli shayʾin mawzūn* (15:19). **Keep all.** Strong "cold-strike."

**17.** السُّوَرْ شَعَّتْ … العُرْجونْ — *ʿurjūn* is the rare Qurʾanic word (*ka-l-ʿurjūni l-qadīm*, 36:39): the old date-stalk the moon returns to. **Keep** — this is a jewel, exactly the register wanted.

**18.** خاطَ … النَّسْجَ … الضِّدَّيْنِ … مَمْنونْ — *khāṭa* (stitched), *naṣj*, *mamnūn* (*ajrun ghayru mamnūn* 41:8; 95:6). **Keep** (volta line). • *al-ḍiddayn* (the two opposites) is a *kalām*/philosophy dual — borderline. To the period ear "two opposites" is fine as plain sense, but if you want to de-philosophize: **النَّقيضَيْنِ** is worse (more technical); keep **الضِّدَّيْنِ** or concretize to the actual pair (**النّارَ وَالغَيْثَ** — the fire and the rain, your own b.12). *Prefer naming the pair.*

**19.** صاغَهُ بَشَرٌ … تَنافَرَ حَدُّهُ … صَنيعُ مَغْبونْ — *ṣāgha* (forged/cast), *ḥadd* (edge), *ṣanīʿ* (handiwork), *maghbūn* (cheated, *taghābun* 64:9): all good. *tanāfara* (jarred/clashed) is period. **Keep.** • مَتْنَيْهِ — *matn* (the blade's body / a text's body): excellent double. **Keep.**

**20.** صُنْعٌ تَعالى بارِئٌ … المَأْفونْ — *ṣunʿ* (*ṣunʿa llāhi* 27:88), *al-Bāriʾ* (59:24), *taʿālā*, *maʾfūn* (feeble-witted): Qurʾanic/archaic. **Keep.** • "يُقاسَ" (be measured/analogized) — *qiyās* is faintly logicians' diction; the sense "be compared" is fine, but if purging: **يُداني** (be approached/rivalled) or **يُساوى**. Minor.

**21.** هاتوا نَظيرَ الحَرْفِ … هِمَمٌ … مَطْحونْ — *hātū* (the challenge-imperative, cf. *hātū burhānakum* 2:111; 27:64) is **the** Qurʾanic taḥaddī verb — excellent. *maṭḥūn* (milled) fine. **Keep.** • نَظيرَ ("the like of") good. *himam* (resolves) fine.

**22.** **لا تَعْبُدوا الرَّقْمَ** — **الرَّقْم ("the number/digit") is a flat anachronism.** To the 7th-c. ear *raqm* is a *mark/stripe woven or stamped on cloth* (*kitābun marqūm*, 83:9/20 — an *inscribed register*, not a numeral). "Do not worship the *digit*" reads as 21st-c. There is **no period word for the abstract numeral-as-idol**; render the *idea* (false reckoning made a god) with reckoning-realia. → **لا تَعْبُدوا العَدَّ** (the counting/tally), **لا تَعْبُدوا الحَصى** (the counting-pebbles — the diviner's reckoning), or **لا تَعْبُدوا الخَطَّ** (sand-divination, *khaṭṭ al-raml*). *Best:* **الحَصى** or **العَدَّ** — both name a *practice*, which an idol can be made of. • التِّمْثالُ صاغَهُ وَهْمٌ — *timthāl* (graven image, *tamāthīl* 21:52; 34:13) is perfect for "idol." **Keep.** • مِنَ الرَّمْلِ الذَّرورِ — *raml dharūr* (drifting/scattering sand): good. **Keep.** *mawhūn* (*wahn*, feebleness, 3:146) fine.

**23.** يَزِنونَ ريحًا … السَّرابَ يَظُنُّهُ وِرْدًا … كَيْلُهُمْ مَظْنونْ — *sarāb* (the mirage, *ka-sarābin bi-qīʿah* 24:39 — **the** Qurʾanic mirage-of-false-deeds, exactly your image), *wird*, *kayl* (measure, 12:60; 17:35), *maẓnūn* (surmised). **Keep all** — register-perfect; *sarāb* is the jewel here.

**24.** عَدَّدوا الحَرْفَيْنِ وَالميمَ … تاجَ النّونْ — *ʿaddadū* (tallied), *ḥarf*, *mīm*, *tāj* (crown): all fine; *tāj* is attested early (the crown). **Keep.** (Meter-flagged by you; lexis is sound.)

**25.** **وَالنّونُ قَوْسٌ حَوْلَ نُقْطَةِ مَرْكَزٍ ‖ نِصْفُ المَدارِ، بِها المَدارُ مَضْمونْ** — **the densest cluster of anachronism in the poem.**
 • **قَوْسٌ** ("bow/arc") — *this one is GOOD*: *qaws* is the archaic bow (and the rainbow, *qaws quzaḥ*); keep it for the curve. **Keep.**
 • **نُقْطَةِ** ("point/dot") — *nuqṭa* as the geometric **point** is post-classical mathematical diction; to the early ear *nuqṭa* is a *drop/blot of ink* — which actually *helps* you (the nūn's diacritical dot is an ink-drop). Reframe as **النُّقْطَةُ = the ink-drop on the nūn**, not "a center-point." Acceptable **only** in the ink sense; **not** as "center-point."
 • **مَرْكَزٍ** ("center") — **purge.** *Markaz* in the geometric-centre sense is modern; classically *markaz* ≈ a *fixed station/post* (where one is set, *r-k-z*). → **قُطْب** (the pivot — *quṭb al-raḥā*, the millstone's iron pivot-pin; also the pole-star), **سُرَّة** (the navel = center, *surrat al-wādī*), or **عَمود** (the tent-pole / axis). *Best:* **قُطْب** or **سُرَّة**.
 • **المَدار** (×2, "the orbit") — **purge** (as in b.14). → **الرَّحى** (the millstone — its turning *is* the circle, with the *quṭb* at its eye), **الهِلال** (the crescent — a half-ring), or **السِّوار** (the bangle — a closed ring). *Best, given the nūn is a curve+dot:* recast as **the bow around the ink-drop is half the RING; by it the RING is made whole** → قَوْسٌ حَوْلَ النُّقْطَةِ نِصْفُ **السِّوارِ** بِها **السِّوارُ** … (or نِصْفُ **الرَّحى** … الرَّحى). This keeps your geometry **and** sounds 7th-c.: a *bangle*/*millstone*, not an *orbit*.
 • مَضْمونْ (guaranteed/contained, *ḍamān*) — fine. **Keep.**

**26.** بِئْرُ القُدامى … تُرْوي الظَّما … وَجِفانُ **زَيْفٍ** … مَدْيونْ — *biʾr* (well), *al-qudāmā* (the ancients), *tarwī*, *al-ẓamā* (thirst), *jifān* (great basins/bowls, *jifānin ka-l-jawāb* 34:13 — Qurʾanic!): **excellent**. • **الزَّيْف ("counterfeit/fakeness")** — **purge.** *Zayf* in the "fake/phony" abstract sense is later; the early root *z-y-f* is of *culled/rejected (debased) coin* (*zuyūf* dirhams), narrowly numismatic and not a desert-realia word for falseness. The water-frame wants a *water* falseness. → **جِفانُ آلٍ** (basins of *mirage*-shimmer, *āl* = the noon-mirage), **جِفانُ سَرابٍ** (of mirage), or **جِفانٌ بُهْرُجٌ / زورٍ** (*bahraj* = debased/sham; *zūr* = falsehood, *qawla z-zūr* 22:30). *Best, for the well/water field:* **آلٍ** or **سَرابٍ** (the cisterns hold only mirage); for moral falseness, **زورٍ**. • *madyūn* (in debt) — fine. **Keep.**

**27.** كَالنَّهْرِ يَشْتَدُّ التِئامًا … المَسيلُ … المَسْكونْ — *nahr*, *iltiʾām* (knitting-together), *masīl* (the water-course/wadi-channel), *maskūn* (the indwelt, *sakīna*): all good archaic water/dwelling realia. **Keep.** • Consider **الوادي** / **الشِّعْب** (the gorge) to thicken if desired.

**28.** كُلُّ الحُروفِ مَكانُها … لا تَنْتَزِعْ حَرْفًا … **مَفاصِلِ البِنا** مَحْصونْ — *maḥṣūn* (fortified, *ḥuṣūn* / *muḥṣanāt*) good; the conditional-gnomic frame is Zuhayr-perfect (**keep the syntax**). • **مَفاصِلِ البِنا** ("the joints of the building/structure") — **two problems.** (i) *al-binā* in the abstract "structure/architecture" sense leans modern; the period word is the *building* you can touch — but better, the period image for "interlocked structure" is **masonry / a wall / a coat-of-mail**, named concretely. (ii) *mafāṣil* ("joints") as an architectural-engineering term reads modern. → recast on a concrete archaic frame: **كُلُّ الحُروفِ … فَكُلُّ لَبِناتِ … الصَّرْحِ مَحْصونْ** (every *brick* of the *citadel*, *ṣarḥ* 27:44; 28:38 — Qurʾanic palace), or the **coat-of-mail** image: **كَكُلِّ حَلْقَةٍ مِنْ سابِغاتِ** (every *ring* of the *long mail-coat*, *sābighāt* 34:11 — *David's mail*, Qurʾanic) where pulling one ring unmakes the weave — which **rhymes with your weaving-thesis**. *Best:* the **sābighāt / dirʿ** (mail-rings) frame — it fuses "locked structure" + "weave" and is impeccably Qurʾanic-archaic.

**29.** الدُّرُّ … مُثيرُهُ مِنْ لُجَّةٍ … الغَريقُ المَحْزونْ — *durr* (pearls), *lujja* (the deep, *baḥrin lujjiyy* 24:40 — Qurʾanic), *gharīq* (the drowning): perfect diver-realia. **Keep.** • مُثيرُهُ ("the one who raises/stirs them up") fine.

**30.** رَجَعْتُ بابَ البَدْءِ … الخَتْمُ بَدْءٌ مَحْضونْ — *khatm* (the seal, *khatama llāhu* 2:7; *khātam an-nabiyyīn* 33:40), *maḥḍūn* (cradled, *ḥiḍn* — the embrace). The loop-close is in register. **Keep.** • "بابَ البَدْءِ" — *bāb al-badʾ* fine; *badʾ* (beginning, *badaʾa l-khalq*) Qurʾanic.

## PART 3 — JĀHILĪ/MUKHAḌRAM LEXICON BY CONCEPT
*(render "math" with desert realia. 4–8 words per concept; gloss; Qurʾanic attestation where it exists. ✓Q = attested in the Qurʾan.)*

### A. ORDER / ARRANGEMENT (the necklace-thread; how stars & camels are set)
- **نِظام** *niẓām* — the **thread of a necklace**; the string on which pearls are *set in order*. The root-image behind "order." (Cf. proverb *infaraṭa niẓāmuhu* = its string snapped.)
- **سِلْك** *silk* — the **threading-string**; *fī silkin wāḥid* = on one thread.
- **سِمْط** *simṭ* — a **strung row** of pearls/beads; a rank set in line.
- **عِقْد** *ʿiqd* — the **strung necklace** itself (the finished order); *naẓm al-ʿiqd*.
- **نَسَق** *nasaq* — things set in **even succession / one row** (date-palms in a line).
- **صَفّ** *ṣaff* — a **drawn-up rank** ✓Q (61:4 *ṣaffan*; 37:1 *al-ṣāffāt*) — the ranked line.
- **سَرْد** *sard* — the **even plaiting of mail-rings**; *qaddir fī s-sard* ✓Q (34:11) = "measure well in the *sizing of the rings*" — God's own order-command to David. **The Qurʾan's own word for measured serial order.**
- **مَوْضون** *mawḍūn* — **close-woven, interlinked** ✓Q (56:15). *(Already in b.10 — the poem's best order-word.)*

### B. THE TRUE / SHORTEST ROAD; the night-march (*surā*) and its guide & stars
- **سُرى / أَسْرى** *surā* — the **night-journey** ✓Q (17:1 *asrā*; 89:4 *yasri*). *(In b.14.)*
- **قَصْد السَّبيل** *qaṣd al-sabīl* — the **straight/shortest way** ✓Q (16:9 *ʿalā llāhi qaṣdu s-sabīl*) — "the *direct* road" — **the period phrase for the optimal path** (your thesis!).
- **مَحَجَّة / الجادَّة** *maḥajja / jādda* — the **clear beaten high-road**; *al-maḥajja al-bayḍāʾ* (the white road).
- **لاحِب** *lāḥib* — a **broad clear-cut track** worn into the desert.
- **دَليل / خِرّيت** *dalīl / khirrīt* — the **guide**; *khirrīt* = the *expert* guide who knows every track-hole. *(dalīl in b.6,8.)*
- **مَنازِل القَمَر** *manāzil al-qamar* — the **lunar mansions** ✓Q (36:39 *qaddarnāhu manāzil*) — the 28 way-stations the moon is "measured" through. *(manzil doubles in b.7–8.)*
- **الجَدْي** *al-Jady* — the **Pole-star** (lit. "the kid"): the fixed pivot the sky turns on; the night-guide's anchor. — **use for "center/pivot"** too.
- **الفَرْقَدان** *al-Farqadān* — the **two calves**: the two bright near-pole stars, proverbial for steadfastness/guidance.
- **سُهَيْل** *Suhayl* — **Canopus**: the great southern star, season- and direction-marker.
- **الثُّرَيّا** *al-Thurayyā* — the **Pleiades**: rain-star, the "necklace" cluster (also an order-image).
- **بَنات نَعْش** *banāt naʿsh* — the **Great Bear** ("daughters of the bier"): circumpolar, never-setting → fixity.
- **الفَجْر / الصُّبْح** the dawn the night-march aims at ✓Q.

### C. CENTER / PIVOT (NOT *markaz*)
- **قُطْب** *quṭb* — the **iron pivot-pin of the millstone** (*quṭb al-raḥā*) **and** the celestial pole; *the fixed point everything turns on*. **The single best replacement for *markaz*/*madār*-center.**
- **سُرَّة** *surra* — the **navel**; *surrat al-wādī* = the heart/center of the valley. The body-center kenning.
- **عَمود** *ʿamūd* — the **tent-pole**; the axis that holds the dwelling up (*ʿamūd al-bayt*); *ʿimād* ✓Q (89:7 *dhāt al-ʿimād*).
- **ثُقْب الرَّحى / قَلْب** the **eye/heart** (the hole at the millstone's center; *qalb* = heart/core).
- **مِحْوَر** *miḥwar* — the **axle-pin** a wheel/pulley turns on (classical, concrete — not the modern abstract "axis of…").
- **وَتِد** *watid* — the **tent-peg / driven stake** ✓Q (78:7 *al-jibāla awtādā* — mountains as *pegs*) — fixity that holds the whole down.

### D. CIRCLE / CURVE / HALF-RING (NOT *madār / dāʾira*)
- **سِوار** *siwār* — the **bangle / bracelet**: a closed ring ✓Q (*asāwira min dhahab*, 18:31; 22:23). **Best "ring/orbit" swap.**
- **قَوْس** *qaws* — the **bow** (and the **rainbow** *qaws quzaḥ*): the arc/curve. ✓Q (53:9 *qāba qawsayn*). *(Already in b.25 — keep.)*
- **هِلال** *hilāl* — the **crescent**: a half-ring, a thin bow of light. (*ahilla* ✓Q 2:189.) — pairs with your moon-return.
- **رَحى** *raḥā* — the **millstone**: its rim *is* the circle, its *quṭb* the center. Best for "wheeling whole."
- **حَلْقَة** *ḥalqa* — a **ring / loop** (of mail, of a door-knocker); the closed circle of linked rings.
- **طَوْق** *ṭawq* — the **collar / neck-ring**; an encircling band.
- **دارَة** *dāra* — the **halo round the moon**; a ring of sand-dunes enclosing a hollow (a desert "circle," concrete — not abstract *dāʾira*).
- **عُرْجون** *ʿurjūn* — the **curved old date-stalk** ✓Q (36:39) the crescent-moon resembles. *(b.17 — keep.)*

### E. COUNTING / NUMBER / DIVINATION (NOT *raqm*)
- **حَصى** *ḥaṣā* — **counting-pebbles**; reckoning by casting pebbles; the diviner's count. **Best "number/counting" swap.** (Also *aḥṣā* "to enumerate exhaustively" ✓Q 72:28; 78:29 *aḥṣaynāhu kitābā* — God's *exact count*.)
- **عَدّ / عَدَد** *ʿadd / ʿadad* — **the tally / number** ✓Q (*ʿadada s-sinīn*, 10:5; 18:11). The plain period word for "count" (use *al-ʿadd* as "the counting," not *al-raqm*).
- **خَطّ الرَّمْل** *khaṭṭ al-raml* — **geomancy / sand-divination**: lines drawn in sand and counted off — the pre-Islamic "calculation." **The period image for false reckoning made a craft.**
- **القِدْح / الأَزْلام** *al-qidḥ / al-azlām* — **the divining-arrows** ✓Q (5:3 *al-azlām*; 5:90 *al-azlām rijsun*) — drawing lots/futures by headless arrows (*maysir*). The condemned reckoning — perfect for "do not worship the count."
- **الكاهِن** *al-kāhin* — **the soothsayer / diviner** ✓Q (52:29; 69:42 *bi-qawli kāhin*) — the reckoner of hidden things (whom the Qurʾan disowns).
- **زَجْر الطَّيْر / العِيافة** *zajr al-ṭayr / ʿiyāfa* — **augury by birds**: another archaic "calculation" of the unseen.
- **مِيزان / قِسْط** *mīzān / qisṭ* — **the balance / exact justice** ✓Q (55:7–9 *al-mīzān*; 21:47 *al-mawāzīn al-qisṭ*) — for *true* measure (vs. the false count). *(mawzūn in b.16.)*
- **كَيْل** *kayl* — **dry-measure (the measuring-scoop)** ✓Q (12:60; 17:35). *(b.23.)*

### F. THE WELL & WATER
- **رِشاء** *rishāʾ* — the **well-rope** the bucket is let down on.
- **دَلْو** *dalw* — the **leather bucket** ✓Q (12:19 *adlā dalwahu*).
- **عَطَن** *ʿaṭan* — the **camels' resting-ground by the well** (where they kneel after drinking).
- **وِرْد** *wird* — the **coming-down to water; the watering-turn** ✓Q (19:86 *wirdā*; 11:98). *(b.9,23.)*
- **مَعين** *maʿīn* — **running/flowing spring-water** ✓Q (23:50; 67:30 *bi-māʾin maʿīn*) — *living* water (vs. the dry cistern).
- **آجِن** *ājin* — **stagnant, stale water** ✓Q-adjacent (cf. *ghayri āsin* 47:15, "non-stale") — the foul standing water; perfect for the *dead* cistern.
- **نَطْفة / رَنَق** a **clear drop** / **turbid dregs** — the clean vs. the riled.
- **جابِية / حَوْض / جُبّ** *jābiya / ḥawḍ / jubb* — the **great basin / cistern / pit-well** (*jifān ka-l-jawābi* 34:13; *al-jubb* 12:10). *(jifān in b.26.)*
- **سَجْل / ذَنوب** *sajl / dhanūb* — a **full bucket / a share of water** (proverbial for one's "draught" of fortune).

### G. WEAVING (the burda; warp & weft)
- **بُرْدة / بُرْد** *burda / burd* — the **striped woollen mantle** (the very cloak Kaʿb won). *(b.10,12,18.)*
- **سَدى** *sadā* — the **warp** (lengthwise threads).
- **لُحْمة** *luḥma* — the **weft** (cross-threads).
- **نَسْج** *nasj* — **weaving / the woven web** (also a spider's *nasj*). *(b.10,14,18 — keep; it is concrete enough.)*
- **مَوْضون** *mawḍūn* — **close-woven, interlinked-rings** ✓Q (56:15). *(b.10.)*
- **سابِغات / دِرْع** *sābighāt / dirʿ* — **the long coat of mail** ✓Q (34:11 *sābighāt*; 21:80 *labūsin*) — woven of rings; David's craft. **Best "interlocked structure" image** (see b.28).
- **حائك / نَوْل** *ḥāʾik / nawl* — **the weaver / the loom**.
- **طِراز** *ṭirāz* — the **woven border-pattern / embroidered hem** (the figured edge of the cloth).
- **سَدّ اللُّحْمة بالسَّدى** the **crossing of weft through warp** — the embrace of two thread-systems (your b.10 image, in technical terms).

### H. FALSE / COUNTERFEIT (NOT *zayf*)
- **آل** *āl* — the **noon-mirage / shimmer** of the desert air. The thing that *looks* like water/an army and is nothing. **Best "false appearance" swap.**
- **سَراب** *sarāb* — the **mirage** ✓Q (24:39 *ka-sarābin bi-qīʿah*; 78:20) — **the Qurʾan's own emblem of works that prove void.** *(b.23 — keep.)*
- **زُور** *zūr* — **falsehood, perjured speech** ✓Q (22:30 *qawla z-zūr*; 25:72). Moral falseness.
- **بَهْرَج** *bahraj* — **debased / sham / passed-off-as-good** (of false coin or goods).
- **بُهْتان** *buhtān* — **a brazen lie / slander** ✓Q (4:112; 24:16).
- **زائف / زَغَل** *zāʾif / zaghal* — **culled (rejected) coin / adulterated metal** (the literal *z-y-f*: debased *dirham*). Use the **coin**-image concretely if at all, not the abstract "zayf."
- **خِداع / غُرور** *khidāʿ / ghurūr* — **deception / delusive vanity** ✓Q (2:9; 57:20 *matāʿu l-ghurūr*) — the deceiving show.
- **باطِل** *bāṭil* — **vain, null, that which perishes** ✓Q (17:81 *zahaqa l-bāṭil*; 21:18) — the void-by-nature (vs. *al-ḥaqq*).

### I. THE SEAL / RETURN / the moon's cycle
- **خَتْم / خاتَم** *khatm / khātam* — **the seal / signet** ✓Q (2:7 *khatama*; 33:40 *khātam an-nabiyyīn*; 83:25 *khitāmuhu misk*). *(b.30.)*
- **عُرْجون** *ʿurjūn* — the **old date-stalk** the moon returns to ✓Q (36:39). *(b.17.)*
- **مَحاق** *maḥāq* — the **dark of the moon / the waning to nothing** (the three nights of no-moon) — the emptying before return.
- **هِلال → بَدْر → مَحاق** the full **lunar cycle**: crescent → full → dark → crescent — the period emblem of departure-and-return (your loop-close).
- **مَطْلَع / مَغيب** *maṭlaʿ / maghīb* — **rising-place / setting-place** ✓Q (97:5 *maṭlaʿ al-fajr*; 18:90 *maṭliʿ al-shams*) — the cyclical horizon-points.
- **حَوْل / كَرَّة** *ḥawl / karra* — the **year's turn / the return-going** ✓Q (2:240 *ḥawl*; 67:4 *karratayn* — "look again, *a second time*") — recurrence.
- **رَجْع** *rajʿ* — the **returning** ✓Q (86:11 *dhāti r-rajʿ* — the sky "of the return," i.e. rain that comes back) — the cyclical comeback (pairs with your *rajaʿtu*, b.30).

## PART 5 — REGISTER CHECKLIST (make Arabic read ~1,300 years old)
*Concrete rules; each with the failure-mode it fixes.*

1. **Prefer the verbal sentence; front the verb.** Open lines with a finite verb (بانَتْ، عَفَتِ، رَأَيْتُ، وَقَفْتُ). MSA's noun-heavy, copula-implied surface is the #1 modern tell. — *Fixes the poem's smoother nominal lines.*

2. **Front the predicate in nominal sentences, and use the asseverative إِنَّ … لَ ….** *Inna l-rasūla la-sayfun…*; *fa-innaka shamsun…*. The "sworn-testimony" word-order is pervasive in the models. Open at least a third of the lines with إِنَّ / قَدْ / لَقَدْ / أَلا / أَمَا.

3. **Render every abstraction as a kenning or named realia.** Death→*ālah ḥadbāʾ*; Fate→*khabṭ ʿashwāʾ*; the unreachable→*sullam*; the center→*quṭb al-raḥā*; counterfeit→*āl/sarāb*. **If a line contains an abstract concept with no body, it is modern.** — *The core fix for bayts 14, 22, 25, 28.*

4. **Purge the post-classical ‑iyya / maṣdar-ṣināʿī abstractions and the "‑ism" texture.** No *al-handasiyya*, *al-markaziyya*, *al-binya*, *al-naẓariyya*; jāhilī Arabic has almost no abstract ‑iyya nominals. Where you need the quality, use the concrete adjective or the thing itself. — *Watch b.10 (bayān-as-rhetoric), b.28 (binā-as-structure).*

5. **Use the conditional-gnomic engine for all sententious lines.** مَنْ … (jussive) … يَلْقَ … ؛ مَهْما تَكُنْ … تُعْلَمِ. Aphorisms ride on شرط/جزاء, not on definitions. — *Keep/extend b.28; model it on Zuhayr.*

6. **Deploy the fossil particles & formulae.** رُبَّ / وَرُبَّ (+ indefinite genitive: *wa-rubba laylin…* "many a night…"), كَمْ (exclamatory: *kam qāʾifin…* — already in b.5), أَبَيْتَ اللَّعْنَ, لِلّهِ دَرُّ, لا أَبا لَكَ, هَيْهاتَ, لَعَمْري. One or two of these per qaṣīda is an instant date-stamp. — *Consider opening a bayt with وَرُبَّ … .*

7. **Prefer the gharīb genitive construction (iḍāfa) over adjective-strings.** *ṣadūq al-sayr* (true-of-march), *ghaḍīḍ al-ṭarf* (downcast-of-eye), *quṭb al-raḥā*, *surrat al-wādī*. The compressed possessive is more archaic than predicate adjectives. — *The poem already does this well (b.9 ṣadūq al-sayr); do more.*

8. **Choose the Qurʾanic-or-older synonym over its MSA replacement.** *wird* not *sharība*; *kayl* not *miqyās*; *ṣarḥ* not *bināya*; *ʿadd/ḥaṣā* not *raqm*; *quṭb* not *markaz*; *siwār/qaws* not *dāʾira/madār*; *āl/sarāb* not *zayf*; *aḥqāb* not *ḥuqab/ḥaqība*. — *The Part 2 swap-table operationalizes this.*

9. **Name specific stars, places, animals, and tools — never the generic class.** *al-Jady, Suhayl, al-Farqadān* not *al-nujūm* (generic); *muhannad* not *sayf*; *yaʿmala/wajnāʾ* not *nāqa*. The jāhilī map/bestiary/toolkit is **specific**. — *Thicken b.7's "stars," b.6's "mounts."*

10. **Keep the rain/water/violence vocabulary technical.** Distinguish *jawd* (downpour) from *riham* (drizzle), *ghayth* (reviving rain) from *maṭar*; *maʿīn* (flowing) from *ājin* (stale). Period poets never said just "rain" or "water." — *Sharpen b.12, b.26, b.27.*

11. **Avoid logicians'/grammarians'/Sufi technical terms** that postdate the period: *al-māhiyya, al-jawhar/ʿaraḍ* (as kalām terms), *al-naqīḍ, al-bayān* (rhetoric), *al-markaz/al-muḥīṭ* (geometry), *al-wujūd* (as ontology). To a mukhaḍram these are anachronisms even when the *roots* are old. — *Watch b.10, b.18 (ḍiddayn), b.20 (qiyās).*

12. **Let realia carry theology; do not state the abstraction the image already proves.** Ḥassān proves perfection with "no eye has seen fairer," not with *al-kamāl*. al-Nābigha proves omnipresence with "you are the night that overtakes me." — **Trust the body; cut the gloss.** *(Tighten any line that both shows and tells.)*
