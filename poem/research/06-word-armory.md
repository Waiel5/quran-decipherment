# THE WORD-ARMORY

> **How to read each entry.** **Jadhr** = the triliteral (occasionally quadriliteral) root, with Ibn Fāris's *aṣl* (the single semantic atom the whole family radiates from) where it sharpens the poet's hand. **Field** = the full semantic spread, including the senses with no English equivalent. **Qur'anic** = the scriptural echo, with sūra cited when the resonance is load-bearing. **★ thunderclap-grade** = can end an aphoristic hemistich with maximal closing force (the *ḍarb* landing). **⚠** = touches a guardrail; use the surface meaning only.

---

## PART 1 — RHYME ARSENALS

Three classes, **strictly separated** (you commit to exactly one rawiyy-skeleton for the whole qaṣīda; mixing -ūr with -ār mid-poem is *sinād al-ridf*, a hard rhyme-defect). All three share **rāʾ (ر) as rawiyy** and are **murdaf** (a long vowel — the *ridf* — immediately before the rāʾ); they differ only in *which* long vowel:

- **(a) -ūr** — ridf = ū (ḍamma-coloured). Bell-like, sonorous, grand. *Majrā* vowel on the rāʾ = ḍamma.
- **(b) -īr** — ridf = ī (kasra-coloured). Bright, keen, piercing; native home of the *faʿīl* intensive-adjective (al-Qadīr, al-Baṣīr). *Majrā* = kasra.
- **(c) -ār** — ridf = ā (fatḥa-coloured, open). Broad, declarative, plural-heavy (the *afʿāl* / *fiʿāl* broken plurals: al-Asrār, al-Anwār). *Majrā* = fatḥa.

> **Iqwāʾ/iṣrāf warning.** Once you pick the class, the rāʾ's vowel is fixed for all 40 lines: -ūr everywhere (ḍamma majrā), or -īr everywhere (kasra), or -ār everywhere (fatḥa). You may NOT rhyme السُّطُور (‑ūr) against التَّفْسِير (‑īr) against الأَسْرَار (‑ār) — those are three different poems. (See 03-arud-rubric §7.)

### CLASS (a) — **‑ūr** (rāʾ rawiyy, ridf ū)

The sonorous register. The ḍamma+ū+r close rings like a struck bell on every *ḍarb* — *exactly* the "thunderclap" landing al-Kāmil wants (03-arud-rubric §8). This is also the class where the theme's keystone puns live (**البُحُور** = seas *and* meters; **السُّطُور** = the written lines).

### CLASS (b) — **‑īr** (rāʾ rawiyy, ridf ī)

The keen/bright register. This is the natural home of the ***faʿīl*** pattern — the intensive adjective that is *also* the morphology of God's Names (al-Qadīr, al-Baṣīr, al-Khabīr, al-Naṣīr) — so a -īr poem can land a Divine Name on the *ḍarb* of almost every line. It is also the home of the ***tafʿīl*** verbal-noun (al-Taqdīr, al-Tadbīr, al-Tafsīr, al-Tasṭīr) — the "measuring/ordering/exposition" abstractions this theme runs on. Brighter and more piercing than -ūr; slightly less "bell," more "blade."

### CLASS (c) — **‑ār** (rāʾ rawiyy, ridf ā)

The open/declarative register. Dominated by the ***afʿāl*** and ***fiʿāl*** broken plurals (al-Asrār, al-Anwār, al-Aqdār, al-Abrār) — so a -ār poem speaks naturally in **plurals**: many lights, many measures, many mysteries. The open ā before the rāʾ is the broadest, most *oratorical* close — superb for sweep and enumeration, slightly less "needle-sharp" than -īr, less "bell" than -ūr. Excellent for the *cataloguing* movements (the seeker counting the chambers).

### ★ RECOMMENDATION — which class is richest for THIS theme

**Primary: Class (a) ‑ūr.** This matches 03-arud-rubric §8's standing recommendation, and the reasons compound for the *armory*:

1. **The two keystone puns live only here.** **البُحُور** = *seas AND poetic meters* (al-Khalīl's "seas") — a poem about the Qur'an's order *versus human meter* that rhymes on *al-buḥūr* fuses its subject and its form in a single rawiyy. And **السُّطُور** (the written lines) puts the Book-as-ordered-script directly on the rhyme. No other class carries both.
2. **The thesis is fully sayable on ‑ūr alone:** *al-nūr* (light/guidance), *al-suṭūr* (the lines), *al-buḥūr* (seas/meters), *al-duhūr* (the aeons it outlasts), *al-ṣudūr* (the hearts it heals / the line-openings), *al-ẓuhūr* (the order made manifest), *al-nushūr* (the scroll/dead unrolled), *al-zabūr* (the Book among Books), *al-shuṭūr* (the qibla-half / the hemistich), *al-judhūr* (root — lexical/mathematical/botanical). That is the entire blueprint, rhyme-ready.
3. **Bell-like ḍarb = the "thunderclap" landing.** The ḍamma+ū+r close is the most resonant of the three (the rāʾ is Arabic's most sonorous liquid; the long ū gives it body). It lands aphorisms hardest — exactly al-Kāmil's strength.
4. **Built-in antithesis-on-the-rhyme:** *al-kufūr* (denial) vs *al-shukūr* (gratitude); *al-nūr* (light) vs *al-ghurūr* (delusion's glitter) — *ṭibāq* available *on the rāwī itself*.

**Strong alternative: Class (b) ‑īr — choose this if you want the *measured-order* thesis to recur literally on every rhyme.** It uniquely lets you land **al-Qadīr** (the Name = exact-measure-power), **al-taqdīr** (the measuring-out, Q25:2), **al-tadbīr** (cosmic ordering, Q32:5), **al-tasṭīr** (the inscribing), and **al-qawārīr ... qaddarūhā taqdīrā** (vessels measured to exact measure, Q76:16) — a chain where *measure* is the rhyme. And the *faʿīl* morphology lands a **Divine Name** (Qadīr/Baṣīr/Khabīr/Naṣīr) on the *ḍarb* of nearly any line. The trade-off: brighter/sharper, slightly less "grand-bell"; and the *tafʿīl* forms (al-tasṭīr, al-taqdīr) carry a *taʾsīs* alif that binds the rhyme a touch more tightly (watch *sinād al-taʾsīs*).

**Use Class (c) ‑ār for a *catalogue/challenge*-dominant poem** (or for individual movements if you were writing in stanzas — but a single qaṣīda commits to one): its plural-heavy palette (al-Asrār, al-Anwār, al-Aqdār, al-Aṭwār) is the voice of *enumeration*, and it holds the **al-asṭār ↔ al-asāṭīr** detonation (true lines vs the deniers' "fables") most directly.

> **Verdict:** ‑ūr for the *grandest, most fused* poem (the pun-bearing default); ‑īr if you want *measure itself* to be the rhyme on every line; ‑ār if the poem leans hardest into *enumeration and challenge*. All three are rich enough for 40 lines without *īṭāʾ* (rhyme-word repetition).

## PART 2 — POWER-LEXICON (non-rhyme keywords)

The load-bearing nouns. These are the words the poem *thinks with*, wherever they fall in the line. Each: jadhr, full field, untranslatable nuance, Qur'anic resonance.

### The Pen-and-Tablet complex (the cosmic writing)

- **نُون (ن)** — *jadhr:* (the letter itself; as a noun, *nūn* = a great fish/whale, and *al-nūn* = the inkwell). **The poem's buried signature — full treatment in §4.** Field: (i) the Arabic letter, the corpus's true *third*-most-frequent letter (8.29% — above mīm 8.12%, ledger H-NEW-1810); (ii) the cosmic whale bearing the earth (Ibn ʿAbbās, via al-Ṭabarī); (iii) the inkwell of light (Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Qatāda); (iv) a letter of God's Names. Untranslatable: a single glyph that *is* a fish, an inkwell, and a verse-opener at once. Qur'anic: **Q68:1 نٓ وَالقَلَمِ وَمَا يَسْطُرُونَ** — the sole Nūn-opener in the corpus (Q068-F-08, CORPUS-EXACT 1/29).

- **القَلَم** — *jadhr:* ق ل م. Core: the reed-pen. *aṣl* (Ibn Fāris): to *pare/trim* (a *qalam* is a *trimmed* reed; *qulāma* = a nail-paring). Field: the instrument of all writing; in the cosmic register, *the Pen that wrote the decree*. Untranslatable: the pen *defined by being cut/pared* — order begins with a cutting. Qur'anic: **Q68:1** (the oath *by the pen*); **Q96:4** *alladhī ʿallama bi-l-qalam* (taught by the pen) — the *first revelation's* pen; *the Pen* of the creation-ḥadīth (*awwalu mā khalaqa Llāhu l-qalam*). The pen that "walked the road" in the volta (blueprint line 24).

- **اللَّوْح (المَحْفُوظ)** — *jadhr:* ل و ح. Core: a tablet, a flat broad surface/plank; *lāḥa* = to gleam/appear (a tablet *gleams*). Field: *al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ* = the Preserved Tablet, the cosmic master-text from which the Qur'an descends. Untranslatable: a writing-surface named from *gleaming-into-view* — the tablet shows. Qur'anic: **Q85:21-22** *bal huwa qurʾānun majīd · fī lawḥin maḥfūẓ* (a glorious Qur'an, in a Preserved Tablet) — the cosmic archetype of the ordered Book. Pairs with *al-Ḥafīẓ* (the Preserving), *aḥṣā* (numbered).

- **الكِتَاب** — *jadhr:* ك ت ب. Core: the Book, the writing; *aṣl:* to *join/gather* letters (*kataba l-jaysh* = he marshalled the army — *kitāba* is the *marshalling* of letters into order!). Field: *al-Kitāb* = the Qur'an, scripture, the decree (*kutiba ʿalaykum* = it is decreed/written for you), the record. Untranslatable: writing *as the marshalling/binding-together of scattered marks into order* — the very thesis. Qur'anic: **Q2:2** *dhālika l-Kitābu lā rayba fīh*; *umm al-Kitāb* (Q3:7, Q13:39); *kitābun masṭūr* / *marqūm* / *maknūn*.

- **التِّبْيَان** — *jadhr:* ب ي ن. Core: the full making-clear, the complete exposition. Field: intensive of *bayān* — *tibyānan li-kulli shayʾ*, an exposition of everything. Untranslatable: clarity *as exhaustive disclosure*, the Book that explains all. Qur'anic: **Q16:89** *wa-nazzalnā ʿalayka l-Kitāba tibyānan li-kulli shayʾ* (We sent down the Book as an exposition of all things). See also *al-Bayān* below.

### The Revelation-and-descent complex

- **الوَحْي** — *jadhr:* و ح ي. Core: revelation; *aṣl:* swift secret communication (*awḥā* = to signal/inspire rapidly and hiddenly — a *waḥy* can be a gesture, Q19:11 Zakariyyā *fa-awḥā ilayhim*). Field: divine inspiration, the casting of the word into the heart. Untranslatable: communication that is *swift, hidden, and total* at once — not speech but *transmission*. Qur'anic: **Q53:4** *in huwa illā waḥyun yūḥā* (it is nothing but a revelation revealed); **Q42:51** the three modes of *waḥy* (inspiration / from behind a veil / by a messenger-angel).

- **التَّنْزِيل** — *jadhr:* ن ز ل. Core: the sending-down, in stages. Field: *tanzīl* = the *graded, sequential* descent of revelation (vs *inzāl*, a single sending-down); *tanzīlun mina r-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*. Untranslatable: revelation *as measured, piecemeal descent* — which is *why* the compression-tail can exist (the Book came down across 23 years and was *arranged* otherwise). Qur'anic: **Q56:80** *tanzīlun min rabbi l-ʿālamīn*; **Q41:2** *tanzīlun mina r-Raḥmān*; **Q17:106** *wa-qurʾānan faraqnāhu... wa-nazzalnāhu tanzīlā* (We sent it down in measured stages).

- **الرُّوح** — *jadhr:* ر و ح. Core: spirit, breath-of-life; *aṣl:* ر و ح ↔ ريح (wind/breath) and *rawḥ* (rest/mercy) and *rāḥa* (ease). Field: *al-Rūḥ* = the Spirit (Gabriel, *al-Rūḥ al-Amīn*; the Spirit of revelation; the divine in-breathing). Untranslatable: the single root binding *spirit*, *wind*, *rest/mercy*, and *fragrance* (*rayḥān*). Qur'anic: **Q26:193** *nazala bihi r-Rūḥu l-Amīn* (the Trustworthy Spirit brought it down); **Q97:4** *tanazzalu l-malāʾikatu wa-r-Rūḥ* (Laylat al-Qadr); **Q17:85** *wa-yasʾalūnaka ʿani r-Rūḥ*.

### The measure-and-order complex (the THESIS core)

- **القَدَر** — *jadhr:* ق د ر. Core: measure, decree, exact apportioning. **The master-concept of the entire poem.** *aṣl* (Ibn Fāris): ق د ر = *the end-point/limit of a thing* — its exact extent. Field: *qadar* = divine decree-as-precise-measure; *qadr* = worth/measure/the Night of Power (Laylat al-Qadr); *miqdār* = an exact quantity; *qādir/qadīr* = powerful-because-measuring. Untranslatable: *destiny and quantity are the same word* — to decree is to *measure*. Qur'anic: **Q54:49** *innā kulla shayʾin khalaqnāhu bi-qadar* (everything by exact measure — al-Qamar); **Q97:1** *laylati l-Qadr*; **Q25:2** *fa-qaddarahu taqdīrā*; **Q13:8** *kullu shayʾin ʿindahu bi-miqdār*. → Root-Play §1.

- **السَّطْر / المَسْطُور** — *jadhr:* س ط ر. Core: the line of writing / what-is-inscribed-in-lines. Field: *saṭr* = a ruled row of script; *masṭūr* = written-out in lines, registered; *yasṭurūn* = they inscribe. Untranslatable: the *row/rule* of writing — order as *alignment into lines*. Qur'anic: **Q52:2-3** *wa-kitābin masṭūr · fī raqqin manshūr* (a Book inscribed in lines, on an unrolled parchment); **Q68:1** *wa-mā yasṭurūn*; **Q17:58** *masṭūrā*. ⚠ Root-sibling *asāṭīr* (fables) — Root-Play §3.

- **النَّظْم** — *jadhr:* ن ظ م. Core: stringing-into-order (as pearls on a thread); composition, arrangement. **The classical term for the Qur'an's compositional miracle** (al-Jurjānī's *naẓm* theory of iʿjāz). *aṣl:* to *thread/string* (a *niẓām* = the thread of a necklace; *intaẓama* = to fall into ordered sequence). Field: *naẓm* = the ordered weaving of words; *al-naẓm* = verse/order itself. Untranslatable: composition *imagined as pearls strung on one thread* — the precise image for anchor B (the single ordering-thread through 114). Qur'anic: not a surface lexeme, but the *concept* the whole iʿjāz tradition (al-Bāqillānī, al-Jurjānī) names; cf. *manḍūd* (Q11:82, Q56:29 — closely-ranked/layered). → Root-Play §5.

- **الفَصْل / الفَاصِلَة** — *jadhr:* ف ص ل. Core: separation, decisive division, the verse-clausula. Field: *faṣl* = a clear cut/decisive judgment (*qawlun faṣl*, the decisive word, Q86:13; *yawmu l-faṣl*, the Day of Decision); *al-fāṣila* = the **verse-end / rhyme-clausula** (the technical term for what al-Bāqillānī's *iʿjāz al-fawāṣil* concerns — anchor A!); *faṣṣala* = to set out distinctly, in detail; *mufaṣṣal* = the clearly-divided (the short final sūras = *al-mufaṣṣal*). Untranslatable: one root for *the decisive judgment*, *the verse-ending*, and *clear detailed exposition*. Qur'anic: **Q41:3** *kitābun fuṣṣilat āyātuh*; **Q86:13** *innahu la-qawlun faṣl*; *al-mufaṣṣal* (the final seventh of the Qur'an). → Root-Play §6.

- **الوَصْل** — *jadhr:* و ص ل. Core: joining, connection, the bond. Field: the antithesis-partner of *faṣl* — *waṣl* = union/joining (in prosody, *al-waṣl* is the letter joined after the rawiyy!); *ṣila* = a bond/kinship-tie. Untranslatable: *connection* set against *faṣl*'s *separation* — the Book both *divides* (fawāṣil) and *binds* (waṣl). Qur'anic: **Q28:51** *wa-laqad waṣṣalnā lahumu l-qawl* (We have connected/conveyed the word to them in succession) — the *linking* of revelation; Q13:21 *yaṣilūna mā amara Llāhu bihi an yūṣal* (joining what God commanded to be joined).

- **الإحْكَام / المُحْكَم** — *jadhr:* ح ك م. Core: making-firm/precise, the unambiguous. Field: *iḥkām* = perfecting, making sound/precise/unbreakable; *muḥkam* = the clearly-decisive verses (vs *mutashābih*, the allegorical, Q3:7); *uḥkimat āyātuh* = its verses were *made firm/precise*; *ḥukm* = judgment/wisdom; *ḥikma* = wisdom. **The direct Qur'anic word for "perfectly-ordered."** Untranslatable: the root binds *firm precision*, *judgment*, *wisdom*, and *governance* — order as *the firmness of the wise*. Qur'anic: **Q11:1** *kitābun uḥkimat āyātuhu thumma fuṣṣilat* (a Book whose verses were *made firm/precise*, then *set out in detail*) — the keystone pairing of *iḥkām* (firmness) and *tafṣīl* (division); **Q3:7** *minhu āyātun muḥkamāt*.

- **البَيَان** — *jadhr:* ب ي ن. Core: clear exposition, the faculty of articulate speech. Field: *bayān* = lucid clarity, eloquence, the making-distinct; *ʿallamahu l-bayān* (He taught him eloquence/clear-speech — Q55:4, al-Raḥmān, a project deep-dive surah); *al-bayyina* = the clear proof. Untranslatable: clarity *as the distinguishing of things from one another* (same root as *bayna*, "between"). Qur'anic: **Q55:3-4** *khalaqa l-insān · ʿallamahu l-bayān* (created man, taught him clear-speech); **Q75:19** *thumma inna ʿalaynā bayānah* (then upon Us is its clear exposition).

### The letter, the number, the one

- **الحَرْف** — *jadhr:* ح ر ف. Core: a letter (of the alphabet); an edge/brink; a way-of-reading. *aṣl:* ح ر ف = *edge/margin/the side of a thing* (a *ḥarf* of a mountain = its sharp edge). Field: the alphabetic letter; the *edge* (*ʿalā ḥarf* = on a brink/precariously, Q22:11 — *yaʿbudu Llāha ʿalā ḥarf*); the *seven aḥruf* (modes of recitation). Untranslatable: the letter *as an edge/brink* — and the same word for worshipping God *on an edge* (waveringly). ⚠ Root-siblings *taḥrīf* (alteration/corruption of scripture) and *inḥirāf* (deviation) — Root-Play §4. Qur'anic: **Q22:11** *man yaʿbudu Llāha ʿalā ḥarf*; the science of *ʿilm al-ḥarf* the project audits (memory: *reference_ilm_al_harf*).

- **العَدَد** — *jadhr:* ع د د. Core: number, count. *aṣl:* ع د د = to *count/enumerate/reckon* (and *ʿidda* = a counted waiting-period; *iʿdād* = preparing-by-counting; *aʿadda* = to make ready). Field: *ʿadad* = number/quantity; *aḥṣā ʿadadahum* = He has counted their number; *ʿiddata s-sinīn* = the count of years. Untranslatable: number bound to *readiness* and to *the appointed counted-term*. Qur'anic: **Q72:28** *wa-aḥṣā kulla shayʾin ʿadadā* (He has enumerated everything in number); **Q18:11** *sinīna ʿadadā*; **Q10:5** *li-taʿlamū ʿadada s-sinīna wa-l-ḥisāb* (that you may know the count of years and the reckoning). ⚠ Number as awe-of-order, never as 19/abjad code (guardrail: ledger §4).

- **الأَحَد** — *jadhr:* أ ح د. Core: One, the absolute unique-indivisible One. Field: *aḥad* = oneness in the sense of *uniqueness admitting no second, no parts, no like* (stronger than *wāḥid*); used of God only as *Allāhu aḥad*. Untranslatable: a Oneness that *negates all comparison* — *aḥad* in negative contexts = "[not] anyone at all." Qur'anic: **Q112:1** *qul huwa Llāhu aḥad* — and **Q112:4** *wa-lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad* (the *aḥad → aḥad* bookend, the corpus FR-centroid, finding #8; the poem's center, blueprint §3). → Root-Play §8.

- **الوَاحِد** — *jadhr:* و ح د. Core: One, the single. Field: *wāḥid* = one (the first of the count; one *of a series*, able to be followed by two); *al-Wāḥid* as Divine Name = the sole, the unrivalled. Untranslatable nuance vs *aḥad*: *wāḥid* is *oneness as unity/uniqueness*; *aḥad* is *oneness as utter indivisible singularity*. The classical pair: *al-Wāḥid al-Aḥad*. Qur'anic: **Q2:163** *wa-ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥid*; **Q12:39** *a-arbābun mutafarriqūna khayrun ami Llāhu l-Wāḥidu l-Qahhār*; **Q14:48** *wa-barazū li-Llāhi l-Wāḥidi l-Qahhār*.

- **الصَّمَد** — *jadhr:* ص م د. Core: the Eternal, the Self-Sufficient Besought-of-all. *aṣl:* ص م د = to *aim/repair toward* (a *ṣamad* is the lord one *resorts to* for every need) AND *solid/without-hollow* (a thing *ṣamad* has no interior cavity — utterly dense, depending on nothing). Field: *al-Ṣamad* = the One every existent turns to in need, who Himself needs nothing; the impenetrably solid. Untranslatable: a Name fusing *the sought-refuge* and *the void-less solid* — depended-on-by-all because dependent-on-none. The single most semantically dense Divine Name (project: top-ranked verse for Ism al-Aʿẓam, Q112:2, finding #16). Qur'anic: **Q112:2** *Allāhu ṣ-Ṣamad* (the sole Qur'anic occurrence — a hapax-Name).

### The sign, the chapter, the seal, the opening

- **الآي / الآيَة** — *jadhr:* أ ي ي. Core: a sign, a verse, a miracle. Field: *āya* = (i) a Qur'anic verse; (ii) a sign/portent in nature (*min āyātihi*); (iii) a miracle/evidentiary wonder; *āy* = the plural-collective. Untranslatable: the **same word** for "verse of scripture," "natural sign," and "miracle" — so every verse *is* a sign *is* a wonder; the poem's thesis (the order is a *sign*) is built into the word. Qur'anic: **Q41:53** *sa-nurīhim āyātinā fī l-āfāqi wa-fī anfusihim* (We will show them Our signs in the horizons and in themselves); *tilka āyātu l-Kitāb*. → Root-Play §8.

- **السُّورَة** — *jadhr:* س و ر. Core: a chapter of the Qur'an. *aṣl:* س و ر = *eminence/an enclosing wall/a tier* (a *sūr* = a city-wall; a *sūra* = a high tier/course of a wall, or a rank of eminence). Field: *sūra* = a bounded chapter, a "walled enclosure" of revelation, a degree of rank. Untranslatable: a chapter imagined as a *walled, ranked enclosure* — the "chambers/gates" image of the blueprint (114 walled rooms). Qur'anic: **Q2:23** *fa-ʾtū bi-sūratin min mithlih* (then bring a *sūra* like it) — the taḥaddī verse itself; **Q24:1** *sūratun anzalnāhā wa-faraḍnāhā*.

- **الفَاتِحَة** — *jadhr:* ف ت ح. Core: the Opening (Sūra 1); the act of opening/unlocking. *aṣl:* ف ت ح = to *open/unlock/grant-victory* (*fatḥ* = victory-as-opening; *fattāḥ* = the Opener, Divine Name; *istaftaḥa* = to seek a decisive opening/judgment). Field: *al-Fātiḥa* = *umm al-Kitāb*, the Opening; *fātiḥat al-shayʾ* = the start of a thing. Untranslatable: opening *that is also victory and decisive judgment*. Qur'anic: the Sūra named (*al-Fātiḥa*, finding #9 — centrality rank 4/114, Q1→Q2 the costliest seam); **Q48:1** *innā fataḥnā laka fatḥan mubīnā*; **Q34:26** *yaftaḥu baynanā* (He will *open/judge* between us). The ring's first door (blueprint A/A′).

- **الخَتْم / خَاتَم** — *jadhr:* خ ت م. Core: sealing, the seal; the conclusion. Field: *khatm* = sealing-shut (God *sealed* the deniers' hearts, *khatama Llāhu ʿalā qulūbihim*, Q2:7); *khātam* = a signet-ring/seal, and **the Seal** (*khātam al-nabiyyīn*, Q33:40 — the Prophet as the seal/last of prophets); *khitām* = the seal/last-sip (*khitāmuhu misk*, Q83:26). Untranslatable: the seal *that both closes and authenticates* — and *that completes a series* (the Seal of Prophets / the close of the Book). Qur'anic: **Q33:40** *khātama n-nabiyyīn*; **Q2:7**; **Q83:25-26** *raḥīqin makhtūm · khitāmuhu misk*. The ring's last seal (blueprint A′, line 40 closing back to line 1).

### The geometry words (the "math wearing the robe of speech")

- **المِحْوَر** — *jadhr:* ح و ر. Core: an axis, a pivot-pin (the axle a wheel turns on). *aṢl:* ح و ر = *to return/revolve* (*ḥāra* = to come back; *al-ḥūr* = the returning; *muḥāwara* = dialogue, words "revolving" between two). Field: *miḥwar* = the axis/axle; the central line of symmetry. Untranslatable: an axis named from *returning/revolving* — perfect for the poem's mirror-axis (blueprint §4, the 20|21 fold) and the ring that *returns* to its start. Qur'anic (root): **Q84:14** *innahu ẓanna an lan yaḥūr* (he thought he would never *return*); **Q58:1** *wa-Llāhu yasmaʿu taḥāwurakumā* (God hears your dialogue — al-Mujādila). Not a Qur'anic *noun*, but its root carries *return/revolve* — usable as the technical "axis" while echoing *yaḥūr*.

- **الدَّائِرَة** — *jadhr:* د و ر. Core: a circle, a ring, a turning-round; a calamity-that-comes-round. *aṢl:* د و ر = *to circle/revolve* (*dāra* = to turn; *dawr* = a turn/cycle; *dār* = a dwelling, the circled-enclosed; *tadwīr* = making-circular). Field: *dāʾira* = (i) a circle/ring (the geometric figure!); (ii) a turn-of-fortune/encircling calamity (*dāʾiratu s-sawʾ*, *nakhshā an tuṣībanā dāʾira*). Untranslatable: the same word for *the geometer's circle* and *the wheel of fate coming round*. **The exact word for the corpus-as-ring** (anchor B: Q1↔Q114 topological ring; al-Khalīl's prosodic *circles*, *dawāʾir al-ʿarūḍ*, are literally called *dāʾira*!). Qur'anic: **Q9:98** *yatarabbaṣu bikumu d-dawāʾir* (awaiting the turns/circles of fortune against you); **Q5:52** *an tuṣībanā dāʾira*.

- **الهَنْدَسَة** — *jadhr:* (Arabized from Persian *handāz/andāza* = measure; root treated as ه ن د س). Core: geometry, engineering, the science of measured form. Field: *handasa* = geometry; *muhandis* = geometer/engineer; the *qanāt*-surveyor's measuring. Untranslatable: a word whose very origin is *measuring/estimating extent* — geometry as *applied measure* (cognate-in-sense to *taqdīr*). Not Qur'anic (Arabized technical term); use as the explicit "geometry/engineering" of the order — *handasat al-naẓm* (the geometry of the composition). Powerful precisely because it names what the project *measures*. ⚠ Use as metaphor of order, not as a claim that the Qur'an teaches geometry.

- **المِيزَان** — *jadhr:* و ز ن. Core: the Balance, the scale; measure/weight/meter. *aṢl:* و ز ن = *to weigh/balance* (*wazn* = weight; *mawzūn* = weighed-out, Q15:19; **and *wazn* = poetic *meter*** — the *mīzān* of verse!). Field: *al-Mīzān* = (i) the cosmic Balance set up for justice (Q55:7, al-Raḥmān); (ii) the scale of deeds on the Last Day (Q21:47, *al-mawāzīn al-qisṭ*); (iii) the *measure/proportion* of all things. Untranslatable: one word for *the scales of justice*, *the weighing of deeds*, *the measure of creation*, and *the meter of poetry*. **A keystone for THIS theme** (order = balance = measure = meter). Qur'anic: **Q55:7-9** *wa-s-samāʾa rafaʿahā wa-waḍaʿa l-mīzān · allā taṭghaw fī l-mīzān · wa-aqīmu l-wazna bi-l-qisṭ* (He raised the heaven and set the Balance — that you not transgress in the Balance — and uphold the weighing in justice) — al-Raḥmān; **Q21:47** *al-mawāzīna l-qisṭa li-yawmi l-qiyāmah*; **Q57:25** *wa-anzalnā... al-mīzān*.

### The inimitability words (the taḥaddī)

- **النَّظِير / لا نَظِير لَهُ** — *jadhr:* ن ظ ر. Core: a like/equal/counterpart; (the phrase) *lā naẓīra lah* = He has no equal. *aṢl:* ن ظ ر = *to look/regard* — a *naẓīr* is one *looked-at-as-matching*, a peer set beside another for comparison. Field: *naẓīr* = a counterpart, a match, a parallel; *naẓra* = a glance; *intaẓara* = to await/look-out-for. Untranslatable: an "equal" defined as *what bears comparison when looked-at-alongside* — so to say *lā naẓīr* is to say *nothing withstands the comparing gaze*. Qur'anic (concept): the matchlessness underlying the taḥaddī (Q2:23, Q17:88); cf. *lam yakun lahu kufuwan aḥad* (Q112:4, "no equal"). → pairs with *al-mithl*.

- **المِثْل / مِن مِثْلِه** — *jadhr:* م ث ل. Core: a like, a similitude, the equal-in-likeness. *aṢl:* م ث ل = *to resemble/stand-up-as-a-likeness* (*mathal* = a parable/proverb; *timthāl* = a statue, a *standing* likeness; *muthūl* = standing-before). Field: *mithl* = a like/match; **the taḥaddī word**: *fa-ʾtū bi-sūratin min mithlih* (bring a sūra *like it*); *bi-mithli hādhā l-Qurʾān* (Q17:88). Untranslatable: likeness *as something that can stand up in the place of* — the challenge is "produce what can *stand in its stead*." Qur'anic: **Q2:23** *bi-sūratin min mithlih*; **Q17:88** *lā yaʾtūna bi-mithlihi wa-law kāna baʿḍuhum li-baʿḍin ẓahīrā* (they could not bring its like, though they backed one another) — the core inimitability verse; **Q10:38**, **Q11:13** (*bi-ʿashri suwarin mithlih*). This is the rhetorical engine of Movements IV-V.

- **السِّرّ** — *jadhr:* س ر ر. Core: a secret, the hidden inmost. (Full root-knot at Class a #9: *secret / joy / repose / confiding*.) Field: *sirr* = the concealed; *asarra* = to confide/whisper; *yaʿlamu s-sirr* = He knows the secret. Untranslatable: the inmost *that is also the seat of joy*. Qur'anic: **Q20:7** *yaʿlamu s-sirra wa-akhfā*; **Q21:110** *innahu yaʿlamu l-jahra mina l-qawli wa-yaʿlamu mā taktumūn*. The *sirr* of the order — the hidden design the project surfaces.

- **اللِّسَان** — *jadhr:* ل س ن. Core: the tongue; a language; eloquence. Field: *lisān* = (i) the physical tongue; (ii) a language (*bi-lisānin ʿarabiyyin mubīn* — in a clear Arabic tongue); (iii) reputation/remembrance (*lisāna ṣidq*, Q26:84, "a tongue of truth [among the later folk]"). Untranslatable: *tongue*, *language*, and *abiding fair-mention* in one word. Qur'anic: **Q26:195** *bi-lisānin ʿarabiyyin mubīn* (in a clear Arabic tongue) — the *language* of the inimitable order; **Q16:103** *lisānu lladhī yulḥidūna ilayhi aʿjamiyyun wa-hādhā lisānun ʿarabiyyun mubīn* (the tongue they hint at is foreign, but THIS is clear Arabic) — a direct iʿjāz/taḥaddī verse.

## PART 3 — ROOT-PLAY (jinās / ishtiqāq opportunities)

The richest device available to this poem: *jinās al-ishtiqāq* (paronomasia by shared root) and *jinās al-i‑ishtiqāq*-adjacent (near-root chiming). Each entry gives the **root**, the **derived family**, and the **poetic move** — how to deploy the cluster so the form enacts the theme. The first eight are the mission's required cluster; I add six more selected for *this* theme.

### §1. ق د ر — measure / decree / the Measuring-Powerful (THE master root)

- **Family:** قَدَر (decree-as-measure) · قَدْر (worth, Laylat al-**Qadr**) · مِقْدَار (an exact quantity) · قَادِر (powerful) · قَدِير (the Measuring-Powerful, Name) · تَقْدِير (the measuring-out) · مَقْدُور (the decreed) · قُدْرَة (power) · اقْتِدَار (mastery, *muqtadir* — cf. Q54:42,55 *muqtadir*, project Q54 closure-frame finding).
- **Poetic move:** the poem's whole thesis collapses into one root. Build a line where *qaddara* (He measured) → *taqdīr* (measuring) → *qadar* (the measure) → *al-Qadīr* (the Measurer) cascade, demonstrating that **order, power, and decree are one word**. Land *bi-qadar* (Q54:49) as the proof-text: *everything by measure*. The supreme move: rhyme **al-taqdīr / al-Qadīr** (Class b) so the rhyme itself *is* the thesis. (Anchors A-D all reduce to "the Book is *measured*"; *qadar* is their lexical name.)

### §2. س ط ر — line / inscribed / "fables" (the script-and-sneer root)

- **Family:** سَطْر (a line of writing) · سُطُور / أَسْطَار (lines) · مَسْطُور (inscribed in lines) · يَسْطُرُون (they inscribe, Q68:1) · تَسْطِير (the inscribing) · أَسَاطِير (fables — *asāṭīr al-awwalīn*, the deniers' jeer ×9).
- **Poetic move:** **the central detonation of the poem.** The deniers called the Book *asāṭīru l-awwalīn* — "lines/fables of the ancients" — using *this very root* to dismiss it. The poem turns the sneer inside-out: *what they called asāṭīr is masṭūr by the Pen* — the same root that means "scribbled old tales" means "inscribed in perfect lines by Q68:1's *yasṭurūn*." Set *asāṭīr* (their charge) against *masṭūr/suṭūr* (the truth) in adjacent feet; the *jinās* makes the refutation *audible*. ⚠ Never let a hemistich rest on *asāṭīr* in a way that reads as conceding it; it must always be the rejected term.

### §3. ب ح ر — sea / meters (the form-is-content root)

- **Family:** بَحْر (sea) · بُحُور / بِحَار (seas) · **بُحُور = the poetic meters** (al-Khalīl's *biḥār al-shiʿr*) · بَحَّار (sailor) · تَبَحُّر (deep mastery, "becoming a sea" in a science) · أَبْحُر (Q31:27 *sabʿatu abḥur*, seven seas of ink).
- **Poetic move:** in a poem *about the Qur'an versus the meters of poetry*, *baḥr* is the one word that means both **the sea** (Q18:109: were the sea ink, God's words would not run dry) and **a poetic meter** (the *baḥr* you are writing *in*). Deploy: "your *buḥūr* [meters] are drops; His sea [*baḥr*] is the ink that never dries." The poet writing *in* a *baḥr* about the One whose words exceed all *biḥār* — *tabaḥḥur* (mastery) is itself "becoming a sea." Pairs directly with §4's inkwell and the cosmic-pen verses (Q18:109, Q31:27).

### §4. ح ر ف — letter / deviation / altered (the edge root)

- **Family:** حَرْف (a letter; an edge/brink) · أَحْرُف (the seven modes) · انْحِرَاف (deviation, swerving off) · مُنْحَرِف (deviant) · تَحْرِيف (corruption/alteration of scripture — *yuḥarrifūna l-kalim*) · مُحَرَّف (altered) · حِرْفَة (a craft) · مُحْتَرِف (artisan).
- **Poetic move:** the *ḥarf* (letter) is an *edge* (*ḥarf*) — and to swerve from it is *inḥirāf* (deviation), to corrupt it is *taḥrīf*. The move: *the Book's every ḥarf holds its edge; no inḥirāf, no taḥrīf has touched it* — the letters stand unswerving while prior scriptures were *muḥarraf*. Plays the preservation-of-the-text claim through a single root: *ḥarf* (letter) that admits no *taḥrīf* (alteration). Pairs with *al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ* (the **Preserved** Tablet). ⚠ Keep *taḥrīf* about *other* texts / about the rejecters' tampering, per the project's "one text" discipline — not a claim of internal variants.

### §5. ن ظ م — order / composition / threaded-string (the iʿjāz-term root)

- **Family:** نَظْم (composition, the stringing-into-order) · نِظَام (a system; the thread of a necklace) · انْتِظَام (falling-into-order) · مَنْظُوم (strung, versified) · مُنْتَظِم (ordered) · نَاظِم (the arranger/poet).
- **Poetic move:** *naẓm* is **the classical name for the Qur'an's compositional iʿjāz** (al-Jurjānī). The image inside the root is *pearls strung on one thread* (*niẓām*). Deploy for anchor B (the single ordering-thread through 114 sūras): "a hundred-and-fourteen pearls on one *niẓām* [thread]; cut the thread [*infiṣām*?] and they scatter — but the thread holds." The *manẓūm* (strung) Book vs the *manthūr* (scattered) — *naẓm* vs *nathr* (verse vs prose) is itself a root-pun the poem can exploit (the Qur'an is neither, or both). Contrast *intaẓama* (fell into order) with the rival's scatter.

### §6. ف ص ل — separation / verse-clausula / set-out-clearly (the division root)

- **Family:** فَصْل (decisive separation/judgment; *yawmu l-faṣl*) · فَاصِلَة (the **verse-ending/clausula** — al-Bāqillānī's *fawāṣil*) · فَصَّلَ (set out in detail) · مُفَصَّل (the clearly-divided; the last seventh of the Qur'an) · فِصَال (weaning, a separation) · فَيْصَل (a decisive arbiter) · مَفْصِل (a joint, where parts divide).
- **Poetic move:** **the technical heart of anchor A.** The *fāṣila* (verse-clausula/rhyme) is where meaning and music meet-and-part. The move: the Book's *fawāṣil* (verse-endings) are each a *faṣl* (decisive cut) — and where the *fāṣila* most varies (rhyme disperses), the *faṣl* (the sense's decisiveness) most concentrates (anchor A, r=−0.86). One root names both the rhyme-ending *and* the decisiveness that runs against it. Pair *faṣṣala* (set out distinctly) with *waṣala* (§ joined) — the Book that *divides* its verses yet *connects* its sense (*faṣl* ↔ *waṣl*, a perfect *ṭibāq* of near-homophone roots).

### §7. و ح د — One / unity / declaring-One (the tawḥīd root)

- **Family:** أَحَد (the indivisible One) · وَاحِد (the sole One) · وَحْدَة (unity) · تَوْحِيد (the declaring/realizing of Oneness) · وَحِيد (alone, solitary — Q74:11 *dharnī wa-man khalaqtu waḥīdā*) · مُوَحِّد (one who affirms Oneness) · اتِّحَاد (union) · وَحْدَه (He alone).
- **Poetic move:** **the poem's center** (blueprint §3: the *aḥad → aḥad* couplet at line 20-21, the Q112 centroid). The cascade *Wāḥid → aḥad → waḥda → tawḥīd* lets the center *enact* Oneness by deriving every word from one root — the form (one root) mirrors the content (one God). The clinching move: *the Book is **wāḥid** [one] because its Author is **Aḥad** [One]; its **waḥda** [unity] is His **tawḥīd** [declared Oneness]* — unity-of-text as proof-of-Unicity. Land it on the *al-Wāḥid al-Aḥad* classical pair. (Distinguish the two carefully: *wāḥid* = one-of/uniqueness; *aḥad* = indivisible-singularity — §Power-Lexicon.)

### §8. أ ي ي — sign / verse / miracle (the āya root)

- **Family:** آيَة (a sign / a verse / a miracle) · آي / آيَات (signs-verses) · (the root's force: a *mark* that *points beyond itself*).
- **Poetic move:** the single word *āya* means **verse**, **natural sign**, and **miracle** at once. The move writes the thesis in one word: *every **āya** [verse] of the order is an **āya** [sign] — therefore an **āya** [miracle]*. The poem can pivot on the triple sense without changing the word — the listener hears "verse," then "sign," then "miracle," as the sentence turns. Pair with *baṣīra/abṣār* (the insight that *reads* the sign) and *taʾwīl* (where the sign points). Direct to Q41:53 (signs in horizons and souls).

### §9. و ز ن — weigh / balance / meter (the mīzān root) [added]

- **Family:** وَزْن (weight/measure) · مِيزَان (the Balance; the meter) · مَوْزُون (weighed-out, Q15:19) · أَوْزَان (meters/weights) · وَزِير (minister, "load-bearer") · مُوَازَنَة (balancing/comparison).
- **Poetic move:** *wazn* is **both the scale of justice and the meter of verse** (the *mīzān al-shiʿr*). In a poem *in meter* about *measured order*, deploy: "He set the **mīzān** [Balance] (Q55:7); your **wazn** [meter] is weighed in it and found wanting." The poet's own *wazn* (al-Kāmil) is subject to the cosmic *Mīzān*. Pairs with §1 (*qadar*/measure) and §3 (*baḥr*/meter) to make a three-root chord: *measure (qadar) + balance (wazn) + sea-meter (baḥr)* = the order's full lexical signature. (Anchor: Q55, al-Raḥmān — the *mīzān* trio Q55:7-9.)

### §10. ف ت ح — open / unlock / grant-victory (the Fātiḥa root) [added]

- **Family:** فَتْح (opening / victory) · فَاتِحَة (the Opening, Sūra 1) · فَتَّاح (the Opener, Name) · مِفْتَاح (a key — *mafātiḥ al-ghayb*, the keys of the unseen, Q6:59) · اسْتِفْتَاح (seeking a decisive opening) · مَفْتُوح (opened).
- **Poetic move:** the ring's first door (blueprint A/A′). *al-Fātiḥa* (the Opening) opens the Book; *al-Fattāḥ* (the Opener) opens hearts; the *mafātīḥ* (keys) of the unseen are His. The move for the ring-closure (line 40 → line 1): *I sought the **istiftāḥ** [decisive opening] and the **Fātiḥa** [the Opening] was the **miftāḥ** [key] — the end **fataḥa** [opened] onto the beginning.* The seeker, returned to the threshold, finds the Opening was the key all along. Pairs with *khatm* (§ sealing) for the open↔seal *ṭibāq* that frames the whole poem (Fātiḥa opens / al-Nās seals / and the seal reopens the Opening).

### §11. ن ز ل — send-down / descend / the hospitality-gift (the tanzīl root) [added]

- **Family:** نَزَّلَ (sent down in stages) · تَنْزِيل (the graded descent) · أَنْزَلَ (sent down) · نُزُول (descent) · مَنْزِل (a stage/way-station; a lunar mansion — *qaddarahu manāzil*, Q10:5!) · نُزُل (a hospitality-gift, the *nuzul* of the blessed/damned) · مُنَزَّل (sent-down).
- **Poetic move:** *manzil* means both a **way-station on a road** and a **lunar mansion** (the measured *manāzil* of the moon, Q10:5, Q36:39) and the **resting-stage** of the traveler. The move ties anchor B's "road" to the *tanzīl*: *the Book descended (**tanazzala**) by **manāzil** [stages] as the moon moves by **manāzil** [measured mansions]* — revelation's descent is itself *measured* like the lunar order. Pairs the *graded descent* (tanzīl, why the text could be re-arranged) with the *measured stations* (manāzil) — descent and measure in one root. (Echo: Q54 al-Qamar; Q10:5 the moon's *taqdīr* into *manāzil*.)

### §12. ر ق م — inscribe-with-marks / the numbered-register (the marqūm root) [added]

- **Family:** رَقَم (a mark/numeral) · مَرْقُوم (inscribed-and-numbered, *kitābun marqūm*, Q83:9,20) · أَرْقَام (numerals) · رَقِيم (the inscribed tablet — *aṣḥābu l-kahfi wa-r-Raqīm*, Q18:9!) · تَرْقِيم (numbering/enumeration).
- **Poetic move:** *raqm* fuses **writing and number** in one root — a *kitāb marqūm* is a book that is *both inscribed and numerated*. **Exactly the poem's fusion of script and mathematics.** The move: *the record is **marqūm** [written-and-numbered] — letter is number, line is count* — the Book where *al-ḥarf* (letter) and *al-ʿadad* (number) are one inscription. *al-Raqīm* (Q18:9, the inscribed tablet of the Cave-folk) is a haunting cognate. ⚠ This is the closest root to numerology's territory: keep *raqm* as the *image* of writing-that-is-also-measured-order, NOT as an abjad-count claim (guardrail). Deploy as the lexical bridge between Part-2's *al-kitāb* and *al-ʿadad*.

### §13. ح ص ي / ح ص ر — enumerate-exhaustively / encompass (the aḥṣā root) [added]

- **Family:** أَحْصَى (to count exhaustively, leave nothing out) · إِحْصَاء (exhaustive enumeration) · حَصْر (encompassing-restriction; counting-without-remainder) · مُحْصَى (fully numbered) · حَصِير (a confining enclosure; see Class b #26).
- **Poetic move:** *aḥṣā* is the Qur'an's own word for **God's exhaustive counting of all things** — *wa-aḥṣā kulla shayʾin ʿadadā* (Q72:28), *kulla shayʾin aḥṣaynāhu fī imāmin mubīn* (Q36:12). The move turns the project's *enumeration of structure* into worship: *I counted (**ʿaddadtu**) the chambers; He had already numbered (**aḥṣā**) them all* — human enumeration is a faint echo of divine *iḥṣāʾ*. Pairs with §1 (*qadar*) and §12 (*raqm*): *measured (qadar) + numbered (aḥṣā) + inscribed (marqūm)*. The blueprint's "I counted, and the count came to One" (line 34) lives here. (Anchor: Q72:28, Q36:12, Q19:94 *aḥṣāhum wa-ʿaddahum ʿaddā*.)

### §14. ع ج ز — inability / the rear / the miracle-by-incapacitation (the iʿjāz root) [added]

- **Family:** عَجْز (inability, impotence) · أَعْجَزَ (to render incapable) · إِعْجَاز (**the inimitability** — rendering-the-challenger-incapable) · مُعْجِزَة (a miracle, "that which incapacitates") · عَاجِز (helpless) · عَجُوز (an old woman; the "rear/spent" of life) · عَجُز (the rear, the *ʿajuz* = **the second hemistich** of a bayt!).
- **Poetic move:** **the name of the whole genre.** *iʿjāz* is not "wonder" but **the act of rendering all rivals *ʿājiz* (incapable)** — the miracle *defined by the opponent's failure*. The move makes the taḥaddī precise: *the **iʿjāz** is your **ʿajz** [incapacity]; the proof is in your empty hands* — inimitability *is* the challenger's demonstrated inability (Q17:88). Bonus prosodic pun: the *ʿajuz* (rear) is the **second hemistich** of every line — so the poet can land the *iʿjāz* claim *on the ʿajuz*, letting the word's two senses (the rear-hemistich / the incapacitation) coincide at the very rhyme. (Anchor: Q2:23, Q17:88; the project's entire *iʿjāz* framework, finding #2-3.)

## PART 4 — THE NŪN (ن): the poem's buried signature

> **The single most deployable hidden device in this poem.** The letter ن is, simultaneously: a *verified empirical finding* (it is the corpus's true third-most-frequent letter, correcting a 1,100-year error), the *sole one-letter opener* of any sūra (Q68), a *cluster of cosmic lore* (the whale, the inkwell), and a *shape* (a vessel holding a single dot) that *is* the poem's thesis in miniature. The poet can thread ن through the qaṣīda so that a later analyst, x-raying the text, finds the *nūn-signature* the way the project found the corpus's hidden architecture.

### 4a. Nūn as a LETTER — the true third (the encoded finding)

**The verified fact (ledger H-NEW-1810):** the corpus letter-frequency rank-order, under locked rules over 329,131 graphemes, is

> **ا (18.01%) > ل (11.60%) > ن (8.29%) > م (8.12%) > ي > و > …**

**ن is the third-most-frequent letter — *above* م (mīm) — by ~0.17 percentage points.** The classical claim that the order is *alif > lām > mīm* (embedded in the prestige of the *ALM* muqaṭṭaʿāt) is **factually wrong** (ledger §3b): **nūn, not mīm, is third.** This is a ~1,100-year correction the project makes — and a *true, checkable* fact the poem can *encode as a hidden signature*.

And at the **verse-ending**, the dominance is overwhelming: **verse-final nūn alone = 50.10%** (3,124 of 6,236 verses — almost exactly *half the Book ends on nūn*; ledger H-NEW-2080). The *fawāṣil* (verse-clausulae) are a nūn-ocean. So ن is not merely frequent — it is the **sound the Book rhymes on**, the rāwī of the whole corpus.

**The deployment:** make ن *the poem's own most-frequent salient letter*, especially at line-ends and in the key terms — *nūr, nūn, naẓm, nūr, nuzūl, nushūr, al-Raḥmān, al-naẓīr, al-nadhīr* — so that the qaṣīda *over-deploys nūn the way the Book does*, and a counting analyst finds the poem's *nūn-loading* mirrors the corpus's. **The buried signature: the poem secretly rhymes-with / leans-on the very letter it reveals to be the hidden third.** (If the poem is in Class (b) -īr or has nūn-heavy power-words, this falls out naturally: *al-Qadīr, al-munīr, al-nadhīr, al-nakīr, al-naṣīr* all carry nūn.)

> ⚠ **Discipline:** state the *frequency fact* (ن is third, ن ends half the verses) as the awe — it is *true and logged*. Do NOT convert it into a count-miracle (e.g. "ن appears N times = 19×k"): Q68's nūn-count is a *queued-Khalifa-claim*, not a confirmed finding (ledger §4). The wonder is the *correction of the classical order* and the *50% verse-final dominance*, both verified — not any abjad total.

### 4b. Nūn as QUR'ANIC OPENER — Q68:1, the corpus-exact singleton

**نٓ ۚ وَالقَلَمِ وَمَا يَسْطُرُونَ** — *Nūn. By the pen and what they inscribe* (Q68:1).

Of all **29** muqaṭṭaʿāt-opener sūras, **exactly one** opens on the single letter ن: al-Qalam (Q68) — verified by direct enumeration (Q068-F-08, **CORPUS-EXACT 1/29**). And it is the **only** verse in the entire corpus that pairs a muqaṭṭaʿ-letter with an immediate **oath on the pen** (a literary singleton). So the poem's signature-letter is *also* the letter that, alone, opens a sūra — *and* it opens it **by swearing on the very act of writing** (*yasṭurūn*, the س ط ر root of §3, the *suṭūr* rhyme of Class a).

**The deployment:** Q68:1 is the poem's *epigraph-in-essence* — **Nūn. By the pen and what they inscribe.** It fuses, in three words, the signature-letter (ن), the instrument (*al-qalam*, Part 2), and the act (*yasṭurūn* → *al-suṭūr*, the rhyme). The poet can open or pivot the poem on an echo of this line; the volta (blueprint line 24, "the pen that walked the road") *is* a gloss on *wa-l-qalam wa-mā yasṭurūn*.

### 4c. Nūn's LORE — the whale and the cosmic inkwell

The classical mufassirūn glossed the bare ن of Q68:1 four ways (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Suyūṭī *Durr al-Manthūr*; project Q068 tafsir-survey):

1. **The great fish / whale (*al-nūn al-ʿaẓīm*)** — *al-nūn* is a name for the great fish; the cosmic whale **upon which the earths rest** (Ibn ʿAbbās, via multiple chains: Mujāhid, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, al-Ḍaḥḥāk → Ibn ʿAbbās). This is also why **Yūnus** (Jonah) is *Dhū l-Nūn* — "the one of the fish/whale" (Q21:87, Q68:48 *ka-ṣāḥibi l-ḥūt*, in *this very sūra*). The signature-letter is a *whale that bears the world*.
2. **The inkwell of light (*al-dawāt*)** — ن is the cosmic **inkwell** from which the Pen (Q68:1's *qalam*) draws to write the decree (Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Qatāda: "a tablet of light or an inkwell"). The letter *is the vessel of the cosmic ink* — and Q18:109 / Q31:27 (*if the sea were ink…*) make the *ink/sea* image scriptural. **Nūn = the inkwell; the Pen = al-qalam; the line = al-suṭūr.** The poem's three sister-images (inkwell / pen / line) are a single Qur'anic-cosmic scene, all keyed to ن.
3. **A tablet of light** (Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, minor variant) — linking ن to *al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ* (Part 2).
4. **A letter of God's Names** (al-Qurṭubī) — ن as initial of a Name (e.g. *al-Nūr*).

**The deployment:** the poet has, in one letter, a **whale bearing the earth** AND an **inkwell of light** AND a **tablet** AND a **Name** — a four-fold cosmic lore-cluster. The richest move fuses the inkwell with the sea-pun (§3): *the sea is His inkwell (ن); were every tree a pen (al-qalam) and the sea ink (Q31:27), His words would not run dry — and the order is what the Pen wrote from the Nūn-inkwell onto the Lawḥ.* The whale-sense lets the poem touch Yūnus / *Dhū l-Nūn* (the prophet *of the nūn*) — swallowed into the dark and written back into light.

### 4d. Nūn's SHAPE — a vessel with a contained dot (the seed of order)

The grapheme **ن** is a **half-circle / open bowl** (a *vessel*, a *cup*, a *crescent*) **holding a single dot** (the *nuqṭa*) at its center-above. Read as an image, ن is:

- **a contained dot** — a *point of order held within a curve*; the seed/atom of meaning cupped in a vessel. (The *nuqṭa*, the dot, is in the lettrist tradition the origin-point of all letters — the *bāʾ*'s dot, the "point under the *bāʾ* of *Bismillāh*" of the famous saying — and ن is a *bowl built around its dot*.)
- **a crescent / a cup / an inkwell in profile** — the shape *is* the inkwell-gloss (4c.2): a curved vessel with the ink (the dot) inside.
- **a half-circle** — *half of the ring/dāʾira* (Part 2): ن is geometrically a **semicircle**, and the poem's whole form is a *ring* (the *dāʾira* of the corpus, anchor B). The signature-letter is *half the circle the poem completes* — the open bowl that the ring closes.

**The deployment — the buried signature, fully assembled:**

> ن is the poem's secret monogram. It is **(a)** the *true third letter* the poem reveals (the corrected order, the 50%-verse-final rāwī); **(b)** the *sole one-letter opener*, Q68:1's **Nūn. By the pen and what they inscribe** — fusing letter + pen + inscription; **(c)** the *whale that bears the world* and the *inkwell of light* from which the cosmic Pen writes the order onto the Tablet; and **(d)** a *shape* — an open vessel cupping one dot, a half-circle, a seed of order held in a curve, *half of the ring the poem itself closes.*

So the poet should: **(1)** load ن at the rhyme-edges and in the key terms (so the poem *leans on nūn* as the Book does); **(2)** echo Q68:1 at the opening or the volta (the letter + pen + line); **(3)** at the **center** (blueprint §3, the *aḥad*-couplet) or the **close**, name or evoke ن as *the contained dot / the inkwell / the half-circle that the poem's full circle completes* — so the buried signature *clicks shut* exactly where the macro-ring does. A later analyst, counting letters and reading the shape-imagery, recovers the *nūn-signature* the way the project recovered the corpus's order: **the poem hides, in its most-frequent letter, the very finding it sings** — that ن, the unregarded third, is everywhere, bearing the lines, cupping the dot, half-drawing the circle the Book completes.

> **One-line seed for the poet (in spirit, not yet metered):** *"By the Nūn — the inkwell of light, the whale of the deep, the half-ring — and by the Pen, and by what they inscribe in lines: the third letter no one counted, that ends half the verses, and bears the world."*
