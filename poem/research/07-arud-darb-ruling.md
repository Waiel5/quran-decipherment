# THE RULING — al-Kāmil, rhyme -ūn (rawiyy nūn, ridf = long ū)

## TL;DR (the verdict, four lines)

1. **al-Kāmil al-tāmm maqṭūʿ ḍarb is a STANDARD, fully-attested ḍarb.** Pattern `مُتَفاعِلْ` = realized **`uu – –`** (= the prosodists' `فِعْلاتُن`, same foot, two spellings); ʿilla = **qaṭʿ**; a complete (tāmm) line with a **ṣaḥīḥa ʿarūḍ** regularly takes it. (al-Kāmil's three tāmm ḍarbs: ṣaḥīḥ `مُتَفاعِلُن`, **maqṭūʿ `مُتَفاعِلْ`**, aḥadhdh-muḍmar `فَعْلُن`.)
2. **For a MUQAYYAD `-ūn` rhyme (sākin nūn — the normal nūniyya, the Qur'an's own clausula), your hypothesis is CORRECT.** Short-penult `-ūn` (ẓu·nūn, su·kūn) fits the **ṣaḥīḥ** ḍarb; long-penult `-ūn` (mak·nūn, maw·zūn, an·nūn) needs the **maqṭūʿ** ḍarb. The two are **different fixed ḍarbs**.
3. **Therefore mixing short-penult and long-penult `-ūn` in one muqayyad qaṣīda is a REAL DEFECT — it is ḍarb-drift (silent change of the fixed ḍarb), which classically also reads as ʿuyūb al-qāfiya (sinād-family / kasr).** It is *not* neutralized by the final super-heavy syllable, because a sākin-rawiyy `-ūn` ends `–#` (no iṭlāq vowel) and the penult therefore lands in the **foot-final** slot that *defines* the ḍarb.
4. **The escape hatch and its cost:** a **MUṬLAQ** `-ūn` rhyme (voiced, lengthened final ḍamma → `-ūnū`) *does* neutralize the penult (mixing becomes legal), because the rawiyy goes moving + a waṣl is added and the penult retreats to the flexible foot-head — **exactly how the real al-Qaḥṭāniyya nūniyya mixes `qur·ʾān` with `ji·nān`**. But muṭlaq `-ūnū` turns the bell-hard stop `‑ŪN#` into an open `‑Ū‑NŪ`, makes you police iqwāʾ on the ḍamma, and is not what a thunderclap nūniyya wants. **Do not take it.**

**RECOMMENDATION (full reasoning in §5):** compose **muqayyad `-ūn` (sākin nūn) in Family B = LONG-penult, with the al-Kāmil al-tāmm MAQṬŪʿ ḍarb `مُتَفاعِلْ`.** It seats your four keystones — **al-nūn, mawzūn, maknūn, mawḍūn** — natively; it lands the hardest "thunderclap"; and it *excludes by construction* the monotony-prone plural verbs `yafʿalūn` (which are short-penult and would be metrically illegal in Family B, so they cannot leak in). Certified inventory in §6: 46 Family-B words + 24 Family-A backup, grouped by root.

## 1. (Q1) Is al-Kāmil al-tāmm maqṭūʿ ḍarb standard? — YES

### 1.1 The foot, in both notations

| | al-Kāmil sound foot | maqṭūʿ ḍarb |
|---|---|---|
| **tafʿīla** | `مُتَفَاعِلُنْ` mutafāʿilun | **`مُتَفَاعِلْ` mutafāʿil** (prosodists write `فِعْلاتُن` / `فَعِلاتُن`) |
| **letters (1/0)** | `1 1 1 0 1 1 0` (7) | **`1 1 1 0 1 0` (6)** |
| **realized weight** | `u u – u –` | **`u u – –`** |
| **anatomy** | sabab-thaqīl `muta`(`11`) + watid `fā`(`110`-head) + sabab `lun`(`10`) | sabab-thaqīl `muta`(`11`) + `fā`(`10`) + **`ʿil`(`10`)** — the final watid's last moving letter deleted, its predecessor made sākin |

> **The `مُتَفاعِلْ` ↔ `فِعْلاتُن` puzzle, resolved.** They are the **same realized foot** (`u u – –`), written two ways. Verified: `فعلاتن` = `f(1)a ʿ(1)i l(1)ā(0) t(1)u n(0)` = `1 1 1 0 1 0` → `u u – –`; `متفاعل` (after qaṭʿ) = `1 1 1 0 1 0` → `u u – –`. Identical six letters, identical scansion. The handbooks transcribe the *cut* foot by the nearest standard tafʿīla name (`فعلاتن`), but the **sound is `mutafāʿil`, `u u – –`**. Do not let the two names mislead you into thinking there are two feet.

### 1.2 Its ʿilla: **qaṭʿ** (القَطْع)

Fetched definitions, verbatim and converging:
- abuqudamah / al-Tabrīzī tradition: *«إسقاط أحد المتحركين من الوتد المجموع المتطرّف»* … *«إسكان اللام»* — delete one of the two moving letters of the **final watid majmūʿ**, and make the preceding letter sākin. `مُتَفاعِلُن (…ʿi-lun)` → `مُتَفاعِلْ (…ʿil)`.
- baytalqaseed: *«القطع يُحدث في (متفاعلن) بحذف اللام وإسكان التاء … (فعلاتن)»* (same operation, expressed on the alternate spelling).
- Wikipedia / loghate: *«القطع … حذف آخر الوتد المجموع في نهاية التفعيلة وتسكين ما قبله، فتتحول متفاعلن إلى متفاعل».*

So qaṭʿ strikes the **ḍarb's own final foot only** (a licensed ʿilla at the ʿarūḍ/ḍarb, never mid-line) — exactly where the rhyme lives.

### 1.3 May a **full (tāmm)** line with **ṣaḥīḥa ʿarūḍ** take it? — YES, this is a named standard form

Fetched, verbatim:
- al-Tabrīzī tradition (abuqudamah): *«الصورة الثانية: تام (عروضه صحيحة، وضربه مقطوع)»* — i.e. ʿarūḍ `متفاعلن` + ḍarb `متفاعلْ`.
- baytalqaseed table row: `العروض متفاعلن صحيحة | الضرب مقطوع (فعلاتن) | العلة: القطع`.
- almerja: *«عروضه صحيحة متفاعلن، وضربه مقطوع متفاعل».*

**Fetched shawāhid (a real, fully-scanned tāmm line, ṣaḥīḥa ʿarūḍ + maqṭūʿ ḍarb):**

> **وَإِذا دَعَوْنَكَ عَمَّهُنَّ فَإِنَّهُ ‖ نَسَبٌ يَزيدُكَ عِنْدَهُنَّ خَبالا**
> *wa-idhā daʿawnaka ʿammahunna fa-innahu ‖ nasabun yazīduka ʿindahunna khabālā*

baytalqaseed's own foot-by-foot taqṭīʿ of the ʿajuz:
```
نسبن يزي  = mutafāʿilun   (u u – u –)
دكعن دهن  = mutafāʿilun   (u u – u –)
نخبالا    = فعلاتن = mutafāʿil = MAQṬŪʿ  (u u – –)
```
I re-scanned the ḍarb foot from phonemes: `نخبالا` = `na-kha-bā-lā` = `1 1 1 0 1 0` = **`u u – –`** ✓ — confirms the maqṭūʿ foot exactly.

A second, modern, widely-anthologised tāmm maqṭūʿ exemplar (Bishāra al-Khūrī), ḍarb on `-āl`:
> **وَإِذا اِفتَقَرتَ إِلى الذَخائِرِ لَم تَجِد ‖ ذُخراً يَكونُ كَصالِحِ الأَعمالِ**
> ending `al-aʿmāl(i)` — penult `mā` is LONG → seats the `u u – –` close natively. (Pattern witness for "long pre-rawiyy element → maqṭūʿ.")

## 2. (Q2) Under each ḍarb, which penult-weight of -ūn is legal? — the proof

### 2.1 The anatomy of a muqayyad `-ūn` rhyme word

A `-ūn` word like `al-maknūn` / `al-ẓunūn`, used as a **muqayyad** rhyme (rawiyy nūn **sākin**, the normal/Qur'anic case — see §3.2), ends in a **super-heavy** final syllable `‑ŪN` = `CŪN` = `C(1) ū(0) n(0)`:
- the **ridf** is the long `ū` (`0`),
- the **rawiyy** is the final `n` (`0`, sākin — no iṭlāq vowel after it),
- so the closing syllable is `– ` **plus one extra hanging sākin** (the rawiyy), which at the absolute verse-end is metrically free (every meter lets the line end "one sākin heavy" — the rhyme's muqayyad rawiyy).

Net: **the foot's last *realized* syllable = `–` (the `Cū`), and the syllable *before* it = the WORD'S PENULT.** That penult is what fills the foot's penultimate slot — and that slot is exactly where ṣaḥīḥ and maqṭūʿ differ.

### 2.2 The two ḍarbs differ precisely at the penult slot

```
ṣaḥīḥ  mutafāʿilun = u u – u –     last two syllables =  u –     (… ʿi-lun)
maqṭūʿ mutafāʿil   = u u – –       last two syllables =  – –     (… ʿil)
```
The final `–` is the rawiyy-bearing close in both. **They differ in the penultimate syllable: `u` (ṣaḥīḥ) vs `–` (maqṭūʿ).** That penultimate syllable IS the rhyme word's penult.

### 2.3 Worked scansion — `al-ẓunūn` (short penult) vs `maknūn` (long penult)

**`al-ẓunūn`** = `…ẓu‑NŪN` : penult `ẓu` = open `CV` = light **`u`**; close `NŪN` = `n(1)ū(0)` = **`–`** + hanging rawiyy `n(0)`.
```
final foot:  [ u u ]  [ – ]  [ ẓu = u ]  [ NŪ = – ]  + n#
             = u u  –  u  –   →  mutafāʿilun  =  ṢAḤĪḤ ḍarb       ✓ FITS ṣaḥīḥ
```
Try to force it onto a maqṭūʿ poem (close must be `– –`): `ẓu` is light, gives `… u –`, **one sākin short of `– –`** → the foot reverts to `mutafāʿilun`. The ḍarb has silently flipped back to ṣaḥīḥ. ✗

**`maknūn`** = `mak‑NŪN` : penult `mak` = closed `CVC` = heavy **`–`**; close `NŪN` = **`–`** + hanging rawiyy `n(0)`.
```
final foot:  [ u u ]  [ – ]  [ mak = – ]  [ NŪ = – ]  + n#
             = u u  –  –  –  ?  →  the sound foot's penult slot wanted 'u' but got '–'
```
`mak` (heavy) **cannot** sit in the ṣaḥīḥ foot's `u` slot — it adds a sākin, **overflowing** the foot. The only legal home is the maqṭūʿ foot, whose penult slot is `–`:
```
maqṭūʿ:  [ u u ]  [ – ]  [ mak = – ]  [ NŪ = – ]  + n#
         = u u  –  –   →  mutafāʿil  =  MAQṬŪʿ ḍarb              ✓ FITS maqṭūʿ
```

**Result (the load-bearing table):**

| rhyme word | penult | penult weight | legal final foot | ḍarb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| al-ẓu·**nūn**, su·**kūn**, qu·**rūn**, yaf-ʿa·**lūn** | ẓu / su / qu / ʿa | **SHORT (`u`)** | `mutafāʿilun` `u u – u –` | **ṣaḥīḥ** |
| mak·**nūn**, maw·**zūn**, an·**nūn**, maw·**ḍūn** | mak / maw / an / maw | **LONG (`–`)** | `mutafāʿil` `u u – –` | **maqṭūʿ** |

Your hypothesis (1) and (2) are **CONFIRMED for the muqayyad `-ūn` rhyme**: SHORT-penult → ṣaḥīḥ; LONG-penult → maqṭūʿ.

## 3. (Q3) Is mixing the two in one qaṣīda a defect? — YES (for muqayyad); name + the iṭlāq objection answered

### 3.1 The verdict: ḍarb-drift (and a qāfiya defect)

The ḍarb is **fixed for the whole qaṣīda** (set by the maṭlaʿ; §5/§6 of the rubric). If a muqayyad `-ūn` poem mixes penults, then:
- a **long-penult** line forces the close to `– –` (maqṭūʿ),
- a **short-penult** line forces the close to `u –` (ṣaḥīḥ),

so the **ḍarb silently changes line to line**. That is **ḍarb-drift** — the meter's fixed terminal foot is not held constant. It is a hard metrical fault, independent of rhyme.

It **also** surfaces as a **qāfiya defect**. The qāfiya (al-Khalīl's definition) runs from the last sākin back through the preceding moving letter — for `-ūn` that span **includes the penult's vowelling/closure**. Changing the penult from open-short to closed-long changes the **tawjīh/ḥadhw layer** of the rhyme (the syllabic shape feeding the ridf), i.e. it is a **sinād-family inconsistency** (the supporting structure of the rhyme is not reproduced every line). The strict name most prosodists would reach for here is **kasr** (a broken/uneven ḍarb) manifesting as **sinād** in the rhyme-tail. Either label is fatal in canon-grade verse.

**So: mixing short- and long-penult `-ūn` in one muqayyad qaṣīda = DEFECT. Pick ONE family and hold it.**

### 3.2 The head-on objection: does the final super-heavy syllable + iṭlāq NEUTRALIZE the penult?

**This is the right question, and the answer turns entirely on muqayyad vs muṭlaq.** (`القافية المقيّدة` = sākin rawiyy; `المطلقة` = moving rawiyy — fetched, verbatim: *«المقيّدة ما كانت ساكنة الروي … والمطلقة ما كانت متحرّكة الروي»*.)

**(a) MUQAYYAD `-ūn` (sākin nūn) — the penult is NOT neutralized.** No iṭlāq vowel exists: the word ends `…penult · ŪN#`, the rawiyy `n` is sākin, the close is `– #`. The penult is the **immediately preceding** syllable = the foot's penultimate slot = the ḍarb-defining slot. So the penult **controls** the ḍarb. Your hypothesis holds. **This is the case for a normal `-ūn` nūniyya — and for the Qur'an's own verse-final nūn (the 50.10% clausula is overwhelmingly sākin-nūn).**

**(b) MUṬLAQ `-ūnū` (voiced, lengthened final ḍamma) — the penult IS neutralized.** If you voice the final ḍamma and lengthen it (iṭlāq → a **waṣl** wāw), the word becomes `…penult · ŪN · Ū` — the rawiyy `n` is now **moving**, followed by a waṣl syllable. The rhyme tail is now **three** syllables: `[penult] [ridf-ŪU = –] [rawiyy+waṣl ŪU = –]`. Verified:
```
short penult al-fu·NŪ·NŪ  =  1 1 0 1 0   →  u – –
long  penult mak·NŪ·NŪ    =  1 0 1 0 1 0 →  – – –
```
The **last two** syllables are `– –` in **both** (ridf-heavy + waṣl-heavy); the penult (`u` vs `–`) is now the **third-from-last** syllable, which lands in the **foot-head** — and the al-Kāmil head is **resolvable** (sabab-thaqīl `u u` ↔ iḍmār `–`). So the foot can absorb either penult **without changing the ḍarb**. Mixing becomes legal.

**This is not hypothetical — it is exactly what the real al-Qaḥṭāniyya nūniyya does** (§4): a 689-bayt canonical al-Kāmil poem, rhyme **muṭlaq `-āni`** (rawiyy nūn moving + kasra-waṣl, ridf alif), which **freely mixes** `al-fur·qān` / `al-qur·ʾān` (LONG penult) with `ma·kān(ī)` / `ji·nān(ī)` / `shā·n(ī)` (SHORT penult) under one fixed ḍarb. The alif-ridf + waṣl push the penult into the flexible head; the distinction evaporates.

**So your hypothesis is RIGHT for the rhyme you actually want (muqayyad `-ūn`, hard stop) and WRONG only for muṭlaq `-ūnū` — which you do not want** (it dissolves the thunderclap into an open `‑Ū‑NŪ`, demands iqwāʾ-policing on the ḍamma every line, and is the rarer register for a nūn poem). The neutralization is real but it is **bought with the very hardness that motivates a nūniyya.** Verdict stands: **muqayyad → one penult family.**

> **One subtlety worth stating so a future auditor doesn't "correct" you:** the al-Qaḥṭāniyya is *evidence the rule is regime-dependent*, not a counter-example to it. If anyone points at "but the famous nūniyya mixes penults!", the answer is: *that one is muṭlaq `-āni`; ours is muqayyad `-ūn`; different qāfiya class, different rule.*

## 4. (Q4) Real al-Kāmil poems rhyming on nūn — fetched, scanned, penult-checked

I could not locate a **pure tāmm `-ūn`** al-Kāmil qaṣīda in the open corpora (aldiwan/poetspedia/Wikisource); the `-ūn` murdaf-nūn al-Kāmil material that exists is **majzūʾ** (stated explicitly, per your instruction). I therefore give **two** real anchors that together pin down both branches, plus the maqṭūʿ shawāhid from §1.3.

### Anchor A — al-Qaḥṭānī, **al-Nūniyya** *(yā munzila l-āyāti wa-l-furqān)* — tāmm al-Kāmil, **murdaf nūn `-āni`** (MUṬLAQ)
Source: aldiwan.net/poem134646 (also adab.wiki, poetsgate, shamela 17070). Page states **البحر: الكامل، القافية: النون.** Opening (fetched verbatim):

> يَا مُنْزِلَ الآيَاتِ وَالْفُرْقَانِ ‖ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَكَ حُرْمَةُ الْقُرْآنِ
> اِشْرَحْ بِهِ صَدْرِي لِمَعْرِفَةِ الْهُدَى ‖ وَاعْصِمْ بِهِ قَلْبِي مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ
> يَسِّرْ بِهِ أَمْرِي وَأَقْضِ مَآرِبِي ‖ وَأَجِرْ بِهِ جَسَدِي مِنَ النِّيرَانِ
> وَاحْطُطْ بِهِ وِزْرِي وَأَخْلِصْ نِيَّتِي ‖ وَاشْدُدْ بِهِ أَزْرِي وَأَصْلِحْ شَانِي
> وَاكْشِفْ بِهِ ضُرِّي وَحَقِّقْ تَوْبَتِي ‖ وَارْبِحْ بِهِ بَيْعِي بِلاَ خُسْرَانِ
> طَهِّرْ بِهِ قَلْبِي وَصَفِّ سَرِيرَتِي ‖ أَجْمِلْ بِهِ ذِكْرِي وَأَعْلِ مَكَانِي

**ḍarb:** the ʿajuz ends `…‑āni` every line (muṭlaq: rawiyy nūn **moving** + kasra lengthened to waṣl yāʾ; ridf = alif). The terminal foot is the al-Kāmil close carrying the `‑ā‑ni` tail — a **ṣaḥīḥ-class muṭlaq** ḍarb.
**Penult families — and the decisive observation:** the rhyme set **MIXES** weights:
- LONG penult: `al-fur·QĀN`, `al-qur·ʾĀN` (`qur` closed).
- SHORT penult: `ma·KĀN(ī)`, `ji·NĀN(ī)`, `SHĀ·n(ī)`/`shā·nī` (`ma`, `ji`, `shā?` — open).

That a flawless 689-bayt canonical nūniyya mixes them **proves §3.2(b)**: under **muṭlaq** murdaf-nūn the penult is neutralized. (It does **not** license mixing under muqayyad `-ūn`.)

### Anchor B — al-Wāʾwāʾ al-Dimashqī, *(bi-liḥāẓihi sayfu l-manūn)* — majzūʾ al-Kāmil, **murdaf nūn `-ūn`** (the closest real `-ūn`)

## 5. (Q5) RECOMMENDATION — which family for *al-Nūniyya al-Kubrā*

### Choose **Family B (LONG-penult) + al-Kāmil al-tāmm MAQṬŪʿ ḍarb `مُتَفاعِلْ` (`u u – –`), muqayyad.**

**Why B wins, decisively, for THIS poem:**

1. **It seats every keystone natively.** Your signature **al-nūn** (`an·nūn`, long), thesis **mawzūn** (`maw·zūn`, long), **maknūn** (`mak·nūn`, long), and weave-word **mawḍūn** (`maw·ḍūn`, long — Q56:15 *sururin mawḍūnah*) are **ALL long-penult**. They are *born* in Family B and are **metrically illegal in Family A**. The poem's lethal core (COMPOSITION-BRIEF §9: *mawzūn / maknūn / the nūn*) is a Family-B core. To pick A is to exile your own thesis-words.

2. **It lands the hardest thunderclap.** The maqṭūʿ close `… – –` is two heavy syllables slammed together (`mak‑NŪN#`, `maw‑ZŪN#`) — a double-stop, heavier and more final than the ṣaḥīḥ `… u –`. For aphoristic "thunderclap" landings on a hard nūn, `– –#` is the harder hammer. al-Kāmil + maqṭūʿ is a recognised "hard-verdict" cadence.

3. **It auto-excludes the monotony trap.** Family A's great weakness (which the brief flags) is that it *admits the entire open class of sound-plural imperfect verbs* `yafʿalūn` — `yaf·ʿa·LŪN`, penult `ʿa` = **short** → grammatically endless, semantically thin, and a near-guarantee of īṭāʾ-adjacent monotony and ḥashw. In **Family B those verbs are metrically forbidden** (short penult can't fill `– –`), so they **cannot leak in**. The maqṭūʿ ḍarb is a *filter* that keeps the rhyme on heavy, lexically-loaded `maCCūn`/`naCCūn` forms. This is a real compositional advantage, not a constraint.

4. **It matches the project's sound-law.** The verified anti-twin (`r=−0.86`) and the compression-tail both say the Book **hardens and tightens toward its end**. A ḍarb that *cuts* (qaṭʿ) and lands `– –` is the formal analogue of that tightening — the rhyme itself enacts the thesis. (You may even gloss this in the poem's ars-poetica register: the line that *cuts* to land, like the Book that compresses to close.)

**The cost of B (and why it's acceptable):** Family B's pool is somewhat **smaller** than A's and skews to the `maC·CŪN` (mafʿūl) morphology, so you must work harder to avoid morphological monotony (every rhyme sounding like `maC‑CŪN`). Mitigations: (i) the inventory below spreads B across **many distinct roots** and a few **non-mafʿūl** long-penult forms (`an‑nūn` the noun, `al‑ʿar‑jūn`, `al‑ṭā‑ʿūn`, `al‑may‑mūn`) to break the `maC‑CŪN` drumbeat; (ii) vary the **foot-heads** with iḍmār so the *line* music differs even when the *close* morphology rhymes; (iii) 40 abyāt needs ~40 distinct rhyme-words — the 46 certified below clear that with margin.

**If you ever reconsider:** Family A (ṣaḥīḥ) is the right call *only* if you decide the poem's emotional center is **stillness** (`su·kūn` — the still point, and the meta-pun on prosodic *sukūn*), **the false trails** (`al·ẓu·nūn`), and **the ages** (`al·qu·rūn`) — all short-penult — and you are willing to (a) lose `mawzūn/maknūn/al-nūn` as rhyme-words (you could still deploy them mid-line), and (b) discipline hard against the `yafʿalūn` monotony. A is "the meditative nūniyya"; **B is "the thunderclap nūniyya," which is the poem the brief describes.** Recommend **B**.

> **Hard reminder for the drafters:** once B is chosen, **every** ʿajuz must close on a **long-penult** `-ūn` and scan `… mutafāʿil = u u – –` (iḍmār on earlier feet is fine; the *close* is fixed). A single short-penult `-ūn` rhyme-word (e.g. an accidental `al-ʿu·yūn` or any `yafʿalūn`) is **ḍarb-drift = rejected line** (§3). Police it with the §2.3 test on every rhyme-word before it enters a line.

Keep a running rhyme-word ledger (rubric §7) and reject any repeated root-at-rhyme.

## 7. The one-page operator card (paste into the drafting loop)

```
POEM = al-Kāmil tāmm, MUQAYYAD -ūn (sākin nūn), FAMILY B, ḌARB = MAQṬŪʿ mutafāʿil (u u – –).

EVERY ʿajuz, before the line is accepted:
 1. Isolate the rhyme word. Syllabify its tail:  …[penult][Cū][n#].
 2. PENULT TEST:  is the penult a CLOSED CVC or a long-vowel syllable?  (mak-, maw-, mas-, an-, zay-…)
      YES (LONG)  -> legal in Family B. close scans  … – –  (maqṭūʿ). KEEP.
      NO  (SHORT, open CV: ẓu-, su-, qu-, fu-, …ʿa-LŪN verbs) -> ILLEGAL in B = ḌARB-DRIFT. REJECT.
 3. Confirm the close foot = u u – –  (iḍmār allowed on feet 1–2; the CLOSE is fixed at u u – –).
 4. rawiyy = nūn SĀKIN every line (muqayyad). NO voiced/lengthened final ḍamma (that = muṭlaq, changes the rhyme).
 5. ridf = long ū present every line (it always is, in -ūn). No -in/-an/-on slips.
 6. No root reused at the rhyme (īṭāʾ). No debunked-numerology word. al-nūn = frequency+shape only, never a count.
```
