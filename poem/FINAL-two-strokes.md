# عَجْزي إعْجازُهُ — FINAL (Direction A: two marked strokes)
Two deliberate, framed breaks in an otherwise-flawless al-Kāmil maqṭūʿ nūniyya:
- **Line 15** — a real **majzūʾ** (short) bayt, ending on السُّكون (silence): قُصور made real, felt not named.
- **Line 20** — the forged word **البَيُّون** (faʿʿūl intensive of بَيان): the lexicon runs out before the Book.
All other 30 lines: clean (all reviewer grammar/meter/diction fixes applied).

## The two strokes — why each is genius-not-error (vs the bake-off's failures)
- **15 (the short line):** a genuine majzūʾ al-Kāmil bayt (2 feet/hemistich) — *actually* a third shorter, not a phantom; ends on **السُّكون** (silence) so form AND content enact قُصور; **zero stage-direction** (felt, not named). Still rhymes nūn.
- **20 (البَيُّون):** a real *faʿʿūl* intensive of بَيان (cf. سُبّوح، قُدّوس) — **audibly distinct** from any existing word (not a respelling like مَوْصون), lands on the **nūn rhyme**, Family-B long-penult; framed by one light phrase only.

## Reverence intact
Both breaks live only in the poet's lines about his own failure; the Book's stillness is never touched. No āya as the poet's wording. The poem stages its *failure* to match.
