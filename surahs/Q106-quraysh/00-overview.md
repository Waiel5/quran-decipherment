## 2. Classical names

- The surah's classical identity is double-anchored to the Quraysh tribe AND to the *īlāf* concept (root Alf, "to bind together / form a treaty / give protection").

## 3. Opening formula — and the Q 105+106 unity question

Q 106 opens with **li-īlāf-i qurayshin** ("for the *īlāf* of Quraysh") — a **prepositional-noun-phrase fragment** that is **grammatically dependent**: the preposition *li-* requires a governing verb. Classical readers identified TWO governing-verb resolutions:

1. **Stand-alone reading** (canonical mushaf): the *li-* of v.1 governs the imperative *fa-l-yaʿbudū* in v.3 ("for the *īlāf* of Quraysh ... let them worship ..."). v.1-2 are nominal preface, v.3 the imperative consequence.
2. **Continuation reading** (Ubayy b. Kaʿb / Ibn Masʿūd codices, per al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 17 *fī ʿadad suwar al-Qurʾān*; al-Wāḥidī *al-Wasīṭ*; al-Zamakhsharī *Kashshāf* on Q 106:1): the *li-* of v.1 is **anaphoric** to Q 105's events — "[Your Lord did this to the People of the Elephant] **for the *īlāf* of Quraysh**." Under this reading Q 105 + Q 106 are ONE surah (parallel to the Ibn ʿAbbās claim about Q 8 + Q 9). Some early codices accordingly omit the bismala between Q 105 and Q 106 (Ubayy reading; this is the classical analog to the Q 8+9 basmala-omission).

This is the **classical analog** of the Q 8 + Q 9 unity claim. The Q 8 + Q 9 unity was **falsified** at Q008-F-01 (FR rank 81/113, root-Jaccard 0.35 rank 13). The Q 105 + Q 106 case is empirically **MIXED** — see Q106-F-01.

## 7. Quick content structure

- v. 1: *li-īlāfi qurayshin* — "For the *īlāf* of Quraysh"
- v. 2: *īlāfihim riḥlata l-shitāʾi wa-l-ṣayf* — "their *īlāf* of the journey of winter and summer"
- v. 3: *fa-l-yaʿbudū rabba hādhā l-bayt* — "let them worship the Lord of this House (= the Kaʿba)"
- v. 4: *alladhī aṭʿamahum min jūʿin wa-āmanahum min khawf* — "who fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear".

A 2+1+1 ring: vv.1-2 the *īlāf* premise → v.3 the imperative → v.4 the divine grace-summary.

Three signature facts:

1. **CORPUS-MAXIMUM iʿjāz signature B (rank 1/114)**: across all 114 surahs, Q 106 has the highest H-NEW-750 sig_B value (+3.433). The composite signature combines high local-cohesion z, low rhyme-entropy z, and high mean-content-distance z. 2. **Three corpus-hapax / corpus-rare lexemes anchoring the trade-cycle frame**: Q 106 is the ONLY surah containing the roots **$tw** (shitāʾ "winter") and **Syf** (ṣayf "summer") — both are corpus hapaxes, attesting only at Q 106:2. The journey-root **rHl** (riḥla "journey") attests only 4 times across 2 surahs (Q 12 Yūsuf and Q 106). The 4-verse surah is therefore named for the *īlāf* concept (root Alf, 22 corpus tokens) but anchored by 3 corpus-hapax-or-rare lexemes referring to the historical Qurayshī seasonal trade-cycle (Yemen in winter / Syria in summer per Ibn Isḥāq, *Sīra*).
