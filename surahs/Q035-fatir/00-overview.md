## 2. Classical names — dual-name resolution

Q 35 has **two attested classical names**, both fully canonical:

1. **Fāṭir** (فاطر, "The Originator") — taken from the divine attribute in v.1 *fāṭir al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ*. This is the **modern-edition standard name** (Cairo edition 1924/1342H, King Fahd printings).
2. **al-Malāʾika** (الملائكة, "The Angels") — taken from the SECOND major opening-vocabulary item in v.1 *jāʿili al-malāʾikati rusulan*. This name is preserved in al-Bukhārī (*Ṣaḥīḥ* §59 *Bāb dhikr al-malāʾika* reading), al-Tirmidhī (kitāb al-tafsīr), and al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān*, nawʿ 17 *fī asmāʾ al-suwar*).

### Empirical resolution — which name is more "structurally distinctive"?

**Q 35 v.1 is structurally unique on BOTH name-axes simultaneously**:

- **fāṭir-axis**: the active-participle lemma *faATir* (Originator) appears 6× in the corpus (Q 6:14, Q 12:101, Q 14:10, Q 35:1, Q 39:46, Q 42:11) — Q 35 is the **only surah named for this attribute** because Q 35's invocation is the OPENING-WORD (the others are mid-pericope).
- **angels-axis**: Q 35 is the **only surah in the entire corpus that mentions *al-malāʾika* (as named angels) in verse 1**. Other v.1 surahs with the root mlk (Q 62, 64, 67) all use *al-mulk* (sovereignty, the king-attribute), NOT *al-malāʾika* (angels).

So both names track distinct empirical fingerprints. The dual-name tradition is **NOT a redundant duplicate**: it points to two structurally-coordinate openings of the surah — divine attribute (fāṭir) AND created-agent class (angels). The classical tradition's dual-name stability for Q 35 reflects the genuine bivalence of v.1.

**This investigation uses Fāṭir as primary** (matching the modern standard) and notes al-Malāʾika as the equally-canonical secondary. See `06-novel-findings.md` Q035-F-03 for the empirical angels-density test and Q035-F-04 for the dual-name angel-coverage test.

## 3. Opening formula — al-ḥamdu li-llāh + angels

Q 35 is one of **5 al-ḥamdu li-llāh openers** in the corpus: {Q 1 (verse 2 after the basmala), Q 6, Q 18, Q 34, Q 35}. al-Zarkashī, *al-Burhān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* 1/181 enumerates this set; the 5-surah list is **CONFIRMED** in the project's classical-quantitative-claims-audit (CC-048).

Within this set, Q 34 and Q 35 are **mushaf-adjacent** — the only two consecutive al-ḥamdu li-llāh openers in the entire corpus. The two openers are syntactically distinct:

| Surah | Opening (after al-ḥamdu li-llāh) | Construction |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Q 1:2 | *rabbi al-ʿālamīn* | bare apposition (Lord-of-worlds) |
| Q 6:1 | *alladhī khalaqa al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* | relative-clause (alladhī) |
| Q 18:1 | *alladhī anzala ʿalā ʿabdihi al-kitāba* | relative-clause (alladhī) |
| Q 34:1 | *alladhī lahu mā fī al-samāwāti* | relative-clause (alladhī) |
| Q 35:1 | *fāṭiri al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ jāʿili al-malāʾikati* | **active-participle apposition** (fāṭir / jāʿil) |

Q 35 is the **lone outlier**: instead of the *alladhī*-relative-clause structure (Q 6, 18, 34), Q 35 uses **double-active-participle apposition** (*fāṭir* + *jāʿil*). This is structurally distinctive within the cluster.

A **second al-ḥamdu li-llāh** appears at **Q 35:34** — the paradise-dwellers' exclamation *al-ḥamdu li-llāh alladhī adhhaba ʿannā al-ḥazana* ("Praise be to God, who has taken away grief from us"). Q 35 thus carries **two al-ḥamdu li-llāh statements**, framing the opening (cosmic-creation theme) and the eschatological recompense (paradise-eligibility). This is a within-surah inclusio at the *al-ḥamdu* lexical anchor.

2. **Q 35 is the lone outlier in the al-ḥamdu li-llāh opener cluster** (active-participle vs. *alladhī*-relative-clause); Q 35 is also the **only mushaf-adjacent al-ḥamdu pair partner** (Q 34→Q 35).

4. **Q 35 has TWO al-ḥamdu li-llāh statements** (v.1 and v.34) creating a within-surah inclusio at the al-ḥamd anchor — this dual-occurrence is **rare in the corpus**: tested at `06-novel-findings.md` Q035-F-05.
5. **Q 35 contains the unique *qiṭmīr* hapax** (Q 35:13) — the date-stone-thread, root q-T-m-r — sole corpus-attestation; classically associated with the smallest unit of measure (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.).

## 9. The "all three are in paradise" Q 35:32 ḥadīth

The Q 35:32 3-fold hierarchy (*ẓālim li-nafsih, muqtaṣid, sābiq bi-l-khayrāt*) generated a major classical exegetical tradition: a famous ḥadīth on the authority of **Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī** (al-Tirmidhī §3309 in *kitāb al-tafsīr*, gradeded *ḥasan gharīb* by Tirmidhī himself) reports the Prophet saying: *hāʾulāʾi kulluhum bi-manzilatin wāḥidatin wa-kulluhum fī al-jannah* — "All these are of the same rank; they are all in paradise."

A parallel ḥadīth from **Usāma b. Zayd** appears in Mishkat al-Maṣābīḥ §2277 (transmitted by al-Bayhaqī in *Kitāb al-baʿth wa-l-nushūr*): *kulluhum fī al-jannah* — "all of them in paradise."

al-Ṭabarī (ad loc.) preserves multiple isnāds (Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, Kaʿb al-Aḥbār, Abū Isḥāq al-Sabīʿī) all converging on the all-three-saved reading.

This ḥadīth tradition is empirically AUDITED in `04-hadith-corpus.md` and `05-classical-claims-audit.md`. The Aḥmad Musnad citation in the brief refers to the parallel chain (Aḥmad transmits the Usāma b. Zayd report at multiple points; the available 9-book Musnad subset does NOT contain this hadith directly — citation traced via Mishkat → al-Bayhaqī chain). See `04-hadith-corpus.md` for the full hadith-number triangulation.
