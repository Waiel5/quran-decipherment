# Q046-F-05 — *aḥqāf* surface-form hapax verification

## Verdict

**VINDICATED.** The form **الأحقاف** ("the sand-dunes," with article) appears exactly once in the entire Quranic corpus, at **Q 46:21**. The root *Ḥ-q-f* (`Hqf`) has a total of **1 corpus attestation** — this is a *root-hapax*, the strongest form of lexical uniqueness.

## Locus

> Q 46:21 — *wa-dhkur akhā ʿĀd, idh andhara qawmahu bi-l-aḥqāf ...* ("And mention the brother of ʿĀd, when he warned his people at the sand-dunes ...").

## Root attestation table (Hqf)

| Locus | Word position |
|:--|:--|
| **Q 46:21** (word 7) | sole corpus attestation |

Root *Ḥ-q-f* is therefore a **corpus-singleton root** — distinct from the surface-form-singleton case (Q 45 *jāthiya*, whose root *jvw* has 3 corpus attestations). Q 46's eponym is more strongly unique than Q 45's: Q 46 names a single-attestation root.

## Interpretation

The corpus-singleton-root status of *Ḥ-q-f* makes Q 46 al-Aḥqāf one of a small number of surahs named after a strictly hapax root (al-Suyūṭī catalogs unique-roots in *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 36 *al-gharīb*). The geographic referent (the sand-dunes of southern Arabia, where the prophet Hūd preached to ʿĀd) is itself unique in the Quran — neither the location nor the lexical form recurs.

This is empirically convergent with the classical commentary: al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān* ad. loc.), al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī all note the al-Aḥqāf as a specific geographic locus, not a recurrent toponym. The root-hapax status formalizes that observation on disk.
