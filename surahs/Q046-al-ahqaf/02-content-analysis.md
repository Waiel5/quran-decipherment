## 2. The corpus-hapax eponym (Q 46:21)

The root ح-ق-ف (Hqf) is a **corpus hapax** (1 attestation, Q 46:21 word 7; verified `data/morphology/root-index.json`). The eponymous geographical reference *bi-l-aḥqāf* is the only place in the Qurʾān where this lexical-geographical name appears — no parallel in any other surah.

This makes Q 46 a member of the **hapax-eponym** sub-class within the corpus's surah-naming taxonomy, alongside surahs whose names depend on a single-occurrence content-token (e.g., Q 27 al-Naml's *naml* root has 3 attestations in Q 27 only — see [[Q027-al-naml/02-content-analysis]]; Q 46 is even tighter at 1/1).

Classical glosses (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī ad Q 46:21; verified at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt` near surah-46 marker): *al-aḥqāf* is the plural of *ḥiqf* — long curving sand-dunes; geographical region of southern Arabia where ʿĀd dwelt. Multiple Companion-traditions identify the locus (between ʿUmān and Ḥaḍramawt; al-Shiḥr; Yemen) — agreement on the morphology, divergence on the geography.

## 3. The Hūd / ʿĀd narrative (Q 46:21-26) — cross-narrative network

Q 46:21-26 is one of **six** Qurʾānic retellings of the ʿĀd-Hūd narrative:
- Q 7:65-72 (al-Aʿrāf) — narrative-cluster prophet-list
- Q 11:50-60 (Hūd) — the named-after-Hūd surah; longest retelling
- Q 26:123-140 (al-Shuʿarāʾ) — formulaic short retelling
- Q 41:13-16 (Fuṣṣilat) — brief retelling within HM-7 internal
- **Q 46:21-26** (al-Aḥqāf) — geographical anchor (the only one to name the *aḥqāf* locus)
- Q 54:18-21 (al-Qamar) — the wind-disaster snapshot

Of these six, Q 46:21-26 uniquely:
- Names the geographical locus (*bi-l-aḥqāf*).
- Uses the kinship epithet *akhā ʿādin* ("the brother of ʿĀd") — hapax (verified: this exact phrase with this morphology occurs only here for Hūd; cf. *akhāhum hūdan* in Q 7:65, Q 11:50, Q 26:124).
- Includes the *bāriqun mumṭirunā* misread (the cloud they thought would bring rain) — Q 46:24-25.
- Punctuates with *kadhālika najzī al-qawma al-mujrimīn* — the formulaic post-destruction stamp (cross-references Q 7:84, Q 11:67, Q 26:139).

## 4. The jinn-listening narrative (Q 46:29-32) — corpus-pair with Q 72 al-Jinn

## 5. Q 46:15 — the gestation-and-nursing fiqh anchor

Q 46:15 reads (verbatim, no-tashkeel): *ووصينا الإنسان بوالديه إحسانا حملته أمه كرها ووضعته كرها وحمله وفصاله ثلاثون شهرا حتى إذا بلغ أشده وبلغ أربعين سنة قال رب أوزعني أن أشكر نعمتك التي أنعمت علي وعلى والدي وأن أعمل صالحا ترضاه وأصلح لي في ذريتي إني تبت إليك وإني من المسلمين* — "We commended to humanity good treatment of his parents; his mother bore him with hardship and bore him with hardship; **his bearing-and-weaning is thirty months**; until when he reached his full strength and reached forty years…"

This is the classical anchor for the **6-month minimum gestation doctrine** — by subtracting Q 31:14's *fiṣāluhu fī ʿāmayn* (24 months weaning) from Q 46:15's *thalāthūna shahran* (30 months bearing+weaning), the residual is 6 months. The fiqh derivation is anchored in classical Sunnī jurisprudence and is the locus of one of the most-famous early *asbāb al-nuzūl* + jurisprudence cases (the Abū Bakr / Umm Bakr / ʿUthmān-era pregnancy adjudication cited in al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 4 — see [[05-classical-claims-audit]]).

The verse also contains the lexical pivot **40 years** (*arbaʿīn sana*) — the only verse in the Qurʾān explicitly placing a 40-year life-stage marker on the human prayer of gratitude. This is the classical anchor for the *prophetic-age archetype* (the Prophet was 40 at first revelation per multiple *sīra* traditions).

## 6. Q 46:10 — the witness from Banū Isrāʾīl

Q 46:10 (verbatim): *قل أرأيتم إن كان من عند الله وكفرتم به وشهد شاهد من بني إسرائيل على مثله فآمن واستكبرتم إن الله لا يهدي القوم الظالمين* — "Say: have you considered, if it is from God and you disbelieve in it, while a **witness from the Children of Israel testified to its like and believed**, while you scorned it…"

Classical traditions (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr ad Q 46:10; al-Suyūṭī's *Asbāb al-nuzūl* via al-Wāḥidī): identify the witness as **ʿAbdallāh b. Salām**, the Jewish convert. Some Companion-traditions (Masrūq, Saʿīd b. Jubayr) demur, identifying the witness as a generic *shāhid min al-yahūd*. The exegetical disagreement is catalogued at al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 70 (*mubhamāt al-Qurʾān*).

## 7. Q 46:35 — the *ūlū al-ʿazm* closing

The closing verse Q 46:35 (verbatim): *فاصبر كما صبر أولو العزم من الرسل ولا تستعجل لهم كأنهم يوم يرون ما يوعدون لم يلبثوا إلا ساعة من نهار بلاغ فهل يهلك إلا القوم الفاسقون* — "Be patient as the Resolute Messengers (*ūlū al-ʿazmi mina al-rusul*) were patient…"

This is the classical anchor for the **5-prophet *ūlū al-ʿazm* doctrine** identifying Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā, Muḥammad as the highest-tier prophets (per al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 67 *fī al-anbiyāʾ wa-l-rusul*; al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī ad Q 46:35; cross-reference Q 33:7 and Q 42:13 which list five prophets in covenant-context). Note: the phrase *ūlū al-ʿazm* is **corpus hapax** — appears nowhere else; the Q 46:35 instance is the entire Qurʾānic textual basis for the doctrine.

## 8. Register dynamics

Q 46 cycles through distinct register types in 35 verses:

1. **Creedal-revelation** (vv. 1-3) → 2. **Polemic-eschatological** (vv. 4-6) → 3. **Scriptural-vindication** (vv. 7-10) → 4. **Inter-scriptural** (vv. 11-12) → 5. **Reassurance / istiqāma** (vv. 13-14) → 6. **Wisdom / family-law** (v. 15) → 7. **Eschatological-reward** (v. 16) → 8. **Polemic-family** (vv. 17-18) → 9. **Eschatological-warning** (vv. 19-20) → 10. **Prophetic-narrative** (vv. 21-26 — Hūd/ʿĀd) → 11. **Warning-historical** (v. 27) → 12. **Polemic** (v. 28) → 13. **Jinn-encounter** (vv. 29-32) → 14. **Eschatological-creedal** (vv. 33-34) → 15. **Paraenetic-closing** (v. 35).

This is a **register-dense surah** for its size (35 verses → 15 register-shifts = ~2.3 verses/register average; comparable to short multi-register Meccan surahs but **denser** than its near-monorhyme prosody alone would predict). The mismatch between **register-diversity** (high) and **rhyme-diversity** (very low — 0.95 bits, 3 finals) is striking; suggests Q 46 uses prosodic uniformity to *bind* register-shifts.

## 13. Thematic synthesis

Q 46 binds **four** structurally-coherent themes:
1. **The Qurʾān as confirming-prior-scripture** (vv. 1-12).
2. **The faithful family vs. the rebellious son** (vv. 13-20, esp. v.15 ↔ v.17).
3. **The destruction of ʿĀd at the aḥqāf** (vv. 21-26) — geographic anchor.
4. **The jinn as co-recipients of the Qurʾān** (vv. 29-32) — cosmic-recipient extension.

These four themes converge on a single underlying thesis: **the Qurʾān is universally received** — by prior-scripture-confirming witnesses (Banū Isrāʾīl, v.10), by destroyed historical communities (ʿĀd, vv.21-26), by faithful families (v.15) AND by other-than-human beings (jinn, vv.29-32). Q 46 is the corpus's **universal-recipient** surah. The closing v.35 *ūlū al-ʿazm* exhortation places Muḥammad in the prophetic continuum — the universal-recipient theme is closed by a universal-prophetic-precedent appeal.

This thematic tightness is consistent with HM-7's moderate FR-roots cohesion (`h-new-570` + [[hawamim-7-cluster-bifurcation]]) and Q 46's near-monorhyme prosody (used to *bind* register diversity).
