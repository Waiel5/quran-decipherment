## 2. Classical names

The surah is named **al-Ghāshiya** after its opening word *al-ghāshiyah* in v.1. Alternative classical naming-traditions:
- **Sūrat al-Ghāshiya** (السورة الغاشية) — al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī, al-Bukhārī's title (no Bukhārī ḥadīth body — see §11).
- **Sūrat Hal Atāka** (سورة هل أتاك) — incipit name, less common but classically attested (al-Qurṭubī mentions both names; al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 17 lists "*Sūrat Hal Atāka Ḥadīthu al-Ghāshiya*" as a full liturgical title).

Q 88 has only the one canonical name (with the *Hal Atāka* incipit form as a stylistic variant). No classical *fadāʾil*-fraction (1/X) hadith for Q 88 — unlike Q 99 (niṣf, refuted), Q 112 (thuluth, refuted), Q 36 (qalb, refuted) — so no analogous numerological refutation arena.

## 3. Opening formula — the *hal atā(ka)* form (corpus-EXACT 2-instance)

Q 88:1 opens with the rhetorical interrogative formula:

> **هَلْ أَتَاكَ حَدِيثُ الْغَاشِيَةِ**
> *hal atāka ḥadīthu al-ghāshiyah*
> "Has the news of the Overwhelming reached you?"

**Empirical fact (this investigation, Q088-F-01)**: The *hal atā(ka)* form as a SURAH-OPENER occurs in EXACTLY 2 surahs corpus-wide:

| Surah | Opener | Form |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| **Q 76 al-Insān** | *hal atā ʿalā al-insāni ḥīnun mina al-dahri* — "Has there come upon man a period of time…" | *hal atā* (no -ka) |
| **Q 88 al-Ghāshiya** | *hal atāka ḥadīthu al-ghāshiyah* — "Has the news of the Overwhelming reached you?" | *hal atāka* (with -ka, addressing the Prophet) |

**Corpus-EXACT pair**. No other surah corpus-wide opens with *hal* + *atā/atāka*.

The interior occurrences of the longer *hal atāka ḥadīthu X* template (the Q 88:1 surface-form template) appear in 5 verses corpus-wide:
- Q 20:9 *hal atāka ḥadīthu Mūsā* — Moses
- Q 51:24 *hal atāka ḥadīthu ḍayfi Ibrāhīm al-mukramīn* — Abraham's honored guests
- Q 79:15 *hal atāka ḥadīthu Mūsā* — Moses (parallel to Q 20:9)
- Q 85:17 *hal atāka ḥadīthu al-junūd* — the hosts (Pharaoh and Thamūd)
- **Q 88:1** *hal atāka ḥadīthu al-ghāshiyah* — the Overwhelming

**Q 88:1 is the ONLY surah-opener using this template**; the other 4 are interior verses. This is documented in `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/mutashabih-lafzi.md` §1 as an unresolved classical-tafsīr family.

The Q 76 ↔ Q 88 surah-opener pair is asymmetric:
- Q 76 *hal atā ʿalā al-insāni* uses the unmarked 3rd-person verb *atā* (no addressee suffix); subject is "a period of time" (*ḥīn*); existential-rhetorical question.
- Q 88 *hal atāka ḥadīthu al-ghāshiyah* uses the 2nd-person-marked verb *atāka* (with -*ka* addressing the Prophet); subject is "the news" (*ḥadīth*); narrative-rhetorical question.

**Both surahs are eschatological**: Q 76 narrates Paradise's *abrār*-rewards; Q 88 inverts to hell-tableau then paradise-tableau (vv.2-16). The pair forms a CHIASTIC eschatology pair (Q 76 paradise-anchored, Q 88 hell-paradise-bipartite).

## 5. ⭐ Content structure — bipartite hell+paradise tableau

The 26-verse surah follows a perfect 4-block bipartite tableau architecture:

| Block | Verses | Theme | Rhyme |
|:-:|:-:|:--|:-:|
| **A — opener** | 1 | rhetorical question — "*hal atāka ḥadīthu al-ghāshiyah*?" | -ah (ة) |
| **B — hell tableau** | 2-7 | downcast faces; toiling-fatigued; entering blazing fire; boiling spring; only ḍarīʿ thorn-food | -ah (ة) shifting at v.6-7 to -ʿ |
| **C — paradise tableau** | 8-16 | content faces; pleased with toil; lofty garden; flowing spring; raised couches; cushions; carpets | -ah (ة) |
| **D — natural-theology pivot** | 17-20 | look at camel, sky, mountains, earth — *kayfa khuliqat / rufiʿat / nuṣibat / suṭiḥat* | -t (ت) |
| **E — paraenesis closing** | 21-26 | "you are only a reminder" + accountability + return + reckoning | -r (ر) shifting to -m (م) at v.25-26 |

The **B↔C bipartite parallelism** is corpus-distinctive: each tableau opens with *wujūhun yawmaʾidhin* + descriptive participle (vv.2 *khāshiʿah* / 8 *nāʿimah*) and continues for ~7 verses. The two tableaux are length-balanced (B=6 verses, C=9 verses) and theme-mirrored (toil→fatigue→fire→bitter-spring→thorn-food vs. content→satisfaction→garden→flowing-spring→couches/carpets).

Classical commentary (al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr) notes this as a paradigmatic example of *muqābala* (counterposition) in *iʿjāz al-balāgha*. Length-balanced tableaux are characteristic of Early Meccan eschatological surahs.

## 10. Classical commentary

- **al-Ṭabarī** (*Jāmiʿ al-Bayān*) glosses *al-ghāshiyah* as the Day of Resurrection / the Hour, citing parallel usage at Q 12:107 (the only other corpus-occurrence of the substantive *ghāshiyah*); root غ-ش-ي covers all flesh "completely" / overwhelms with terror.
- **al-Zamakhsharī** (*al-Kashshāf*) emphasizes the rhetorical-narrative function of *hal atāka*: a question to which the answer "yes" is presupposed; the addressee is the Prophet ﷺ, but ultimately every hearer.
- **al-Rāzī** (*Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb*) treats vv.17-20 (*afa-lā yanẓurūna ilā al-ibili kayfa khuliqat…*) as a 4-fold natural-theology argument: the camel (animals), the sky, the mountains, the earth — ascending and descending the cosmic scale.
- **Ibn Kathīr** (*Tafsīr*) cites the Friday/ʿĪd paired-recitation hadith via Muslim, Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd; emphasizes the *hal atāka* opener as a genre-marker for "warning surahs" (with Q 79, Q 85, Q 51 as parallels at interior-verse level).
- **al-Suyūṭī** (*al-Itqān*, multiple nawʿ): catalogues Q 88 in mufaṣṣal-awsāṭ (nawʿ 19); lists the *hal atāka* opener under nawʿ on rhetorical-questions and oaths-and-ʿawqāf; classifies the surah's eschatology as *al-yawm al-ākhir* category-prime.
- **al-Biqāʿī** (*Naẓm al-Durar*) reads Q 87→Q 88 as a deliberate compositional pair: Q 87 establishes the *sabbiḥ* doxological frame; Q 88 fills it with the eschatological content (Paradise/Hell tableaux).
