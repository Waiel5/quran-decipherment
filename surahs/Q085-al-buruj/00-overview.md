## 2. Classical name

Named **al-Burūj** (الْبُرُوج) after the opening oath in v.1: *wa-l-samāʾi dhāti al-burūj*. The Arabic *burūj* (sg. *burj*) classically denotes "stations / towers / signs of the zodiac" — a polysemic noun whose specific referent is debated in classical tafsīr.

Classical disagreement on the referent of *al-burūj* (Ibn Kathīr ad loc., citing al-Ṭabarī's collation):

- **The 12 zodiacal stations through which the sun and moon traverse** (Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī's preferred reading): the sun travels each *burj* in one month; the moon traverses each in 2⅓ days, totaling 28 lunar mansions plus a 2-night hidden phase.
- **The bright/giant stars** (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Suddī majority): *al-burūj* = the stars themselves, paralleling Q 25:61 (*tabāraka alladhī jaʿala fī al-samāʾi burūjan*).
- **Beautiful constructed forms** (al-Minhāl b. ʿAmr): "the beautiful creation" — an aesthetic-cosmic reading.

Cross-references in the corpus: *burūj* occurs in 4 verses (Q 4:78 *fī burūjin mushayyada*, Q 15:16, Q 25:61, Q 85:1). Only Q 4:78 reads "fortified towers" — the other three are cosmic/celestial.

Specifically Q 85's opener is the SECOND of three corpus-EXACT instances of the formula *wa-l-samāʾi dhāti X* (heaven possessing X):

The **3-instance corpus-EXACT *dhāti X*-noun-of-heaven oath pattern** locks Q 85 in tight formal parallel with Q 51 and Q 86.

## 4. ⭐ Unique structural feature — the *aṣḥāb al-ukhdūd* historical massacre passage

vv. 4-9 narrate the *aṣḥāb al-ukhdūd* — the **People of the Trench** — a historical massacre of believers burned alive in a fiery ditch by their disbelieving persecutors. Classical attribution (Muḥammad b. Isḥāq, *al-Sīra al-Nabawiyya*, on the authority of the historical record):

> The killer of the People of the Ditch was **Dhū Nuwās** (al-Yemenite Ḥimyarite king Yusuf Asʿar Yathʾar), c. **523 CE**, who massacred ~20,000 Christians of Najrān because they refused to apostatize from Christianity to Judaism.

This dating places the *ukhdūd* episode roughly **45 years before the Hijra**, within living memory of the early Meccan audience — making this surah a memorial-and-warning text for a recent historical atrocity. The classical exegetical literature (Ibn Kathīr, ad loc.) preserves multiple isnāds:

- **Suhayb-ḥadīth (Boy-and-King narrative)** — narrated by Suhayb b. Sinān al-Rūmī, recorded in **Muslim, Ṣaḥīḥ #7325** (Kitāb al-Zuhd) and **al-Tirmidhī #3424** — tells the story of a boy who learns sorcery from a king-appointed sorcerer, simultaneously meets and is converted by a hidden monk, performs miracles by Allāh's name, refuses to apostatize despite torture, and instructs the king to invoke "*bismi-llāhi rabbi al-ghulām*" to kill him — provoking mass conversion and the king's order for the trench-massacre.
- **Ibn Isḥāq narration** — preserved in *al-Sīra*, attributing to Dhū Nuwās of Yemen as the historical agent.
- **Other classical Companions and tābiʿūn** (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda) — all confirm the historical-massacre interpretation.

The passage's structural function: a near-contemporary martyrdom-narrative anchoring the Quran's eschatological-warning rhetoric in living history. **This is the only sustained historical-martyrdom narrative in the entire short-Meccan-tail (Q 78-114).**

## 5. ⭐ Unique structural feature — the *lawḥun maḥfūẓ* (Preserved Tablet) statement

v.22: **في لوح محفوظ** — *fī lawḥin maḥfūẓ* — "In a Preserved Tablet."

This 2-token phrase is **CORPUS-EXACT-UNIQUE to Q 85:22** (computed: see `Q085-F-01-lawh-mahfuz-uniqueness-prereg.md` and `Q085-F-01-lawh-mahfuz-findings.md`). The bigram *lawḥ maḥfūẓ* occurs in exactly one verse in the entire 6,236-verse corpus. Furthermore:

- The token *lawḥ* (singular indefinite "tablet") is corpus-hapax (1 occurrence; the other "tablet" tokens are plural *al-alwāḥ* / *alwāḥ* in Q 7:145, 7:150, 7:154, 54:13).
- The token *maḥfūẓ* (passive participle "preserved") is corpus-hapax (1 occurrence; the related form *maḥfūẓan* "guarded" appears in Q 21:32 of the heaven-as-protected-roof).
- **Q 85 is the ONLY surah** in the entire corpus where the L-W-Ḥ root (tablet) and the Ḥ-F-Ẓ root (preservation) co-occur.

The **doctrinal weight** of this corpus-EXACT-unique phrase is enormous: *lawḥun maḥfūẓ* became the master-term in classical Islamic theology for the celestial archetype of the Quran (and, in some readings, of all divine decrees / *qadar* / the Mother-of-the-Book *umm al-kitāb*). Mainstream classical exegesis (Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī) reads v.22 with the immediately-preceding v.21 *bal huwa qurʾānun majīd* — together stating: "Nay! It is a glorious Quran. In a Preserved Tablet."

The **corpus-EXACT singular** anchoring of the foundational Islamic doctrine of the heavenly preserved-Quran archetype in a SINGLE 4-letter / 2-word phrase at the very last verse of an Early Meccan 22-verse surah is one of the most strikingly load-bearing single-occurrence formulas in the corpus.

## 6. ⭐ Unique structural feature — six exclusive divine names + 2-name closing

Q 85 carries an unusually-dense terminal divine-name cluster:

- v.8: *al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥamīd* (the Mighty, the Praiseworthy)
- v.9: shahīd (Witness — implicit divine attribute)
- v.12: *baṭsh rabbika la-shadīd* (the seizing of your Lord is severe)
- v.13: *huwa yubdiʾ wa-yuʿīd* (He originates and returns)
- v.14: ***al-Ghafūr al-Wadūd*** (the Forgiving, the **Loving**)
- v.15: *Dhū al-ʿArshi al-Majīd* (Owner of the Glorious Throne)
- v.16: *faʿʿālun li-mā yurīd* (Doer of what He intends)
- v.20: *muḥīṭ* (Encompassing)
- v.21: *qurʾānun majīd* (a glorious Quran)
- v.22: *lawḥun maḥfūẓ* (preserved Tablet)

The name **al-Wadūd** (الْوَدُود — "the Loving") in v.14 is rare in the corpus (occurs in only **2 verses**: Q 11:90 and Q 85:14). Its placement here, paired with *al-Ghafūr*, is a classical theological touchstone — a singular pairing of mercy and severity-of-punishment within seven consecutive verses. The pairing **al-Ghafūr al-Wadūd** is corpus-EXACT-unique (Q 11:90 has *innaha rabbī raḥīmun wadūd*, not the *al-Ghafūr al-Wadūd* compound).

## 11. The Q 85:21-22 closing — preservation doctrine

The closing 2-verse couplet **بل هو قرآن مجيد / في لوح محفوظ** (*bal huwa qurʾānun majīd / fī lawḥin maḥfūẓ* — "Nay! It is a glorious Quran / In a Preserved Tablet") is the **primary scriptural locus** for the doctrine of the heavenly preserved-Quran archetype. Classical readings (Ibn Kathīr ad loc.):

> *fī al-malaʾi al-aʿlā maḥfūẓun min al-ziyādati wa-l-naqṣi wa-l-taḥrīfi wa-l-tabdīl* — "In the highest gathering, preserved from any increase, decrease, distortion, or change."

The doctrine of *al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ* developed through classical Islamic theology to encompass not only the celestial Quran-archetype but, in some kalām readings, the totality of divine pre-decree (*qadar*) and the *umm al-kitāb* (Mother of the Book — Q 13:39, Q 43:4). The **single-occurrence anchoring** of this doctrine at Q 85:22 is itself a major architectural fact: a foundational doctrine instantiated by a corpus-EXACT-unique phrase.
