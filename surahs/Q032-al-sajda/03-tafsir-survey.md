# Q 32 al-Sajda — Tafsir Survey

This file surveys five+ classical mufassirūn on Q 32. Sources are the project's on-disk extracts in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/`. Disagreements and empirical correlates are flagged.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āyāt al-Qurʾān* (d. 310 AH)

al-Ṭabarī treats Q 32:1-3 as a three-part assertion of Quranic authenticity against Quraysh doubt:
1. ALM is mystery (citing the *ʿawniyya* view that the muqaṭṭaʿāt have inscrutable divine meaning).
2. *tanzīl al-kitāb* is divine deposit (not human invention).
3. *afterāhu* (Q 32:3) refutes the Meccan accusation of fabrication.

On Q 32:5 (*yudabbiru al-amra*), al-Ṭabarī cross-references Q 10:3 — the cosmic-management formula is a stable Quranic theological construction. The *alfa sanatin mimmā taʿuddūn* (the thousand-year shortcut) is treated as a cosmic-timekeeping anchor, with classical scholars dividing into:
- al-Ḍaḥḥāk: the day-of-cosmic-decree.
- Ibn ʿAbbās (via the chain in al-Ṭabarī): the time from divine command to its earthly accomplishment.

On Q 32:15 (the sajda-verse), al-Ṭabarī notes the obligatory prostration for the reciter (concords with al-Bukhārī *Kitāb sujūd al-Qurʾān*). The verse is treated as defining the *believer*-type — those who fall in prostration UNDER REMINDER (*idhā dhukkirū bihā*). This *under-reminder* clause separates Q 32:15's behavioral-sajda from Q 22:18's cosmic-roll-call (where prostration is ontological, not response-to-reminder).

**Empirical correlate**: al-Ṭabarī's *under-reminder* clause structurally distinguishes Q 32:15 from cosmic-roll-call sajdas. Q032-F-01 finds Q 32:15 is empirically NOT a cosmic-cluster member; al-Ṭabarī's reading is vindicated at the typological level.

## 2. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (d. 606 AH)

al-Rāzī's commentary on Q 32 (per `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-99names-extract.md` cross-references and the available Arabic OpenITI text) emphasizes:

1. **The *yudabbiru al-amr* clause (Q 32:5)** as a *tadbīr cosmic-axis* that anchors a 3-surah cosmic-management network: Q 10:3, Q 13:2, Q 32:5. al-Rāzī reads this as Quran-internal theological cross-reference; the three verses use *yudabbiru al-amra* in slightly varying contexts but with parallel theological function.

2. **The Q 32:5 thousand-year cosmic shortcut** as a peculiarity of cosmic timekeeping. al-Rāzī discusses scholarly views on whether the shortcut is:
   - Numeric (1000 earth-years = 1 cosmic-day),
   - Functional (cosmic decree is realized in 1000 earth-years),
   - Symbolic (the number 1000 is large-without-specific-cardinality).
   He does not commit to one reading.

3. **The Q 32:15 *yusabbiḥūna bi-ḥamdi rabbihim* couplet** — al-Rāzī notes this is a tasbīḥ-with-praise coupling that recurs in Q 17:44, Q 39:75. The pattern points at a corpus-wide tasbīḥ-network; al-Rāzī's *cosmic-tasbīḥ doctrine* (treated more fully in his commentary on Q 17:44) treats this as evidence that the entire cosmos engages in continuous tasbīḥ.

4. **The Q 32:16-17 Tahajjud verses + *qurrati aʿyun* expression**. al-Rāzī treats *fa-lā taʿlamu nafsun mā ukhfiya lahum min qurrati aʿyun* as the *reward-incomparability* clause: the reward is so distinctive that it cannot be known by any soul. He cites the Prophetic hadith narrating this as *what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and what has not occurred to the heart of any human* (a popular hadith, corpus-attested in al-Bukhārī among others).

**Empirical correlate**: al-Rāzī's cross-referencing of Q 32:5 to Q 10:3 and Q 13:2 is consistent with the *yudabbiru* lexical-network (corpus-rare phrase corpus-distinctively recurring).

## 3. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (d. 671 AH)

al-Qurṭubī treats Q 32 with primary focus on the legal-liturgical implications:

1. **The sajda-verse Q 32:15** — al-Qurṭubī catalogs the divergent madhāhib on prostration at this point: Ḥanafī, Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī all agree on the obligatory or recommended prostration; the differences are in the wording of the *takbīr* and the prostration-formula.

2. **The Tahajjud reward (Q 32:17)** — al-Qurṭubī treats this as a *fadāʾil al-aʿmāl* verse; he cites multiple ḥadīth on the night-prayer reward.

3. **The Mūsā parallel (Q 32:23-24)** — al-Qurṭubī notes that the *aʾimmatan yahdūna bi-amrinā lammā ṣabarū* phrase has been used by some sects (Shīʿī commentators) to ground theological doctrines about the imāmate; al-Qurṭubī rejects sectarian readings and treats it as a historical statement about the Banū Isrāʾīl.

**Empirical correlate**: The liturgical-focus of al-Qurṭubī aligns with the project's identification of Q 32 as a dual-liturgical-anchor (al-Tirmidhī #2975 nightly + al-Bukhārī #870/#1037 Friday-fajr).

## 4. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (d. 774 AH)

Ibn Kathīr's commentary on Q 32 (Arabic OpenITI on disk) emphasizes:

1. **The Tahajjud verses (Q 32:16-17)** — Ibn Kathīr cites the al-Tirmidhī al-Munjiya hadith (Q 32 + Q 67 pre-sleep recitation) AS THE PRIMARY ḤADĪTH-ANCHOR for this passage. He uses the verse to legitimize the night-prayer practice and to interpret *tatajāfā junūbuhum* literally (their sides physically forsake their beds).

2. **The eschatological warning (Q 32:12)** — Ibn Kathīr treats the death-bed regret of the deniers as a parallel to Q 23:99-100 (*ḥattā idhā jāʾa aḥadahum al-mawtu qāla rabbi irjiʿūn*). The cross-reference is to the regret-at-death theological pattern.

3. **The Mūsā parallel (Q 32:23-24)** — Ibn Kathīr treats this as legitimizing-precedent: as Mūsā received the Book and the Banū Isrāʾīl had right-guiding imāms (after they were patient), so too Muḥammad receives the Book and his community should be patient.

**Empirical correlate**: Ibn Kathīr's anchor-citation of al-Tirmidhī #2975 connects Q 32:16-17 directly to the al-Munjiya nightly pairing — empirical FR(Q 32, Q 67) = 0.7534 is the project's confirmation of this classical anchor.

## 5. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr* (d. 911 AH)

al-Suyūṭī treats Q 32 across multiple nawʿ in *al-Itqān*:

1. **Nawʿ 1 (Meccan/Medinan classification)**: Q 32 = Meccan, revelation order ~75/114.
2. **Nawʿ 6 (muqaṭṭaʿāt as Meccan markers)**: Q 32's ALM-opener is consistent with the Meccan-marker convention.
3. **Nawʿ 30 (sujūd al-tilāwah)**: Q 32:15 is listed as canonical sajda-verse #10 (of the 14-15 canonical list).

In *al-Durr al-manthūr*, al-Suyūṭī compiles the ḥadīth-tradition on Q 32:
- The Friday-fajr Sajda+Insān hadith (al-Bukhārī).
- The al-Munjiya pre-sleep Sajda+Mulk hadith (al-Tirmidhī).
- Various *fadāʾil* hadiths on the Tahajjud verses.

**Empirical correlate**: al-Suyūṭī's classification of Q 32 as muqaṭṭaʿāt-opener consistent with Meccan-marker convention is supported by cross-finding-008 (muqaṭṭaʿāt-as-introduction-markers). His liturgical anchoring is replicated by cross-finding-028.

## 6. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar* (d. 885 AH)

al-Biqāʿī's *munāsabāt* analysis (PDF on disk at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf`) treats Q 32 in relation to Q 31 and Q 33:

- **Q 31 → Q 32**: Q 31 (Luqmān) ends with *al-mafātīḥ al-khams* (the five keys of the unseen at Q 31:34). Q 32 opens with ALM-tanzīl, asserting the BOOK as the antidote to the unseen-future-knowledge gap. The seam is *thematic-continuity*: from the limits of human knowledge (Q 31:34) to the revealed-Book that addresses those limits (Q 32:2).

- **Q 32 → Q 33**: Q 32 closes with the *intiẓār* (waiting) injunction. Q 33 opens with *yā ayyuhā al-Nabī* (prophetic-personal address) and proceeds to deal with the Aḥzāb cycle (the Battle of the Trench). al-Biqāʿī reads this as a deliberate *thematic-pivot* from the *cosmic-tanzīl* + *waiting* of Q 32 to the *prophetic-personal-warfare* of Q 33.

**Empirical correlate**: The Q 32 → Q 33 transition is empirically the corpus's third-most expensive canonical-adjacency (h-new-720 δ = 0.3631, 4.4% of L_mushaf). al-Biqāʿī's *thematic-pivot* reading is empirically vindicated at law-strength.

## 7. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq al-tanzīl* (d. 538 AH)

al-Zamakhsharī, the Muʿtazilī-rhetorical commentator, emphasizes Q 32's rhetorical and grammatical structures:

1. **The Q 32:5 *yudabbiru al-amra min al-samāʾi ilā al-arḍ*** — al-Zamakhsharī notes the *min al-samāʾi ilā al-arḍ* phrasing (downward-cosmic-direction) as rhetorically signaling divine sovereignty.

2. **The Q 32:7 *aḥsana kulla shayʾin khalaqahu*** — al-Zamakhsharī addresses the apparent universal-quantifier (*kulla shayʾin* = "every thing") and discusses interpretive options for how *aḥsana* (best/most-beautifully) applies to all created things.

3. **The Q 32:9 sequence *thumma sawwāhu wa-nafakha fīhi min rūḥihi*** — al-Zamakhsharī's grammar-rhetorical focus on the *thumma* sequence and the divine-spirit-breathing.

**Empirical correlate**: al-Zamakhsharī's rhetorical structures on Q 32:5 and 32:7 are vocabulary-level observations that the project's TF-vector analysis treats descriptively but does not test for hypothesis-grade significance.

## 9. Honest limits

- The mufassirūn surveyed are the canonical core; full coverage would include al-Wāḥidī, al-Ṭabarsī, Abū al-Suʿūd, Abū Ḥayyān al-Andalusī.
- Empirical correlates are noted where they exist; many classical observations (e.g., al-Zamakhsharī's rhetorical analyses) are at a finer grain than the project's TF-vector instruments can resolve.
- The Q 32:5 thousand-year shortcut is the subject of classical numerological speculation; we do not engage with that here. The empirical work treats Q 32:5 as a lexical-syntactic data point.
