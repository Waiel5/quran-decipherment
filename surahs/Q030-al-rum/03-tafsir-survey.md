## 1. Central exegetical convergence — Q 30:2-5 as iʿjāz al-ghayb

All consulted mufassirūn agree on the historical-prophecy reading of Q 30:2-5:

### Ibn Kathīr (full asbāb al-nuzūl)

(`30/1.json`, full extract): The verses concern the **Battle of Issus / Adhriʿāt and Bushrā** (622 CE), where Persian general Shahrbarāz under Chosroes II defeated the Roman armies of Caesar (Heraclius). Ibn Kathīr reports that:
- The Quraysh (idolaters) celebrated, since the Persians (also non-Scripturalists) defeated the People of the Book.
- Muslims grieved, since the Romans were closer in religion (People of the Book).
- Abū Bakr made a wager with the Quraysh on the Roman victory within "biḍʿi sinīn" — initially set at 5 years, then extended to within 9 years on the Prophet's correction.
- Within 6-7 years (Battle of Nineveh, 627 CE), the Romans under Heraclius defeated the Persians, and Abū Bakr (or his heirs after his death) collected the wager.

Ibn Kathīr cites:
- al-Tirmidhī on the authority of Ibn ʿAbbās — *ḥasan ḥadīth*
- al-Tirmidhī on the authority of Niyār ibn Mukram al-Aslamī — *ḥasan*

This is the classical anchor for *iʿjāz al-ghayb* (knowledge of the unseen).

### al-Ṭabarī — qirāʾa dispute

(`ar-tafsir-al-tabari/30/2.json`): al-Ṭabarī notes a recensional dispute on whether the verb is read as **passive** *ghulibati al-Rūm* (with ḍamma, "the Romans have been defeated") or **active** *ghalabati al-Rūm* (with fatḥa, "the Romans have defeated").

- **Passive reading** (*ghulibati*): the dominant Hafs-Kufan reading; Romans were defeated by Persians (matches Battle of Issus 622 CE).
- **Active reading** (*ghalabati*): attested from Ibn ʿUmar and Abū Saʿīd via certain chains; would fit the post-Hudaybiyya Roman victory at Nineveh 627 CE.

al-Ṭabarī judges the passive reading correct *li-ijmāʿi al-ḥujjati min al-qurrāʾ ʿalayhi* — by consensus of authoritative reciters. The dominant reading is passive, with the future-tense *sa-yaghlibūn* (v3) carrying the prophecy.

### al-Qurṭubī — Battle of Badr correlate

(`ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/30/2.json`): al-Qurṭubī cites al-Tirmidhī from Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī: *lammā kāna yawmu Badr* (when the day of Badr came), the Romans appeared/prevailed over the Persians, and the believers rejoiced — and v4 *yawmaʾidhin yafraḥu al-muʾminūn* descended in this context. al-Qurṭubī labels this *ḥadīth gharīb*.

The Roman victory at Nineveh (627 CE) is conventionally dated approximately to the post-Badr / pre-Khaybar period in classical chronology — supporting the *yawmaʾidhin yafraḥu al-muʾminūn* synchronization.

### al-Jalālayn — concise

(`en-al-jalalayn/30/2.json`): "The Byzantines — who are among the People of the Scripture — have been vanquished; the Persians vanquished them ... The Meccan disbelievers rejoiced in this defeat ... and said to the Muslims 'We shall vanquish you as the Persians vanquished the Byzantines.'"

### al-Wāḥidī (asbāb al-nuzūl)

(`en-asbab-al-nuzul-by-al-wahidi/30/2.json`): Detailed: Chosroes sent Shahryarāz (= Shahrbarāz); Caesar's commander Juhannas (= Iuhannes) at Adhriʿāt and Bushrā. Persian victory; Prophet and Companions in Mecca felt sad; the Prophet "disliked that the Magians, who did not have a revealed Scripture, have the upper hand over the People of the Book." The verses then descended.

### al-Wāsiṭ

(`ar-tafsir-al-wasit/30/2.json`): records the wager-tradition with the four-she-camel stake and the seven-year time frame.

## 3. Block B exegesis — *afa-lā* refrain (vv. 8, 20-25)

### Ibn Kathīr on Q 30:8

The *a-wa-lam yatafakkarū fī anfusihim* ("Have they not reflected within themselves?") is read by Ibn Kathīr as a rebuke-interrogative. The *fī anfusihim* (within themselves) is interpreted by some as "in the privacy of their own thinking" and by others as "about themselves as creatures."

### al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*) — pedagogy of cognitive imperatives

(Note: al-Rāzī's full Q 30 is in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/` only as Arabic OpenITI raw, accessed via project notes; we cite the doctrinal claim.)

al-Rāzī reads the cluster of cognitive imperatives in Q 30:21-24 (*li-qawmin yatafakkarūn / li-l-ʿālimīn / li-qawmin yasmaʿūn / li-qawmin yaʿqilūn*) as a pedagogical hierarchy:
- *yatafakkarūn* (the reflective) — accessible to ordinary thinkers.
- *al-ʿālimūn* (the knowing) — those who have advanced.
- *yasmaʿūn* (the hearing) — those who hear with attention.
- *yaʿqilūn* (the reasoning) — those who employ disciplined reasoning.

## 4. Q 30:21 — *li-tasakanū ilayhā wa-jaʿala baynakum mawaddatan wa-raḥmatan* — marriage as sign

This verse is the classical anchor for the marriage-as-divine-sign doctrine. Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabarī, and Jalālayn all converge on the reading: the *azwāj* (mates, spouses) are themselves a sign of Allah's mercy, with *mawadda* (love) and *raḥma* (mercy) given as ontological gifts. This verse is widely cited in Islamic family-law treatises as a foundation of the marriage contract's divine grounding.

## 5. Q 30:30 — *fiṭra* doctrine

*fa-aqim wajhaka li-l-dīni ḥanīfan; fiṭrata Allāhi al-latī faṭara al-nāsa ʿalayhā*

This is the corpus-anchor for the **fiṭra** doctrine — the primordial nature given by Allah to all human beings. Classical reading (Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabarī): every child is born with a natural disposition toward tawḥīd (monotheism); subsequent religious deviation is a corruption of this original orientation. The famous Bukhārī ḥadīth #1359 (each child is born upon the *fiṭra*; his parents Judaize, Christianize, or Zoroastrianize him) is the primary linkage. See `04-hadith-corpus.md`.

## 6. Q 30:39 — ribā vs zakāh contrast

*wa-mā ātaytum min riban li-yarbuwa fī amwāli al-nāsi fa-lā yarbū ʿinda Allāh*

This is Quran's first chronological mention of *ribā* (per al-Suyūṭī's chronology — Q 30 rev-order 84, before the Medinan ribā prohibitions in Q 2 and Q 3). The early-Meccan reading (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī) treats this as a moral-non-juridical condemnation; the juridical prohibition awaits Medinan revelation. This is the **gradualist-revelation** anchor for the ribā prohibition.

## 7. Q 30:60 — concluding *fa-ṣbir*

The final verse *fa-ṣbir inna waʿda Allāhi ḥaqq* is read by all surveyed mufassirūn as a direct address to the Prophet, paralleled by Q 40:55, 40:77 (also *fa-ṣbir*) and Q 73:10 (*wa-ṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn*). The frame-bracket with Q 30:6 (*waʿda Allāh, lā yukhlifu Allāhu waʿdahu*) is noted by al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-durar*) — the surah opens and closes on the unchanging promise.

## 8. Munāsabah Q 29 → Q 30 (al-Biqāʿī)

al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-durar* establishes the thematic flow: Q 29 ends with *wa-l-ladhīna jāhadū fīnā la-nahdiyannahum subulanā* (and those who strive in Us, We will guide to Our ways) — Q 30 then opens with the historical-vindication of that promise, the Roman victory after defeat. This is the classical "test → vindication" *munāsabah* — directly relevant to the present revisit (cross-finding-008 + H-NEW-93).

## 9. Honest summary of tafsir convergence and divergence

- **CONVERGENCE**: Q 30:2-5 = iʿjāz al-ghayb (Roman-Persian war prophecy); Q 30:21 = marriage-divine-sign; Q 30:30 = fiṭra doctrine.
