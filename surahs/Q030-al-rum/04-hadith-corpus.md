The fiṭra-cluster is the most-cited Quran-anchored ḥadīth-cluster for Q 30:30. The doctrine: every human is born on the *fiṭra* (Islamic primordial nature); deviations are post-natal corruptions.

## 3. Q 30:21 — marriage as sign

The marriage-as-sign verse (Q 30:21) is referenced indirectly in numerous ḥadīth on marriage (Bukhārī Kitāb al-Nikāḥ, multiple chapters) but no single ḥadīth explicitly cites Q 30:21 by chapter-and-verse in the primary corpus consulted. The doctrinal connection is established in tafsīr literature (see `03-tafsir-survey.md` §4) rather than ḥadīth-direct.

## 4. Recitation practices and faḍāʾil

No direct *faḍāʾil* (virtues) ḥadīth on Q 30 alone in the consulted Bukhārī / Tirmidhī corpus searches. Q 30 is sometimes cited in compilations of "the surahs revealed at Mecca during the period before Hijra" but is not paired with a specific recitation-virtue tradition in the way Q 36 (*Yā Sīn*), Q 67 (*al-Mulk*), or Q 112 (*al-Ikhlāṣ*) are.

## 5. The Roman-Persian war as an eschatological-temporal marker

Beyond Q 30:1-5, ḥadīth literature has a separate "**End-of-Empires**" theme:

| # | Narrator | Content | Source |
|:-:|:--|:--|:--|
| Tirmidhī #2284 | Abū Hurayra | "When Kisrā is ruined, there will be no Kisrā after him; and when Caesar is ruined, there will be no Caesar after him." | `tirmidhi.json` idInBook=2284 |

This ḥadīth, while not directly an asbāb al-nuzūl for Q 30, places the Persian and Roman empires in a parallel eschatological frame consonant with Q 30:1-5's worldview. The Persian Empire fell to the Rāshidūn caliphate in 642 CE (Battle of Nahāwand); the Eastern Roman Empire persisted until 1453, but its territorial decline began in the same period.
