## 1. al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

**On Q 13 chronology** (Ṭabarī, intro to Q 13):
al-Ṭabarī, in the introductory section to Sūrat al-Raʿd, presents BOTH Meccan and Medinan classifications:
- Meccan attribution: cited via certain Ibn ʿAbbās chains, plus Mujāhid and ʿIkrima.
- Medinan attribution: cited via other Ibn ʿAbbās chains (notably the chain via ʿAṭāʾ); plus al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī.
- al-Ṭabarī himself does NOT take a definitive position; he reports the dispute without resolving it.

**SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED tag**: this position is reported in modern surveys (e.g., al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 13, summarizing al-Ṭabarī; Ibn Kathīr explicitly cites both views with isnāds). The OpenITI raw of al-Ṭabarī (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/`) was not re-confirmed by the specialist for the introductory note specifically; the position is documented as triangulated.

**On Q 13:13 (yusabbiḥu al-raʿd)**: al-Ṭabarī presents three classical positions:
1. Personification reading: "al-raʿd" is the angel-in-charge-of-thunder; the angel praises God.
2. *Tasbīḥ al-jamād* reading: the thunder-as-natural-event praises God in its mode (parallel to Q 17:44 *wa-in min shayʾin illā yusabbiḥu bi-ḥamdihi*).
3. Naturalistic reading: the noise of thunder is itself the praise.

al-Ṭabarī favours position 1 (the angel-of-thunder), citing a chain via Ibn ʿAbbās → ʿIkrima.

## 2. al-Rāzī (Fakhr al-Dīn) — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (al-Tafsīr al-kabīr)

**On Q 13 chronology**: al-Rāzī (intro to Q 13) discusses the dispute and notes that the surah's content-register includes both Meccan-style cosmological-signs (vv. 2-4, 12-15) and Medinan-style discourse against the People of the Book (especially v. 43 if read as referring to ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām). al-Rāzī leans toward a *primarily Meccan with Medinan insertions* reading.

**On Q 13:13 (the thunder verse)**: al-Rāzī devotes substantial discussion to the metaphysics of *kayfa yusabbiḥu al-raʿd?* — "how does thunder praise?" He surveys:
1. The angelic interpretation (al-Ṭabarī's position).
2. The *tasbīḥ al-jamād* doctrine: every existent praises God in its mode, including thunder (al-Rāzī cites Q 17:44 as the proof-text).
3. A **rationalist-physics interpretation** where al-Rāzī discusses Aristotle's *Meteorologica* on the cause of thunder (*al-thunder*) as the friction of clouds, and proposes that the *tasbīḥ* is the praise of the Maker through the thunder's existence as a created thing — a natural-theology argument.
4. Most importantly, al-Rāzī argues that Q 13:13 demonstrates the Quranic doctrine of *tawḥīd* via cosmological signs — even violent natural events are subordinate to divine praise.

al-Rāzī's discussion is one of the classical iʿjāz anchors for Q 13:13.

**On Q 13:31 (the iʿjāz-singular verse)**: al-Rāzī devotes a long section to *wa-law anna qurʾānan suyyirat bihi al-jibāl* — discussing whether the verse implies the Quran COULD have moved mountains and refrains from doing so, or whether it asserts that the Quran's iʿjāz is not in moving-mountains literalism. al-Rāzī's position: the verse's logic is that **even if there were such a Qurʾān (one that moves mountains), unbelievers would still not believe** — establishing that disbelief is moral-volitional, not evidential. This fits the classical *iʿjāz al-maʿnā* (al-Khaṭṭābī) and *iʿjāz al-fawāṣil* (al-Bāqillānī) framing.

## 3. al-Qurṭubī — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**On Q 13 chronology**: al-Qurṭubī (intro to Q 13) lays out the dispute most systematically:
- He attributes the Meccan position to: Ibn ʿAbbās (one chain), Mujāhid, ʿIkrima, ʿAṭāʾ, al-Jaḥdarī.
- He attributes the Medinan position to: Ibn ʿAbbās (a different chain via ʿAṭāʾ), al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī.
- al-Qurṭubī himself **leans Meccan-with-some-Medinan-verses** (specifically: vv. 31 and 43 are flagged as Medinan inserts in some traditions).

**On Q 13:43 (man ʿindahu ʿilm al-kitāb)**: al-Qurṭubī discusses the ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām identification (citing al-Tirmidhī's hadith) and the alternative: the verse refers generally to *ahl al-kitāb* (those-of-knowledge among the People of the Book). He also notes a third position: it refers to ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib (a Shīʿī interpretation, citing certain Sunnī sources who note the dispute without endorsing).

**On Q 13:11 (lā yughayyiru mā bi-qawmin)**: al-Qurṭubī gives a classic exposition of human-agency-and-divine-decree, citing Q 8:53 (the parallel formula) and noting that the verse does NOT support libertarian free-will on its own — it must be read with Q 81:29 (*wa-mā tashāʾūna illā an yashāʾa Allāh*).

## 4. Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**On Q 13 chronology**: Ibn Kathīr (intro to Q 13) cites both positions but leans **Meccan**, noting that the cosmological-signs theme (vv. 2-4, 12-15) is characteristically Meccan. He acknowledges al-Suyūṭī's Medinan classification but does not endorse it.

**On Q 13:13 (thunder verse)**: Ibn Kathīr cites the hadith from Aḥmad in his Musnad (and via al-Tirmidhī, see `04-hadith-corpus.md`) that "*when you hear the thunder, say: subḥān al-ladhī yusabbiḥu al-raʿdu bi-ḥamdihi wa-l-malāʾikatu min khīfatihi*" — making the verse the source of a *du'āʾ al-raʿd* tradition.

**On Q 13:43 (ʿilm al-kitāb)**: Ibn Kathīr endorses the ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām identification, citing al-Tirmidhī's hadith with isnād. He notes the chain is ḥasan-but-not-ṣaḥīḥ (hassan ghayr maʿrūf in al-Tirmidhī's grading).

## 5. al-Suyūṭī — *al-Durr al-manthūr fī al-tafsīr al-maʾthūr* + *al-Itqān*

**On Q 13 chronology** (al-Itqān, nawʿ 1 — Meccan-Medinan classification):
- al-Suyūṭī's catalog classifies Q 13 as **Medinan** (rev #96, in the chronological catalog).
- Source: `data/revelation-order.csv` Q 13 row carries `period=Medinan` (Tanzil Egyptian Standard, sourced from the al-Suyūṭī tradition).
- al-Suyūṭī, in *al-Durr al-manthūr* (intro to Q 13), cites multiple isnāds for the Medinan classification, primarily via the Ibn ʿAbbās → ʿAṭāʾ chain.

**On Q 13:13 (thunder verse)**: al-Suyūṭī (Durr al-manthūr) cites the *du'āʾ al-raʿd* hadith from Aḥmad and a chain via Mālik → Sumayy → Abū Ṣāliḥ → Abū Hurayra (the *subḥān Allāh wa-bi-ḥamdihi* phrase that appears in Q 13:13's tasbīḥ — confirmed in the hadith corpus search per `04-hadith-corpus.md`). The phrase *subḥān Allāh wa-bi-ḥamdihi* itself is a high-frequency phrase across the 9-book hadith corpora (4 attestations in Bukhārī alone, 7 in Muslim) — but most do NOT explicitly cite Q 13:13 as the source. The Q 13:13-specific *du'āʾ al-raʿd* tradition is in al-Tirmidhī, *al-Daʿawāt*.

**On Q 13:43**: al-Suyūṭī cites multiple chains for the ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām identification, including a chain via Ibn Mardawayh from ʿAṭāʾ from Ibn ʿAbbās.

## 6. al-Zamakhsharī — *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq ghawāmiḍ al-tanzīl*

**On Q 13:1 (المر)**: al-Zamakhsharī catalogs the muqaṭṭaʿāt in his usual *al-Kashshāf* style, noting that Q 13's 4-letter combination is corpus-unique. He gives the standard MIxed-position: that the muqaṭṭaʿāt's meaning is unknowable but their function is *iʿjāz* (challenging the Arab listener to compose like the Quran from these letters).

**On Q 13:13**: al-Zamakhsharī's *Kashshāf* takes the *jamād-yusabbiḥ* doctrine seriously as a balagha topic. He notes that the verse uses the present-tense *yusabbiḥu* (continuous-aspect): thunder *continuously* praises, not just once. al-Zamakhsharī highlights the rhetorical force of pairing *yusabbiḥu* with *bi-ḥamdihi* (with-His-praise) — a tasbīḥ coupled to ḥamd, the most-elevated form of praise in classical balagha categories.

**On Q 13:28 (palindrome verse)**: al-Zamakhsharī notes the *radd al-ʿajuz ʿalā al-ṣadr* device structurally — *bi-dhikri Allāhi taṭmaʾinnu al-qulūb* echoes *taṭmaʾinnu qulūbuhum bi-dhikri Allāh* in inverse word-order. This is the closest the classical commentary tradition comes to identifying the chiastic structure documented in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/classical-on-rad-verse-28.md`.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī — *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

**On Q 13 *munāsabah* with Q 12 and Q 14**: al-Biqāʿī (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf`, ALR-cluster section per `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md`) treats the Q 12 → Q 13 → Q 14 transition as a thematic rather than letter-family-based progression:
- Q 12 (ALR, continuous-narrative) → Q 13 (ALMR, didactic-cosmological): the seam is "from the prophetic-narrative to the cosmological-theological reflection on prophetic revelation".
- Q 13 (ALMR) → Q 14 (ALR, Ibrāhīm-prayer): the seam is "from the corporate cosmological reflection to the personal-prophetic prayer".

**The Q 13 → Q 14 *munāsabah*** is empirically vindicated in the project: the canonical-adjacency cost is **0.0497 length-units** (very low; H-NEW-720 s=13), and the 4-axis architectural distance is **0.486** (Q013-F-05). al-Biqāʿī's qualitative claim of strong munāsabah corresponds to the empirical near-twin signature.

**The Q 12 → Q 13 *munāsabah*** is empirically the OPPOSITE — the canonical-adjacency cost is **0.2158** (top-15 expensive). al-Biqāʿī treats the seam as thematically-connected; the empirical metric flags it as costly. The munāsabah works at the THEME level (revelation), but the architectural-axis level shows a register-shift.

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī — *Majmaʿ al-bayān fī tafsīr al-Qurʾān* (Imāmī)

al-Ṭabarsī, in his *Majmaʿ al-bayān* on Q 13:43, gives the Imāmī Shīʿī interpretation of *man ʿindahu ʿilm al-kitāb* — that the referent is **ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib** (cited via ḥadīth from Imām Muḥammad al-Bāqir and Imām Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq). This is a key Shīʿī proof-text. The Sunnī tradition reads the same verse as referring to ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām.

This is a genuine intra-classical disagreement on the same verse: Sunnī tradition leans ʿAbd Allāh b. Salām (Tirmidhī chain); Shīʿī tradition leans ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib (Bāqir/Ṣādiq chain). The Q013-F-05 chronology audit flags this as one factor in the "contested" classification.
