# Q 62 al-Jumuʿah — Content Analysis

11-verse close reading at the verse-block level, with structural-thematic decomposition. Each block is keyed to the empirical findings established in `00-overview.md` and `01-empirical-profile.md`.

## 1. The opening cosmic-glorification + Khawātim-echo (v. 1)

> **يسبح لله ما في السماوات وما في الأرض الملك القدوس العزيز الحكيم**
> *yusabbiḥu li-Llāhi mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi al-Maliki al-Quddūsi al-ʿAzīzi al-Ḥakīm*
> "Whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth glorifies God, the Sovereign, the Holy, the Mighty, the Wise."

**Three structural functions in a single verse:**

1. **Imperfect-tense musabbiḥa**: opens with *yusabbiḥu* (imperfect 3ms) — the **continuous-aspect** form of the cosmic-glorification formula, paired with Q 64 al-Taghābun (also يسبح, the only other imperfect musabbiḥa). H-NEW-58c verifies the cluster's perfect-vs-imperfect tense binary structure: Q 62↔Q 64 share 37 verbatim characters, while all 6 cross-tense pairs (Q 57/59/61 × Q 62/64) share exactly 0. The imperfect-tense form classically signifies *ongoing / continuous* glorification — "everything is continually glorifying God" — vs the perfect-tense forms of Q 57, 59, 61 ("everything has glorified God"). Theological reading (al-Rāzī, al-Zamakhsharī): the imperfect addresses the present moment of Madīnan community-formation; the perfect addresses cosmic-historical fact.

2. **Khawātim-echo composite quotation**: the closing 4 names *al-Maliki al-Quddūsi al-ʿAzīzi al-Ḥakīm* are a **dual-source verbatim lift** from Q 59:22-24 — `الملك القدوس` from Q 59:23 and `العزيز الحكيم` from Q 59:24. Q062-F-01 verifies this composite-quotation reading. **Q 62:1 carries 4 of the 14 Khawātim-context names; only 3 verses in the entire Quran carry ≥3 Khawātim-context names (Q 59:23 with 8, Q 59:24 with 5, Q 62:1 with 4).** Q 62:1 is structurally the **only extra-Q 59 verse** carrying multiple Khawātim names under the broader-inventory reading.

3. **Mushaf-positional anchor**: Q 62:1 is the OPEN of the back-Medinan musabbiḥāt-block; immediately after Q 61 al-Ṣaff's perfect-tense *sabbaḥa lillāh*. The seam Q 61→Q 62 carries fraction-residual 0.0085 (low/smooth) — the perfect→imperfect tense-binary boundary is empirically smooth at the canonical-adjacency-cost level.

The verse's three functions converge: cosmic-theological (musabbiḥa), inter-textual (Khawātim composite quotation from Q 59), structural-positional (back-Medinan musabbiḥāt block opener). This convergence is the empirical correlate of Q 62's 4-cluster meta-hub status (H-NEW-89): the same verse simultaneously instantiates membership in C5_musabbiḥāt + C8_Khawātim_extended.

## 2. The Prophetic mission to the unlettered (vv. 2-4)

> **هو الذي بعث في الأميين رسولا منهم يتلو عليهم آياته ويزكيهم ويعلمهم الكتاب والحكمة وإن كانوا من قبل لفي ضلال مبين** *(v. 2)*
> "He is the one who raised up among the unlettered a Messenger from themselves, reciting His verses to them, and purifying them, and teaching them the Book and the Wisdom — and they had certainly been before in clear error."

> **وآخرين منهم لما يلحقوا بهم وهو العزيز الحكيم** *(v. 3)*
> "And others of them have not yet joined them — and He is the Mighty, the Wise."

> **ذلك فضل الله يؤتيه من يشاء والله ذو الفضل العظيم** *(v. 4)*
> "That is the favor of God; He gives it to whom He wills — and God is the possessor of immense favor."

**Three blocks in three verses:**

- **v. 2 — the four-verb mission**: *baʿatha* (raised up) + *yatlū* (recites) + *yuzakkīhim* (purifies) + *yuʿallimuhumu al-kitāba wa-l-ḥikma* (teaches the Book + Wisdom). This four-verb formula appears in two other verses corpus-wide — Q 2:129, Q 2:151, Q 3:164 — all in long Medinan surahs, all in the context of Abraham's prayer (Q 2:129) or the post-Hijra community-formation (Q 2:151, Q 3:164). The Q 62:2 occurrence is the **shortest carrier** of the four-verb formula: 4 short verses (Q 62:2-5) frame what the longer Medinan chapters expand over multiple-page narratives. Compression by structural-positional necessity.
- **v. 3 — the *ākharīna* phrase** (و**آخرين** منهم لما يلحقوا بهم) — "and OTHERS of them who have not yet joined them." This is the asbāb-al-nuzūl crux: Tirmidhī #3394 records the question to the Prophet, *"Who are these others?"*, and the Prophet's hand on Salmān al-Fārisī (Persian companion): *"If faith were at the Pleiades, men from this man's people would attain it."* The verse is the canonical Quranic anchor for the **inclusion of non-ʿArab believers** within the umma — a foundational theological-community claim. *wa-huwa al-ʿAzīzu al-Ḥakīm* closes the verse with the same name-pair that opens v. 1 (an inclusio at the 3-verse pericope level).
- **v. 4 — the *faḍl* (favor) summation**: God's prerogative in selecting whom He wills. This closes the Prophetic-mission block and pivots to the polemical block at v. 5.

The Prophetic-mission block (vv. 2-4) is a tightly-compressed parallel to the long-Medinan community-formation passages of Q 2 / Q 3. The structural compression is the same kind that distinguishes Q 62 from those long surahs: 11 verses doing what the long Medinan surahs do over hundreds of verses. This is consistent with H-NEW-660 short-Medinan compression-pattern findings.

## 3. The donkey-and-books parable (v. 5)

> **مثل الذين حملوا التوراة ثم لم يحملوها كمثل الحمار يحمل أسفارا بئس مثل القوم الذين كذبوا بآيات الله والله لا يهدي القوم الظالمين**
> "The example of those who were entrusted with the Torah and then did not carry it [is] like the example of a donkey carrying volumes [of books]. Wretched is the example of the people who deny the signs of God; and God does not guide the wrongdoing people."

**A parable unique to Q 62 in the corpus.**

The donkey-carrying-books image (*al-ḥimāru yaḥmilu asfārā*) appears nowhere else in the Quran. Classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī ad loc., al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* ad loc., al-Zamakhsharī *al-Kashshāf* ad loc.) reads:

- *ḥumlū al-tawrāta* — "those entrusted with the Torah": the Jewish community in Madīnah, addressed in the context of failed scriptural responsibility.
- *thumma lam yaḥmilūhā* — "then did not carry it": failure to *act* on Torah's teachings; carrying-the-letter-without-the-spirit.
- *ka-mathali al-ḥimāri yaḥmilu asfārā* — "like the donkey carrying volumes": physical bearing of the texts without comprehension. *asfār* (sing. *sifr*) is the classical Hebrew loanword for "bound book / volume" (cf. *seder*, *sefer*); the choice of word is itself an inter-textual signal toward the Hebrew bookmaking tradition.
- The parable is **didactic-incisive**, not narrative; one of Q 62's Madīnan-polemic distinctions.

**Lexical hapax check**: *asfār* in this construction (the *yaḥmilu asfārā* phrase as a unit) is corpus-unique to Q 62:5. The root س-ف-ر appears multiple times in the Quran (سفر as "journey", سَفَرَة as "scribes" in Q 80:15-16 *bi-aydī safara kirām*) but the **specific construction "ḥimār yaḥmilu asfārā"** is found nowhere else.

**Function in the surah's flow**: v. 5 pivots from the affirmation-block (vv. 1-4 cosmic glorification + Prophetic mission) to the polemic-block (vv. 5-8 Jewish-community failure). It is the structural inflection point of the surah.

## 4. The death-challenge dialogue (vv. 6-8)

> **قل يا أيها الذين هادوا إن زعمتم أنكم أولياء لله من دون الناس فتمنوا الموت إن كنتم صادقين** *(v. 6)*
> "Say: 'O you who are Jews, if you claim that you are allies of God to the exclusion of [other] people, then wish for death — if you are truthful.'"

> **ولا يتمنونه أبدا بما قدمت أيديهم والله عليم بالظالمين** *(v. 7)*
> "But they will never wish for it, because of what their hands have put forth — and God is Knowing of the wrongdoers."

> **قل إن الموت الذي تفرون منه فإنه ملاقيكم ثم تردون إلى عالم الغيب والشهادة فينبئكم بما كنتم تعملون** *(v. 8)*
> "Say: 'Indeed, the death from which you flee — it will surely meet you. Then you will be returned to the Knower of the unseen and the seen, and He will inform you of what you used to do.'"

**A "death-challenge" rhetorical move appearing in three places in the Quran**:
- Q 2:94-95 (long Medinan, identical address to Jewish exclusivity claim)
- Q 62:6-8 (this surah, brief Medinan)
- (a partial parallel at Q 4:78 *ay-namā takūnū yudrikkumu al-mawt* — "wherever you are, death will reach you" — without the specific Jewish-exclusivity framing)

The Q 62 version is the most-compact deployment of the challenge. The structural logic mirrors v. 5's parable: assert exclusive divine-favor → demonstrate the assertion's emptiness via consistency-test (wish for death) → confirm the *moral* bankruptcy via predictive-test (they won't wish) → seal with universal closure (death is universal; God knows what hands have put forth).

The challenge connects back to v. 4 (*dhālika faḍlu Allāhi yuʾtīhi man yashāʾ*) — God's prerogative in selecting recipients of favor, and the failure of any group to claim exclusive entitlement to that favor.

## 5. The Friday-prayer institution (vv. 9-11)

> **يا أيها الذين آمنوا إذا نودي للصلاة من يوم الجمعة فاسعوا إلى ذكر الله وذروا البيع ذلكم خير لكم إن كنتم تعلمون** *(v. 9)*
> "O you who have believed, when [the adhān] is called for the prayer on the day of al-Jumuʿah, then proceed to the remembrance of God and leave commerce. That is better for you, if you only knew."

> **فإذا قضيت الصلاة فانتشروا في الأرض وابتغوا من فضل الله واذكروا الله كثيرا لعلكم تفلحون** *(v. 10)*
> "And when the prayer has been concluded, disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of God, and remember God much that you may succeed."

> **وإذا رأوا تجارة أو لهوا انفضوا إليها وتركوك قائما قل ما عند الله خير من اللهو ومن التجارة والله خير الرازقين** *(v. 11)*
> "But when they saw a transaction or a diversion, [O Muḥammad], they rushed to it and left you standing. Say: 'What is with God is better than diversion and than a transaction — and God is the best of providers.'"

**The institutional core of the surah** — the three verses that founded the Friday congregational-prayer obligation in Islamic law.

**v. 9 — the obligation**: *idhā nūdiya li-l-ṣalāti min yawmi al-jumuʿati* (when called to prayer on Friday) → *fa-saʿaw* (then proceed) → *wa-dharū al-bayʿ* (and leave commerce). Three pre-cepts:
- The temporal trigger: the adhān on Friday.
- The action: *saʿaw* (proceed; classically read as "hasten" — al-Rāzī notes the verb does NOT mean "run", but rather *make-this-your-priority-of-effort*).
- The exclusion: *wa-dharū al-bayʿ* — leave the marketplace. (Classical fiqh derived the obligation that commerce during the Friday-call is invalidated; the *bayʿ* is the legal anchor for this.)

**v. 10 — the post-prayer release**: *fa-idhā quḍiyat al-ṣalāt* (when prayer is concluded) → *fa-intashirū fī al-arḍ* (disperse in the land) → *wa-btaghū min faḍli Allāh* (seek from God's bounty). The post-prayer return to commerce is **commanded**, not merely permitted. The prayer is a temporal frame, not a withdrawal from worldly life.

**v. 11 — the asbāb-al-nuzūl**: *idhā ra'aw tijāratan aw lahwan* (when they saw a transaction or a diversion) → *infaḍḍū ilayhā* (they rushed to it) → *wa-tarakūka qā'iman* (and left YOU standing). The historical referent (per Bukhārī's #936 chain via Ibn ʿAbbās via Jābir b. ʿAbd Allāh, Muslim #2058): a trade caravan from Syria entered Madīnah during the Prophet's Friday khuṭba, accompanied by a drum announcing its arrival; Madīnan congregants left the mosque to greet the caravan, leaving the Prophet standing in the pulpit (*qā'iman*). The verse rebukes this and reasserts the priority: *mā ʿinda Allāhi khayrun mina al-lahwi wa-mina al-tijāra* — what is with God is better than the diversion and the transaction.

**Function**: the surah's last 3 verses are the *legal-foundation block* for the Friday institutional prayer — the most-cited Quranic anchor for Friday-prayer fiqh in classical jurisprudence (al-Shāfiʿī *al-Risāla*; al-Sarakhsī *al-Mabsūṭ*; Ibn Qudāma *al-Mughnī*).

## 6. Surah-level structural decomposition

| Block | vv. | Function | Key signature |
|:--:|:-:|:--|:--|
| Cosmic-glorification + Khawātim-echo | 1 | musabbiḥa-imperfect; 4-name composite quotation of Q 59:23 + Q 59:24 | Khawātim-extended cluster anchor |
| Prophetic mission | 2-4 | four-verb mission formula (recital, purification, teaching, wisdom) | parallel to Q 2:129, Q 2:151, Q 3:164 (long-Medinan) |
| Donkey-and-books parable | 5 | corpus-unique didactic image | Madīnan polemic against Jewish-community failure |
| Death-challenge dialogue | 6-8 | exclusivity-test + universal-death affirmation | parallel to Q 2:94-95 |
| Friday-prayer institution | 9-11 | foundational fiqh-anchor | cluster C7_friday eponym; asbāb-al-nuzūl (trade caravan) |

**Surah-structural reading**: Q 62 is a **dual-themed cosmic-affirmation-then-community-formation surah** — vv. 1-4 establish the cosmic-theological frame (everything glorifies God; the Prophet's mission to the unlettered with universal scope); vv. 5-11 ground that frame in the concrete Madīnan community-formation moment (the failure of one community-of-the-Book; the universal death-affirmation; the Friday institutional prayer founding moment). The structural inflection at v. 5 (the donkey-and-books parable) marks the pivot from cosmic to communal.

The surah's 4-cluster meta-hub status (H-NEW-89) is empirically realized in this content structure: vv. 1-4 belong to the cosmic-musabbiḥāt + Khawātim-extended thematic cluster; vv. 9-11 belong to the Friday-liturgical cluster; the surah straddles both as a content-unit, not just as a cluster-graph node.
