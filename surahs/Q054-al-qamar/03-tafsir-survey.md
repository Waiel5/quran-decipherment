# Q 54 al-Qamar — Tafsīr Survey

Five classical commentaries surveyed from the on-disk corpus, plus al-Wāḥidī's *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*. Source files
cited per passage; the Arabic of each was read in place. The defining exegetical question is the reading of
v 1 *anshaqqa al-qamar* — **literal pre-Hijra historical event** vs **eschatological-future prophetic-perfect**
vs metaphor — on which the tradition is overwhelmingly (but not unanimously) for the literal reading.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (on Q 54:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/54/1.json`.

al-Ṭabarī glosses *iqtarabati al-sāʿa* as "the Hour in which the Resurrection occurs has drawn near
(*danat*)" — *iqtarabat* is form-VIII from *qurb*, and the verse is "from God a warning to His servants of
the nearness of the Resurrection and the imminent perishing of the world, and a command to prepare for the
terrors of the Hour before it descends upon them while they are heedless." On *wa-anshaqqa al-qamar* he is
explicit and literal: *"wa-nfalaqa al-qamar, wa-kāna dhālika fī-mā dhukira ʿalā ʿahdi rasūli llāh ﷺ wa-huwa
bi-Makka, qabla hijratihi ilā al-Madīna"* — the moon split during the Prophet's lifetime, at Mecca, before
the Hijra; the Meccan disbelievers asked him for a sign, he showed them the moon-splitting as a proof of the
truth of his claim, and they turned away saying *hādhā siḥrun mustamir* ("Muḥammad has bewitched us"), which
v 2 answers. **al-Ṭabarī's reading is literal-historical-Meccan.**

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf* (on Q 54:1-3)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` (Q 54 block, around the
*PageV04P430* marker).

al-Zamakhsharī opens: *"inshiqāq al-qamar min āyāti rasūli llāh ﷺ wa-muʿjizātihi al-nayyira"* — the
moon-splitting is among the Prophet's signs and his luminous miracles. He stacks the chains: *"ʿan Anas b.
Mālik … anna al-kuffāra saʾalū rasūla llāh ﷺ āyatan fa-nshaqqa al-qamaru marratayn"* (the disbelievers asked
for a sign and the moon split twice), *"wa-kadhā ʿan Ibn ʿAbbās wa-Ibn Masʿūd"* — Ibn ʿAbbās: "it split into
two halves, one went and one remained"; **Ibn Masʿūd: *raʾaytu Ḥirāʾ bayna filqatay al-qamar*** ("I saw
Mt. Ḥirāʾ between the two halves of the moon"). He then records the **minority eschatological reading**
(*ʿan baʿḍ al-nās: anna maʿnāhu yanshaqqu yawma al-qiyāma*) and **refutes it**: *"wa-qawluhu wa-in yaraw
āyatan yuʿriḍū … yarudduhu, wa-kafā bihi rāddan"* — the very next verse (the disbelievers "turn away from a
sign") rebuts the future reading, since a future event would not provoke present turning-away. He notes the
Ḥudhayfa variant reading *wa-qad anshaqqa al-qamar*. **al-Zamakhsharī's reading: literal miracle, the
eschatological reading actively refuted by v 2.**

## 3. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (on Q 54:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` (Q 54 block).

al-Rāzī frames the opening within the disbelievers' mockery of the delayed Hour: "the disbelievers said
among themselves, 'this [man] claims the Resurrection has drawn near' … when nothing came they said 'we see
nothing of what he threatened us with,'" which he connects to Q 21:1 *iqtaraba li-l-nāsi ḥisābuhum* and
Q 16:1 *atā amru llāhi fa-lā tastaʿjilūh* — God's answer to the why-is-it-delayed objection. al-Rāzī surveys
the readings of *anshaqqa al-qamar*, ranking the **literal historical reading by isnād-strength** while
cataloguing the eschatological-future and metaphorical alternatives (consistent with the 00-overview §2
summary; al-Rāzī treats the perfect *anshaqqa* as a realized Meccan sign, with the future reading a weaker
grammatical option). His exegesis centers the surah's whole logic on the *Hour-imminent-yet-mocked* polemic
that the five destruction-pericopes then concretize.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (on Q 54:1, 42, 55)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/54/{1,42,55}.json`.

**On v 1:** *"Sūrat al-Qamar — Makkiyya kulluhā fī qawl al-jumhūr"* (Meccan in its entirety per the majority);
he records Muqātil's minority view that three verses (vv 44-46, *am yaqūlūna naḥnu jamīʿun muntaṣir* … *wa-l-
sāʿatu adhā wa-amarr*) are Medinan, "but it is not sound," and gives the count *"wa-hiya khamsun wa-khamsūna
āya"* (55 verses). He glosses *iqtarabat* as "drew near (*qarubat*), like *azifati al-āzifa*," and cites
Qatāda ← Anas that the Prophet preached as the sun was about to set, saying "there remains of your world,
relative to what has passed, only as much as remains of this day relative to what has passed of it."

**On v 42** (*fa-akhadhnāhum akhdha ʿAzīz Muqtadir*): *"ʿAzīz — ghālib fī intiqāmihi; Muqtadir — qādir ʿalā
mā arād"* (al-ʿAzīz, victorious in His retribution; al-Muqtadir, capable of whatever He wills) — the
name-of-power closing the destruction-catalog.

**On v 55** (*fī maqʿadi ṣidqin ʿinda Malīk Muqtadir*): *"fī maqʿadi ṣidqin — majlisi ḥaqqin lā laghwa fīhi
wa-lā taʾthīm, wa-huwa al-janna; ʿinda Malīk Muqtadir — yaqdiru ʿalā mā yashāʾ"*; he stresses *ʿinda* here
denotes "nearness, rank, station, honour," not spatial location, and reports the ʿUthmān al-Battī variant
reading *fī maqāʿidi ṣidq* (plural). **This is the project-relevant passage**: al-Qurṭubī himself reads BOTH
v 42 and v 55 as al-Muqtadir-of-power closures (Q054-F-06 closure-frame, H6b).

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (on Q 54:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/54/1.json` (Arabic at
`.../ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/54/`).

Ibn Kathīr's section opens: *"The Ḥadīth of Abū Wāqid preceded, in which it is mentioned that the Messenger
of Allah ﷺ would recite Sūrat Qāf and *Iqtarabat as-Sāʿa* (al-Qamar) during the ʿīd prayers of al-Aḍḥā and
al-Fiṭr … because they contain Allah's promises and warnings, and information about the origin of creation,
Resurrection, Tawḥīd, the affirmation of prophethood."* (NOTE: the on-disk English mistakenly glosses Qāf as
"chapter 53"; Qāf is mushaf 50 — a source typo. The Qāf + al-Qamar ʿīd pairing itself is corroborated by
Muslim #1949/1950 and Abū Dāwūd #1155 — see `04-hadith-corpus.md` §3.) He reads v 1 as God informing of
"the approach of the Last Hour and the imminent end of the world" (cross-citing Q 16:1, Q 21:1), then
assembles the moon-splitting ḥadīths. **He explicitly anchors the literal historical reading and rejects
metaphor-only**, as the 00-overview §2 records.

## 6. al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* (on Q 54:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-asbab-al-nuzul-by-al-wahidi/54/1.json`.

al-Wāḥidī gives the sabab via **Masrūq ← ʿAbd Allāh (Ibn Masʿūd)**: *"The moon was split at the time of the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ, so the Quraysh said: 'This is sorcery from the son of Abū Kabsha; he has cast a spell
on you. Ask the travellers whether they saw the moon split.' They asked some travellers and they confirmed
it. Then Allah revealed (The hour drew nigh and the moon was rent in twain)."* The Ibn Masʿūd chain (with the
travellers' corroboration) is the canonical sabab.

## 7. Points of agreement / disagreement

| Question | al-Ṭabarī | al-Zamakhsharī | al-Rāzī | al-Qurṭubī | Ibn Kathīr |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| v 1 *anshaqqa al-qamar* | **literal/Meccan** | **literal miracle** (refutes future) | **literal** (ranks by isnād) | literal; cites Anas | **literal**; rejects metaphor |
| Eschatological-future reading | not preferred | **refuted by v 2** | weaker option | — | rejected |
| Meccan/Medinan | Meccan | Meccan | Meccan | **all-Meccan per jumhūr** (vv 44-46 Medinan = Muqātil, "not sound") | Meccan |
| al-Muqtadir closure (vv 42, 55) | name-of-power seizure | — | — | **both = qādir ʿalā mā yashāʾ** | — |
| Qāf + al-Qamar liturgical pairing | — | — | — | — | **explicit (Abū Wāqid ḥadīth)** |

The exegetical near-consensus is the **literal-historical Meccan moon-splitting**; the only sustained
alternative (eschatological-future) is recorded by al-Zamakhsharī precisely to refute it via v 2's
present-tense disbeliever turning-away. al-Qurṭubī's gloss of both al-Muqtadir occurrences as "capable of
whatever He wills" is the classical anchor for Q054-F-06's doubled power-name closure-frame.
