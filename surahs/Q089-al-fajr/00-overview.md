## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Fajr** after the opening word of v.1 *wa-l-fajr* ("by the dawn"). The Arabic *fajr* designates the breaking of dawn — the temporal marker of the morning prayer (*ṣalāt al-fajr*) in Islamic ritual practice, and one of the corpus's small set of cosmic-temporal oath-anchors (paralleling *wa-l-ḍuḥā* of Q 93, *wa-l-shams* of Q 91, *wa-l-layl* of Q 92, *wa-l-ʿaṣr* of Q 103).

Q 89 sits at the head of the **short-Meccan-tail oath-cosmic-temporal pentad** (Q 89-93), the inner core of the H-NEW-1070 oath-opener cluster (CONFIRMED at p=0.0004 corpus-wide). Its mushaf-position s=89 places it as the FIRST member of the short-mufaṣṣal oath-arc Q 89-103 in canonical reading order.

### Classical disagreement on the v.1-4 referents (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.; al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* vol. 31; Ibn Kathīr ad loc.)

- **al-fajr (the dawn)**: (a) the universal daybreak (al-Ṭabarī majority); (b) the Day of Sacrifice's dawn (Ibn ʿAbbās via *al-Durr al-manthūr*); (c) the entire month of Muḥarram dawn (al-Suyūṭī catalog); (d) the dawn of the prayer (Mujāhid).
- **layālin ʿashr (ten nights)**: (a) the first 10 of Dhu al-Ḥijja — Ḥajj's pilgrimage nights (Ibn ʿAbbās majority, supported by Bukhārī sound-hadith); (b) the last 10 of Ramaḍān containing Laylat al-Qadr (al-Ḍaḥḥāk minority view); (c) the first 10 of Muḥarram (al-Ṭabarī alternative).
- **al-shafʿ wa-l-witr (the even and the odd)**: (a) all created things — the world's pairs (*shafʿ*) vs God's unicity (*witr*) (Ibn ʿAbbās via Ibn Kathīr); (b) the Day of Sacrifice + Day of ʿArafa (Mujāhid); (c) the prayers — the even-numbered (*ẓuhr* + *ʿaṣr*) vs odd-numbered (*maghrib* + *witr*) (al-Suddī minority); (d) Adam (created from no other) + his wife (al-Suyūṭī catalog).
- **al-layli idhā yasr (the night as it passes)**: (a) any night that travels and ends (al-Ṭabarī generic); (b) the Night of Muzdalifa during Ḥajj (Ibn Kathīr); (c) the Laylat al-Qadr (al-Ḍaḥḥāk).

Multiple classical authorities (al-Rāzī, al-Suyūṭī) note that the **Ḥajj-pilgrimage reading** (Day of Sacrifice + 10 nights of Dhū al-Ḥijja + ʿarafa-day + Muzdalifa-night) is the most-COHERENT reading: the four oath-elements together pick out the 10-day Ḥajj cycle as the cosmic-temporal frame. This reading is consistent with Q 89's overall theme of testing-and-recompense and provides intra-surah unity to the v.1-4 cluster.

## 7. ⭐ Famous classical verse — *inna rabbaka la-bi-l-mirṣād* (Q 89:14)

Q 89:14 *inna rabbaka la-bi-l-mirṣād* ("indeed, your Lord is ever in observation / lying in wait") closes the destroyed-civilizations catalog with one of the corpus's most-cited eschatological-judgment verses. The phrase enters classical Islamic discourse on:
- divine surveillance (*muraqaba*) — al-Ghazālī *Iḥyāʾ* on tawakkul and rajāʾ.
- judgment-day immediacy — al-Ṭabarī ad loc., al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ* vol. 31.
- Sufi *muḥāsabat al-nafs* (self-reckoning) tradition — Ibn ʿAṭāʾ Allāh *al-Ḥikam*.
- Imāmī Shīʿī fadāʾil literature — the verse is taken as evidence that God awaits the wronged at the bridge of judgment.

The root of *al-mirṣād* (ر-ص-د) appears 7 times in the Quran (Q 9:107; Q 72:9; Q 72:27; Q 78:21; Q 89:14; Q 89:5 — wait, no, *mirṣād* is at v.14 only; *qasamun li-dhī ḥijr* is unrelated — see §5 Classical claims audit for verification). Q 89:14 is the verse's most-cited classical-tafsir-anchored instance.
