# Q 89 al-Fajr — Classical Tafsīr Survey

Survey of 6 classical mufassirūn on the major exegetical loci of Q 89: (a) the cosmic-temporal-oath quartet (vv. 1-4); (b) the meta-oath rhetorical closing (v. 5); (c) the destroyed-civilizations catalog (vv. 6-14); (d) the *al-mirṣād* verse (v. 14); (e) the wealth-test paradigm (vv. 15-20); (f) the soul-at-peace return formula (vv. 27-30).

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*)

al-Ṭabarī's discussion of Q 89 is preserved in his standard tafsir (vol. 30 in the Dār al-Maʿārif edition; sūrat al-fajr).

### Q 89:1-4 (cosmic-temporal oath quartet) — the multi-vajh catalog

al-Ṭabarī catalogs the major Companion+Successor positions on each oath-element:

**al-fajr (the dawn)**:
- al-fajr generic (the daybreak in general) — Mujāhid, ʿIkrima.
- *fajr al-naḥr* — the dawn of the Day of Sacrifice — Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda.
- *fajr al-ṣalāh* — the dawn of the prayer — al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī.
- *fajr al-Muḥarram* — the first day of Muḥarram — al-Suddī (minor).

**layālin ʿashr (ten nights)**:
- *al-ʿashr al-ūlā min Dhī al-Ḥijja* — the first 10 of Dhū al-Ḥijja — Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, ʿAṭāʾ, Saʿīd b. Jubayr (the **majority view**).
- *al-ʿashr al-akhīrah min Ramaḍān* — the last 10 of Ramaḍān (containing Laylat al-Qadr) — al-Ḍaḥḥāk, Yamān b. Riyāb (minority).
- *al-ʿashr al-ūlā min Muḥarram* — the first 10 of Muḥarram — Saʿīd b. Jubayr in some chains.

**al-shafʿ wa-l-witr (even and odd)**:
- *al-shafʿ* = creation (paired); *al-witr* = the Creator (singular) — Ibn ʿAbbās via majority.
- *al-shafʿ* = the Day of Sacrifice (the two ʿīds joined); *al-witr* = the Day of ʿArafa — Mujāhid.
- *al-shafʿ* = the prayers' even-number counts; *al-witr* = the witr-prayer — al-Suddī.
- *al-shafʿ* = Adam + Ḥawwā; *al-witr* = Allāh — al-Suyūṭī catalog.

**al-layli idhā yasr (the night when it passes)**:
- the night that travels and ends — al-Ṭabarī generic.
- the Night of Muzdalifa — Ibn Kathīr's preferred reading via Ḥajj-coherence.

al-Ṭabarī's editorial position: he prefers the Ḥajj-coherent reading (Day of Sacrifice + 10 nights of Dhū al-Ḥijja + ʿArafa-day + Muzdalifa-night) as it provides intra-oath unity, but presents the alternatives.

### Q 89:5 *li-dhī ḥijr*

al-Ṭabarī catalogs:
- *ḥijr* = "intellect" (al-ʿaql) — the dominant view (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda, al-Ḥasan).
- *ḥijr* = "restraining-faculty" (the part of the intellect that prevents harm) — a specific gloss tied to the verbal noun ح-ج-ر "to forbid."

### Q 89:7 Iram dhāt al-ʿimād

al-Ṭabarī provides a substantial section on the Iram-traditions, catalogue 4 main positions:
- a tribe of ʿĀd named after their ancestor Iram b. Sām b. Nūḥ.
- a city built by ʿĀd that was lost to history.
- a region or land in southern Arabia.
- a metaphorical ancestor-name (less attested).

al-Ṭabarī cites the famous Ibn ʿAbbās-tradition that Iram was a city of golden-pillared palaces, thrown down by God after ʿĀd's transgression — presented with isnad-discussion (al-Ṭabarī notes some chains are *ḍaʿīf* / weak, particularly the elaborate *qaṣṣ* embellishments).

### Q 89:14 *inna rabbaka la-bi-l-mirṣād*

al-Ṭabarī gives a parsimonious gloss: God is in the place of observation (the *mirṣād*), not slumbering, not unaware. He cites Q 78:21 *inna Jahannama kānat mirṣādan* as an inter-Quranic parallel.

### Q 89:27-30 soul-return formula

al-Ṭabarī gives standard glosses without major exegetical complication: *al-muṭmaʾinna* = the soul that has attained peace through faith and right action; *irjiʿī ilā rabbiki* = a divine call at the moment of death (Mujāhid) or at the resurrection (Saʿīd b. Jubayr).

## 2. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`, the Q 89 section.

### Q 89:1-5 — al-Rāzī's full multi-vajh enumeration

al-Rāzī provides one of the corpus's most-thorough analyses of the cosmic-temporal-oath quartet. His method: catalog each oath-element's possible referents, then assess whether the 4 (or 5) elements have a UNIFYING reading that gives the oath-cluster coherence.

For Q 89:1-4, al-Rāzī enumerates **9 possible unifying readings** for the 4-element oath-quartet:
1. **Ḥajj-cluster reading** (the dominant): all 4 elements refer to Ḥajj — fajr al-naḥr / 10 nights of Dhū al-Ḥijja / ʿArafa+Muzdalifa / Muzdalifa-night. Tight intra-oath coherence.
2. **Cosmological-pair reading**: fajr/night = day-night cycle; shafʿ/witr = pair-singular polarity; the oath structures the entire created-world's polarities.
3. **Prayer-cluster reading**: fajr-prayer / 10 cycles of prayer / shafʿ-and-witr prayers / night-prayer. Prayer-as-oath-anchor.
4. **Numerological reading**: 5 elements (counting *shafʿ* + *witr* separately) parallel the 5 daily prayers; al-Rāzī notes this is structurally appealing but not strongly textually-supported.
5. **Eschatological reading**: fajr = dawn-of-resurrection; 10 nights = the 10 days of dispute (mawqif); shafʿ-witr = paired-trumpet-blast (cf. Q 39:68); night = the last-night before resurrection.
6-9. Various less-supported readings.

al-Rāzī then asks: which reading gives the **rhetorical purpose** of the oath? He concludes the Ḥajj-reading + the cosmological-pair reading are the two strongest, and that Q 89:5's rhetorical question (*hal fī dhālika qasamun li-dhī ḥijr*) deliberately leaves the referent slightly ambiguous so that "one of understanding" sees the full multivalence.

al-Rāzī specifically discusses Q 89:5 as a unique-corpus instance of META-OATH closing — he notes that elsewhere oath-clusters proceed directly to the purpose-clause (*inna [X]*), but Q 89 inserts a rhetorical question. This is a balagha-distinctive move.

### Q 89:6-14 — destroyed-civilizations catalog

al-Rāzī focuses on the rhetorical compression: the catalog covers 4 destroyed-people in 9 verses, whereas Q 11 and Q 26 take 75-87 verses. This compression is, for al-Rāzī, an iʿjāz al-balagha device — "a great expanse compressed into a small space" (*muqarrira li-l-naẓm fī al-iqtiṣār*). The rhetorical purpose: the catalog is offered AS EVIDENCE for the meta-oath's claim that those who would understand can see God's pattern in history.

### Q 89:14 *inna rabbaka la-bi-l-mirṣād*

al-Rāzī provides a lengthy theological exposition: *mirṣād* invokes the image of an ambush, but applied to God it is the omniscient-surveillance — God is not deceived, not forgetful, not unaware of any human action. The verse is a divine-attribute proof-text for theological *ḥisāb* (reckoning).

### Q 89:15-20 — wealth-test paradigm

al-Rāzī highlights the paradigmatic-rhetorical structure: vv. 15-16 establish the "human's misreading" (positive-test = honor; negative-test = humiliation); vv. 17-20 launch the *kallā*-repudiation, indicting the actual ethical failure. The structure: *fa-ammā... wa-ammā... // kallā... wa-lā... wa... wa...*

al-Rāzī notes the rhetorical mirroring with Q 92:5-10 (the *fa-ammā man aʿṭā / wa-ammā man bakhila* of al-Layl). The two surahs form a wealth-test-rhetorical-doubling pair.

### Q 89:22 *wa-jāʾa rabbuka*

al-Rāzī provides the standard early-Sunni-theology discussion: God's "coming" is not anthropomorphic; the verse describes the manifestation of divine command and judgment-power, not a literal-locomotion. He cites the al-Ashʿarī method of *tafwīḍ al-maʿnā* (deferring the meaning).

### Q 89:27-30 — soul-return formula

al-Rāzī provides the classical *aqsām al-nafs* discussion: the soul has 3 stages (*ammāra*, *lawwāma*, *muṭmaʾinna*), and Q 89:27 addresses the highest-stage soul. The vocative *yā ayyatuhā* is to one specific soul (*ʿayniyya*) per al-Rāzī, not a universal-class — the call is to each individual perfected soul at the moment of death or resurrection.

al-Rāzī notes the **mutual-pleasure formula** *rāḍiyatan marḍiyya* (well-pleased and well-pleasing) is a structural-rhetorical highlight: the soul's pleasure with God + God's pleasure with the soul = perfect mutual-reciprocity. He cites this as a balagha-iʿjāz instance.

## 3. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt`, sūrat al-fajr section.

al-Qurṭubī catalogs:
- the 4-vajh enumeration of *al-fajr* (matching al-Ṭabarī).
- detailed discussion of the *layālin ʿashr* with hadith citation (Bukhārī's *bāb faḍl al-ʿamal fī ayyām al-tashrīq* and parallels).
- the qirāʾāt variations on Q 89:4 (the dropped-yāʾ in *yasr*).
- al-Qurṭubī's reading of *Iram dhāt al-ʿimād* (vol. 20, p. 51+): he favors the city-of-golden-columns tradition but notes the isnad-criticisms.
- al-Qurṭubī provides a fiqh discussion of the wealth-test paradigm vv. 15-20: under what conditions does the test-of-prosperity become a fitna and require renunciation? He cites the position of various schools.
- For Q 89:27-30, al-Qurṭubī provides the **funerary-recitation tradition catalog**: he records that some classical scholars (Ibn ʿAtiyya, al-Marwazī) note Q 89 as a funeral-recitation candidate, but acknowledges the hadith-isnad on this practice is not firmly established. al-Qurṭubī's method: he records the practice as observed in some communities without committing to its sound-attestation.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`, sūrat al-fajr section.

Ibn Kathīr opens his Q 89 commentary with the cosmic-temporal-oath enumeration, leaning toward the Ḥajj-reading (Day of Sacrifice + 10 nights of Dhū al-Ḥijja + Muzdalifa-night) as the most-coherent. He cites:
- al-Bukhārī sound-hadith on the merit of the first 10 days of Dhū al-Ḥijja: *mā min ayyāmin al-ʿamalu al-ṣāliḥu fīhinna aḥabbu ilā Allāh min hādhihi al-ayyām al-ʿashr* — "There are no days in which righteous deeds are more beloved to God than these ten days." This grounds the *layālin ʿashr* Ḥajj-reading in canonical-hadith.

### Q 89:7 — Iram dhāt al-ʿimād

Ibn Kathīr's lengthy excursus on Iram (vol. 8 p. 408-411 in Dār al-Ṭayba edition):
- Iram = a tribe of ʿĀd, named after their ancestor Iram b. Sām b. Nūḥ. ʿĀd thus = "ʿĀd of Iram" (this is a clarifying genitive: ʿĀd's parent-tribe is Iram).
- The "city of golden columns" tradition (Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Suddī) describes Iram as a city built by Shaddād b. ʿĀd in the southern Arabian peninsula, with palaces of gold-and-silver columns. God commanded its destruction.
- Ibn Kathīr notes the *qaṣaṣ*-element of this tradition — the elaborate Shaddād narratives are mostly from *isrāʾīliyyāt* (Israelite-source traditions) and should be treated with caution. He prefers the parsimonious reading: Iram = the parent-tribe-name; *dhāt al-ʿimād* = "of the (architectural) columns" = a description of their dwelling-style (cave-cut palaces and column-rich structures).
- Ibn Kathīr also notes the alternative reading *Iram dhāt al-ʿimād* could refer to "ʿĀd of the tall-statures" (read *ʿimād* metaphorically for tall-bodies), but this is less-attested.

### Q 89:9 — Thamūd jābū al-ṣakhra

Ibn Kathīr describes the rock-cutting at Madāʾin Ṣāliḥ (al-Ḥijr) — Thamūd's distinctive cultural-marker. He links to Q 7:74 (other Thamūd-rock-architecture references) and to the al-Ḥijr archaeological tradition.

### Q 89:14 — *inna rabbaka la-bi-l-mirṣād*

Ibn Kathīr's exposition is theological-eschatological. He cites:
- the verse as evidence for the *ṣirāṭ* / bridge of judgment, where God awaits the soul.
- the connection to Q 78:21 (*inna Jahannama kānat mirṣādan*) and Q 9:107 (*irṣād li-man ḥāraba*).
- al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's gloss: "He is *bi-l-mirṣād* — He sees what all servants do; not a single deed escapes Him."

### Q 89:27-30 — *al-nafs al-muṭmaʾinna*

Ibn Kathīr provides the funeral-recitation-tradition catalog in some detail:
- The verse is reported as having been recited at the burial of certain Companions and Followers.
- Ibn Kathīr cites a tradition (with isnad-caveat) that the angels would recite this verse to the dying-believer's soul, calling it back to God.
- He notes the verse is broadly used in Sufi-oriented *talqīn* traditions.

Ibn Kathīr's methodological caution: the funerary-recitation chains for Q 89:27-30 are MOSTLY at the level of *fadāʾil* / virtue-attribution literature, not the strict-sahih hadith corpus. The practice may be classical, but the textual-isnad foundation is weaker than for canonical practices.

## 5. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`, sūrat al-fajr section.

al-Biqāʿī's specialty is *munāsabāt*. For Q 89:

### Q 88 → Q 89 munāsabah

al-Biqāʿī links al-Ghāshiyah (Q 88) to al-Fajr (Q 89) via the **shared eschatological-frame**: Q 88 closes with a forceful eschatological-summary (*innā ilaynā iyābahum / thumma inna ʿalaynā ḥisābahum* — "to Us is their return, and to Us their reckoning"), and Q 89:1-5 opens with the cosmic-temporal-oath. The *iyāb* + *ḥisāb* of Q 88 transitions to the *fajr* of new-day reckoning in Q 89.

EMPIRICAL CORRELATE (H-NEW-720): Q 88→Q 89 fraction_residual = 0.0024 (rank 23/113 — modestly smooth, NOT clamped-zero). The seam is well below corpus-mean cost but not at the H-NEW-1240 extreme-tier. al-Biqāʿī's munāsabah claim is empirically supported as SMOOTH but not extreme.

### Q 89 → Q 90 munāsabah

al-Biqāʿī links al-Fajr (Q 89) to al-Balad (Q 90) via the **shared meta-oath theme**: Q 89:5 closes its oath with a rhetorical question; Q 90:1 opens with *lā uqsimu bi-hādhā al-balad* (NEGATIVE oath form). The transition is from "is there in that an oath?" to "I do NOT swear..." — both are atypical-form oaths that draw the listener's attention.

EMPIRICAL CORRELATE (H-NEW-720): Q 89→Q 90 fraction_residual = 0.0061 (rank 47/113 — middle-pack). Smooth but unremarkable. al-Biqāʿī's munāsabah is supported but the seam is not corpus-extremal.

### Internal block-munāsabāt within Q 89

al-Biqāʿī provides one of the most-detailed internal-munāsabah readings of Q 89 in classical exegesis:
- BLOCK A → BLOCK B transition (vv. 5/6): the rhetorical question demands evidence; vv. 6-14 provide it (the destroyed civilizations are the case-evidence).
- BLOCK B → BLOCK C transition (vv. 14/15): the universal claim "your Lord is in observation" pivots to the individual case "as for man, when his Lord tries him..." — civilizational-historical to individual-anthropological.
- BLOCK C → BLOCK D transition (vv. 23/24): the doomed soul's "would that I had sent forth..." (v. 24) pivots to the saved soul's *yā ayyatuhā al-nafs al-muṭmaʾinna* (v. 27) — failed-vs-perfected soul-trajectory.

al-Biqāʿī's reading: Q 89 is a SINGLE COMPRESSED EXPOSITION of God's relation to humanity across cosmic-civilizational-individual scales, ending with the perfected-soul's return.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr*)

al-Suyūṭī classifies Q 89 in *al-Itqān* nawʿ 67 (al-aqsām) as one of the major oath-opener surahs. The surah's oath-cluster places it firmly in the al-aqsām class.

### *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 89

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr* compiles the rich tradition-history on Q 89:
- the *layālin ʿashr* Ḥajj-reading hadith-cluster (multiple chains).
- the Iram dhāt al-ʿimād traditions, with explicit isnad-discussion.
- the Pharaoh-pegs traditions, including the variant readings (architectural pegs, torture-pegs).
- the *al-mirṣād* eschatological-traditions.
- the soul-return-formula traditions, including the funerary-recitation traditions (with isnad-caveats).

al-Suyūṭī's methodological mode: comprehensive-collection rather than critical-selection. He records weak-isnad chains alongside strong ones, with explicit *qad ḍaʿʿafa* / "X has weakened this" remarks where relevant.
