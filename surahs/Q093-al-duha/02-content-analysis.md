## 1. Verse-by-verse summary

**v 1** — والضحى
*By the morning brightness / the forenoon.* The opening oath (qasam). *al-ḍuḥā* (root DHw) is the bright
forenoon, the light of full day. al-Qurṭubī notes the gloss "the daytime" (paired against the night of
v 2), with secondary readings (the time Allāh spoke to Mūsā; the hour the sorcerers fell prostrate).

**v 2** — والليل إذا سجى
*And by the night when it settles / grows still.* (roots lyl, sjw — *sajā* = settles, darkens, becomes
calm.) The oath's second term. This *wa-l-layl idhā [verb]* frame echoes the opening of the
chronologically-prior Q 92 al-Layl (*wa-l-layl idhā yaghshā*) — Ibn Kathīr cross-references the two
explicitly. Light (forenoon) / dark (settled night) is the merism of the oath.

**v 3** — ما ودعك ربك وما قلى
*Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor does He hate you.* (roots wdE = *waddaʿa*, take-leave-of/forsake;
rbb = Lord; qly = *qalā*, hate/detest.) This is the *jawāb al-qasam* (answer to the oath) and the
emotional core of the surah — the direct refutation of the taunt that occasioned the revelation (see
`04-hadith-corpus.md`). al-Ṭabarī: *mā tarakaka rabbuka wa-mā abghaḍaka.*

**v 4** — وللآخرة خير لك من الأولى
*And the Hereafter is better for you than the first [life].* (roots Axr = *al-ākhira*; xyr = better;
Awl = *al-ūlā*, the first/former.) The reassurance widens: the trajectory is upward; what is to come
exceeds what has passed.

**v 5** — ولسوف يعطيك ربك فترضى
*And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied.* (roots ETw = *yuʿṭīka*, give; rbb;
rDw = *fa-tarḍā*, be pleased.) The future-gift promise. al-Qurṭubī surveys the readings: worldly victory
+ reward in the Hereafter; the *ḥawḍ* and the intercession (*shafāʿa*); Ibn ʿAbbās's "a thousand palaces
of pearl." The *wa-la-sawfa yuʿṭīka… fa-tarḍā* is the most expansive consolation-clause of the surah.

**v 6** — ألم يجدك يتيما فآوى
*Did He not find you an orphan and give [you] shelter?* (roots wjd = *wajadaka*, found you; ytm = orphan;
Awy = *āwā*, shelter.) The first past-favor. Ibn Kathīr unpacks the biography: the father died before
birth, the mother (Āmina) when he was six, then ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, then Abū Ṭālib's protection. The
**orphan (ytm)** named here is the surah's single lexical hinge (it recurs at v 9).

**v 7** — ووجدك ضالا فهدى
*And He found you lost / unguided, and He guided [you].* (roots wjd; Dll = *ḍāllan*, astray/seeking;
hdy = *hadā*, guided.) The second past-favor. The mufassirūn read *ḍāllan* not as "in error" but as
"not-yet-knowing the Law / seeking" — divine guidance to prophethood.

**v 8** — ووجدك عائلا فأغنى
*And He found you poor and enriched [you].* (roots wjd; Eyl = *ʿāʾilan*, poor/dependent; gny = *aghnā*,
enriched.) The third past-favor. The triad **orphan → lost → poor** is matched and reversed by the divine
acts **sheltered → guided → enriched**, each verse closing on the *fa-[verb]* response.

**v 9** — فأما اليتيم فلا تقهر
*So as for the orphan, do not oppress [him].* (roots ytm = orphan; qhr = *taqhar*, oppress/subdue.) The
first future-command — and it re-uses **ytm**, the only root carried over from the favor block (v 6 → v 9).
The recipient of the first favor (the sheltered orphan) becomes the object of the first command (do not
oppress orphans).

**v 10** — وأما السائل فلا تنهر
*And as for the one who asks / the beggar, do not repel [him].* (roots sAl = *al-sāʾil*, the asker;
nhr = *tanhar*, rebuff/repel.) The second future-command. Positionally it answers the second favor
(*ḍāllan*/guided), but lexically it shares NO root with v 7 — the parallel here is thematic, not lexical.

**v 11** — وأما بنعمة ربك فحدث
*And as for the favor of your Lord, proclaim [it].* (roots nEm = *niʿma*, favor/blessing; rbb = Lord;
Hdv = *ḥaddith*, narrate/proclaim.) The third future-command. al-Qurṭubī (via Mujāhid): the *niʿma* is
the Qurʾān / prophethood — *fa-ḥaddith* = "convey what you were sent with." The surah closes on *rabb*
(third occurrence), inclusio-style, returning to the *rabbuka* of vv 3 and 5.

## 2. Thematic blocks

| Block | Verses | Register | Theme |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **A. Oath** | 1-2 | qasam | light (forenoon) / dark (settled night) merism |
| **B. Consolation (jawāb al-qasam)** | 3-5 | reassurance | not-forsaken (3) · Hereafter-is-better (4) · future-gift *fa-tarḍā* (5) |
| **C. Past-favor triad** | 6-8 | recollection | *wajadaka* orphan→sheltered, lost→guided, poor→enriched |
| **D. Future-command triad** | 9-11 | imperative | *fa-ammā* orphan→do-not-oppress, asker→do-not-repel, blessing→proclaim |

The macro-structure is a **temporal arc with a chiastic hinge**: the past-favor triad (C) is answered by
the future-command triad (D), and the hinge is the **orphan** — the first favor (v 6 *wajadaka yatīman*)
and the first command (v 9 *fa-ammā al-yatīma*) are bound by the shared root `ytm` (Q093-F-01 Arm B). The
surah moves from cosmic oath → personal reassurance → biographical gratitude → ethical mandate.

## 3. Content register

**Consolation + ethical-imperative.** Q 93 is a personal address to the Prophet (the *-ka* suffixes
throughout: *waddaʿaka, rabbuka, laka, yuʿṭīka, yajidka, wajadaka*) — there is no legal, eschatological-
descriptive, or polemical block. The fire/judgment imagery absent here distinguishes it from its
chronological neighbor Q 92 al-Layl (which has *nāran talaẓẓā*). The closing commands (vv 9-11) widen the
personal consolation into a universal ethic (orphan-care, almsgiving, gratitude-proclamation).

## 4. Vocabulary distinctness

- **`wjd` (wajadaka) anaphora** — the root appears in vv 6, 7, 8 and NO other Q 93 verse: a three-fold
  anaphoric "He found you…" opening the past-favor triad (Q093-F-01 Arm B, B-H1). This triple-*wajadaka*
  is the surah's signature construction.
- **`ammā… fa-` (the conditional-particularizing frame)** — vv 9, 10, 11 each open *(wa-)ammā [X] fa-[lā]
  [verb]* — a three-fold parallel imperative frame matching the three-fold *wajadaka* favor frame.
- **`ytm` (orphan) as the lexical hinge** — vv 6 and 9 only: the single root that bridges the favor block
  and the command block (Q093-F-01 Arm B, B-H2).
- **`rbb` (rabb) inclusio** — vv 3, 5, 11: the consolation opens (*rabbuka* v 3, v 5) and the surah closes
  (*bi-niʿmati rabbika* v 11) on the Lord, framing the whole as a Lord-addressed reassurance.
- **The maqṣūra rhyme** — 8 of 11 verses close on the *-ā/-ay* maqṣūra (*sajā, qalā, al-ūlā, fa-tarḍā,
  fa-āwā, fa-hadā, fa-aghnā*), the strong yāʾ-letter monorhyme (72.7%, `h-new-700.json`).

## 5. Repetition patterns

- **Triple *wajadaka …fa-…*** (vv 6-8): orphan→sheltered, lost→guided, poor→enriched.
- **Triple *(wa-)ammā [X] fa-…*** (vv 9-11): orphan, asker, blessing.
- **Double *rabbuka*** in the consolation (vv 3, 5) + closing *rabbika* (v 11).
- **The favor↔command positional mapping** (6↔9, 7↔10, 8↔11): only the first pair (6↔9, *ytm*) is
  lexically realized; the other two are thematic/positional (zero shared root) — see Q093-F-01 Arm B.

## 6. Cross-surah content references

- **Q 93:2 ↔ Q 92:1** — the *wa-l-layl idhā [verb]* oath-frame (*sajā* / *yaghshā*); Ibn Kathīr
  cross-references Q 93:1-2 with Q 92:1-2 directly on disk.
- **Q 93 ↔ Q 94 al-Sharḥ** — the classically-paired "one surah" (Ṭāwūs, ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, via
  al-Rāzī on Q 94:1); al-Rāzī ties Q 94:1 (*alam nashraḥ*) to Q 93:6 (*alam yajidka yatīman*) as a
  syntactic continuation — i.e. the orphan-favor verse is the explicit junction the classical pairing
  hangs on. Empirically the two surahs are FR-rank-4 neighbors but share ZERO boundary roots
  (Q093-F-01 Arm A).
- **Q 93:5 (*fa-tarḍā*) ↔ the intercession ḥadīth** — al-Qurṭubī cites the *ḥawḍ*/*shafāʿa* readings and
  the ṣaḥīḥ-Muslim Ibn ʿAmr report (Ibrāhīm/ʿĪsā prayer → "innā sa-nurḍīka fī ummatika").
- **Q 93:6-8 (the biography of favors)** — the orphan/guidance/wealth triad is the Qurʾān's most compact
  autobiographical statement of the Prophet's early life, expanded in the sīra.
