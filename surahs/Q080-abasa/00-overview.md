## 2. Classical name + the occasion-of-revelation

The surah is named **ʿAbasa** ("He Frowned") after the opening word of v.1. The verbal-form *ʿabasa* is 3rd-person singular masculine past, from the root ʿ-b-s ("to frown, scowl, contract the brow"). The classical-tafsīr consensus is that the **implicit subject is the Prophet Muhammad himself** — and that the surah-opening is a **divine reproach to the Prophet** for an incident with the blind Companion **ʿAbd Allāh ibn Umm Maktūm**.

### The classical occasion-of-revelation (asbāb al-nuzūl)

The canonical occasion-of-revelation hadith is preserved in **Sunan al-Tirmidhī**, kitāb al-tafsīr, **idInBook 3415** (chapter 47), narrated by ʿĀʾisha, graded by al-Tirmidhī himself as **ḥasan-gharīb**:

> أُنْزِلَ ﴿عبَسَ وَتَوَلَّى﴾ فِي ابْنِ أُمِّ مَكْتُومٍ الأَعْمَى أَتَى رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ فَجَعَلَ يَقُولُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ أَرْشِدْنِي. وَعِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ رَجُلٌ مِنْ عُظَمَاءِ الْمُشْرِكِينَ. فَجَعَلَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ يُعْرِضُ عَنْهُ وَيُقْبِلُ عَلَى الآخَرِ، وَيَقُولُ: أَتَرَى بِمَا أَقُولُ بَأْسًا؟ فَيُقَالُ: لاَ. فَفِي هَذَا أُنْزِلَ.

> "*'He frowned and turned away'* was revealed about Ibn Umm Maktūm, the blind man. He came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saying: 'O Messenger of Allah! Guide me.' At that time, there was a revered man from the idolaters with the Messenger of Allah. So the Messenger of Allah turned away from him and faced the other man, saying: 'Do you think there is something wrong with what I am saying?' He said: 'No.' So it was about that that it was revealed."

The narration ends with al-Tirmidhī's own grading: *qāla Abū ʿĪsā: hādhā ḥadīthun ḥasanun gharīb* ("Abū ʿĪsā [al-Tirmidhī himself] said: This is a *ḥasan-gharīb* hadith.").

The hadith is also preserved in al-Ḥākim's *al-Mustadrak* and in al-Ṭabarī's *Tafsīr* with multiple isnāds tracing back through Hishām b. ʿUrwa from his father from ʿĀʾisha. The ʿĀʾisha chain establishes that the rebuke was directed at the Prophet for ignoring Ibn Umm Maktūm (a sincere, blind, poor convert) in favor of one of the *ʿuẓamāʾ al-mushrikīn* (the notable disbelievers — variously identified in later tafsīr as al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra, ʿUtba b. Rabīʿa, Abū Jahl, or unnamed Qurayshī notables).

### Classical interpretive divergence

The classical tafsīr tradition is virtually unanimous on the IDENTITY of the subject (the Prophet) but divergent on the THEOLOGICAL framing:

- **The Prophet rebuked** (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, al-Suyūṭī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Bayḍāwī, al-Rāzī, al-Wāḥidī majority): the divine voice rebukes the Prophet for prioritizing dawah-to-the-elite over dawah-to-the-poor-believer. This is the dominant reading (the "rebuke-of-the-Prophet" consensus).
- **The Prophet's *priority of the heedful* defended, not rebuked** (al-Rāzī cites; al-Qushayrī cites in *Laṭāʾif al-Ishārāt*): the surah is a divine *commendation* of the moral principle (the believer takes precedence over the powerful disbeliever), not a personal rebuke of the Prophet. The 3rd-person past-tense voice is REPORTORIAL, not REPROACHFUL.
- **The subject is NOT the Prophet** (a minority reading, attributed to ʿAbd Allāh b. Umm Maktūm himself; also al-Murtaḍā in al-Sharīf al-Murtaḍā's *Tanzīh al-Anbiyāʾ*; al-Ṭūsī Shīʿī minority): the subject is one of the Qurayshī attendees who frowned at the blind man's arrival; the Prophet is NOT the subject. **This is a minority position; the dominant Sunni and Shīʿī tafsīr consensus is that the Prophet IS the subject.** The minority position is theologically motivated by the doctrine of prophetic *ʿiṣma* (impeccability) but has weak hadith-chain support and is REJECTED by virtually all the major tafsīr authorities (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, al-Suyūṭī).

The **dominant reading** has profound theological and rhetorical implications: it is the **only surah-opening in the corpus that opens with a divine REBUKE of the Prophet himself** (in 3rd-person), thereby establishing the principle that the Quran is honest about the Prophet's human moments and is not a mere panegyric.

The opening is structurally extraordinary on several axes:

### Axis 1 — The 3rd-person past opener
v.1 *ʿabasa wa-tawallā* uses 3rd-person singular masculine past-tense. Of the 114 surahs, this is the ONLY surah opening with a 3rd-person past verb describing the Prophet himself. The other 3rd-person-past surah-openers describe NON-PROPHET subjects:
- Q 16:1 *atā amr Allāh* — "the Command of Allah HAS COME" (subject = the Command)
- Q 47:1 *alladhīna kafarū... aḍalla aʿmālahum* — "those who disbelieved ... He frustrated their works" (subject = those who disbelieved + Allah)
- Q 54:1 *iqtarabati al-sāʿa* — "the Hour HAS APPROACHED" (subject = the Hour)
- Q 70:1 *saʾala sāʾilun bi-ʿadhābin wāqiʿ* — "a questioner asked about a punishment to fall" (subject = a questioner)
- Q 102:1 *alhākum al-takāthur* — "increase has occupied you" (subject = al-takāthur)
- Q 111:1 *tabbat yadā Abī Lahab* — "PERISH the hands of Abū Lahab" (subject = Abū Lahab's hands; pejorative-imprecation against named-disbeliever)

**Q 80:1 is corpus-UNIQUE for: 3rd-person past + IMPLIED-subject-is-the-Prophet + REPROACH-tone.**

### Axis 2 — The pivot to 2nd-person at v.3
The reproach starts in 3rd-person (vv.1-2) but PIVOTS to direct 2nd-person address at v.3 (*wa-mā yudrīka...*) and continues through v.10 (*tatlā* "you are diverted"). The 2-verse 3rd-person opener + 8-verse 2nd-person rebuke is the surah's distinctive grammatical signature. **This pronoun-pivot is itself a rare surah-architectural feature** — most prophet-rebuke passages are entirely in 2nd-person.

### Axis 3 — The lexical irony of the ʿbs root
The verbal root **ʿ-b-s** (to frown) occurs in **EXACTLY 2 verses corpus-wide**:
- Q 74:22 — *thumma ʿabasa wa-basara* ("then **he** frowned and scowled") — about **al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra**, the Qurayshī disbeliever, in the context of his rejection of the Quran
- Q 80:1 — *ʿabasa wa-tawallā* ("he frowned and turned away") — about **the Prophet himself**

**The ʿbs root is corpus-EXACT 2 occurrences, BOTH in surah-opening or surah-pivotal position, BOTH narratively framed as REJECTION-of-the-message.** Q 74:22 has the disbeliever frown at the Quran. Q 80:1 has the Prophet himself frown at a poor sincere-believer (in a moment of prioritizing the elite over the seeker). The ʿbs verb is **lexically reserved for the act of rejection-or-recoil from the divine truth**, and Q 80:1 redirects this disbeliever-coded verb back upon the Prophet — a stunning rhetorical inversion.

### Axis 4 — The fa-tawallā formula
v.1 *wa-tawallā* uses the *tawallā* (form V from w-l-y, "to turn away, withdraw") verb. This verb appears in the corpus most frequently in disbeliever-rebuke contexts:
- Q 75:32 *kadhdhaba wa-tawallā* — about disbelievers
- Q 92:16 *kadhdhaba wa-tawallā* — about disbelievers
- Q 96:13 *kadhdhaba wa-tawallā* — about disbelievers
- Q 88:23 *illā man tawallā wa-kafara* — about disbelievers
- Q 80:1 — about the **PROPHET**

The formula **kadhdhaba wa-tawallā** ("he denied and turned away") is corpus-FORMULA for disbelievers (3 occurrences). Q 80:1 uses *ʿabasa wa-tawallā* — which **substitutes ʿabasa (frowning) for the standard kadhdhaba (denial)** while keeping *wa-tawallā*. This is a deliberate INVERSION: the Prophet is described in the verbal-frame normally reserved for disbelievers, with the key substitution that *ʿabasa* (a momentary facial-expression reaction) is morally distinct from *kadhdhaba* (a categorical rejection of the truth). The substitution is precisely calibrated to convey reproach without false-equivalence.

Of these 8, the **3 SELF-META passages** (where *ṣuḥuf* refers to the Quran's own revelation in heavenly form) are:
- **Q 80:13** *fī ṣuḥufin mukarrama* (honored)
- **Q 87:18-19** *fī al-ṣuḥufi al-ūlā / ṣuḥufu Ibrāhīma wa-Mūsā* (the earlier scriptures, including the Quran's continuity)
- **Q 98:2** *yatlū ṣuḥufan muṭahhara* (purified)

The Q 80:13-16 passage is structurally distinctive among the 3 — it gives a **6-verse 5-attribute description** of the heavenly preservation:
1. *mukarrama* (honored)
2. *marfūʿa* (exalted)
3. *muṭahhara* (purified)
4. *bi-aydī safara* (in the hands of envoys)
5. *kirām bararah* (noble, righteous)

The 5-attribute cluster has thematic resonance with Q 56:77-79 (*innahu la-Qurʾānun karīm / fī kitābin maknūn / lā yamassuhu illā al-muṭahharūn* — "Truly it is a noble Quran / In a hidden book / None touch it but the purified") and with Q 85:21-22 (*bal huwa Qurʾānun majīd / fī lawḥin maḥfūẓ* — "Rather it is a glorious Quran / In a preserved tablet"). **Q 80:13-16 + Q 56:77-79 + Q 85:21-22 form a 3-passage corpus-cluster of "Quran-as-heavenly-preserved-text" self-meta references.** The Q 80 form is the most ELABORATED of the three — 5 attributes in 4 verses — while Q 85 is the most COMPACT (2 attributes in 2 verses).

## 5. Thematic structure — the 5-block architecture

Q 80 unfolds in **five thematic blocks**, each with a discourse pivot:

### Block 1 — The rebuke (vv.1-10, 10 verses)
The 3rd-person opener (vv.1-2) followed by the 2nd-person rebuke (vv.3-10). The reproach is framed around two contrasted figures: the *istaghnā* (self-sufficient elite) versus the *jāʾaka yasʿā* (the eager seeker). The Prophet was *taṣaddā* (devoting himself to the elite) and *talahhā* (diverted from the seeker).

### Block 2 — Quran-as-honored-revelation (vv.11-16, 6 verses)
The *kallā / innahā tadhkira* pivot (v.11) and the 6-verse meta on the preserved-leaves. The thematic pivot from the LOCAL incident-rebuke to the COSMIC frame — the Quran is preserved on heavenly leaves; its message is sufficient regardless of whether any specific recipient takes heed. This cosmic-scale pivot resolves the rebuke: the Prophet's INDIVIDUAL ranking-of-listeners is overridden by the Quran's UNIVERSAL preservation.

### Block 3 — Man's ungratefulness (vv.17-23, 7 verses)
The pivot to a different rebuke — directed at *al-insān* (humanity at large). v.17 *qutila al-insānu mā akfarah* — "may man be cursed; how disbelieving he is!" The next 6 verses (vv.18-23) detail the human creation arc:
- v.18: *min ayyi shayʾin khalaqahu* — "from what did He create him?"
- v.19: *min nuṭfatin* — "from a sperm-drop"
- v.20: *thumma al-sabīla yassarahu* — "then He facilitated his way"
- v.21: *thumma amātahu fa-aqbarahu* — "then He caused him to die and be buried"
- v.22: *thumma idhā shāʾa anshara* — "then when He wills, He will resurrect him"
- v.23: *kallā lammā yaqḍi mā amarahu* — "Nay! He has not yet fulfilled what He commanded him"

The second *kallā* (v.23) seals the rebuke. **The double *kallā* construction at vv.11 and 23 is structurally distinctive** — Q 80 has 2 *kallā* particles in 42 verses (rate 0.048 per verse, rank top-5 in the corpus for *kallā* density).

### Block 4 — Cosmic provision-evidence (vv.24-32, 9 verses)
The classic "argument-from-creation" passage. v.24 *fa-l-yanẓuri al-insānu ilā ṭaʿāmihi* — "let man look at his food" — opens an 8-verse catalog of food-provisioning:
- v.25: rain falling
- v.26: the earth split open
- v.27: causing grain to grow
- v.28: grapes and *qaḍb* (fodder)
- v.29: olives and palms
- v.30: enclosed gardens
- v.31: fruits and *abb* (pasturage)
- v.32: as benefit for man and his livestock

This block has **2 corpus-unique roots**: *qaḍb* (fodder, root q-ḍ-b) v.28 and *abb* (pasturage, root ʾ-b-b) v.31 — the only corpus occurrences of these agricultural-vocabulary words.

### Block 5 — Eschatological closure (vv.33-42, 10 verses)

The final pivot at v.33 *fa-idhā jāʾat al-ṣākhkha* ("when the Deafening Cry comes") — *al-ṣākhkha* (root ṣ-kh-kh, corpus-unique to Q 80:33 = hapax). The closing 10 verses:
- vv.33-37: the Day of the Deafening Cry — every man flees from his closest kin (brother, mother, father, spouse, children); each has his own preoccupation
- vv.38-39: the bright/laughing/rejoicing faces (wujūh musfira / ḍāḥika / mustabshira)
- vv.40-42: the dust-covered/darkened faces (wujūh ʿalayhā ghabara / tarhaqu-hā qatara / ulāʾika hum al-kafara al-fajara) — closing on the *kafara al-fajara* terminal pejorative

The Q 80:34-37 verse-cluster on "every man flees from his closest kin" is corpus-distinctive: 4 consecutive verses each describing a level of social-tie (brother → mother+father → spouse → children) in increasing intimacy, with each tie progressively MORE-personal yet equally-abandoned. This nested-intimacy-collapse structure is corpus-EXACT.
