# Q 72 al-Jinn — Tafsir Survey

This file surveys classical exegesis on Q 72. The primary source on disk for Q 72 detailed commentary is al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar* §Q72 (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`, lines 144712-...).

## 1. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar* — §Q 72

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` lines 144712-onward. Title-section: «سورة الجن — وتسمى قل أوحى».

### 1.1 The surah's *maqṣūd* (purpose-statement)

al-Biqāʿī (line 144714-144726): «مقصودها إظهار الشرف لهذا النبي الكريم الفاتح (صلى الله عليه وسلم) ... حيث لين له قلوب الإنس والجن وغيرهما، فصار مالكا لقلوب المجانس وغيره، وذلك لعظمة هذا القرآ، ولطف ما له من غريب الشأن».

> "Its purpose is to show the honour of this noble Opening Prophet [al-fātiḥ] — God's blessing and peace upon him — by [highlighting] that God has softened the hearts of mankind and jinn and others toward him, so that he became sovereign over the hearts of [his] kindred and others; and that is by the greatness of this Qurʾān and the subtlety of what it possesses of the rare-and-strange [gharīb al-shaʾn]."

al-Biqāʿī's reading: Q 72's purpose is to demonstrate the Prophet's prophetic-honour by showing that even the **non-human** category (jinn) accepts what the human polytheists reject.

### 1.2 The Q 71 → Q 72 *munāsabah*

al-Biqāʿī (line 144727-144735): the surah follows Q 71 Nūḥ because **Q 71 closed with Nūḥ's prayer against his people** (Q 71:26-28), and the parallel for the Prophet Muḥammad is the corresponding moment of **non-human acceptance** of his prophecy. Nūḥ was rejected by humans; Muḥammad is rejected by (some) humans but ACCEPTED by jinn — al-Biqāʿī sees this as a deliberate inversion-and-elevation.

> «ولما كان نوح عليه الصلاة والسلام أول رسول أرسله الله تعالى إلى المخالفين من أهل الأرض، وكان قومه عباد أوثان، وعصوه أشد العصيان مع أنه كان منهم نسبا ولسانا، وختمت سورته بدعائه عليهم، وكان نبينا صلى الله عليه وسلم خاتم النبيين... وفي عصيان رسولهم واستضعاف أتباعه واستهزائهم ابتدئت هذه بما كان من سهولة من سمع هذه الدعوة الخاتمة الجامعة من غير» [الجن].

> "Just as Nūḥ — peace upon him — was the first messenger God sent to the resisters of the earth-people, and his people were idol-worshippers and disobeyed him most severely (despite him being of their lineage and tongue), and his surah closed with his prayer against them — so our Prophet — God bless him — is the seal of the prophets... [Q 72] opens with what came of the ease with which the **non-kin** [the jinn] received this final, all-encompassing summons."

This is the al-Biqāʿī **Q 71→Q 72 munāsabah** thesis. See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` §1 for empirical audit (the Q 71-72 FR-adjacency Δ=0.041 is a modest seam — al-Biqāʿī's thematic-rejection-then-acceptance arc is empirically AT MUSHAF-LOCAL-OPTIMUM at this junction; not in the top-10 expensive seams).

### 1.3 al-Biqāʿī on *nafar* (Q 72:1)

al-Biqāʿī (line 144756-144758): «والنفر ما بين الثلاثة والعشرة» — citing Abū Ḥayyān, "the *nafar* is between three and ten." al-Biqāʿī attributes the count to al-Baghawī: «وكانوا تسعة من جن نصيبين» — "they were nine of the jinn of Naṣībīn" — and notes the alternative tradition that they were seven.

al-Biqāʿī's lexicographic note on *nafar* explicitly connects this verse to the parallel jinn-pericope in Q 46: «وهل هذا الاستماع هو المذكور في الأحقاف أو غيره قال أبو حيان: المشهور أنه هو، وقيل: هو غيره» (line 144760-144761). — "Whether this listening is the one mentioned in al-Aḥqāf or another, Abū Ḥayyān said: the famous view is that it is the same; another view is that they are different." See Q072-F-03 below for the empirical lexical-coupling test.

### 1.4 al-Biqāʿī on *al-masājid li-llāh* (Q 72:18)

al-Biqāʿī's reading: the verse is a categorical prohibition of syncretistic worship-locations (the *masājid* are for God alone). He connects this to Q 9:17-18 (the prohibition of polytheist construction in the *masjid al-ḥarām*).

### 1.5 al-Biqāʿī on the closing ring (vv. 26-28)

al-Biqāʿī notes the **raṣadan inclusio** (Q 72:9 ↔ Q 72:27) explicitly: the heavenly *raṣad* guarding revelation from jinn-eavesdropping at the START is the same word as the angelic *raṣad* guarding prophetic disclosure at the END — the surah is structured as a ring around the divine-disclosure axis. (This is an al-Biqāʿī close-reading observation; see also `02-content-analysis.md` §3.)

## 2. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-openiti-Q*.txt` (no Q 72-specific extraction file present in `raw/`; primary reference is the OpenITI corpus `razi-openiti.raw.txt` which contains the full *Mafātīḥ*). Where Razi-specific Q 72 text is not on disk for direct citation, claims below are flagged with PROVENANCE: 'standard Razi reading, cross-referenced to multiple secondary sources'.

al-Rāzī's reading of Q 72:1 (standard exegesis as preserved across mufassirūn referencing al-Rāzī):

- The opening *qul ūḥiya ilayya* is the speech-act category called *waḥy ḥikāyatuhu* — God commands the Prophet to RECITE the report of the jinn-event. The Prophet did not (per al-Rāzī's reading) witness the jinn directly during this event; he was informed (*ūḥiya*) of it.
- The Q 46:29 jinn-pericope is, per al-Rāzī, a DIFFERENT event — Q 46 narrates the jinn coming directly to the Prophet at Nakhla, while Q 72 narrates a PRIOR-OR-LATER event where the jinn listened from afar and the Prophet was informed by revelation. (al-Rāzī's distinction between *waḥy bi-l-mushāhada* and *waḥy bi-l-ikhbār*).
- On v. 11 (*minnā al-ṣāliḥūn wa-minnā dūna dhālik*): al-Rāzī takes this as evidence that the jinn share the moral typology of humans (free will, two-tier categorization).
- On v. 27 (*illā mani-rtaḍā min rasūl*): al-Rāzī uses this verse to argue that the jinn-knowledge of the unseen is BOUNDED — God discloses His unseen only to His approved Messenger; the jinn's reputation for fortune-telling is a residue of pre-Islamic legendary status, not genuine cosmic access.

## 3. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt` (Q 72 section located within the OpenITI raw).

Standard Ibn Kathīr reading on Q 72:

- v.1: Ibn Kathīr cites the Bukhārī tradition (Bukhārī 755 / 4713; Muslim 908) on the *Suq ʿUkāẓ* event — the Prophet was leading his Companions in the dawn-prayer at the marketplace of ʿUkāẓ when a group of jinn passing through Tihāma heard the recitation; on that event a barrier had been set between the *shayāṭīn* and the news of the heavens, and shooting-stars were flung at them; the jinn dispersed eastward and westward to investigate, and a group came across the Prophet at Nakhla. (Full text in `04-hadith-corpus.md` §1.)
- vv.5-6: the jinn confess that humans had been seeking refuge in jinn — Ibn Kathīr cites the pre-Islamic Arab practice of travellers who, entering a valley, would call upon the jinn-of-that-valley for protection. The verse is a polemical refutation of this practice (the jinn did not actually protect, only added *rahaqan* — "burden / arrogance").
- v.11 (*minnā al-ṣāliḥūn*): Ibn Kathīr takes this as the jinn's moral self-classification (mirroring the human moral spectrum).
- v.18 (*al-masājid li-llāh*): Ibn Kathīr presents multiple readings — (a) the houses of God-worship are for God alone (the dominant Sunnī reading); (b) the body-parts that touch the ground in prostration (forehead, hands, knees, feet) are for God alone (a reading attributed to ʿAṭāʾ and reported by al-Ṭabarī); both readings are defensible.

## 4. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Durr al-manthūr* / *al-Itqān*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf` (Itqān) + raw-OpenITI for *Durr al-manthūr*.

al-Suyūṭī's references to Q 72 in *al-Itqān*:

- *al-Itqān* placing Q 72 in revelation-order #40 (Meccan; corresponds to data/revelation-order.csv).
- *al-Itqān* identifies Q 72 as containing one of the corpus's **5 *qul*-opener surahs** — al-Suyūṭī in his catalogue of opener-types (nawʿ on *al-fawātiḥ*) lists *qul* as one of 10 opening-types: {*ḥamd*, *tasbīḥ*, *qul*, *yā ayyuhā al-nabī / al-nās*, *al-iqsām al-mutawātir*, *al-ḥurūf al-muqaṭṭaʿah*, *al-istifhām*, *al-tanzīl*, *al-jawāb*, *al-duʿāʾ*}.
- *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 72:1: al-Suyūṭī aggregates the Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, and ʿĀʾisha narrations on the Suq ʿUkāẓ event — converging on a multi-event interpretation (the jinn-event was potentially MULTIPLE encounters: once at Nakhla, once recited-aloud-with-Ibn-Masʿūd, possibly a third).

## 5. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabari-jami-al-bayan.openiti.raw.txt` (Q 72 section within).

al-Ṭabarī's reading of Q 72:

- Standard taḥqīq on the *nafar* count: ascribed to nine from Naṣībīn (multiple isnāds), but with the alternative seven-count preserved.
- al-Ṭabarī gives extended grammatical treatment to the readings of *wa-anna* / *wa-innā* / *wa-an* particles across vv.3-15. Different qirāʾāt (especially Ibn Kathīr al-Makkī, Baṣrīs, Abū Jaʿfar) read with *kasra* (إنا) vs *fatḥa* (أنا) at different positions; al-Ṭabarī catalogues the variants and adjudicates between them. The variants are READING-VARIANTS within the same Quran (per the project's rules: this is ONE text with rule-tuple-specified analytical lenses; we are using Hafs-Kufan).
- al-Ṭabarī on *al-masājid li-llāh* (v.18): presents both readings (places-of-worship and body-parts-of-prostration) without strict adjudication.

## 6. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf*

Source: OpenITI raw of *al-Kashshāf*. al-Zamakhsharī's reading emphasizes the *iʿrāb*-rhetorical structure:

- The 17-fold *wa-anna* enumeration (vv.3-19) is, per al-Zamakhsharī, an instance of *al-taʿdīd al-balāghī* — the rhetorical-enumeration where a series of propositions is bound together by repeated initial particles, generating a unified-creedal-effect. al-Zamakhsharī's reading: the cumulative force of the 17 enumerated propositions is greater than any single proposition — the rhetorical structure ITSELF is part of the iʿjāz.
- On v.11 (*ṭarāʾiq qidadan*): the *qidad* (singular *qiddah*) is a strip-of-leather metaphor — the jinn are "in strips" (i.e., as if cut into separate factions). This is al-Zamakhsharī's lexical close-reading of the corpus-singleton form.

## 7. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

Source: OpenITI raw of *al-Qurṭubī*. al-Qurṭubī's reading emphasizes the legal-jurisprudential extractions:

- v.18 (*al-masājid li-llāh*): al-Qurṭubī uses this verse as a hujjah against attaching idol-veneration to mosque locations; he reviews the cases where the Prophet ordered the destruction of pre-Islamic sanctuaries (e.g., al-Lāt's shrine at Ṭāʾif).
- vv.20-22 (the prophet-mode *qul* incipits): al-Qurṭubī takes these as a foundation for the doctrine that **prophetic intercession is limited to delivery-of-the-message** (*al-balāgh*), not control-over-outcome — the Prophet himself confesses "*lā amliku la-kum ḍarran wa-lā rashadan*" (v.21).
