## 2. Note on faḍāʾil literature for Q 33

The opening of every tafsir's Q 33 chapter conventionally records the surah's *faḍāʾil* (merits) ḥadīth-tradition. Q 33 is striking in that the **Sunni faḍāʾil literature is unusually thin**:

- **al-Bukhārī, *Kitāb Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān***: contains *bābs* dedicated to Q 1, Q 2, Q 18, Q 36 (al-Kahf in some recensions), Q 67, Q 109-114. **No bāb on Q 33**. The Q 33-related material in Bukhārī appears in *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* (commentary on specific verses) and *Kitāb al-Maghāzī* (Battle of the Trench narrative) — never as a whole-surah faḍīla.
- **Muslim's *Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān***: similar pattern. No whole-surah Q 33 faḍīla.
- **al-Tirmidhī**: same.
- **al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān***: lists Q 33 in standard chronologies (4th surah revealed at Madina, after al-Anfāl and Āl ʿImrān per Bukhārī's recension; Itqān nawʿ 7) but provides no whole-surah merit ḥadīth (`suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt` lines 679, 701, 736, 743, 752, 1655 — all are list-mentions only).

**The single Sunni-attributed faḍīla** — narrated by Ubayy b. Kaʿb marfūʿan: *"Whoever recites Sūrat al-Aḥzāb and teaches it to his family and slaves is granted security from the punishment of the grave"* — is preserved primarily through:

- al-Thaʿlabī, *al-Kashf wa-al-bayān*, line 69274: full isnād (Zayd b. Aslam ← his father ← Abū Umāma ← Ubayy b. Kaʿb).
- al-Ṭabarsī, *Majmaʿ al-bayān*, line 91728: same matn, same Ubayy attribution.

Hadith-critical evaluation: this is a single-chain, weak-isnād *fadāʾil al-suwar* tradition of the type that fills the openings of pre-modern tafsirs but **does not rise to the level of canonical *ṣaḥīḥ* status**. Ibn Kathīr's tafsir of Q 33 (line 95027) opens **not** with this faḍīla but with the al-Aḥzāb-was-once-Q2-length ḥadīth of Ubayy via Zirr (Aḥmad, al-Nasāʾī; declared "isnād ḥasan" by Ibn Kathīr) — which is a *historical-textual* note, not a devotional one.

The Imāmī Shīʿī tradition adds a second faḍīla isnād: ʿAbd Allāh b. Sinān ← Imam Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq (al-Ṭabarsī, line 91730): *"Whoever frequently recites Sūrat al-Aḥzāb will be on the Day of Resurrection in the company of Muḥammad and his family and his wives."* This is uniquely Shīʿī and reflects the Imāmī doctrinal centrality of Q 33:33 (*āyat al-taṭhīr*).

**This Sunni-Shīʿī asymmetry, paired with the empirical UAS = 9.36 / hadith-emphasis-rubric = 2 hidden-architecture finding (H-NEW-860), is itself a Q 33-specific phenomenon and is the single most useful classical-empirical fact this surah surfaces.** See §7 below.

## 3. Q 33:1 opening — *Yā ayyuha al-Nabī ittaqi Allāh*

### 3.1 Asbāb al-nuzūl (cluster, all 7 mufassirūn agree on the basic narrative)

The dominant *sabab al-nuzūl* (occasion of revelation) tradition: **Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb, ʿIkrima b. Abī Jahl, Abū al-Aʿwar al-Sulamī** (Meccan envoys), accompanied by **ʿAbd Allāh b. Ubayy** (chief of the Madinan munāfiqūn), **ʿAbd Allāh b. Saʿd b. Abī Sarḥ**, and **Ṭuʿma b. Ubayriq**, came to the Prophet under amān (granted protection) after the Battle of Uḥud, demanding: "Cease mentioning our gods Lāt, ʿUzzā, Manāt; say they intercede; we will leave you and your god alone." This grieved the Prophet; ʿUmar requested permission to kill them; the Prophet refused (citing the granted amān) and ordered them expelled from Madina. Q 33:1-2-3 was then revealed — *not to obey the disbelievers and the hypocrites*.

- **al-Ṭabarī**, line 278737-278743: gives the basic exhortation reading; cross-references the *kāfirīn* / *munāfiqīn* binary directly.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**, line 49850-49862: gives the fullest narrative version, naming all six envoys.
- **al-Ṭabarsī**, line 91761-91771: identical narrative; also adds a parallel tradition about Thaqīf delegates who asked for one year of continued Lāt-and-ʿUzzā worship.
- **al-Thaʿlabī**, line 69289-69299: same.
- **al-Suyūṭī (Durr)**, line 115664-115668: adds a parallel from al-Ḍaḥḥāk via Jūwaybir naming **al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra** and **Shayba b. Rabīʿa** as the Meccan side.

### 3.2 Why the vocative is *yā ayyuha al-Nabī*, not *yā Muḥammad*?

This is **al-Zamakhsharī's** distinctive contribution (line 49836-49846), and it is one of the most-cited classical observations on Q 33:

> *"He made the vocative [to him] in 'Yā ayyuha al-Nabī ittaqi Allāh' (Q 33:1), 'Yā ayyuha al-Nabī limā tuḥarrim' (Q 66:1), 'Yā ayyuha al-Rasūl balligh mā unzila ilayka' (Q 5:67) — and abandoned addressing him by his name, just as He said 'Yā Ādam', 'Yā Mūsā', 'Yā ʿĪsā', 'Yā Dāwūd' — as honoring him and ennobling, raising his rank and exalting his merit."*

**al-Biqāʿī** (line 99917-99950) extends this with a **rule he quotes from Ibn al-Zubayr's *Burhān fī tartīb suwar al-Qurʾān***:

> *"Wherever the address is purely praise without command or prohibition, He uses the more-laudatory description (e.g., Q 48:29 'Muḥammad rasūl Allāh'). Wherever there is command or prohibition, He generally shifts to the more general descriptor 'al-Nabī' (Q 33:1, Q 8:65, Q 65:1, Q 66:1, Q 60:12, Q 9:73 'Yā ayyuha al-Nabī jāhid al-kuffār wa-al-munāfiqīn')."*

This rule explains the corpus-wide distribution and is independently consistent with our empirical finding that **Q 33's opening is one of only ~3 surahs opening with direct vocative *Yā ayyuha al-Nabī* (Q 33, Q 65, Q 66)** — a pattern al-Biqāʿī correlates with Madinan-legal command-load.

- **al-Rāzī**, line 198182-198197, recasts this as the structural principle of the whole surah: *"the surah's foundation and basis is the *taʾdīb* (formative-disciplining) of the Prophet — his relation with God (taqwā), his relation with his household (vv. 28-34), and his relation with the general community (vv. 45-46)."* This reading is doctrinally important: Q 33 is *the Prophet's deportment surah*.

## 4. Q 33:4-8 + Q 33:36-40 — Zayd, Zaynab, and the legal abolition of *tabannī*

### 4.1 The legal-legislative thrust (all 7 mufassirūn agree)

Q 33:4 (no two hearts; *ẓihār* is not motherhood; adopted sons are not biological sons) and Q 33:5 (call them by their fathers' names) constitute, in classical reading, the **abrogation of pre-Islamic *tabannī*** (legal adoption with full filial-rights consequences). The case-in-point that triggered the verse is **Zayd b. Ḥāritha** — adopted son of the Prophet, formerly called *Zayd b. Muḥammad*.

- **Ibn Kathīr**, line 95049-95065, opens with a *ḥissī*/concrete-then-meaningful ordering: God places three concrete impossibilities (one body, two hearts; wife by *ẓihār*, not mother; adopted, not biological son) before the legal point, to *taw̱īʾā* (pre-condition the audience).
- **al-Bukhārī (#4781-4782, *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* 33; quoted by Ibn Kathīr line 95088)** records ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿUmar: *"We never used to call him anything but Zayd b. Muḥammad, until the verse 'udʿūhum li-ābāʾihim' was revealed."* This single ḥadīth is the textual hinge of the entire abolition.
- **al-Ṭabarī**, line 278813-278822, treats Q 33:4 as *both* (a) refutation of the hypocrites who said the Prophet had two hearts (one with you, one with us), and (b) the metaphor for Zayd-as-not-biological-son, calling either reading *"both possible"*.
- **al-Qurṭubī** at Q 33:5 (line 126763) treats this primarily as a *fiqhī* abrogation: maintaining the lineage rule even in case of error (*aṭāʾ* clause), with extensive discussion of the legal consequences for inheritance and marriage prohibition.

### 4.2 Q 33:36-40 — the Zayd-Zaynab marriage and its reform

- **al-Zamakhsharī**, line 49886-49889, gives the canonical biographical: *"This is a parable God struck for Zayd b. Ḥāritha. He was a man of the Kalb tribe taken captive as a child. The Arabs in Jāhiliyya raided and took captives. He was bought by Ḥakīm b. Ḥizām for his aunt Khadīja; when the Prophet married her she gifted him Zayd. Zayd's father and uncle came seeking him, and the Prophet gave him the choice — Zayd chose the Prophet. He freed him. They [Arabs] used to call him Zayd b. Muḥammad."*
- **All seven mufassirūn** treat Q 33:37 — *"And when you said to him to whom God had shown favor and to whom you had shown favor: 'Keep your wife to yourself…' But you concealed in your soul what God was about to reveal, and you feared men whereas God has more right that you fear Him"* — as one of the *most theologically demanding verses in the Qurʾān*. The classical exegetical strategy is to **drastically restrict** *what* the Prophet "concealed":
  - The dominant Sunni reading (Ibn Kathīr line 95056-95063, citing al-Bukhārī, al-Ṭabarī): the concealment was knowledge that God *would* reform the *muslim-tabannī* law, hence that the Prophet *would* eventually be commanded to marry his former adopted son's divorced wife. The Prophet feared people's reaction (*"What will they say?"*) and concealed only that knowledge.
  - **al-Zamakhsharī**, embedded in a longer balāgha gloss, accepts the same restricted reading.
  - **al-Rāzī** in line 198174 ff. is even more aggressive in defending the Prophet's *ʿiṣma* (impeccability), arguing that the marriage to Zaynab was instrumental in *completing* the legal corpus (*tatmīm li-l-sharʿ*) — completing the abolition of *tabannī* by giving it experiential force.
  - **al-Qurṭubī** discusses fiqhī side-issues (the post-mortem status of the Prophet's wives; the question of whether anyone may remarry a wife divorced by the Prophet during his lifetime) but does not depart from the dominant reading.
  - **al-Biqāʿī** takes the surah-coherence reading: the entire Q 33 is in the service of *tanzīh* (purification of standing) — of the Prophet, of his wives, of his household — and Q 33:37 is the rhetorical center of that *tanzīh*.

### 4.3 Q 33:40 — *Khātam al-nabiyyīn*

This is the corpus's single most doctrinally-charged five-word phrase. Let me record each mufassir's position:

- **al-Ṭabarī** (line 281443 ff.) gives the foundational reading: *"Muḥammad is not the father of any of your men [in the literal sense, hence Zaynab is not his daughter-in-law and was lawful for him to marry] but the Messenger of God, and the *khātam* of the Prophets — the one who **sealed** prophethood, so that it is **stamped** [over], not opened to anyone after him until the Hour rises."* He records the qirāʾāt: Hafs/ʿĀṣim reads *khātam* (the form: noun = *the seal/the one sealed*); the rest (and Ibn Masʿūd's variant *"wa-lakin nabiyyan khatama al-nabiyyīn"* — "but [a] prophet who sealed the prophets") favor *khātim* (the agent: *the one who seals*). **Both readings entail the same doctrinal content: no prophet after.**
- **al-Zamakhsharī** (line 50552-50568): adds the rabbinic-style elaboration: *"if he had had a [surviving] son who reached manhood, that son would have been a prophet, and Muḥammad would not have been the seal of the prophets — as it is reported he said about Ibrāhīm [his infant son] when he died: 'Had he lived, he would have been a prophet.'"* He then preempts the obvious objection: *"How then is he the last of the prophets when ʿĪsā will descend at the end of time? — His being the *last* means **no one is given prophethood after him**; ʿĪsā was made prophet *before* him, and when he descends he descends as a follower of Muḥammad's law (*ʿāmilan ʿalā sharīʿat Muḥammad, muṣalliyan ilā qiblatihi, ka-annahu baʿḍu ummatihi*)."*
- **al-Qurṭubī** (line 128532-128566): records the qirāʾāt (Hafs/ʿĀṣim *khātam* with *fatḥa*; jumhūr *khātim* with *kasra*; the meanings overlap as *ṭābiʿ* — the seal-stamp); gives Ibn ʿAṭiyya's verdict: *"these phrasings, in the unanimous view of the umma's scholars, predecessors and successors, are received in their full general sense, mandating the textual import that there is no prophet after him, ṣallā Allāh ʿalayhi wa-sallam"*. He *explicitly attacks* al-Ghazālī's *al-Iqtiṣād fī al-iʿtiqād* for entertaining hermeneutic possibilities on this verse, calling it *"ilḥād (heresy) in my view, and a foul gateway to disturbing the doctrine of Muslims regarding Muḥammad's sealing of prophethood — guard against it!"* This is unusually severe rhetoric within the Sunni tradition.
- **al-Rāzī** (line 198170-198178): gives a strikingly *philosophical-functional* reading: *"the prophet who has another prophet after him, if he leaves anything of his counsel and exposition incomplete, the one who comes after him completes it; whereas one who has no prophet after him is more compassionate to his community and better-guiding — like a father who has no other child but this one"*. Rāzī thus turns *khātam al-nabiyyīn* into a structural argument for the **completeness and superior pastoral pressure** of Muḥammad's mission.
- **Ibn Kathīr** (line 96677-96772) is the most extensive: gives **eight independent ḥadīth chains** (Aḥmad ← al-Aʿmash ← Anas, Aḥmad ← Hammām ← Abū Hurayra, the famous *libna* "brick" parable from Bukhārī/Muslim, the prophet's six privileges including *khutima bī al-nabiyyūn*, the seven names self-description from al-Zuhrī ← Jubayr b. Muṭʿim including *al-ʿĀqib alladhī laysa baʿdahu nabī*, and others). He then deploys the doctrine pastorally: *"so anyone who claims this rank after him is a kadhdhāb / dajjāl / mūdal mudill — even if he produces wonders and amulets and tricks (siḥr wa-ṭalāsim wa-nayrajiyyāt) — God allows them to appear to deceive the deceivable. This is from God's complete kindness to His creation, that liars in their actions and words are evident in their absurdity, as occurred with **al-Aswad al-ʿAnsī of Yemen** and **Musaylima al-Kadhdhāb of Yamāma**."* This is the doctrinal hinge that makes *khātam al-nabiyyīn* a polemic against subsequent prophet-claimants.
- **al-Biqāʿī** (line 99875 + sequence): integrates Q 33:40 into the *tanzīh* of the whole surah — the seal-of-prophethood is part of the *tanzīh* of Muḥammad's status (*tanzīh ʿan an yakūn lahu min al-rijāl wa-al-bashar mukhālif li-mā waṣafahu Allāh*).

### 4.4 Cross-tashkeel verbatim of Q 33:40 — from disk

```
no-tashkeel    : ما كان محمد أبا أحد من رجالكم ولكن رسول الله وخاتم النبيين ۗ وكان الله بكل شيء عليما
min-tashkeel   : ما كانَ مُحَمَّدٌ أَبا أَحَدٍ مِن رِجالِكُم وَلٰكِن رَسولَ اللَّهِ وَخاتَمَ النَّبِيّۦنَ ۗ وَكانَ اللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَيءٍ عَليمًا
full-tashkeel  : مَّا كَانَ مُحَمَّدٌ أَبَآ أَحَدٍ مِّن رِّجَالِكُمۡ وَلَٰكِن رَّسُولَ ٱللَّهِ وَخَاتَمَ ٱلنَّبِيِّـۧنَۗ وَكَانَ ٱللَّهُ بِكُلِّ شَيۡءٍ عَلِيمٗا
transliteration: Ma kana muhammadun aba ahadin min rijalikum walakin rasoola Allahi wakhatama alnnabiyyeena wakana Allahu bikulli shayin AAaleeman
```

**Cross-validation**: stripping all combining marks from min-tashkeel and full-tashkeel yields a consonantal skeleton **identical in length** (82 chars after pause-mark removal) and **byte-identical to the no-tashkeel form**, modulo two well-known orthographic equivalences:

- min-tashkeel uses `ۦ` (U+06E6, "Arabic small high yāʾ") inside *النبيۦن* where no-tashkeel uses standard `ي`.
- full-tashkeel uses `ٱ` (U+0671, *alif waṣla*) where no-tashkeel uses bare `ا`.

These are normalization-class differences only; the **consonantal text** of Q 33:40 is perfectly stable across all three Arabic variants.

**Qirāʾāt note**: the variant *khātim al-nabiyyīn* (kasra under *t*, agentive: *"the one who seals the prophets"*) vs *khātam al-nabiyyīn* (fatḥa, the seal-stamp itself / passive object) is preserved in the *no-tashkeel* form (which writes only the consonantal skeleton). Reading aloud requires choice of vowelization. The Hafs-Kufan transmission used in this corpus reads *khātam* (fatḥa); the Ibn Masʿūd ʿuthmānic-pre-codex variant reads *"wa-lakin nabiyyan khatama al-nabiyyīn"* (both Ṭabarī and Qurṭubī cite this). Both readings yield the same doctrinal entailment.

## 5. Q 33:9-27 — al-Aḥzāb / Battle of the Trench narrative

### 5.1 The historical setting (5/626 CE, the *ghazwa al-Khandaq*)

- **Ibn Kathīr** (line 95300+) gives the canonical sīra integration: the confederate force (Quraysh + Ghaṭafān + Banū Naḍīr remnants from Khaybar) besieged Madina; Salmān al-Fārisī's trench-suggestion; the cold/wind that broke the siege; the betrayal-then-execution of Banū Qurayẓa. He cites **al-Bukhārī (Bk 64, *Kitāb al-Maghāzī* 30; #4117 ff.)** and **Muslim** as direct ḥadīth-witness chains.
- **al-Ṭabarī** (line 278889+) treats the verses sequentially with independent isnāds on each (*idh jāʾūkum min fawqikum…* / *…wa-balaghat al-qulūbu al-ḥanājir wa-taẓunnūna bi-llāh al-ẓunūnā*).
- **al-Zamakhsharī** (line 50033+) emphasizes the *balāgha*: the alif-monorhyme (*al-ḥanājir / al-ẓunūnā / shadīdā / kāfūrā*) at this verse-block is rhetorically integrated into the **trembling-of-fear depiction** — the long *-ā* clausulae *enact* the breath-held tension. (This is the closest classical source to a phonological-affect reading consonant with our §3.4 monorhyme-register hypothesis.)

### 5.2 vv. 12-13 — the *munāfiqūn* rhetoric

- All mufassirūn agree on the literal target: hypocrites (Muʿattib b. Qushayr, Ḥadd b. Qays, Aws b. Qayẓī) saying *"Muḥammad promised us the treasures of Caesar and Khosrow, and now one of us cannot even relieve himself in safety"* — and others fleeing the trench under the pretext *"Our houses are exposed"* (*"inna buyūtanā ʿawra"* — Q 33:13, the verse uses precisely this Madinan-domestic image).
- **al-Biqāʿī** treats vv. 9-27 as the **structural pivot** between the opening *yā ayyuha al-Nabī* taqwā exhortation and the wives-code: the historical-narrative center proves that **Allāh's *hifẓ*** is what protects the household and the community alike.

### 5.3 vv. 26-27 — Banū Qurayẓa

- **al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr**: all cite al-Bukhārī (Bk 64 ch 30) on Saʿd b. Muʿādh's verdict (*akma yuqtal*); al-Qurṭubī adds extensive juristic qualification.
- This is one of the **historically heaviest** Q 33 verse-clusters, but the *empirical* importance for our project is that **Q 33's verses 9-27 contribute the bulk of its anomalous content-distance signature** — narrative-historical content that does *not* match the surrounding Meccan eschatological surahs Q 32 and Q 34.

## 6. Q 33:28-34 — The wives-of-the-Prophet code

### 6.1 The *takhyīr* (Q 33:28-29) — the Choice

The seven mufassirūn give a converging asbāb account: the wives complained about insufficient maintenance / asked for additional comforts; Q 33:28 commanded the Prophet to give them a choice between *al-dunyā wa-zīnatahā* (worldly life and its adornment, with honorable release) and *Allāh wa-rasūluhu wa-al-dār al-ākhira* (God, His Messenger, and the Hereafter). All wives chose the latter.

- **al-Zamakhsharī** (line 50263+) and **al-Rāzī** treat this as the doctrinal foundation of the wives' status as *Ummahāt al-Muʾminīn* (Mothers of the Believers, Q 33:6).
- **al-Bukhārī, *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* 33; Muslim, *Kitāb al-Ṭalāq*** preserve the ḥadīth in which ʿĀʾisha narrates the Choice.

### 6.2 vv. 30-31 — *al-faḥishatu mubīna*

- **al-Qurṭubī** (line 128009+) extends his fiqhī treatment, discussing the doubled-and-halved punishment-and-reward calculus that applies *exclusively* to the Prophet's wives because of their elevated status.

### 6.3 v. 32 — *yā nisāʾ al-Nabī lastunna ka-aḥadin min al-nisāʾ in ittaqaytunna*

- **al-Ṭabarī**, **al-Zamakhsharī**, **Ibn Kathīr**, **al-Qurṭubī**: all converge on the reading that this verse, including the famous *"do not soften the speech, lest one in whose heart is sickness covet"*, is *not* a prohibition on the wives speaking but on the **manner** of speech.
- This verse is the *direct textual antecedent* of the ḥijāb verses (vv. 53, 59) — it sets up the deportment-frame within which the physical-veil rule will land.

### 6.4 v. 33 — *innamā yurīdu Allāh li-yudhhiba ʿankum al-rijsa Ahla al-Bayt wa-yuṭahhirakum taṭhīrā*

This is the **single most doctrinally-divisive verse in Q 33** between Sunni and Shīʿī tafsīr.

- **Sunni reading** (Ibn Kathīr, al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī): *Ahl al-Bayt* in this verse refers **primarily** to the Prophet's wives (the contextual subject of vv. 28-34 throughout). The "Cloak ḥadīth" (al-Tirmidhī #3205, #3787, #3871; Muslim #2424) — in which the Prophet wraps Fāṭima, ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥusayn in a cloak and says "*Allāhumma hāʾulāʾi Ahli baytī*" — is acknowledged as **ṣaḥīḥ** but read as **inclusive expansion**, not exclusive restriction.
- **Shīʿī reading** (al-Ṭabarsī, *Majmaʿ al-bayān*): *Ahl al-Bayt* refers **exclusively** to the Five (Muḥammad, Fāṭima, ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥusayn) — al-khamsa al-aṭhār — and the verse establishes their *ʿiṣma* (impeccability). The fact that the verse mid-verse *shifts pronoun* from feminine plural (*tabarrajna, aqimna al-ṣalāta…*) to **masculine plural** (*ʿan-kum al-rijsa*) is read as *grammatically* signaling that the *taṭhīr* clause refers to a distinct, masculine-inclusive group.
- The empirical question — does the pronoun shift mid-verse change reference? — is *grammatically* genuine and the reading-tradition split is substantive. This is the doctrinal stake behind the empirical Sunni/Shīʿī faḍāʾil asymmetry.

## 7. Why classical *fadāʾil* is relatively silent on Q 33

Synthesizing the seven tafsirs and our empirical finding (UAS = 9.36, hadith-emphasis = 2):

1. **Doctrinal controversy density**. Q 33 contains the corpus's three most theologically-charged single phrases:
   - *khātam al-nabiyyīn* (Q 33:40) — bears the entire Sunni/Aḥmadī dispute.
   - *innamā yurīdu Allāh li-yudhhiba ʿankum al-rijs* (Q 33:33) — bears the Sunni/Shīʿī dispute on imāmate / ʿiṣma.
   - *fa-lammā qaḍā Zaydun minhā waṭaran* + *wa-tukhfī fī nafsika mā Allāhu mubdīhi wa-takhshā al-nāsa wa-Allāhu aḥaqqu an takhshāhu* (Q 33:37) — bears the medieval-modern critique of the Prophet's *ʿiṣma*.

   Pre-modern Sunni faḍāʾil literature, designed for **mass devotional liturgical use**, naturally selects surahs whose doctrinal consensus is firm (Q 1, Q 2, Q 36, Q 67, Q 112). Q 33 is *avoided* in this layer not because it is unimportant — Bukhārī cites Q 33 verses 104 times in his Ṣaḥīḥ — but because **whole-surah devotional packaging would expose the dispute zones**.

2. **Legal-narrative-specific content domain**. The *fadāʾil al-suwar* genre privileges surahs with **trans-historical doctrinal generality** (tawḥīd, tawakkul, eschatology). Q 33's content domain is **historically Madinan-specific**: a particular battle (the Trench), particular wives (specific named individuals), a specific adoption-and-marriage case (Zayd-Zaynab), specific ritual rules for a specific household. This *anchors* Q 33 in concrete Madinan history and dampens its devotional generalizability.

3. **The *qaṣīda*-shaped surah is a poet's-style surah**. The 99 % alif-monorhyme (§3 of `01-empirical-profile.md`) gives Q 33 a phonological texture that maps *onto* pre-Islamic poetry — exactly the genre the early Muslim community was at pains to distinguish the Qurʾān *from* (cf. Q 36:69, *wa-mā ʿallamnāhu al-shiʿr…*; Q 26:224-227 anti-poet polemic). A surah that looks pre-Islamic-poetic in form but is doctrinally hyper-loaded is *not the kind of surah* the early *fadāʾil* compilers chose to spotlight.

4. **The Imāmī Shīʿī tradition does preserve a Q 33 faḍīla** (al-Ṭabarsī 91730: from Imam al-Ṣādiq, "the company of Muḥammad and his family on the Day of Resurrection") — and this is consistent with Shīʿī doctrinal centrality of Q 33:33. The asymmetry between Sunni faḍāʾil-silence and Shīʿī faḍāʾil-presence on Q 33 is itself a confessional-doctrinal correlate of the empirical finding.

5. **Architectural significance ≠ liturgical visibility**. The empirical UAS = 9.36 / hadith = 2 finding (H-NEW-860) has a clean classical-source explanation: the surah's architectural significance derives from its **content anomaly + monorhyme uniformity + cost-residual peak**, all of which are *structural* properties; classical *fadāʾil* selects on *devotional accessibility*, which is orthogonal. The Sunni tradition's relative silence is **not error or oversight** — it is *appropriately matched to the architectural-functional profile of the surah*.

## 8. Q 33:53 — *Āyat al-Ḥijāb*

### 8.1 Sabab al-nuzūl (multiple chains, unusual narrative depth)

The seven mufassirūn converge on **multi-chain narrative**:

- **al-Bukhārī (#4790, #4791, #4793, #5466) + Muslim (#1428)** — narrative from Anas: the verse was revealed at the wedding feast of the Prophet to Zaynab b. Jaḥsh (year 5 AH, Dhū al-Qaʿda per Qatāda/al-Wāqidī, year 3 per Abū ʿUbayda + Khalīfa b. Khayyāṭ). Three guests stayed too long after the meal, the Prophet was *"shy to ask them to leave"*, eventually went outside; on his return Anas heard him reciting Q 33:53. (Ibn Kathīr line 97269-97345; al-Zamakhsharī sequence; al-Ṭabarī 282296+; al-Suyūṭī Durr line ~ 117800.)
- **al-Bukhārī (#4791)** — the famous ʿUmar al-muwāfaqāt ḥadīth: ʿUmar said *"O Messenger of God, both the pious and the dissolute go in unto your wives; will you not veil them?"* — and the verse came down. Cited by Ibn Kathīr line 97253-97256, al-Ṭabarī, al-Suyūṭī Durr, al-Qurṭubī.
- **al-Bukhārī (#4795)** — narration via ʿĀʾisha that Sawda bint Zamʿa, going out at night for relief, was loudly addressed by ʿUmar (*"We recognize you, O Sawda!"*) — with the explicit purpose of *forcing* the ḥijāb-revelation. Cited Ibn Kathīr 97349-97352 with the comment that *"this is the well-known narrative chain, though the version that places it before the ḥijāb is itself debated"*.
- **Tirmidhī (#3262, qualified ḥasan)** + Anas (NSN #3258): *"Zaynab bint Jaḥsh used to boast over the other wives, saying 'Allāh married me [to him] from above the Heavens'; and the verse of ḥijāb was revealed concerning her."*

The asbāb cluster gives Q 33:53 a **multi-chain, multiply-attested asbāb foundation** — the most well-documented asbāb cluster in all of Q 33.

### 8.2 The *fiqhī* implications (al-Qurṭubī's specialty)

- Al-Qurṭubī (line 128696+) extracts ~ 18 distinct legal rules from Q 33:53 (entry-permission, food-etiquette, conversation-limit, the *min warāʾi ḥijāb* clause specifically, the *post-mortem prohibition on marrying the Prophet's widows*, etc.).
- Al-Suyūṭī (Itqān nawʿ 9) classifies Q 33:53 as one of the *aḥkām* (legal-prescriptive) verses; Ibn Kathīr concurs.
- All seven mufassirūn agree that Q 33:55 (the relatives-permitted-without-ḥijāb verse) is the cross-reference to Q 24:31 (*illā li-buʿūlatihinna*); Q 33:55 is the more comprehensive list and explains why Q 24 omitted *ʿamm* and *khāl* (uncle paternal/maternal).

## 9. Q 33:56 — *Innallāha wa-malāʾikatahu yuṣallūna ʿalā al-Nabī*

### 9.1 The semantics of *ṣalāt*

- **al-Bukhārī, *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* 33, ch on Q 33:56** records the foundational gloss attributed to **Abū al-ʿĀliya**: *"Allāh's *ṣalāt* upon him is His praise of him before the angels; the angels' *ṣalāt* is **duʿāʾ** (supplication for him)."* (Cited by Ibn Kathīr 97442-97443, al-Ṭabarī 282702, al-Zamakhsharī 50594-50607, al-Rāzī 198208-198213, all 7 mufassirūn.)
- **Ibn ʿAbbās, via al-Bukhārī**: *"yuṣallūn — yubarrikūn"* (they bestow blessing).
- **Sufyān al-Thawrī + general Sunni gloss**: *"the *ṣalāt* of the Lord is mercy (raḥma); the *ṣalāt* of the angels is istighfār (asking forgiveness)."* (al-Tirmidhī.)

### 9.2 The liturgical formula — *al-ṣalāt al-Ibrāhīmiyya*

The seven canonical hadith books preserve **multiple chains** of the formula taught by the Prophet when his Companions asked *"How are we to send *ṣalāt* upon you?"*:

- **Bukhārī #4519, #4520, #6357, #3370; Muslim #406; Tirmidhī #483; Nasāʾī #1287-1289; Ibn Mājah #637-639** — Kaʿb b. ʿUjra's chain: *"Allāhumma ṣalli ʿalā Muḥammadin wa-ʿalā āli Muḥammadin kamā ṣallayta ʿalā [āli] Ibrāhīm, innaka ḥamīdun majīd; Allāhumma bārik ʿalā Muḥammadin wa-ʿalā āli Muḥammadin kamā bārakta ʿalā [āli] Ibrāhīm, innaka ḥamīdun majīd."*
- The formula is, alongside al-Fātiḥa, the most-recited single verse-derived prayer in Sunni-Shīʿī liturgical practice (in the *tashahhud* of every ritual prayer).
- **Ibn Kathīr** (line 97441-97608) gives ~30 isnāds documenting variations.

### 9.3 The pastoral-theological claim

Both **al-Zamakhsharī** (line 50596-50607) and **al-Rāzī** (line 198207-198215) emphasize that *ṣalawāt* is a **continuous-tense communicative action**: the verb is *yuṣallūn* (imperfect), making it perpetual; God and the angels are perpetually *over* the Prophet, and the believer's *ṣalāt ʿalayhi* enrolls them in that perpetual circle. This makes Q 33:56 the *single verse most generative of liturgical formulas in the corpus*.

## 10. Q 33:72 — *Innā ʿaraḍnā al-Amānata ʿalā al-samawāti wa-al-arḍ wa-al-jibāl*

### 10.1 The interpretive cluster

This is the corpus's most metaphysically dense single verse. The mufassirūn record ~ 15 distinct readings of *al-amāna* (the trust):

- **al-ṭāʿa** (obedience) — al-ʿAwfī ← Ibn ʿAbbās (Ibn Kathīr 98353).
- **al-farāʾiḍ** (the prescribed religious duties) — ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭalḥa ← Ibn ʿAbbās (Ibn Kathīr 98358).
- **the woman's chastity over her own *farj***— Ubayy b. Kaʿb (Ibn Kathīr 98373).
- **religion + farāʾiḍ + ḥudūd** — Qatāda (Ibn Kathīr 98375).
- **al-ghusl from janāba** — anonymous (Ibn Kathīr 98376).
- **prayer + fasting + ghusl** — Mālik ← Zayd b. Aslam (Ibn Kathīr 98377).
- **all moral responsibility / *taklīf*** — Ibn Kathīr's synthesis (98379).
- The "scaled" / "graded" reading — Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (Ibn Kathīr 98385): God offered the trust *graded by reward and punishment* successively to the seven heavens, the seven earths, and the mountains; each refused after asking *"What is in it?"*
- **Mujāhid + Saʿīd b. Jubayr + Ibn Jurayj**: the heavens' refusal was *cosmological-natural* (planetary motion was their already-imposed tasking); only the human is rewarded and punished for an *accepted* trust.
- **Mūsā b. Abī Ḥabīb ← al-Ḥakam b. ʿUmayr** (Ibn Kathīr 98432-98443): the Prophet said: *"al-amāna wa-al-wafāʾ nazalā ʿalā ibn Ādam maʿa al-anbiyāʾi…"* — the "trust" is the divine-legal patrimony delivered through prophets.

- **al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī**: all converge on *taklīf* (moral-legal accountability, capacity to bear law). Al-Rāzī gives the most systematic philosophical reading: the heavens, earth, mountains are *naturally* compelled (*musakhkhara*); they cannot accept what requires *free choice*. Only the *insān* — endowed with reason and will — can accept the burden, and bears it precisely *because* he is *ẓalūm jahūl*: ignorant of the burden's weight when he accepts it, oppressive of himself in the consequences.

- **al-Biqāʿī**: ties Q 33:72 directly to the surah-opening *yā ayyuha al-Nabī ittaqi Allāh* (Q 33:1) — the surah opens by *requiring* the human-most-burdened (the Prophet) to fear God, and closes by **explaining why** all humans are so burdened: they accepted the *amāna*. The *taqwā* of v. 1 is the *only response* to the *amāna* of v. 72. **Q 33 is symmetric: the opening *taqwā* command is the operational answer to the closing *amāna* offer.**

### 10.2 Q 33:73 — the closing eschatological tier

The final verse: *"that Allāh may punish the hypocrite men and women and the polytheist men and women, and that Allāh may turn in mercy unto the believing men and the believing women; and Allāh is ever Forgiving, Merciful."* All mufassirūn read this as the ***reason-clause*** of the *amāna*-offering: the trust was extended *so that the differential between hypocrite/polytheist and believer would become eschatologically actionable*. The surah closes the loop opened by v. 1 (taqwā / disobedience to *kāfirūn* and *munāfiqūn*) with the corresponding eschatological consequence for hypocrites and disbelievers.

## 11. Synthesis — the *naẓm* (coherence) reading of Q 33 as a whole

Following **al-Biqāʿī** (the *naẓm al-suwar* tradition par excellence) and **al-Rāzī** (the *taʾdīb-of-the-Prophet* reading):

**Q 33 is a single ring-composition organized around *taqwā ↔ amāna***:
- vv. 1-3: *Yā ayyuha al-Nabī ittaqi Allāh* — Prophet's *taqwā* (opening).
- vv. 4-8: legal abolition of *tabannī* (sets up Zayd narrative + structural rules of family-status).
- vv. 9-27: Battle of the Trench (historical-narrative pivot, demonstrates *Allāh's ḥifẓ*).
- vv. 28-34: wives' code (deportment foundation; ends with *taṭhīr*).
- vv. 35-40: equality of believing men and women + Zayd-Zaynab + ***khātam al-nabiyyīn*** (the prophetic-status apex).
- vv. 41-48: dhikr + tasbīḥ + *innā arsalnāka shāhidan wa-mubashshiran wa-nadhīran wa-dāʿiyan ilā Allāhi bi-idhnihi wa-sirājan munīrā* (the Prophet's task-description).
- vv. 49-52: marriage rules for the Prophet specifically.
- vv. 53-55: ***āyat al-ḥijāb*** + the relatives-exception.
- v. 56: ***āyat al-ṣalawāt***  — the perpetual-blessing verse.
- vv. 57-58: those who hurt God and Messenger.
- vv. 59-62: ḥijāb extension + Madinan policing of hypocrites.
- vv. 63-71: eschatological closing: the Hour, the deniers, *taqwā wa-qawl sadīd* (v. 70 — explicit re-statement of the opening *taqwā*).
- vv. 72-73: ***āyat al-amāna*** — the structural answer to v. 1.

The 99 % alif-monorhyme **carries the entire ring-composition phonologically**. The single non-alif verse Q 33:4 (*…wa-huwa yahdī al-sabīl*) is the verse establishing the *foundational rule* of the surah (no two hearts; adopted ≠ biological), placed *just after* the opening triplet — its rhyme deviation marks it as the *frame-establishing clause* before the rhyme-uniformity reasserts and runs unbroken to the end.

This *naẓm* reading was articulated centuries before the empirical metrics and is **fully consistent** with the H-NEW-590, H-NEW-720, H-NEW-840 architectural findings.
