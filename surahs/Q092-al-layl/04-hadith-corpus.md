# Q 92 al-Layl — Ḥadīth Corpus

All ḥadīth verified by reading the Arabic text on disk in
`data/literature/hadith/ahmedbaset-json/db/by_book/the_9_books/{collection}.json`. Numbers are the
collection's **idInBook** (the canonical in-book number). Tashkeel stripped only for matching; texts read
in full. Three distinct ḥadīth families cite Q 92.

## Family 1 — Predestination + "iʿmalū fa-kullun muyassar" (citing Q 92:5–10)

The most important Q 92 ḥadīth. ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib: at a funeral in Baqīʿ al-Gharqad the Prophet, poking
the ground with a stick, said *"there is none among you but his place in the Garden or the Fire is already
written … (and whether he is) wretched or felicitous."* When asked "shall we not then rely on it and abandon
deeds?" he replied **"Act — for everyone is made easy toward (that for which he was created)"** (*iʿmalū
fa-kullun muyassar*), then recited **Q 92:5–10** (*fa-ammā man aʿṭā wa-ttaqā … fa-sa-nuyassiruhu li-l-ʿusrā*).

**Significance.** This is the *locus classicus* for the predestination-vs-deeds debate: the surah's
*nuyassiru* ("We ease/facilitate") is read as God *facilitating* each soul toward the path written for it.
The ḥadīth ties the surah's giver/miser bifurcation (Q092-F-01) directly to the qadar question.

## Family 2 — Liturgical recitation (al-Layl as a prayer-surah; the Muʿādh report)

The Prophet named al-Layl among the surahs fit for leading congregational prayer, reproaching Muʿādh b.
Jabal for reciting al-Baqara behind him (lengthening the prayer for working men): **"O Muʿādh, are you a
trial-maker (*fattān*)? … why did you not pray with *Sabbiḥ isma rabbika al-aʿlā, wa-l-Shams wa-ḍuḥāhā,
wa-l-Layli idhā yaghshā*?"**

**Significance + empirical correlate.** The Prophet groups al-Layl with **al-Shams (Q 91), al-Ḍuḥā
(implied via the cluster), al-Aʿlā (Q 87), and al-ʿAlaq (Q 96)** as suitable congregational surahs — short,
moderate-length oath/admonition pieces. This liturgical cluster has a **content-geometry correlate**: in
the Fisher-Rao matrix, Q 91 is Q 92's rank-13 neighbor and Q 93 al-Ḍuḥā is rank-4 (H-NEW-111), and the
Q 91 → Q 92 seam is the **cheapest in the entire mushaf** (H-NEW-720, rank 1/113). The ḥadīth's liturgical
pairing tracks the empirical short-Meccan cohesion block (`01-empirical-profile.md` §1, §5).

## Family 3 — Qirāʾāt of v3 (the ʿAlqama / Abū al-Dardāʾ report)

Abū al-Dardāʾ tested the Kūfan visitors (companions of Ibn Masʿūd) on how they recited v3. Ibn Masʿūd's
reading was **wa-l-dhakara wa-l-unthā** (without *wa-mā khalaqa*); Abū al-Dardāʾ affirmed he heard the
Prophet recite it that way. The canonical Ḥafṣ/ʿuthmānī text (our `quran-no-tashkeel.json` v3) is
**wa-mā khalaqa al-dhakara wa-l-unthā**.

| Collection | idInBook | Note (verified verbatim) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **al-Bukhārī** | **3579** | Faḍāʾil aṣḥāb al-nabī (Companions); ʿAlqama recites *wa-l-layli idhā yaghshā* to Abū al-Dardāʾ |
| **al-Bukhārī** | **3580** | Companions; parallel |
| **al-Bukhārī** | **3598** | Companions; "*kayfa qaraʾa Ibn Umm ʿAbd {wa-l-layl}*" — the Ibn Masʿūd reading |
| **al-Bukhārī** | **4736** | Tafsīr; ʿAlqama in Shām, recites *wa-l-layli … wa-l-dhakara wa-l-unthā* |
| **al-Bukhārī** | **4737** | Tafsīr; Abū al-Dardāʾ: "I bear witness I heard the Prophet recite thus … and these people want me to recite *wa-mā khalaqa al-dhakara wa-l-unthā* — by God I will not follow them" |
| **al-Bukhārī** | **6043** | al-Adab; the same ʿAlqama-in-Damascus narrative frame |

**Significance.** A rare ḥadīth-attested qirāʾa variant for a single Q 92 verse. The project's canonical
text is the Ḥafṣ reading (*wa-mā khalaqa*); the variant is documented here for completeness and does NOT
affect the surah's root-set used in Q092-F-01 (the v3 roots *xlq, *kr, Anv* are not part of the giver/miser
blocks). Counting note: under the Ibn Masʿūd reading v3 would lose the *xlq* (khalaqa) root-token; the
canonical Ḥafṣ text retains it. We use the Ḥafṣ text throughout (rules-tuple).

## Additional note — *infāq* / *al-khalaf* angel ḥadīth (cited by al-Qurṭubī on v5)

al-Qurṭubī (ayah 5) adduces the Muslim ḥadīth (Kitāb al-Zakāt, Abū Hurayra) of the two angels who descend
each morning, one praying *"O God, give the spender a replacement (khalafan)"* and the other *"give the
withholder ruin (talafan)"* — read as the mechanism behind *aʿṭā … bi-l-ḥusnā* (v5–6) vs *bakhila* (v8).
This is the thematic-spending parallel to the surah's giver/miser antithesis (verified via al-Qurṭubī's
citation; the Muslim Zakāt ḥadīth itself is in `muslim.json`).
