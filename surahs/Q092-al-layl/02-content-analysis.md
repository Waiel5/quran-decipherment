# Q 92 al-Layl — Content Analysis

Content register: **wisdom-eschatological** (oath + moral-bifurcation + warning + pure-intention exemplar).
The surah is a single sustained argument: human strivings diverge (v4), so two roads open (vv 5–10), and
since wealth cannot save (v11) while guidance and both worlds belong to God (vv 12–13), the Fire awaits the
denier (vv 14–16) and the godfearing giver is spared (vv 17–21). Per-verse QAC roots from
`data/morphology/root-index.json`; text from `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json`.

## Block 1 — the oath-triad and its thesis (vv 1–4)

| v | text | roots |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | *wa-l-layli idhā yaghshā* | lyl (layl), g$w (yaghshā) |
| 2 | *wa-l-nahāri idhā tajallā* | nhr (nahār), jlw (tajallā) |
| 3 | *wa-mā khalaqa al-dhakara wa-l-unthā* | xlq (khalaqa), *kr (dhakar), Anv (unthā) |
| 4 | *inna saʿyakum la-shattā* | sEy (saʿy), $tt (shattā) |

The oath swears by **three opposed cosmic pairs** — night that *covers* (yaghshā) ↔ day that *shines forth*
(tajallā), and the creation of *male* ↔ *female* — then delivers the payload in v4: **your strivings are
divergent (*la-shattā*)**. al-Ṭabarī marks v4 as the *jawāb al-qasam* (the answer to the oath) on Qatāda's
authority, glossing *shattā* as *mukhtalif* — "divergent": some are believing/obedient, some
disbelieving/disobedient (`spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/92.json` ayah 4). The cosmic dualities of
vv 1–3 are thus the **rhetorical scaffold** for the moral duality of vv 5–10: as night/day and male/female
are opposed-yet-paired, so are the two human paths.

This pair-oath → soul-bifurcation template is **shared with the preceding surah Q 91 al-Shams** (sun/moon/
day/night/heaven/earth/soul → *qad aflaḥa man zakkāhā · wa-qad khāba man dassāhā*), which is the empirical
basis of the corpus-cheapest Q 91→Q 92 seam (H-NEW-720, rank 1/113; see `01-empirical-profile.md` §5).

## Block 2 — the giver/miser antithesis (vv 5–10) — THE CENTREPIECE

This is the surah's structural heart and the subject of **Q092-F-01**. Two perfectly parallel triads:

| | GIVER pole (vv 5–7) | MISER pole (vv 8–10) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| protasis 1 | *fa-ammā man **aʿṭā wa-ttaqā*** (gives + fears God) | *wa-ammā man **bakhila wa-staghnā*** (hoards + self-sufficient) |
| protasis 2 | *wa-**ṣaddaqa** bi-l-ḥusnā* (affirms the best) | *wa-**kadhdhaba** bi-l-ḥusnā* (denies the best) |
| apodosis | *fa-sa-nuyassiruhu li-l-**yusrā*** (→ ease) | *fa-sa-nuyassiruhu li-l-**ʿusrā*** (→ hardship) |
| roots | ETw, wqy, Sdq + Hsn, ysr | bxl, gny, k\*b + Hsn, ysr |

**The antithesis is built on a constant FRAME with swapped POLES.** The frame `[ammā] man … · wa-V bi-l-ḥusnā
· fa-sa-nuyassiruhu li-l-…` is held byte-for-byte constant; only the verbs and the final noun flip:
aʿṭā↔bakhila, ittaqā↔istaghnā, ṣaddaqa↔kadhdhaba, yusrā↔ʿusrā. The two blocks therefore share exactly the
frame roots **{Hsn (al-ḥusnā), ysr (nuyassiruhu / yusrā≈ʿusrā same root)}** and have **fully disjoint pole
markers** {ETw, wqy, Sdq} vs {bxl, gny, k\*b}. J(giver, miser) = 2/9 = **0.2222**, which Q092-F-01 shows is
**significantly ABOVE** a length-matched random same-surah block-pair (z = +2.65, p = 0.033) — i.e. this
muqābala behaves exactly as the H-NEW-2360 corpus law predicts: **overlap-positive and frame-driven**, not
content-disjoint.

The phrase *bi-l-ḥusnā* ("the best") is read by Ibn ʿAbbās (via Qurṭubī, ayah 6) as *al-khalaf min Allāh*
("God's recompense / the replacement He gives for what is spent") — tying the believer's giving (v5) to
trust in divine recompense (v6). The miser, conversely, "denies the best" — denies that recompense exists.

## Block 3 — wealth cannot save; the divine claim (vv 11–13)

| v | text | roots |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 11 | *wa-mā yughnī ʿanhu māluhu idhā taraddā* | gny (yughnī), mwl (māl), rdy (taraddā) |
| 12 | *inna ʿalaynā la-l-hudā* | hdy (hudā) |
| 13 | *wa-inna lanā la-l-ākhirata wa-l-ūlā* | Awl (ūlā), Axr (ākhira) |

v11 closes the miser's account: his **wealth (*māl*)** — the very thing his *istaghnā* (v8) trusted — will
not avail *when he perishes* (*taraddā*, which al-Ṭabarī's authorities gloss as falling into Hell,
`ar-tafsir-al-tabari/92.json` ayah 11). Note the root **gny** recurs: the miser *staghnā* (deemed himself
self-sufficient, v8) but his wealth *lā yughnī* (does not avail, v11) — a deliberate root-echo binding the
two. vv 12–13 are the surah's theological pivot: **guidance is God's to give** (*inna ʿalaynā la-l-hudā*)
and **both the Hereafter and the former world belong to Him** (*lanā l-ākhirata wa-l-ūlā*) — establishing
the authority behind the two-paths verdict.

## Block 4 — the Fire and al-ashqā (vv 14–16)

| v | text | roots |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 14 | *fa-anzartukum nāran talaẓẓā* | n*r (anzartukum), nwr (nār), lZy (talaẓẓā) |
| 15 | *lā yaṣlāhā illā l-ashqā* | Sly (yaṣlā), $qw (ashqā) |
| 16 | *alladhī kadhdhaba wa-tawallā* | k\*b (kadhdhaba), wly (tawallā) |

The warning of a *blazing Fire* (*nār talaẓẓā*), to be entered only by **al-ashqā** ("the most wretched") —
defined in v16 as the one who **kadhdhaba wa-tawallā** ("denied and turned away"). Note *kadhdhaba* (k\*b)
re-appears: it is the miser's verb in v9 (*kadhdhaba bi-l-ḥusnā*), now generalised to the denier of the
whole message. The denier-of-the-best (v9) and the denier-who-turns-away (v16) are the same character.

## Block 5 — al-atqā and the pure-intention coda (vv 17–21)

| v | text | roots |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 17 | *wa-sa-yujannabuhā l-atqā* | jnb (yujannabu), wqy (atqā) |
| 18 | *alladhī yuʾtī mālahu yatazakkā* | Aty (yuʾtī), mwl (māl), zkw (yatazakkā) |
| 19 | *wa-mā li-aḥadin ʿindahu min niʿmatin tujzā* | AHd (aḥad), End (ʿinda), nEm (niʿma), jzy (tujzā) |
| 20 | *illā btighāʾa wajhi rabbihi l-aʿlā* | bgy (ibtighāʾ), wjh (wajh), rbb (rabb), Elw (aʿlā) |
| 21 | *wa-la-sawfa yarḍā* | rDw (yarḍā) |

The antithesis of *al-ashqā* (v15) is **al-atqā** (v17, root wqy — the same root as the giver's *ittaqā*
in v5): the most God-fearing, who will be *kept far* from the Fire. He is defined (vv 18–20) as the one who
**gives his wealth to purify himself** (*yuʾtī mālahu yatazakkā*) — not to repay any favour (v19) but
**solely seeking the Face of his Lord, the Most High** (*ibtighāʾa wajhi rabbihi l-aʿlā*). The surah closes
on the promise *wa-la-sawfa yarḍā* — "and he shall surely be well-pleased."

**The two superlatives al-ashqā ↔ al-atqā (vv 15, 17) form a second antithesis** closing the surah, echoing
the giver/miser pair of Block 2. Note the *wqy* root frames the believer at both ends: *ittaqā* (v5) →
*al-atqā* (v17). The classical asbāb al-nuzūl tradition reads vv 17–21 as describing Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq
(Ibn ʿAbbās via Qurṭubī, ayahs 17, 19 — Abū Bakr freeing the tortured Bilāl "seeking only God's Face, not
to repay a debt"); see `03-tafsir-survey.md` and `05-classical-claims-audit.md`.

## Repetition / refrain patterns

- **Constant antithesis frame** `fa-sa-nuyassiruhu li-l-{yusrā/ʿusrā}` (vv 7, 10) — the only near-verbatim
  internal repetition, differing in one root (ysr-as-yusr ↔ ʿsr).
- **Root-echo binding:** gny (istaghnā v8 → yughnī v11); k\*b (kadhdhaba v9 → kadhdhaba v16); wqy (ittaqā
  v5 → atqā v17); mwl (māl vv 11, 18 — the miser's hoarded wealth ↔ the atqā's given wealth).
- **Perfect `-ā` monorhyme** across all 21 verses (yaghshā … yarḍā), `h-new-700.json` frac = 1.000.

## Vocabulary distinctness

41 distinct QAC roots in 71 words (root/word density 0.58 — high lexical compression typical of short
oath surahs). The surah is built around **two antonymic verb-pairs** (aʿṭā/bakhila, ittaqā/istaghnā,
ṣaddaqa/kadhdhaba) and **two nominal antitheses** (yusrā/ʿusrā, ashqā/atqā). No proper name appears; God is
named once as *rabb* (v20).
