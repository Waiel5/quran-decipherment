## 2. Q 19:1 — KHYʿṢ muqaṭṭaʿāt: positions

The 5 letters **kāf-hāʾ-yāʾ-ʿayn-ṣād** generated more interpretive variation than perhaps any other muqaṭṭaʿāt. al-Ṭabarī (`tabari-openiti-Q019.txt` offset ~700–4000) records six distinct schools:

1. **Letter-from-divine-name**: Each letter abbreviates a divine attribute. Al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr: *kāf* = *kāfī* (the Sufficient) or *kabīr* (the Great); *hāʾ* = *hādī* (the Guide); *yāʾ* = *raḥīm* (Most Merciful) — note this requires reading *yāʾ* as final letter of *raḥīm*; *ʿayn* = *ʿalīm* / *ʿaẓīm*; *ṣād* = *ṣādiq*. Al-Thaʿlabī (`thaclabi-openiti-Q019.txt` p.205): "*kāf li-khalqih, hādin li-ʿibādih, yaduhu fawqa aydīhim, ʿālim bi-bariyyatih, ṣādiq fī waʿdih*" — al-Kalbī's recension.
2. **Greatest divine name** (*ism Allāh al-aʿẓam*): Al-Ṭabarī cites ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib using *yā kahyaʿaṣ ighfir lī* as a vocative invocation. Al-Suddī: it is the **al-ism al-aʿẓam** that, when invoked, is answered.
3. **Quranic name**: Qatāda (per al-Ṭabarī) — "an *ism* of the Qurʾān."
4. **Surah-name marker**: Al-Qushayrī (per al-Qurṭubī) — "an announcement of the surah's name."
5. **Divine oath**: ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib + Ibn ʿAbbās — "an oath sworn by God."
6. **Praise of God by Himself**: Al-Kalbī (per al-Thaʿlabī) — *thanāʾ athnā Allāh bihi ʿalā nafsihi*.

Al-Rāzī's *Mafātīḥ* extends this with a *17-section discussion* on the muqaṭṭaʿāt at the surah's head, recapitulating his Q 2 muqaṭṭaʿāt treatment — this is logged as a recurring theme in [[h-new-97-name-letter-joint|H-NEW-97]] honest-disclosures.

**Q 19's KHYʿṢ singularity** (the only 5-letter set in the corpus) is **not flagged as architecturally significant by classical mufassirūn** — most discuss letter-by-letter interpretations within the surah but do not contrast the 5-letter form against the rest of the muqaṭṭaʿāt-29 typology. This is a NULL on classical recognition of the structural-uniqueness — see `05-classical-claims-audit.md` claim #1.

## 3. Q 19:7 — Yaḥyā as a name "not given before" (*lam najʿal lahu min qablu samiyyā*)

Al-Zamakhsharī (`zamakhshari-openiti-Q019.txt` p.3): "*lam yusamma aḥadun bi-Yaḥyā qablahu* — no one was named Yaḥyā before him; this attests that distinguished names are praiseworthy, and the Arabs sought such names in naming their children, because they are more distinctive (*anbah*) and more praised (*anwah*) and more elevated above slander." Al-Zamakhsharī then cites a poem (*sanaʿu l-asāmī musbilīn azran*) and the proverbial *anā ibnu l-ʿajāʾ*.

Al-Ṭabarsī (`tabarsi-openiti-Q019.txt`) and al-Rāzī (`razi-openiti-Q019.txt`) link this to v.65's *hal taʿlamu lahu samiyyā* — the same root *s-m-y* echoes in the eschatological closing about the divine name — bookending the surah's name-and-naming theme.

## 4. Q 19:16–40 — Maryam pericope

### Q 19:17 *fa-ttakhadhat min dūnihim ḥijāban* — the seclusion of Maryam

Al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās: "she withdrew to a place east of her family and took a curtain to keep her family from seeing her" — connecting this *ḥijāb* with menstrual / private-space practices in temple-precinct contexts. Al-Qurṭubī expands this into a substantial *fiqh* discussion of seclusion-norms.

### Q 19:24 — *fa-nādāhā min taḥtihā* — "He called to her from beneath her"

Two major positions:
1. **Voice of ʿĪsā from her womb / from beneath the palm**: al-Ṭabarī cites multiple Companion-era opinions; al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, and Ibn ʿAbbās favor the infant-ʿĪsā reading.
2. **Jibrīl from beneath the slope** of the hill: this becomes the dominant reading by al-Ṭabarsī and Ibn Kathīr. Al-Ṭabarsī specifically contrasts the two readings and notes both are well-attested.

### Q 19:27–28 *yā ukhta hārūn* — "O sister of Aaron"

Al-Ṭabarī (`tabari-openiti-Q019.txt` offset 69405): "the *ahl al-taʾwīl* differ on why she was called *ukhta hārūn*, and which Hārūn this Hārūn is whom Allah mentioned." Three positions:
1. **Tribal-association**: *yā ukhta hārūn* = "O of-Aaron's-tribe" — naming her by her ancestor Aaron-the-priest's lineage. Al-Ṭabarī favors this.
2. **Honorific-name**: A righteous man named Hārūn was contemporary with Maryam, and she is metaphorically linked to him as a paragon of piety.
3. **Christological misreading rebuttal**: Some classical sources invoke ḥadīth (Aḥmad — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`) that the Mughīra b. Shuʿba report from Najrān-Christians clarifies this, with the Prophet stating *kānū yusammūna bi-asmāʾ anbiyāʾihim* ("they were named after their prophets").

### Q 19:29–33 — Cradle speech of ʿĪsā

Universal among mufassirūn: this is the **iʿjāz** of Q 19. Al-Rāzī (`razi-openiti-Q019.txt`) treats it as one of the most-discussed Quranic miracle-narratives. Al-Zamakhsharī notes the parallelism between v.33 *al-salām ʿalayya yawma wulidtu wa-yawma amūtu wa-yawma ubʿathu ḥayyā* (ʿĪsā, first person) and v.15 *salāmun ʿalayhi yawma wulida wa-yawma yamūtu wa-yawma yubʿathu ḥayyā* (Yaḥyā, third person). This parallel is the **structural pillar** linking blocks A and B.

## 5. Q 19:41–50 — Ibrāhīm-Āzar dialogue

Al-Ṭabarsī treats the 5× *yā abati* (vv. 42–46) as a model of *adab al-khiṭāb* ("etiquette of address"): Ibrāhīm's gentleness toward his polytheist father is the paradigm of *daʿwa* (invitation). Al-Rāzī highlights the formal difference between Ibrāhīm's *yā abati* (4 times) and Āzar's reply *yā ibrāhīm* (v.46) — Ibrāhīm uses honorific-affectionate, Āzar uses bare-name — narrating the moral asymmetry of the exchange.

Al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-durar* (extracted into `biqai-openiti-Q019.txt`) traces *munāsaba* between vv. 49–50 (Ibrāhīm's reward = Isḥāq + Yaʿqūb + *lisān ṣidq ʿaliyyan*) and v.97 (the language-facilitation closing): both about the *lisān* / "tongue" — al-Biqāʿī reads this as the surah's structural ring-closure on the language-theme.

## 6. Q 19:51–58 — Brief roster + sajda verse

Al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* nawʿ 19 (sajda verses) lists Q 19:58 as one of the **canonical 14 sajda āyāt**, with the gesture of prostration recommended on its recitation. The verse's collective *prophet ancestry* statement (Ādam → Nūḥ → Ibrāhīm → Isrāʾīl) frames the surah's prophet-roster as a *single genealogical chain*, not 5 separate stories.

Al-Rāzī highlights that **Idrīs is mentioned in only 2 places in the Quran** (Q 19:56 and Q 21:85) and treats vv. 56–57 as the locus classicus for the ascension-of-Idrīs tradition. *makānan ʿaliyyan* "a lofty place" — al-Ṭabarī cites multiple traditions placing Idrīs in the 4th heaven; al-Bayḍāwī and al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* (`suyuti-durr-openiti-Q019.txt`) compile the larger ḥadīth corpus on this.

## 7. Q 19:71 — *wa-in minkum illā wāriduhā* "there is none of you but will pass over [Hell]"

Most-disputed verse in the surah. Al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī all dedicate substantial sections (10+ pages each) to whether *wuruūd* means "passing over" (i.e., crossing the bridge / *ṣirāṭ* over Hell — and pious are saved while wrongdoers fall) or "entering Hell" (and being subsequently extracted). The Darimī ḥadīth #2074 (`data/literature/hadith/Q019-citations-raw.json`) is the key transmissional anchor on this.

## 8. Q 19:88–95 — *qālū ittakhadha l-raḥmānu waladā* — Christological polemic

The classical anti-trinitarian centerpiece. Al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr all read vv. 88–93 as the **most rhetorically intense polemic in the Quran**: the heavens almost split, the earth nearly torn, mountains nearly fallen — for the assertion that God has a son. Al-Rāzī notes the deliberate use of **al-Raḥmān** (not Allāh) throughout this section: of Q 19's 12 al-Raḥmān tokens, 7 cluster in the eschatological closing (vv. 58, 61, 69, 75, 78, 85, 87, 88, 91, 92, 93, 96 — verified per `02-content-analysis.md`). The argument is: those who claim a child for *the Most Merciful* are repudiating the divine name they invoke.

## 9. Q 19:97 — *fa-innamā yassarnāhu bi-lisānika* — language-facilitation closing

Al-Biqāʿī's reading (above §5) ties this to v.50's *lisān ṣidq ʿaliyyan*. Al-Rāzī notes the **twin verse Q 44:58** uses identical opening *fa-innamā yassarnāhu bi-lisānika* with a different terminal clause (*laʿallahum yatadhakkarūn*). Both are at the close of their respective surahs; both are language-of-revelation comments. Al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* nawʿ 64 (parallel verses) lists this twin among its illustrative pairs.

## 10. Asbāb al-nuzūl — the Najāshī tradition

Al-Qurṭubī (`qurtubi-openiti-Q019.txt` first 1500 chars) records the long version: the Quraysh sent ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ and ʿAbd Allāh b. Abī Rabīʿa to the Najāshī to recover the muhājirūn after Badr. The Prophet sent ʿAmr b. Umayya al-Ḍamrī with a letter. Jaʿfar b. Abī Ṭālib was summoned and **recited Sūrat Maryam (KHYʿṢ)** before the Najāshī and the bishops. Both wept until their beards were drenched. The Najāshī said: *inna hādhā wa-lladhī jāʾa bihi mūsā la-yakhruju min mishkāt wāḥida* ("this and what Mūsā brought issue from one and the same niche"). He refused to surrender the muhājirūn.

Ibn Kathīr (`ibn-kathir-openiti-Q019.txt` opening, ~offset 200): cites Muḥammad b. Isḥāq via Umm Salama and Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal via Ibn Masʿūd, both narrating that Jaʿfar **recited the head of this surah** before the Najāshī.

This tradition is the **dominant asbāb-al-nuzūl framing** for Q 19 in tafsir tradition — but its empirical status is mixed: the Aḥmad and Ibn Isḥāq chains differ on what was recited (sometimes Q 19; sometimes Q 5; sometimes simply "from the Qurʾān"). See claim audit #4.

## 11. Faḍāʾil ("virtues") tradition

Al-Thaʿlabī (`thaclabi-openiti-Q019.txt` opening) and al-Ṭabarsī both cite Ubayy b. Kaʿb's *faḍl* tradition: "whoever recites Sūrat Maryam is given reward by the count of those who believed Zakariyyāʾ and disbelieved him, and Yaḥyā, Maryam, ʿĪsā, Mūsā, Hārūn, Ibrāhīm, Isḥāq, Yaʿqūb, Ismāʿīl — ten good deeds — and by the count of those who claimed God had a child, and those who did not so claim." This *faḍl* hadith **lists the surah's named prophets explicitly** — a useful classical confirmation that the surah is recognised as a multi-prophet roster.

The Ubayy *faḍl* tradition is widely deemed *mawḍūʿ* ("fabricated") by post-classical isnad-criticism but is faithfully preserved in early-classical *faḍāʾil* compilations.
