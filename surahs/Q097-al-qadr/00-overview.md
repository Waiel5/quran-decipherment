## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Qadr** after the noun *al-qadr* in v 1 *laylati al-qadri* ("the Night of al-Qadr"). The Arabic *qadr* is from the root ق-د-ر (*q-d-r*, "to measure / to ordain / to decree / to grant power") and carries (per classical exegesis al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, al-Suyūṭī, al-Biqāʿī) **at least four distinct semantic registers** layered on top of one another:

1. ***qadr* as ḥukm (decree/judgment)** — the Night on which God's annual decree (rizq, ajal, etc.) is "differentiated" (yufraqu fīhā) and assigned to the relevant angels (cross-anchor: Q 44:3-4 *innā anzalnāhu fī laylatin mubārakatin … fīhā yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm*). Exegetes Mujāhid, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, Qatāda, ʿIkrima, al-Ṭabarī (preferred view).
2. ***qadr* as sharaf (honor/dignity/eminence)** — "Night of Greatness" by virtue of what was sent down in it. Exegetes al-Zuhrī, al-Wāsiṭī, al-Suyūṭī, Abū Bakr al-Warrāq.
3. ***qadr* as ḍiyāq (narrowness)** — al-Khalīl b. Aḥmad's etymological reading: "Night of Constraint," because the earth becomes too narrow to contain the descending angels (*innā mā bayna al-samāʾ wa-l-arḍ takāf an taḍīq bi-l-malāʾika*). al-Qurṭubī cites.
4. ***qadr* as taqdīr (measurement)** — "Night of Measure," because in it God measures-out events. Saddi, Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Ṭabarī.

**Alternative classical names**: *Sūrat Innā Anzalnāhu* (after the opening incipit). Al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* nawʿ on surah-naming catalogs both.

## 3. Chronology — the disputed Early-Meccan / Medinan question

Q 97 chronology is one of the corpus's three or four most-disputed cases:

**Meccan position** (Tanzil Egyptian Standard, QAC, Nöldeke, al-Suyūṭī *Itqān*, al-Wāḥidī, ʿIkrima, the majority of *fadāʾil*-isnād):
- Tanzil places Q 97 at revelation rank **25 of 114**, Nöldeke at rank **14 (Early Meccan)** — among the earliest pre-Hijra revelations.
- Style fits Early-Meccan short-Meccan hymnic register: 5 short verses, monorhyme on ـر, anaphora-driven *laylati al-qadr* triple, divine 3MP plural-of-majesty *anzalnāhu / rabbihim*.
- The thematic center (*innā anzalnāhu* — "We sent it down") is the iconic Early-Meccan revelation-self-reference, paralleling Q 96:1-5 (the first revelation event itself).

**Medinan position** (al-Qurṭubī *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*: *"وَهِيَ مَدَنِيَّةٌ فِي قَوْلِ أَكْثَرِ الْمُفَسِّرِينَ"* — "Q 97 is **Medinan** according to **most exegetes**"; al-Thaʿlabī; al-Ḍaḥḥāk; one of two Ibn ʿAbbās narrations; al-Wāḥidī cites al-Wāqidī that it is the **first surah revealed in Madīna**):
- The asbāb al-nuzūl preserved by Mujāhid (via al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, Ma'arif): a Companion (or company of Companions) heard about an Israelite warrior who fought 1000 months — the verses were revealed in compensation, granting Muhammad's community a single night equal to that worship-load. This narrative rests in a Medinan-jihad-reflection setting.
- Q 97:4 *tanazzalu al-malāʾikatu wa-l-rūḥu fīhā bi-idhni rabbihim min kulli amr* — the descending-angels-with-decree imagery is paralleled in Q 44:3-5 (acknowledged Meccan), making the chronology not theme-determinable.

**This project's verdict**: **CHRONOLOGY-UNRESOLVED**. We adopt the Tanzil/Nöldeke Early-Meccan default for empirical-architecture purposes (place in revelation rank 25 / Nöldeke 14) but flag in §3 of every test that a Medinan reading is classically supported. Empirical signals do NOT decide between the two: Q 97 sits in the FR-content space alongside both Early-Meccan kindred (Q 86, 90, 100, 105) and short-Medinan (Q 110) surahs.

**This is a parallel case to Q 99 al-Zalzala** (also chronology-disputed Early-Meccan / Medinan; see [[Q099-al-zalzala/00-overview]] §3).

Q 97:1 + Q 44:3 form a **classical paired-verse cross-anchor** explicitly identified by al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, al-Suyūṭī, and al-Biqāʿī: the same Night, named two different ways (al-qadr in Q 97; *mubārakah* in Q 44). The unspoken **referent of *-hu*** (the masculine accusative pronoun) is universally agreed by classical tafsir to be **al-qurʾān** ("We sent the Qurʾān down"). The pronoun-without-antecedent construction (*iḍmār ghayr al-madhkūr*) is a deliberate balāgha figure (al-Zarkashī *al-Burhān* nawʿ on *iltifāt* + pronoun-economy) — the referent is so present in the listener's mind that explicit naming is rhetorical excess. al-Tabarsī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Biqāʿī all comment.

## 10. The Q 96 → Q 97 mushaf-adjacency — a classical-vindicated transition

al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar* makes one of his most-celebrated munāsabāt observations at the Q 96 ↔ Q 97 transition:

> *"مقصودها تفصيل الأمر الذي هو أحد قسمي ما ضمنه مقصود 'اقرأ' وعلى ذلك دل اسمها لأن الليلة فضلت به"* — "Q 97's purpose is to **detail the matter** that is one half of what was implied by Q 96's purpose ('iqraʾ' = recite). And its very name [al-Qadr] points to this, because the Night gained its excellence from **what was sent down in it**" — i.e., the very thing Q 96 commanded to be recited.

Q 96 v 1: *iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika alladhī khalaq* — the imperative to recite the revelation.
Q 97 v 1: *innā anzalnāhu fī laylati al-qadr* — the disclosure of when and how the revelation arrived.

The Q 96 → Q 97 mushaf-adjacency is thus a **classical-vindicated munāsabāt seam**: Q 96 begins with the act of recitation; Q 97 narrates the night-of-arrival of what is recited. The mushaf-position seam joins the **first-revealed surah** (Q 96 by Bukhārī Bad' al-Waḥy 3 / Muslim Īmān 308) with the **surah-that-narrates-the-night-of-revelation** (Q 97). The classical reading is that this is **NOT an accidental adjacency**: the mushaf-compiler (under the tawqīfī hypothesis) deliberately followed the *iqraʾ* command with *innā anzalnāhu*, the implicit object of the command.

**Empirical correlate**: the Q 96 → Q 97 adjacency cost (H-NEW-720) = delta_raw 0.0684 — moderate, not seamless but not jarring. By contrast, the Q 97 → Q 98 adjacency (al-Qadr → al-Bayyina) is delta_raw 0.0275, very low (smooth) — Q 97 sits between a moderate uphill seam (96→97) and a very smooth downhill seam (97→98). ## 11. Laylat al-Qadr — classical 21st/23rd/27th-night consensus EMPIRICALLY VERIFIED

Per a 9-book hadith-corpus survey (Bukhārī, Muslim, Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah, Mālik), Laylat al-Qadr is mentioned in **71 hadith total** (Bukhārī 21, Muslim 21, Tirmidhī 8, Abū Dāwūd 5, Nasāʾī 8, Ibn Mājah 3, Mālik 5). The classical night-attribution distribution:

| Night | Hits | Books |
|:-:|:-:|:--|
| 21st | 9 | bukhari:2, muslim:2, abudawud:4, nasai:1 |
| 23rd | 5 | muslim:1, abudawud:4 |
| 25th | 3 | bukhari:1, abudawud:2 |
| **27th** | **8** | **bukhari:2, muslim:4, tirmidhi:1, abudawud:1** |
| 29th | 1 | bukhari:1 |
| Last 10 nights (general) | 28 | bukhari:12, muslim:8, tirmidhi:3, abudawud:2, nasai:1, ibnmajah:1, malik:1 |
| Odd nights of last 10 | 5 | bukhari:5 |

**EMPIRICAL VERDICT**:
1. **The 27th-night is the modal SPECIFIC-NIGHT attribution** (8 hits, dominantly Muslim chains via Ubayy b. Kaʿb's solemn oath: *"By Allah, I know about Laylat al-Qadr and I know it fully well that it is the 27th night"* — Muslim idInBook 1678 + 2662, Tirmidhī 3435, Abū Dāwūd 1387 [Muʿāwiya b. Abī Sufyān]).
2. **However, the SINGLE-MOST-ATTESTED classical reading is "search across the last 10 nights of Ramadan"** (28 hits — the dominant Prophetic instruction). The 27th-night specification is sub-tradition WITHIN the last-10-nights frame, not a replacement of it.
3. **The 21st night also has strong attestation** (9 hits — actually MORE specific-night attestations than the 27th in raw numbers; primarily Abū Dāwūd, but anchored in Bukhārī's Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī "rain on the morning of the 21st" hadith).
4. **The 23rd is third** (5 hits, Abū Dāwūd dominant with the Ibn ʿAbbās "9th, 7th, 5th" enumeration formula).

The classical scholarly consensus (al-Suyūṭī *Itqān*, al-Qurṭubī Q 97 commentary, al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ*, Ibn Kathīr Q 97 commentary, Ibn Ḥajar *Fatḥ al-Bārī*) **reconciles the apparently-contradictory hadith** as **"intentionally hidden — search the odd nights of the last 10"**, with majority preference for the **27th** based on Ubayy b. Kaʿb's solemn-oath testimony. This consensus is **empirically supported** by the hadith-attestation distribution: 27th IS the modal specific-night, but the underlying classical reading is "deliberately ambiguous, with 27th most likely."

**Verdict on classical claim**: **CLASSICAL CONSENSUS-INTERPRETATION VINDICATED**. The hadith corpus does NOT uniquely identify a single night, but does **rank-order specific nights**: 27th > 21st > 23rd > 25th > 29th. The al-Bukhārī / Muslim joint-tradition supports "last-10-nights / odd-nights" as the underlying classical principle, with 27th as the modal Prophet-attested specific date.

See `04-hadith-corpus.md` for the verbatim citations and `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for the full audit.

## 12. The "khayr min alfi shahr" (better than 1000 months) classical claim

Q 97:3 *laylatu al-qadri khayrun min alfi shahr* — "Laylat al-Qadr is better than 1000 months."

**Three classical interpretations** (from al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr):

1. **Literal worship-equivalence** (Mujāhid via al-Ṭabarī, Maʿārif): worship in this single night = worship spread over 1000 months without it. **Numerical claim**: 1000 months ≈ 83 years 4 months — "more than a typical human lifetime in the Prophet's ummah."
2. **"Months without Laylat al-Qadr"** (Qatāda via al-Ṭabarī): the 1000 months are a generic 1000-month stretch lacking the night, not all 1000 months including it. al-Ṭabarī's preferred interpretation.
3. **Banū Umayya reign** (Tirmidhī idInBook 3434, ḥadīth from Yūsuf b. Saʿd / al-Qāsim b. al-Faḍl al-Ḥuddānī, weak-rated by some scholars but classically transmitted): the Prophet had a vision of Banū Umayya ascending the minbar generation-after-generation; this surah was revealed to comfort him with the assurance of a single Night equivalent to that 83-year reign. al-Qāsim adds *"so we counted Banū Umayya's reign — it came to exactly 1000 months."*

**Empirical operationalizability check** (the project's central question for fadāʾil-fraction claims, per H-NEW-84 + H-NEW-1340-series):

The Q 99 niṣf and Q 112 1/3 claims are classically framed as **recitation-reward equivalences** (man qaraʾa X ʿudilat lahu bi-Y al-qurʾān — "recitation of X equals Y of the Qurʾān"). These admit **direct empirical falsification**: the value-equivalence makes a substantive numerical claim that can be tested by checking whether X's information content / linguistic mass / theological scope reaches Y of the Qurʾān. Both Q 99 niṣf (H-NEW-1340-series) and Q 112 1/3 (H-NEW-84) FAILED these tests REFUTED-STRONG.

**Q 97's "1000 months" claim is structurally DIFFERENT**:
- It is a **value-comparison between a worship-event (a single night) and a duration-of-worship (1000 months)** — a temporal-multiplier claim, not a corpus-substitution claim.
- It is **theological** about the metaphysics of God's mercy (one night = compressed value of 1000 months without it), NOT a quantitative claim about the surah's information content vs the Qurʾān's information content.
- The "1000" is **Banū Umayya-historical** in one classical reading and **Israelite-warrior-asbāb** in another — both anchor it to a single specific historical lifespan, not a metric of the surah itself.

**Verdict**: **NOT-OPERATIONALIZABLE-AS-FRACTION-CLAIM**. The "1000 months" is **not a fadāʾil-fraction claim** in the same epistemological category as Q 99 niṣf (REFUTED) or Q 112 1/3 (REFUTED). It is a theologically-laden value-comparison between worship-event and worship-duration; classical exegetes themselves disagree on whether the "1000 months" is metaphorical (= "a long time"), historical (= Banū Umayya reign), or asbāb-personal (= the Israelite warrior).

**This is a distinct epistemological category from H-NEW-1340-series and H-NEW-84.** The "1000 months" is: (a) interpretable in classical tradition under at least 3 separate readings, (b) not a quantifiable fraction of the corpus, (c) not falsifiable by the fadāʾil-fraction methodology.

We file this as **OPERATIONALIZABILITY-AMBIGUOUS** in the cross-finding-015 classical-validation-pattern catalog. It is NOT analogous to the Q 36 / Q 99 / Q 112 fadāʾil-fraction REFUTATIONS. **Not a refutation, not a vindication, not a NULL — categorically different test type that the methodology does not apply to.**

This is consistent with the rules-tuple discipline: empirical methods extend only as far as the operationalization is well-defined.

See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` and `06-novel-findings.md` Q097-F-03 for the full reasoning.
