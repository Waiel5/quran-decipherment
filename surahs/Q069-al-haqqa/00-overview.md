## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Ḥāqqa** after the opening word — *al-ḥāqqa*, the active-feminine-singular participle of root ḥ-q-q. Classical glosses (per Zamakhshari *al-Kashshāf* ad loc.):

- *al-sāʿa al-wājibatu al-wuqūʿ al-thābitatu al-majīʾ* — "the Hour whose occurrence is necessary, whose coming is firmly fixed"
- *al-latī fīhā ḥawāqqu al-umūr min al-ḥisāb wa-l-thawāb wa-l-ʿiqāb* — "that in which the realities of accounts, reward, and punishment occur"
- *al-latī taḥuqq fīhā al-umūr* — "that in which matters become realized / verified"
- *al-ghālibatu li-kulli khaṣm* — "that which prevails over every disputant" (al-Biqāʿī's reading)

The name is theologically reflexive: *al-ḥāqqa* IS the day on which truth becomes manifest — the very *act* of the surah is to declare the reality of that reality.
