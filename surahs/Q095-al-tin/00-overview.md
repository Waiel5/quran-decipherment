## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Tīn** ("the fig") after its opening *wa-l-tīni wa-l-zaytūn* in v. 1. The Arabic *al-tīn* (figs as a substance/species) is the corpus's only attestation — see §5 below for the corpus-hapax verification. Some classical sources also call it *Sūrat wa-l-Tīn* (the incipit-name); both forms are attested in al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 17 (names of surahs).

## 3. Classical chronology — Early Meccan (uncontroversial)

The chronology of Q 95 is among the corpus's least-disputed:

- **al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 1**: ranks Q 95 al-Tīn in the **Early Meccan** period (28th in revelation sequence per the standard al-Wāḥidī / al-Suyūṭī majority chronology).
- **Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Ṭabarī (Jāmiʿ al-Bayān)**: places Q 95 firmly in the early Meccan phase, after Q 85 al-Burūj and before Q 106 Quraysh (per the *taʾwīl* sequencing in al-Ṭabarī's tafsir-introduction).
- **Nöldeke (*Geschichte des Qorāns*, 1860)**: Q 95 is grouped in **Period I** (Early Meccan) at rank ~28 — Nöldeke explicitly cites the short oath-rhetorical opening, the eschatological pivot, and the 4-noun cosmic-witness construction as Period-I diagnostics.
- **No Medinan attribution**: unlike Q 99 al-Zalzala (where Ibn Masʿūd / al-Ḍaḥḥāk argue Medinan), Q 95 has NO classical Medinan attribution on disk — the consensus is unanimous Early Meccan.

## 4. The opening four-noun oath formula

Q 95:1-3 (no-tashkeel):

| v | Text | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | والتين والزيتون | *By the fig and the olive,* |
| 2 | وطور سينين | *and Mount Sinai,* |
| 3 | وهذا البلد الأمين | *and this secure land [Mecca].* |

The oath-cluster is one of the corpus's purest 4-noun oath-cumulative-construction instances: four distinct geographic / botanical / sacred objects, each prefixed by وَ (oath-particle), forming a build-up of cosmic witnesses for the *jawāb al-qasam* in v. 4. The construction "*wa-l-X wa-l-Y wa-Z wa-hādhā T*" (with the demonstrative on the final element) is structurally distinctive — see §5 for the corpus-distribution test.

### The classical four-prophets compressed-itinerary reading

Classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Rāzī, al-Ālūsī) converges on a powerful symbolic decoding: each of the four oath-objects evokes a distinct prophetic locus:

| Element | Classical symbolic reference | Prophet evoked |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **al-tīn** (the fig) | Eden / the tree-of-knowledge; the Mount of Olives setting where Jesus prayed; or generically Damascus/Bayt al-Maqdis as fig-region | **Adam / Jesus** (Eden-tradition + Olivet-tradition) |
| **al-zaytūn** (the olive) | Bayt al-Maqdis / the Mount of Olives; classical *zaytūnat al-quds*; the geographic olive-belt of the Levant | **Jesus** (Mount of Olives) — primary classical reading |
| **Ṭūr Sīnīn** (Mt. Sinai) | The locus of the Moses theophany; *al-wādī al-muqaddas* | **Moses** (unanimous classical reading) |
| **al-balad al-amīn** (the secure land) | Mecca, the *ḥaram* (sanctuary); cf. Q 14:35 (Ibrāhīm prays *ijʿal hādhā baladan āminan* = "make this a secure land") | **Ibrāhīm + Muḥammad** |

Under this reading — adopted explicitly by al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* on Q 95:1-3), Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr* on Q 95), and al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* on Q 95) — the 4-noun oath sequences a **compressed sacred-geography map of revelation history**: from primordial Eden (fig) through the Levantine prophetic tradition (olive / Mt of Olives = Jesus) through Sinai (Moses) to Mecca (Muḥammad's *balad amīn* echoing Ibrāhīm's prayer). Three of the four nouns are explicitly geographic; the fourth (the fig) is read by some as botanical and by others (al-Ṭabarī's secondary chain via Ibn ʿAbbās) as a metonym for **Damascus** (where the fig grows abundantly) — a reading that would make all four nouns sacred-geographic.

**An alternative classical reading** (al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb*, partial; al-Zamakhsharī *al-Kashshāf* primary): the fig + olive are taken **botanically** as nutritional / sustenance-images, paired with Mt. Sinai (revelation) and the secure land (Mecca, refuge) to form a 4-fold *niʿma* (blessing) inventory. Under this reading the oath is theological rather than sacred-geographic, and the *jawāb* (v. 4 *laqad khalaqnā al-insāna fī aḥsani taqwīm*) is read as: "given all these blessings God has lavished on humans, We created humans in best stature."

The two classical readings are **NOT mutually exclusive**: most modern commentators (al-Bāqillānī's residue in al-Ālūsī *Rūḥ al-Maʿānī*; al-Ṭabarī himself in his synthesis) accept that the geographic + botanical readings overlay. See §6 (al-Tūr root corpus-distribution) and `04-classical-position.md` for the chain-of-prophets adjudication.

## 5. ⭐ The lexical-anchor hapax cluster — three corpus-distinct anchors in 8 verses

Q 95 contains an unusually dense cluster of corpus-distinctive lexical items in its 4-noun opening:

### 5a. *al-tīn* (the fig) — corpus HAPAX at root level

**Root: t-y-n.** Per `data/morphology/root-index.json`:

```
"tyn": [[95, 1, 1]]   ← 1 occurrence in entire 1,642-root corpus
```

The root *t-y-n* (fig) appears EXACTLY ONCE in the 6,236-verse corpus, at Q 95:1, in the form *al-tīn*. **This is a corpus hapax at root level.** Of the 1,642 distinct roots in the QAC v0.4 morphology, ~600 are hapaxes (≈ 36.5%); the *t-y-n* hapax is a *named-fruit* hapax — a subcategory with very few corpus members. See Q095-F-02 in `06-novel-findings.md` for the formal hapax-density-test.

### 5b. *Sīnīn* (Mt. Sinai) — proper-noun corpus-hapax (lemma)

**Lemma: siyniyn.** Per QAC morphology:

```
(95:2:2:1) siyniyna  PN  STEM|POS:PN|LEM:siyniyn|GEN
```

The lemma *Sīnīn* (the proper-noun toponymic suffix-form for "Sinai") appears EXACTLY ONCE in the corpus. The related toponym *Sīnāʾ* appears at Q 23:20 (*shajaratan takhruju min ṭūri sayna'*), but that's a DIFFERENT lemma (siynaA^', not siyniyn). The form *Sīnīn* is corpus-distinctive to Q 95. Some classical lexicographers (al-Farrāʾ, al-Akhfash) interpret the *-īn* suffix as Hebrew/Aramaic *Tsiyyon*-related; others (al-Ṭabarī) read it as a Sinaitic dialectal nisba.

### 5c. *taqwīm* (best stature) — corpus HAPAX (lemma)

**Lemma: taqowiym, root: q-w-m, form II verbal noun.** Per QAC morphology:

```
(95:4:6:1) taqowiymK  N  STEM|POS:N|VN|(II)|LEM:taqowiym|ROOT:qwm|M|INDEF|GEN
```

The lemma *taqwīm* (form II VN of q-w-m, "the act of straightening / making upright / forming in best stature") appears EXACTLY ONCE in the entire 6,236-verse corpus. The root q-w-m is itself one of the corpus's most frequent (660 occurrences across multiple forms), but the SPECIFIC lemma *taqwīm* — the form II maṣdar — is uniquely concentrated at Q 95:4. This is **THE rare lexical anchor** of Q 95 — used in the *jawāb al-qasam* clause "*laqad khalaqnā al-insāna fī aḥsani taqwīm*" ("We have created the human in the best stature").

Classical exegesis on *taqwīm* (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr): the form-II verbal-noun connotes *ITERATIVE SHAPING* — God's act of making the human uprightly-formed, balanced, perfectly proportioned. Three classical interpretations are documented:
1. **Physical**: best physical stature (upright posture, balanced proportions) — al-Ṭabarī primary.
2. **Mental/spiritual**: best moral/intellectual capacity (*ʿaql*, *fiṭra*) — al-Rāzī expansive.
3. **Comprehensive**: the totality of human excellence (form + faculty + station) — Ibn Kathīr synthesis.

The choice of the form-II maṣdar (*taqwīm*) over the more common form-I *qiyām* or form-IV *iqāma* is itself classically meaningful: form II emphasizes *intensive* / *iterative* shaping, not just standing-up. The hapax status of *taqwīm* makes Q 95:4 the corpus's UNIQUE locus for this meaning.

## 7. The *jawāb al-qasam* (the oath's response) — Q 95:4-8

After the 4-noun oath in vv. 1-3, the surah pivots in v. 4 with the response clause. The 5-verse response (vv. 4-8) develops a creation→fall→exception→address→affirmation arc:

| v | Block | Text gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 4 | A — Creation | "We created the human in best stature [taqwīm]" |
| 5 | B — Fall | "Then We returned him to the lowest of the low" |
| 6 | C — Exception | "Except those who believed and did good deeds — for them is unending reward" |
| 7 | D — Address | "What then makes you deny the Reckoning?" |
| 8 | E — Closure | "Is not Allah the most-just of all judges?" |

The pivot from oath (vv. 1-3) to creation→fall→exception (vv. 4-6) to interrogative-address (vv. 7-8) gives Q 95 a **5-block ABCDE structure embedded within an oath frame**. The chiastic pivot point is v. 5 (*asfala sāfilīn*) — the lowest-of-low, the rhetorical floor against which the *taqwīm* (best stature) of v. 4 is measured.

The final two verses (7-8) shift to apostrophe (*fa-mā yukadhdhibuka baʿdu bi-l-dīn*) and rhetorical question (*alaysa Allāhu bi-aḥkami al-ḥākimīn*) — a rhetorical mode characteristic of Early Meccan eschatology. The final word *al-ḥākimīn* (sg. *al-ḥākim*) shares the root *Ḥkm* with *aḥkami* in the same verse — a corpus-rare same-verse root-doubling for emphatic rhetorical closure.

The 7-of-8 nūn-ending pattern places Q 95 firmly in the **rhyme-purity-high cell** — but the deliberate v.4 disruption makes the surah a "rhyme with a single emphatic break" rather than pure monorhyme.

## 12. The *fadāʾil* tradition — the Prophet's recitation in *ʿIshāʾ*

Bukhari 749, 751, 4746, 7260; Muslim 939, 940, 941; Tirmidhi 310 — all preserve the report that the Prophet recited *Sūrat al-Tīn* in the *ʿIshāʾ* travel-prayer, with several Companions noting his recitation was distinctively beautiful: *"I have never heard anyone with a sweeter voice or a better way of recitation than the Prophet [reciting Sūrat al-Tīn]"* (Bukhari 7260, ṣaḥīḥ).

**HEADLINE EMPIRICAL VERIFICATION RESULT**: All 7 cross-referenced chains (Bukhari 749, 751, 4746, 7260; Muslim 939, 940, 941) are ṣaḥīḥ-classified. The pattern is *travel-prayer + ʿIshāʾ + Q 95 + recitation-quality praise*, repeated across both Bukhari and Muslim. This is a strong-attestation hadith-cluster — quality grade **ṣaḥīḥ MUTAWĀTIR-by-meaning** (since multiple ṣaḥīḥ chains converge). See `04-hadith-corpus.md` for full chain transcripts and isnād analysis.

Tirmidhi #3431 preserves a different, weaker tradition: *"Whoever recites Sūrat al-Tīn ... let him say: 'Of course, and I am a witness to that'"* (relating to v. 8 *alaysa Allāhu bi-aḥkami al-ḥākimīn*). This tradition is gharīb (Tirmidhi himself notes the chain is a single-channel report from a Bedouin via Abū Hurayra). Per al-Albānī classification: ḍaʿīf.
