# Q 47 — Tafsīr Survey

Five classical mufassirūn. All citations are scholar + work + passage, per Protocol §1.6. Where the project's on-disk OpenITI extracts contain Q 47 passages, file paths are cited; where the citation rests on the secondary apparatus (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* or other catalogued sources), the secondary source is named explicitly.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

**Source**: al-Ṭabarī (d. 310 AH), *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*, on Q 47.

**Q 47:1** — al-Ṭabarī treats *aḍalla aʿmālahum* as the structural opening for the surah's two-camp logic: deeds become *ḍalāl* (lost/astray) for those who block the path of Allah. He treats the lack-of-preface as intentional — Q 47 launches mid-argument because it answers earlier Meccan surahs' theological framing with a Medinan praxis-instruction.

**Q 47:2** — the *bi-mā nuzzila ʿalā Muḥammad* clause is read as a *taʿyīn* (specification) clause distinguishing this revelation from prior scriptures. al-Ṭabarī emphasizes that Muḥammad's revelation is *al-ḥaqq min rabbihim* — explicitly aligning the surah's *belief* condition with Muḥammad-revelation specifically.

**Q 47:4** (*fa-ḍrabū al-riqāb*) — al-Ṭabarī treats this as a **tactical-level battle injunction** (not a categorical universal), discussing *thakhanahum* (subdue/dominate) — when may *fidāʾ* (ransom) be taken vs when must captives be killed/freed. He cites companion-era practice (ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb's policy) and explicitly excludes non-combatants. The *ḥattā taḍaʿa al-ḥarbu awzārahā* clause is read as a temporal qualifier — the injunction holds *until war has laid down its burdens*, not as a perpetual command.

**Q 47:31** (*la-nablu wannakum*) — al-Ṭabarī reads this as a meta-statement on divine knowledge: God already knows, but the *ibtilāʾ* (testing) is a public manifestation so that the *mujāhidīn* and *ṣābirīn* are *known to themselves and to each other*. This is a recurring theological structure (cf. Q 2:155, Q 3:140-141).

## 2. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

**Source**: al-Rāzī (d. 606 AH), *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, on Q 47. Cross-references in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-99names-extract.md` provide al-Rāzī's general method anchors.

**Q 47:2** — al-Rāzī's *bi-mā nuzzila ʿalā Muḥammad* analysis is read as a *taʿyīn* (specification) clause. Comparing to Q 3:144 ("Muhammad is only a messenger"), Q 33:40 ("seal of the prophets"), and Q 48:29 ("Muhammad messenger of Allah"), al-Rāzī observes that the 4 explicit-naming verses each play a different role: Q 3:144 is *istidrāk* (clarification after Uḥud); Q 33:40 is *takhṣīṣ* (finality-doctrine); Q 47:2 is *taʿyīn* (revelation-source); Q 48:29 is *taʿrīf* (identification with characteristics). This per-verse-functional differentiation is one of al-Rāzī's distinctive contributions.

**Q 47:4** — al-Rāzī treats *fa-ḍrabū al-riqāb* as *iḥkām al-jihād* — establishing the legal-juridical framework for the conduct of war. He distinguishes:
- The *imma mannan baʿdu* (free release with favor) option
- The *imma fidāʾan* (ransom) option
- The conditioning clause *ḥattā taḍaʿa al-ḥarbu awzārahā* (until war lays down its burdens)
- The wisdom-clause *wa-law yashāʾu Allāhu la-ntaṣara minhum wa-lākin li-yablu baʿḍakum bi-baʿḍin* (Allah could defeat them directly, but tests one by means of another).

**Q 47:20** — al-Rāzī notes that the verse SELF-NAMES the surah's genre: *fa-iḏā unzilat sūratun muḥkamatun wa-dhukira fīhā al-qitāl*. He cites this as the classical anchor for the alternate name *sūrat al-Qitāl*.

**Q 47:24** (*afa-lā yatadabbarūna al-Qurʾān*) — al-Rāzī reads this as the FOUNDATIONAL verse for the discipline of *tadabbur* (deep reflection on the Qur'an). He treats it as a meta-rhetorical address to those who fail to *muddabir* the text — anchor-verse for ʿulūm al-Qurʾān as a science. This is the closest Qurʾānic self-instruction to *tafsīr-as-discipline*.

## 3. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**Source**: al-Qurṭubī (d. 671 AH), *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, kitāb of Q 47.

al-Qurṭubī's distinctive contribution to Q 47 tafsīr is the **legal-extraction (aḥkām) lens**:

- **Q 47:4**: al-Qurṭubī extracts a multi-clause jurisprudential reading — (a) *fa-ḍrabū al-riqāb* establishes neck-strike as the lawful battlefield-execution form; (b) *ḥattā idhā athkhantumūhum* establishes a threshold of victory (subjugation, not extermination); (c) *fa-shuddū al-wathāq* establishes captive-binding as the post-victory step; (d) *fa-immā mannan baʿdu wa-immā fidāʾan* establishes the two options of post-victory disposition (free release or ransom); (e) *ḥattā taḍaʿa al-ḥarbu awzārahā* establishes the temporal boundary of the injunction. He cites Mālik's *Muwaṭṭaʾ* and al-Shāfiʿī on the jurisprudential extensions.

- **Naming**: al-Qurṭubī explicitly prefers the name *sūrat al-Qitāl* (after Q 47:20) over *sūrat Muḥammad* (after Q 47:2), arguing that *al-qitāl* is the more characteristic theme of the surah and that the name *Muḥammad* appears in 4 surahs whereas *al-qitāl* with its juridical attendant content is concentrated here.

- **Hudaybiyya-cluster pairing**: al-Qurṭubī notes the Q 47-Q 48 thematic pairing — Q 47 establishes the war-conduct framework; Q 48 narrates the specific Hudaybiyya event in which that framework was tested by the Treaty.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**Source**: Ibn Kathīr (d. 774 AH), *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*, on Q 47. OpenITI Arabic on disk + Darussalam English partial.

**Q 47:2** — Ibn Kathīr emphasizes the *iqtirān* (linkage) of three conditions in this verse for sin-effacement (*sayyiʾāt-takhfīf*): (a) belief, (b) righteous deeds, (c) belief-in-what-was-revealed-to-Muḥammad. This *triple condition* is read as the Medinan refinement of the more general Meccan dual condition (belief + righteous deeds).

**Q 47:15** (4-river parable) — Ibn Kathīr cites a chain of asanīd connecting the verse to descriptions of Paradise's rivers in fadāʾil-of-paradise hadith. He treats the 4-river structure as eschatological-architectural (a specific topology of Paradise), not a metaphor of generic abundance.

**Q 47:22** — Ibn Kathīr cites the Bukhārī tafsīr-cluster at #4623-4625 in which the Prophet himself instructed recitation of *fa-hal ʿasaytum* (Q 47:22) in the context of severing-kinship warnings. (See `04-hadith-corpus.md` for the verified Bukhārī numbers — this is a corpus-VERIFIED Ibn Kathīr citation.)

**Q 47:38** (closing *infāq* verse) — Ibn Kathīr reads the closing *innamā yabkhalu ʿan nafsihi* as the theological framework for the entire surah: stinginess is self-harm, not divine harm; the Allāh-al-ghanīy clause anchors the divine self-sufficiency that frames the entire war-and-infāq instructional pericope.

## 5. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf*

**Source**: al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538 AH), *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq al-tanzīl*, on Q 47.

al-Zamakhsharī is distinctive for his *balāgha* (rhetorical) lens on Q 47:

- **Q 47:1** — al-Zamakhsharī notes the *iltifāt*-effect of the unprefixed opening (no *bismillāh* as preface in narrative-time; jumping straight to the relative clause *alladhīna kafarū*). This is read as a rhetorical compression — the surah skips the conventional opening because its argument starts mid-stream.

- **Q 47:15** (4-river parable) — al-Zamakhsharī treats the parallel-syntactic structure (4 *anhārun min X ghayru/lam-yataghayyar/laḏḏatun/muṣaffā*) as a deliberate balāgha-architecture — each river has a contrastive modifier that pairs with a worldly-imperfection (water-staleness, milk-souring, wine-distress, honey-impurity). This is one of the Qur'ān's most-formally-symmetric parable structures.

- **Q 47:20** — al-Zamakhsharī notes the *muḥkama* (clear) qualifier for the surah whose mention is *qitāl* — the surah is muḥkama precisely because its juridical content cannot be metaphorized. This is a meta-classification: *sūrat al-Qitāl* is a *muḥkama* surah, not a *mutashābih* surah.

## 6. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar* (supplementary)

**Source**: al-Biqāʿī (d. 885 AH), *Naẓm al-Durar*, on Q 46→Q 47→Q 48. PDF: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf`.

al-Biqāʿī's distinctive contribution is the **3-surah munāsabah**: Q 46 (al-Aḥqāf) → Q 47 (Muḥammad) → Q 48 (al-Fatḥ) form a **war-permission → war-instruction → conquest-promise** sequence:

- Q 46:35 (al-Aḥqāf closing): "Be patient as the firmly-resolute messengers were patient" — implicit war-permission.
- Q 47:4 (the al-riqāb verse): the instruction-frame for what comes next.
- Q 48:1-3 (al-Fatḥ opening): "We have given you a manifest victory" — the conquest-promise that frames Hudaybiyya as a *fatḥ*.

This 3-surah ring is partially-empirically vindicated (Q 47-Q 48 TSP-cheap pair) and partially-NULL (3-tuple cohesion test Q047-F-03 returned NULL). See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for the full audit.

## 7. Convergences & disagreements

| Issue | al-Ṭabarī | al-Rāzī | al-Qurṭubī | Ibn Kathīr | al-Zamakhsharī |

| Q 47-Q 48 pair | not emphasized | not emphasized | EMPHASIZED | EMPHASIZED | not emphasized |

**Convergences**:
1. Q 47:4 is a tactical-level conditional injunction, not a perpetual universal.
2. Q 47:2's *bi-mā nuzzila ʿalā Muḥammad* clause is a *taʿyīn* (specification) of the revelation-source.
3. Q 47:20 self-names the surah's qitāl-genre (al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Zamakhsharī explicit; others implicit).

**Disagreements**:
1. Surah-name preference: al-Qurṭubī chooses *al-Qitāl*; others retain *Muḥammad*.
2. Q 47-Q 48 munāsabah: al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr foreground the Hudaybiyya-cluster pairing; al-Biqāʿī extends to a 3-surah ring (Q 46-47-48); al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī treat each surah more independently.
3. Q 47:15 4-river parable interpretation: Ibn Kathīr eschatological-literal; al-Zamakhsharī balāgha-symmetric; al-Ṭabarī both.
