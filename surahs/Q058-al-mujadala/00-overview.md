## 2. Classical name and the eponymous woman

The surah is named **al-Mujādala** after the opening verb *tujādiluka* ("she pleads with you") in v.1. The Arabic root is j-d-l ("to twist a rope tightly", metaphorically "to argue back-and-forth"). The unnamed pleader of v.1 is identified by the classical asbāb al-nuzūl tradition as **Khawla bint Thaʿlaba** (also rendered *Khuwayla bint Mālik b. Thaʿlaba* in some chains) — the wife of **Aws b. al-Ṣāmit**, brother of ʿUbāda b. al-Ṣāmit (the Anṣārī Companion).

al-Ṭabarī ad loc. preserves the disagreement on her nasab in five chains:
- *Khawla bint Thaʿlaba* (majority view)
- *Khuwayla bint Thaʿlaba* (variant pronunciation)
- *Khuwayla bint Khuwaylid* (al-Wāqidī chain)
- *Khuwayla bint al-Ṣāmit* (variant)
- *Khuwayla bint al-Dulayj* (Abū l-ʿĀliya chain)

The narrative arc preserved across al-Wāḥidī's *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* and al-Ṭabarī's *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* (verified at Q058-F-03):

Aws b. al-Ṣāmit pronounced upon his wife the pre-Islamic divorce-formula *anti ʿalayya ka-ẓahri ummī* ("you are to me as the back of my mother"), the formula known as *ẓihār* (from the noun *ẓahr* = back). In pre-Islamic legal practice, *ẓihār* was equivalent to permanent dissolution. Khawla came to the Prophet pleading for relief — "I have grown old and my flesh has wasted in his service" — and the Prophet at first told her *ḥurmta ʿalayhi* ("you are unlawful to him"). She kept pleading: *ashkū ilā Allāhi fāqatī* ("I complain to God of my poverty"). Then the surah was revealed.

The eponymy is a tight three-axis lock:
1. The surah TITLE is from her ACT (the verb *tujādilu* in v.1).
2. The surah CONTENT abolishes the legal mechanism by which her husband had erased her status (vv.2-4).
3. The surah CONCLUSION (v.22) identifies the believing community as *ḥizb Allāh*, "the party of God," as the destined-victorious party — a frame in which the small-and-weak (Khawla) is upheld against the powerful-and-formal (the inherited *ẓihār* of pre-Islamic patriarchy).

al-Wāḥidī (in the rūḥ-poetic-tafsīr extension preserved in *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* §58:1, here in English): "A hundred thousand proximate angels, glorifying and hallowing, were diving in the oceans of bows and prostrations and raising the voices of glorification and hallowing at the Exalted Threshold, but no one talked about them. But that poor, weak woman — the disputer who wept before the Threshold in burning and bewilderment and who complained of her despair — look how the Splendorous Qurʾān wrote the inscription of exaltation on the cape of her secret whispering: 'God has heard the words of her who disputes with thee concerning her husband.'"

ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb is reported by al-Wāḥidī to have said to those who would silence her in his caliphate: *uskutū! aḥtarimū hādhihi al-marʾata al-ḍaʿīfata; hādhihi al-latī samiʿa Allāhu kalāmahā min fawqi sabʿi samāwātin* ("Be silent! Show respect for this poor woman! She is the woman whose words the Real heard from beyond the seven levels of heaven").

## 6. The classical abrogation claim — Q 58:12-13 najwā-charity

**Brief asked:** "Q 58:12-13 najwā-charity verses + claimed mid-revelation abrogation: classical claim audit."

**Verdict:** **CONFIRMED** at the classical-textual-attestation layer (Q058-F-03).

| Audit dimension | Sources | Verdict |
|:--|:--:|:--|
| Independent classical sources attesting abrogation | 3 (al-Wāḥidī Asbāb v13; al-Ṭabarī Tafsīr v12; al-Ṭabarī Tafsīr v13) | A1 ≥ 2 ✓ |
| Independent isnad chains | 3 (ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib named; Mujāhid via Ibn Abī Najīḥ; Qatāda) | A2 ≥ 1 ✓ |
| Lexical-surface markers in the verse pair | 5 / 5 (singular *ṣadaqa* in v.12; plural *ṣadaqāt* in v.13; *fa-idh lam tafʿalū*; *wa-tāba Allāhu ʿalaykum*; *fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāta wa-ātū al-zakāta*) | A3 = 5 ✓ |

The classical narrative arc:

Q 58:12 commands believers, when consulting the Prophet privately (*idhā nājaytum al-rasūla*), to first present a charity (*ṣadaqatan*) before their consultation. al-Wāḥidī preserves the canonical ʿAlī tradition: *qāla ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu: inna fī Kitābi Allāhi laʾāyatan mā ʿamala bihā aḥadun qablī wa-lā yaʿmalu bihā aḥadun baʿdī: yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū idhā nājaytum al-rasūla fa-qaddimū bayna yaday najwākum ṣadaqatan, kāna lī dīnārun fa-bāʿtuhu bi-ʿasharati darāhima fa-kuntu kullamā nājaytu al-rasūla qaddamtu bayna yaday najwāya dirhaman, thumma nusikhat bi-l-āyati al-ukhrā: aʾashfaqtum an tuqaddimū bayna yaday najwākum ṣadaqāt* ("There is one verse in God's Book that no one acted on before me, and no one will act on after me. I had a piece of gold which I exchanged for ten silver pieces, and whenever I conferred with the Messenger I spent one silver piece in charity until I spent them all. Then the verse was abrogated by another verse [Q 58:13]").

al-Ṭabarī independently transmits the same tradition via Mujāhid (Ibn Abī Najīḥ chain). Qatāda's transmission is preserved in al-Ṭabarī's commentary on Q 58:13: *ay farīḍatāni wājibatāni lā rajʿata li-aḥadin fīhimā fa-nasakhat hādhihi al-āyatu mā kāna qablahā min amri al-ṣadaqa fī al-najwā* ("Two binding obligations admitting no withdrawal; this verse abrogated what came before it of the najwā-charity command").

The lexical-surface evidence is consistent: Q 58:12 uses *ṣadaqatan* (singular accusative) at the foot of the operative clause; Q 58:13 uses *ṣadaqāt* (plural accusative), preserves the same construction *bayna yaday najwākum*, and explicitly says *fa-idh lam tafʿalū wa-tāba Allāhu ʿalaykum* ("and since you have not done it and Allāh has turned to you in mercy"), then prescribes the *fa-aqīmū al-ṣalāta wa-ātū al-zakāta* substitute.

This is a **canonical example of intra-surah abrogation** in the Qurʾān (al-Suyūṭī *Itqān*, nawʿ 47, treats it alongside fewer than 5 attested cases). The classical-claim audit verdict: **CLASSICAL CLAIM TEXTUALLY VERIFIED at multiple independent isnads + multiple independent classical-tafsīr sources + complete lexical-surface evidence within the verse pair**.

The claim that the period of practice was VERY brief is preserved in the same tradition: ʿAlī's "no one before me, no one after me" suggests the obligation may have been operative for only a short time — possibly only the duration of ʿAlī's ten consultations. This is a striking case of QURʾANIC INTRA-SURAH LEGAL EVOLUTION recorded within the same surah's text.

Q 58 is therefore the Quran's **single explicit lexical-introduction of the *ḥizb*-binary** as a theological category (Q058-F-04).
