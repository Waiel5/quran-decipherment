# Q 39 al-Zumar — Overview

## 1. Name and Etymology

The surah's eponym derives from the masculine plural noun *zumar* (singular *zumra*) — "throngs", "companies", "groups marching in formation". The root *z-m-r* in classical Arabic carries the sense of a company that walks together with cadence, a band moving as a unit, and by extension a chorus or a column of marchers. Lane's *Lexicon* (vol. 3, p. 1247) traces *zumra* to *zamara* "to fill" or "to make full" (a vessel, a gathering); thus a *zumra* is a gathered, filled-out company.

The root *z-m-r* appears EXACTLY twice in the entire Quran, both within this surah, both in the eschatological ledger of vv. 71-73:

- **Q 39:71** — *wa-sīqa alladhīna kafarū ilā Jahannama **zumaran*** ("and those who disbelieved are driven to Hell in throngs")
- **Q 39:73** — *wa-sīqa alladhīna ittaqaw rabbahum ilā al-Jannati **zumaran*** ("and those who feared their Lord are driven to the Garden in throngs")

This is corpus-EXACT, and the eponym is doubly load-bearing: the surah names itself after its terminal eschatological motif. This is verified from QAC v0.4 morphology.

A secondary classical name is *Sūrat al-Ghuraf* — "the Surah of the High Chambers" — from v. 20 (*lakin alladhīna ittaqaw rabbahum lahum **ghurafun*** *min fawqihā ghurafun mabniyyatun*) and v. 75 (*ḥāffīna min ḥawl al-ʿarsh*), both of which provide imagery of the elevated dwellings and the angelic ring around the throne. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ*, opening of Q 39) records this alternative name and attributes it to Wahb b. Munabbih: "*qāla Wahb b. Munabbih: man aḥabba an yaʿrifa qaḍāʾa Allāhi…*"

Per the pre-registered Q039-F-01 test of this work (PASS-DIRECTED, perm-p = 0.000300 on variance), the 6-surah tanzīl-cluster has a STRONGLY-CONCENTRATED Nöldeke distribution (variance 39.92 vs null mean ~967.5). The cluster is effectively a single Late-Meccan compositional moment.

The classical chronology assignment is consistent with Nöldeke. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr*, opening of Q 39) records the surah as Meccan, citing Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Qurṭubī agrees. The lone debated verses are vv. 52-54 (some traditions place these as Medinan via the famous *yā ʿibādiya alladhīna asrafū* asbāb al-nuzūl that involves Wahshī b. Ḥarb's conversion), but classical consensus retains the surah as Meccan with these verses as Meccan-revealed and only USED with reference to Wahshī later. We treat the surah as integrally Meccan per Nöldeke and the dominant classical tradition.

## 4. Opening Formula — *tanzīl al-kitāb min Allāh al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm*

Q 39:1 is six orthographic words: *تنزيل الكتاب من الله العزيز الحكيم* — "The revelation of the Book from Allah, the Mighty, the Wise". This is the **tanzīl-al-kitāb book-self-announcement opener**. It places Q 39 in the H-NEW-1100 corpus-EXACT 6-surah cluster:

| Surah | Verse | Form |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Q 32:2 | v.2 | *tanzīl al-kitāb lā rayba fīh min rabb al-ʿālamīn* |
| **Q 39:1** | **v.1** | ***tanzīl al-kitāb min Allāh al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm*** |
| Q 40:2 | v.2 | *tanzīl al-kitāb min Allāh al-ʿAzīz al-ʿAlīm* |
| Q 41:2 | v.2 | *tanzīl min al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm* |
| Q 45:2 | v.2 | *tanzīl al-kitāb min Allāh al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm* |
| Q 46:2 | v.2 | *tanzīl al-kitāb min Allāh al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm* |

Three observations:

1. **Q 39 is alone in placing the formula at v.1, not v.2** — every other tanzīl-cluster surah opens with muqaṭṭāʿat at v.1 (الم or حم) and the formula at v.2. Q 39, having no muqaṭṭāʿat, places the formula immediately. This is corpus-EXACT placement of the opener at the truly-first verse.

2. **Q 39, Q 45, Q 46 use the IDENTICAL formula** *al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm*. They form a sub-triple within the cluster. The other three surahs vary the divine-name pair (Q 40 *al-ʿAlīm*, Q 41 *al-Raḥmān al-Raḥīm*, Q 32 *rabb al-ʿālamīn*).

3. **At v.1, three orthographic words are divine-name tokens**: *Allāh*, *al-ʿAzīz*, *al-Ḥakīm*. With six total words, this is divine-name-density 0.500 — TIED with Q 40:2, Q 45:2, Q 46:2 for the densest tanzīl-cluster verse, and joining Q 59:23 as the corpus-MAX divine-name density tier (≥0.500).

The opener also fulfills cross-finding-008 (muqaṭṭāʿat as book-introduction markers): Q 39 self-references *al-kitāb* in its first 2 words, in a phrase that explicitly declares its own scriptural status. cross-finding-008 documents that 24/29 muqaṭṭāʿat surahs reference *kitāb* or *qurʾān* in v.1-3 (p = 3 × 10⁻¹²). Q 39 does so without bearing the marker, putting it in the "scripture-announcement-content" Pattern-B layer that cross-finding-012 identifies.

## 6. Rhyme — Nūn-dominant *fāṣila*

70.7% of Q 39's verses end in the letter ن (53 of 75). The next-most-common is ر at 8.0% (6 verses), then م at 6.7% (5 verses). This is consistent with the broader Late-Meccan phonological signature where nūn-ending fāṣila dominates (see also Q 40, Q 41, Q 45, Q 46 — all nūn-rhymed at >60%). The rhyme entropy is 1.095 nats (h-new-750 entry), which is moderate — neither monorhymed (Q 55, Q 113) nor highly variegated.

The minor rhyme-shifts in Q 39 cluster around topical breaks:
- v. 72 ends in ر (*al-mutakabbirīn*) — a strong jarring deflection from the dominant nūn, marking the moment the disbelievers are told to enter Jahannam.
- v. 75 ends in ن (*al-ʿālamīn*) — the closing benediction's nūn fits the dominant rhyme, providing closure.

## 7. Key Thematic Blocks

The surah subdivides into recognizable units that classical tafsir tradition (al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar* at Q 39 in particular) has systematized. The blocks reflect a canonical Late-Meccan tawḥīd-eschatology architecture:

### Block A (vv. 1-6): Tawḥīd Declaration and Cosmic Witness
The surah opens with the *tanzīl al-kitāb* declaration, immediately pivots to a tawḥīd-imperative: *fa-ʿbud Allāh mukhliṣan lahu al-dīn* (v. 2), then critiques the polytheist intercession-doctrine (*alā li-llāhi al-dīn al-khāliṣ wa-alladhīna ittakhadhū min dūnihi awliyāʾa mā naʿbuduhum illā li-yuqarribūnā ilā Allāh zulfā* v. 3). The block closes with cosmic imagery: heavens and earth created in truth, day-night cycle, sun-moon subordination (vv. 5-6).

### Block B (vv. 7-9): Existential Address
The grim descriptions of human ingratitude (*idhā massa al-insāna ḍurrun daʿā rabbahu munīban ilayhi* v. 8) contrast with the *qul-imperative* mode that begins to dominate from v. 8 onward.

### Block C (vv. 10-21): Qul-Cluster and Eschatological Outcomes
A concentrated *qul* cluster (vv. 10, 11, 13, 14, 15) establishes the prophetic announcement-mode. v. 14 is the doctrinal anchor: *quli Allāha aʿbudu mukhliṣan lahu dīnī*. Block transitions to fire/garden description (vv. 16-21), including the *al-Ghuraf* imagery (v. 20).

### Block D (vv. 22-29): Rhetorical-Theological Heart
**v. 22**: *a-fa-man sharaḥa Allāhu ṣadrahu li-l-Islām fa-huwa ʿalā nūrin min rabbihi…*
**v. 23**: *Allāh nazzala aḥsana al-ḥadīthi **kitāban mutashābihan mathāniya tatashaqqaru minhu juludu alladhīna yakhshawna rabbahum…*** — the foundational classical *mathānī* doctrine anchor (cross-referenced at Q 15:87 and the foundational MASTER-LEDGER §3 entry on T5 *mathānī* topology test).
**v. 24-29**: parable of the slave with multiple owners (*ḍaraba mathalan rajulan fīhi shurakāʾu mutashākisūna…* v. 29) — illustrating the contradiction of polytheism.

### Block E (vv. 30-31): Mortality of the Prophet
**v. 30**: *innaka mayyitun wa-innahum mayyitūn* — the affirmation of the Prophet's mortality. This verse is famously cited by Abū Bakr in his eulogy at the death of the Prophet (Bukhari #3511, with Q 3:144 invoked alongside it). See also 04-hadith-corpus.md.

### Block F (vv. 32-52): Theological Confrontation
Long extended dialogue/refutation cycle. Refrain *qul* imperatives (vv. 38, 39, 43, 44, 46). The block builds to the powerful v. 42: *Allāh yatawaffā al-anfusa ḥīna mawtihā…* (Allah takes the souls at the time of their death) — one of the corpus's most metaphysically explicit statements of divine custody of consciousness.

### Block G (vv. 53-61): Mercy-Apex and Consequence
**v. 53**: *qul yā ʿibādiya alladhīna asrafū ʿalā anfusihim lā taqnaṭū min raḥmati Allāh inna Allāha yaghfiru al-dhunūba jamīʿan innahu huwa al-Ghafūru al-Raḥīm* — the unmatched-mercy verse. This is the surah's apex. Bukhari #4604, Muslim #229, Tirmidhī #3321, Abū Dāwūd #4275, Nasāʾī #4013 all transmit the asbāb al-nuzūl reporting that polytheist murderers and adulterers came to the Prophet asking whether their sins could be expiated; Q 39:53 + Q 25:68-70 was the response. Block continues with the consequence vv. 54-61.

### Block H (vv. 62-67): Cosmic Sovereignty
**v. 67**: *wa-mā qadarū Allāha ḥaqqa qadrihi wa-l-arḍu jamīʿan qabḍatuhu yawma al-qiyāmati wa-l-samawātu maṭwiyyātun bi-yamīnihi subḥānahu wa-taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn* — the cosmic-power verse, transmitted in Bukhari #4605, #7131, #7132 and Muslim #6872, #6873 with the famous *ḍaḥika al-nabī ḥattā badat nawājidhuh* reaction to a Jewish scholar's report.

### Block I (vv. 68-75): Eschatological Throng-Cycle
**v. 68**: the trumpet (the first blast).
**v. 70**: the books are laid open and judgment commences.
**vv. 71-72**: disbelievers driven *zumaran* into Jahannam.
**vv. 73-74**: pious driven *zumaran* into the Garden, with the angelic *salām ʿalaykum ṭibtum*.
**v. 75**: angels in concentric ring around the Throne, glorifying their Lord; final benediction *wa-qīla al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabb al-ʿālamīn*.

## 8. Classical Positioning

al-Suyūṭī (*Itqān*, nawʿ on classification of surahs by genre) places Q 39 with the tawḥīd-iʿtiqād cluster of Late Meccan surahs. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar* opening of Q 39, ~25 manuscript pages of preface in the Cairo edition) frames the surah's *gharaḍ* (overarching purpose) as: *iqāmat al-ḥujja ʿalā tawḥīd Allāh tabāraka wa-taʿālā fī al-ulūhiyya wa-l-rubūbiyya wa-aḥkāmihi al-fiʿliyya wa-l-jazāʾiyya yawm al-qiyāma* — "the establishment of the proof for the oneness of Allah, exalted be He, in divinity and lordship, and in His acts and recompense on the Day of Resurrection". al-Qurṭubī agrees with the tawḥīd-eschatology framing and notes the classical alternative name *Sūrat al-Ghuraf*.

Al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, opening of Q 39) provides the most extensive opening framing, treating the surah as a discourse on divine sincerity (*ikhlāṣ*) and its opposite (*shirk*). The *xlS* root attestations at vv. 2, 3, 11, 14 are central to his reading.

The classical-modern positioning consensus is consistent: Q 39 is one of the canonical Late-Meccan tawḥīd-iʿtiqād major surahs, immediately preceding the Hawamim cluster, sharing their *tanzīl al-kitāb* opener formula but lacking their muqaṭṭāʿat. It is sometimes paired with Q 38 (Ṣād) and Q 40 (Ghāfir) as part of the classical *al-mathnā* (after the seven *ṭiwāl*, before the ḥawāmīm). Q 39 is a meditation on sincere devotion, the inadequacy of polytheist intercession, the mortality of all human beings (including the Prophet), and the divine custody of souls and the cosmos.

## 11. The Two Famous Verses

Two Q 39 verses are among the most-quoted in the entire Quran:

**Q 39:53** — *qul yā ʿibādiya alladhīna asrafū ʿalā anfusihim lā taqnaṭū min raḥmati Allāh inna Allāha yaghfiru al-dhunūba jamīʿan innahu huwa al-Ghafūru al-Raḥīm* — "Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah; indeed Allah forgives all sins; He is the Forgiving, the Merciful." Per Bukhari #4604 + Muslim #229 + Tirmidhī #3321 + Abū Dāwūd #4275 + Nasāʾī #4013, the verse was revealed in connection with polytheist murderers and adulterers seeking expiation; Tirmidhī's transmission via Asmāʾ bint Yazīd has the Prophet reciting the verse as expansive of mercy *wa-lā yubālī* ("and He does not mind").

**Q 39:67** — *wa-mā qadarū Allāha ḥaqqa qadrihi wa-l-arḍu jamīʿan qabḍatuhu yawma al-qiyāmati wa-l-samawātu maṭwiyyātun bi-yamīnihi subḥānahu wa-taʿālā ʿammā yushrikūn* — "And they have not appraised Allah with true appraisal: the whole earth will be in His grip on the Day of Resurrection, and the heavens will be folded in His right hand…" Bukhari #4605, #7131, #7132 + Muslim #6872, #6873 transmit the famous *ḍaḥika al-nabī ḥattā badat nawājidhuh* ("the Prophet smiled until his molars showed") response to a Jewish scholar's report on this image.

These two verses anchor the surah in two opposite registers: divine mercy expansive beyond despair (v. 53), divine sovereignty surpassing human comprehension (v. 67). Together they establish the surah's theological poles.
