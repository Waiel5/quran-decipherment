# Q 39 al-Zumar — Verse-by-Verse Content Analysis

This document presents a verse-cycle-level reading of Q 39 organized around the surah's nine identifiable thematic blocks (per al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar* segmentation cross-checked against al-Rāzī's *Mafātīḥ* introduction to Q 39). Verse-numbers cited from the Hafs-Kufan canonical numbering. Arabic phrases given without diacritics where computational identity matters; with full vocalization where prosodic recitation is the point.

## Block A (vv. 1-6) — Tawḥīd Declaration and Cosmic Witness

**v. 1** *تنزيل الكتاب من الله العزيز الحكيم* — "The revelation of the Book from Allah, the Mighty, the Wise."

The opening is a declarative statement, not a vocative, address, or oath. It sets the scriptural identity of the surah explicitly and immediately. Three of the six orthographic words are divine-name tokens (*Allāh*, *al-ʿAzīz*, *al-Ḥakīm*) — divine-name density 0.500, tied with Q 40:2, Q 45:2, Q 46:2 as the corpus-MAX density tier. The phrase *tanzīl al-kitāb min Allāh al-ʿAzīz al-Ḥakīm* is verbatim shared with Q 45:2 and Q 46:2; Q 40:2 differs only in *al-ʿAlīm* (instead of *al-Ḥakīm*) — see H-NEW-1100.

**v. 2** *إنا أنزلنا إليك الكتاب بالحق فاعبد الله مخلصا له الدين* — "We have sent down the Book to you in truth, so worship Allah making your devotion sincere to Him."

The first occurrence of the *xlS* root in Q 39 (the doctrinal anchor of the surah). The imperative *fa-ʿbud* structures the entire surah's address to the Prophet; *mukhliṣan lahu al-dīn* will be repeated at vv. 11 and 14 with intensifying force. al-Rāzī notes that the order — first declaring the Book's truth, then commanding sincere devotion — establishes that *ʿibāda mukhliṣa* (sincere worship) is the GROUND for receiving scripture.

**v. 3** *ألا لله الدين الخالص ۚ والذين اتخذوا من دونه أولياء ما نعبدهم إلا ليقربونا إلى الله زلفى…* — "Indeed, sincere religion is for Allah alone; and those who have taken protectors besides Him [say]: We worship them only that they may bring us nearer to Allah."

The second xlS attestation (*al-dīn al-khāliṣ*). The verse anchors the Quranic critique of polytheist intercession-doctrine (*shafāʿa* via lesser deities, common in pre-Islamic Mecca and in some Christian Trinitarian formulations as the surah elsewhere implicitly addresses). The pre-Islamic Meccan polytheist self-defense — *we don't really worship them, we just use them as proximity-tools to Allah* — is quoted *and* refuted. This is the surah's central argumentative move.

**v. 4** *لو أراد الله أن يتخذ ولدا لاصطفى مما يخلق ما يشاء ۚ سبحانه ۖ هو الله الواحد القهار* — "Had Allah willed to take a son, He would have chosen what He pleased from what He creates. Glory be to Him! He is Allah, the One, the Subduer."

A counterfactual refutation of the divine-son doctrine. *al-Wāḥid al-Qahhār* (the One, the Subduer) appears 6 times in the corpus across surahs (Q 12:39, Q 13:16, Q 14:48, Q 38:65, Q 39:4, Q 40:16) — a Late Meccan / Hawamim signature. *al-Qahhār* is the dominating-force divine name; it pairs naturally with *al-Wāḥid* against Trinitarian or polytheist multiplicities.

**v. 5** *خلق السماوات والأرض بالحق ۖ يكور الليل على النهار ويكور النهار على الليل…* — "He created the heavens and the earth in truth; He wraps the night around the day and the day around the night..."

Cosmic-creation imagery; the verb *yukawwiru* (he wraps/coils) on the day-night cycle is one of Q 39's two distinctive Late-Meccan astronomical phrases (the other being the *taqashʿarra minhu juludu* at v. 23 — the only verb-form *taqashʿarra* in the corpus, applied to skin shivering at hearing the Quran). *yukawwiru* recurs only at Q 81:1 (*idhā al-shamsu kuwwirat*) — the eschatological darkening of the sun.

**v. 6** *خلقكم من نفس واحدة ثم جعل منها زوجها وأنزل لكم من الأنعام ثمانية أزواج…* — "He created you from one soul, then made from it its mate, and sent down to you eight pairs of cattle..."

The *nafs wāḥida* (one soul) anthropogenic-creation formula appears at 5 sites in the corpus: Q 4:1, Q 6:98, Q 7:189, Q 31:28, Q 39:6. (See Q004-F-04 PRE-REG: *nafs wāḥida* twin between Q 4:1 and Q 39:6 — DIRECTIONAL outcome, MASTER §10.27 entry.) Q 39:6 also includes the famous *thalāth ẓulumāt* phrase: *yakhluqukum fī buṭūni ummahātikum khalqan min baʿdi khalqin fī ẓulumātin thalāth* — embryological-darkness imagery.

## Block B (vv. 7-9) — Existential Address

**v. 7** *إن تكفروا فإن الله غني عنكم…* — "If you disbelieve, Allah is independent of you..."

A swift pivot from cosmic to ethical: divine self-sufficiency, ingratitude as the crux of human waywardness. The verse establishes the *kfr*-vs-*shukr* polarity that runs through the whole surah.

**v. 8** *وإذا مس الإنسان ضر دعا ربه منيبا إليه…* — "And when adversity touches man, he calls upon his Lord, turning [in repentance] to Him..."

The first *qul* imperative is at v. 9 below; v. 8 prepares for the qul-cluster by sketching the human ethical inconsistency: turning to Allah in distress, forgetting in ease. *munīban ilayhi* — turning back, in repentance — is a Late-Meccan tawḥīd-iʿtiqād specialty term.

**v. 9** *أمن هو قانت آناء الليل ساجدا وقائما يحذر الآخرة ويرجو رحمة ربه ۗ قل هل يستوي الذين يعلمون والذين لا يعلمون ۗ إنما يتذكر أولو الألباب* — "Is one who is devoutly obedient during periods of the night, prostrating and standing, fearing the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord — [is he like one who is otherwise]? Say: Are those who know equal to those who do not know? Only people of understanding remember."

The first *qul* of the surah, at v. 9. The rhetorical *hal yastawī* construction (an "are X and Y equal?" question demanding a negative answer) is one of the corpus's most-cited rhetorical devices — al-Suyūṭī (*Itqān*, nawʿ on *istifhām al-taqrīrī*) treats this as a paradigmatic case. Q 39:9 also contains *qānit ānāʾa al-layl* — "devoutly obedient during the periods of the night" — picked up later by tafsir tradition as a description of the Prophet himself, of Abū Bakr, of ʿUthmān (depending on which transmitter).

## Block C (vv. 10-21) — Qul-Cluster and Eschatological Outcomes

**v. 10** *قل يا عباد الذين آمنوا اتقوا ربكم…* — "Say: O My servants who have believed, fear your Lord..."

The first vocative *yā ʿibādi* (O My servants) — paralleled with the famous v. 53 vocative for the transgressors. v. 10 is the call to the believers; v. 53 is the call to the despairing.

**v. 11** *قل إني أمرت أن أعبد الله مخلصا له الدين* — "Say: I have been commanded to worship Allah making my devotion sincere to Him."

Third *xlS* attestation. The first-person *qul* statement is structurally exact: the Prophet recites the very command he conveys.

**v. 12** *وأمرت لأن أكون أول المسلمين* — "And I have been commanded to be the first of those who submit."

*awwal al-muslimīn* — the first of the muslimūn (here muslimūn means "submitters", the basic theological term, before its later ethno-religious specialization). The phrase recurs at Q 6:14, Q 6:163 — the Late Meccan tawḥīd-iʿtiqād foundation.

**v. 13** *قل إني أخاف إن عصيت ربي عذاب يوم عظيم* — "Say: I fear, if I were to disobey my Lord, the punishment of a great Day."

The Prophet's own fear of consequences, declared. This personal humility before divine command is the rhetorical glue of the surah.

**v. 14** *قل الله أعبد مخلصا له ديني* — "Say: Allah I worship, making my devotion sincere to Him."

Fourth and final *xlS* attestation. The four mukhliṣ-attestations (vv. 2, 3, 11, 14) form a quasi-refrain at the surah's first quarter — locking the doctrinal axis.

**v. 15** *فاعبدوا ما شئتم من دونه ۗ قل إن الخاسرين الذين خسروا أنفسهم وأهليهم يوم القيامة ۗ ألا ذلك هو الخسران المبين* — "So worship what you will besides Him. Say: Indeed the losers are those who lose themselves and their families on the Day of Resurrection..."

The "go ahead and worship what you will" rhetorical concession, paired with the ultimate-loss verdict. *al-khasirūn* — "the losers" — is the surah's primary judgment-term for the wicked.

**vv. 16-18**: imagery of fire above and below, contrasted with "those who turn away from idols" (al-ṭāghūt) — vv. 17-18 — "and those who hear the word and follow the best of it" (*alladhīna yastamiʿūna al-qawla fa-yattabiʿūna aḥsanahu*) — those Allah has guided.

**vv. 19-20**: counter-tableau — "those who feared their Lord, for them are upper chambers (*ghuraf*) above which are upper chambers built [for them]" (*lakin alladhīna ittaqaw rabbahum lahum ghurafun min fawqihā ghurafun mabniyyatun*) — the eponymous *Sūrat al-Ghuraf* alternative-name imagery. Rivers flowing beneath. The classical *jannāt al-firdaws* architecture.

**v. 21** Cosmic image: *alam tara anna Allāha anzala min al-samāʾi māʾan…* (Don't you see that Allah sends down water from the sky, then makes it springs in the earth, then produces crops of various colors, then they wither and you see them yellow, then He makes them debris...) — natural-cycle as a sign of mortality and renewal.

## Block D (vv. 22-29) — Rhetorical-Theological Heart

**v. 22** *أفمن شرح الله صدره للإسلام فهو على نور من ربه ۚ فويل للقاسية قلوبهم من ذكر الله ۚ أولئك في ضلال مبين* — "So is one whose breast Allah has expanded to [accept] Islam and he is upon a light from his Lord [like one whose heart rejects it]? So woe to those whose hearts are hardened against the remembrance of Allah; those are in clear error."

The opening *a-fa-man… fa-huwa…* construction is a parallel to v. 9's *hal yastawī*: rhetorical question demanding the negative inference. *sharaḥa al-ṣadr* (the breast expanded) — the famous classical phrase meaning the heart opened to truth — recurs at Q 6:125, Q 16:106, Q 20:25 (Mūsā's prayer *rabbi shraḥ lī ṣadrī*), and Q 94:1 (*alam nashraḥ laka ṣadrak*).

**v. 23** *الله نزل أحسن الحديث كتابا متشابها مثاني تقشعر منه جلود الذين يخشون ربهم…* — "Allah has sent down the best statement: a Book in which the parts are alike, with paired-themes (*mathāniya*); the skins shiver of those who fear their Lord at it..."

This is one of the corpus's most-cited verses, on multiple axes:

1. **Classical *mathānī* doctrine anchor**: The verse explicitly self-describes the Quran as *kitāban mutashābihan **mathāniya*** ("a Book of self-resemblance, paired-themes"). Together with Q 15:87 (*sabʿan min al-mathānī*), this is the primary scriptural basis for the classical doctrine of paired-repeated themes (wrongdoers/righteous, heaven/hell, mercy/wrath). al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī all take *mathāniya* to refer to the Quran's structural pairing logic. (The T5 TDA test of the project, NULL outcome, attempted to detect *mathānī* topology empirically — see MASTER-LEDGER §3 R-011 entry.)

2. ***taqashʿarru minhu juludu*** — "the skins shiver of it" — the verb *taqashʿarra* is corpus-UNIQUE to this verse. It denotes the involuntary somatic response to divine speech: a goosebumps-shudder. al-Suyūṭī notes the medical phenomenon (*qushaʿrīra* — chills before fever) being repurposed as a sign of pious fear.

3. **The contrast structure**: the verse pairs the shiver-of-skins (*taqashʿarra*) with the softening-of-skins-and-hearts (*talīnu juluduhum wa-qulūbuhum*) for the same "those who fear their Lord". Two physiological responses — one stiffening (the divine word arrests the body), the other softening (the divine word melts the resistance) — a paradox of pious receptivity.

**v. 24** *أفمن يتقي بوجهه سوء العذاب يوم القيامة…* — "So is one who shields himself with his face from the evil of the punishment on the Day of Resurrection [like one who is safe]?"

A wrenching image: the pious shield themselves with their faces from punishment (i.e. they will literally be face-down before the punishment, seeking protection but having nothing else). This is one of the surah's most viscerally physical eschatological images.

**vv. 25-28**: short eschatological vignettes; v. 27 *wa-laqad ḍarabnā li-l-nāsi fī hādhā al-Qurʾāni min kulli mathalin laʿallahum yatadhakkarūn* (We have set forth for mankind in this Quran every kind of parable, that they may take heed) — meta-textual self-reference to the Quran's *amthāl* tradition.

**v. 29** *ضرب الله مثلا رجلا فيه شركاء متشاكسون ورجلا سلما لرجل هل يستويان مثلا* — "Allah sets forth a parable: a man having multiple owners disputing among themselves, and a man wholly belonging to one man — are these two equal in comparison?"

The slave-with-multiple-owners parable — Q 39's signature *mathal*. Per H-NEW-1170 (corpus-EXACT *ḍaraba mathalan* construction tradition), Q 39:29 is one of the canonical *amthāl* of the Quran. The parable's logic: a slave shared between disputing partners is in chaos; a slave belonging to one master is at peace. So is the muwaḥḥid (one-Lord-server) in peace; the polytheist in chaos.

## Block E (vv. 30-31) — Mortality of the Prophet

**v. 30** *إنك ميت وإنهم ميتون* — "Indeed you will die, and indeed they will die."

A two-clause verse, 6 orthographic words. Classical commentaries (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, al-Zamakhsharī) note this is a remarkably terse statement of the Prophet's mortality, addressed to the Prophet himself.

**v. 31** *ثم إنكم يوم القيامة عند ربكم تختصمون* — "Then indeed, on the Day of Resurrection, you will dispute before your Lord."

Pairs death (v. 30) with the post-resurrection dispute (v. 31). These two verses are the mid-axis of the surah's eschatological argument: human mortality is universal; the dispute is forensic before the divine judge.

**Bukhari #3511** (verified): ʿĀʾisha narrates that when the Prophet died, ʿUmar refused to accept his death. Abū Bakr stood, recited Q 3:144 (*wa-mā Muḥammadun illā rasūlun…*) AND Q 39:30 (*innaka mayyitun wa-innahum mayyitūn*), and brought the community to acceptance. This makes Q 39:30 one of the most historically-load-bearing verses in the entire Quran — its recitation by Abū Bakr at the death-of-Prophet is the founding moment of post-prophetic Islam.

## Block F (vv. 32-52) — Theological Confrontation

**v. 32** *فمن أظلم ممن كذب على الله…* — "Who is more unjust than one who lies about Allah?" — the *aẓlam* (more-unjust) vocative is the surah's signature judgment-form.

**v. 33** *والذي جاء بالصدق وصدق به أولئك هم المتقون* — "And the one who has come with the truth and accepts it — those are the godfearing." Corpus-UNIQUE phrase *jāʾa bi-al-ṣidq*; classical tradition splits on whether *alladhī* refers to the Prophet or to a generic believer (al-Ṭabarī catalogues both opinions).

**vv. 34-37**: rhetorical sequence on divine custody: *a-laysa Allāhu bi-kāfin ʿabdah* (v. 36) — "Is not Allah sufficient for His servant?" — corpus-UNIQUE phrase. The rhetorical *a-laysa…* with negation expects affirmative. *kāfin* (sufficient) is a Late Meccan tawḥīd-iʿtiqād specialty noun.

**v. 38** *ولئن سألتهم من خلق السماوات والأرض ليقولن الله…* — "If you ask them who created the heavens and the earth, they will surely say: Allah." The polytheist-monotheist concession — even the Meccan polytheists admit Allah's creator-status. This sets up the surah's central argumentative move: if you concede Allah's creator-status, why do you take intercessors besides Him?

The two *qul* imperatives at v. 38 (*qul afa-raʾaytum mā tadʿūna min dūni Allāhi* — "do you not see what you call upon besides Allah") + (*qul ḥasbiya Allāh* — "say: Allah suffices me") are the rhetorical pivot. *ḥasbiya Allāh* (Allah suffices me) is one of the corpus's foundational tawakkul-formulas; it recurs at Q 9:129.

**vv. 39-40** *qul yā qawmi ʿmalū ʿalā makānatikum innī ʿāmilun fa-sawfa taʿlamūn* — "Say: O my people, work according to your position; I am working — soon you will know..." — the Prophetic verse-formula of patient confrontation. Recurs at Q 6:135, Q 11:93, Q 11:121.

**v. 42** *الله يتوفى الأنفس حين موتها والتي لم تمت في منامها فيمسك التي قضى عليها الموت ويرسل الأخرى إلى أجل مسمى…* — "Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that did not die [yet] during their sleep. Then He retains those for which He has decreed death and releases the others until a specified time..."

One of the corpus's most metaphysically explicit verses on divine custody of consciousness. *yatawaffā al-anfus* (He fully-receives the souls) — the term *tawaffī* contrasts with *imāta* (causing-to-die): *tawaffī* is the receiving-back, the resumption of divine custody. The verse asserts that sleep is a form of *tawaffī* — souls return to divine custody nightly. Classical Sufi literature (al-Ghazālī's *Iḥyāʾ ʿulūm al-dīn*, ch. on death) builds extensively on Q 39:42.

**vv. 43-46**: more *qul* (43, 44, 46) — the surah's qul-cluster reaches its second density-peak. v. 44 is the corpus-EXACT *qul li-llāhi al-shafāʿatu jamīʿan* — "Say: To Allah belongs all intercession". This refutes the polytheist intercession-doctrine of v. 3 in the divine-claim mode.

**vv. 47-52**: the qul-cluster gives way to a recapitulation of human ingratitude (*idhā massa al-insāna ḍurr daʿānā* v. 49) and the cycle of forgetting in ease (v. 50).

## Block G (vv. 53-61) — Mercy-Apex and Consequence

**v. 53** *۞ قل يا عبادي الذين أسرفوا على أنفسهم لا تقنطوا من رحمة الله ۚ إن الله يغفر الذنوب جميعا ۚ إنه هو الغفور الرحيم* — "Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah; indeed Allah forgives all sins; He is the Forgiving, the Merciful."

This is the surah's apex and one of the most-quoted verses in the Quran. The vocative *yā ʿibādiya* (O My servants) — note the possessive — is the same form as v. 10 (*yā ʿibādi alladhīna āmanū*) but the second-occurrence here is to those who have *transgressed against themselves*: a wider, more inclusive call. *asrafū ʿalā anfusihim* (gone beyond bounds at their own expense) — the *isrāf* root means excessive, over-the-line behavior; here it carries the sense of self-inflicted wrong.

**Asbāb al-nuzūl (verified hadith chain)**:

- **Bukhari #4604** (Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr via Yaʿlā b. Muslim): a group of polytheists who had killed many and committed adultery many times came to the Prophet and asked whether their sins could be expiated. Q 25:68-70 (*and those who do not call upon another god with Allah, nor kill the soul which Allah has prohibited…*) and Q 39:53 were revealed in response.
- **Muslim #229** (Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr via Yaʿlā b. Muslim, parallel chain): identical asbāb al-nuzūl narrative.
- **Tirmidhī #3321** (Asmāʾ bint Yazīd via Shahr b. Ḥawshab, *ḥasan gharīb* per Tirmidhī's own classification): the Prophet recited Q 39:53 *wa-lā yubālī* — "and He does not mind" — adding the universal-mercy framing.
- **Abū Dāwūd #4275** (Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr): same asbāb chain.
- **Nasāʾī #4013** (Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr): identical chain with addition: "Allah will exchange their associationism (*shirk*) for faith, their adultery for chastity".

The verse is universally classified as Meccan despite the asbāb al-nuzūl involving polytheists who came to Medina in some traditions; the dominant interpretation is that the verse was revealed in Mecca with general mercy-scope, then APPLIED with respect to the Wahshī b. Ḥarb (the killer of Ḥamza) conversion later. al-Qurṭubī treats both options.

**v. 54** *وأنيبوا إلى ربكم وأسلموا له من قبل أن يأتيكم العذاب…* — "And turn back to your Lord and submit to Him before the punishment comes upon you..."

The mercy-call is followed by the deadline-warning. *anībū* (turn back) recurs in Q 39 thrice (vv. 8, 17, 54).

**vv. 55-61**: extended exhortation to repent before the punishment, with three "lest the soul say" (*an taqūla nafsun*) clauses — vv. 56, 57, 58 — the only verses in the corpus that triple-stack the *an taqūla nafsun* construction. v. 56: *yā ḥasratā ʿalā mā farraṭṭu fī janbi Allāh* (Oh, my regret over what I neglected regarding Allah's side) — a hypothetical death-bed confession.

## Block H (vv. 62-67) — Cosmic Sovereignty

**vv. 62-63** *الله خالق كل شيء وهو على كل شيء وكيل ۖ له مقاليد السماوات والأرض* — "Allah is the Creator of everything and He is over everything Disposer; to Him belong the keys of the heavens and the earth..."

The *maqālīd* (keys) of the heavens and earth — a classical Sufi-mystical phrase, treated by al-Ghazālī and Ibn ʿArabī as referring to the divine names that "unlock" cosmic realities. The exact phrase recurs only at Q 42:12.

**vv. 64-66**: more *qul* (64), and the poignant *wa-laqad ūḥiya ilayka wa-ilā alladhīna min qablika la-in ashrakta la-yaḥbaṭanna ʿamaluk* (v. 65) — "Indeed it has been revealed to you and to those before you: if you associate, your work will surely be in vain". A direct address to the Prophet warning against shirk — even the Prophet's own work would be invalidated by associationism. This is one of the most striking Late-Meccan Prophetic-self-disciplining verses.

**v. 67** *وما قدروا الله حق قدره والأرض جميعا قبضته يوم القيامة والسماوات مطويات بيمينه ۚ سبحانه وتعالى عما يشركون* — "And they have not appraised Allah with true appraisal; the whole earth will be in His grip on the Day of Resurrection, and the heavens will be folded in His right hand. Glorified and exalted is He above what they associate."

The cosmic-sovereignty image: at the resurrection, the entire earth is held in the divine grip; the heavens are folded in the divine right hand. *qabḍatuhu* (His handful/grip) and *maṭwiyyāt* (folded) — both anthropomorphic divine-action verbs.

**Hadith verification**:
- **Bukhari #4605, #7131, #7132**: ʿAbd Allāh b. Masʿūd narrates that a Jewish scholar (or "rabbi" / *ḥabr*, or "man from the People of the Book") came to the Prophet and reported: "Allah will hold the heavens on a finger, and the earths on a finger, and the mountains/trees on a finger, and the water and dust on a finger, and all the creation on a finger; then He will say: I am the King." The Prophet smiled until his molars showed (*ḍaḥika al-nabī ḥattā badat nawājidhuh*), confirming the rabbi's report, and recited Q 39:67.
- **Muslim #6872, #6873**: parallel chains via Ibn Masʿūd / Manṣūr / Ibrāhīm / ʿAbīda al-Salmānī.
- **Tirmidhī #3322** (al-Aʿmash + Manṣūr via Ibrāhīm via ʿAbīda via ʿAbd Allāh): *ḥadīth ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ* per Tirmidhī's classification.

The hadith provides classical interpretive licence: the Prophet's smiling reaction was treated by classical theologians (al-Ashʿarī, al-Bāqillānī, al-Ghazālī) as a model for the divine-attribute discourse: the *finger*-imagery is to be neither denied (*taʿṭīl*) nor anthropomorphized (*tashbīh*); it is to be received with the affective response of the Prophet himself — surprise, smile, recitation of the verse as confirmation.

## Block I (vv. 68-75) — Eschatological Throng-Cycle

**v. 68** *ونفخ في الصور فصعق من في السماوات ومن في الأرض إلا من شاء الله ۖ ثم نفخ فيه أخرى فإذا هم قيام ينظرون* — "And the trumpet will be blown, and whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on the earth will fall dead except whom Allah wills; then it will be blown again, and behold, they will be standing, looking."

The corpus's most explicit two-blast trumpet eschatology. The first blast strikes everyone dead (*ṣaʿiqa*); the second blast restores them (*qiyāmun yanẓurūn*). Classical eschatological literature (al-Suyūṭī's *al-Budūr al-Sāfira*, Ibn Kathīr's *al-Nihāya fī al-fitan wa-l-malāḥim*) builds on Q 39:68 for the structure of the *al-nafkhatān* (the two trumpet-blasts).

**v. 69** *وأشرقت الأرض بنور ربها ووضع الكتاب وجيء بالنبيين والشهداء…* — "And the earth will glow with the light of its Lord, and the Book will be placed, and the prophets and witnesses will be brought..."

The forensic-resurrection setting: divine-light illuminates the resurrection ground, the record-book is laid open, the prophets and witnesses are summoned.

**v. 70** *ووفيت كل نفس ما عملت…* — "And every soul will be paid in full what it did..." — the full-recompense formula, reprised throughout the corpus.

**vv. 71-72**:
*وسيق الذين كفروا إلى جهنم زمرا ۖ حتى إذا جاءوها فتحت أبوابها وقال لهم خزنتها ألم يأتكم رسل منكم يتلون عليكم آيات ربكم وينذرونكم لقاء يومكم هذا ۚ قالوا بلى ولكن حقت كلمة العذاب على الكافرين*
"And those who disbelieved are driven to Hell in throngs; until, when they arrive there, its gates are opened, and its keepers say to them: Did messengers not come to you from among yourselves, reciting your Lord's verses to you and warning you of the meeting of this Day of yours? They will say: Yes; but the word of punishment has come true upon the disbelievers."

*قيل ادخلوا أبواب جهنم خالدين فيها ۖ فبئس مثوى المتكبرين*
"It will be said: Enter the gates of Hell, abiding therein. So wretched is the abode of the arrogant."

**vv. 73-74**:
*وسيق الذين اتقوا ربهم إلى الجنة زمرا ۖ حتى إذا جاءوها وفتحت أبوابها وقال لهم خزنتها سلام عليكم طبتم فادخلوها خالدين*
"And those who feared their Lord are driven to the Garden in throngs; until, when they arrive there and its gates are opened, its keepers say to them: Peace be upon you; you have done well; so enter to abide therein eternally."

The two stanzas are the surah's verbatim parallel construction. Per Q039-F-03 (this work, PASS-DIRECTED), the *wa-sīqa alladhīna* paired-incipit construction is corpus-EXACT — only Q 39:71 and Q 39:73 begin with *wa-sīqa* in the entire corpus. The *zumar* root (eponym of the surah) appears EXACTLY twice — both here.

Note three structural details:
1. **Verbatim-parallel except polarity**: *kafarū* / *ittaqaw rabbahum*; *Jahannam* / *al-Janna*; *futiḥat* / *wa-futiḥat* (the only addition to v. 73 is the conjunction *wa*; classical commentators read this as the "Garden's gates are opened" being a connected continuous action, while Hell's gates are an abrupt opening).
2. **Keepers' speech polarity**: Hell's keepers ask an interrogative (*alam yaʾtikum rusulun*); Garden's keepers offer a benediction (*salāmun ʿalaykum ṭibtum*). Hell is the place of forensic-questioning; Garden is the place of greeting.
3. **The Hell-side has v. 72 as a coda; the Garden-side has v. 74 as a coda**:
   - v. 72 — the disbelievers ENTER (*udkhulū*) at command, told it is "wretched abode of the arrogant".
   - v. 74 — the believers SAY (*qālū*) — they do not need a command — *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi alladhī ṣadaqanā waʿdah wa-awrathanā al-arḍa natabawwaʾu min al-jannati ḥaythu nashāʾu* — "Praise be to Allah who has fulfilled His promise to us and given us the earth as inheritance, that we may dwell in the Garden where we will." The *awrathnā al-arḍ* (gave us the earth as inheritance) phrase is corpus-significant — it recurs at Q 7:128, Q 21:105, Q 39:74 only.

**v. 75** *وترى الملائكة حافين من حول العرش يسبحون بحمد ربهم ۖ وقضي بينهم بالحق وقيل الحمد لله رب العالمين* — "And you will see the angels surrounding the Throne, glorifying their Lord with His praise; and judgment will be rendered between them in truth, and it will be said: 'Praise be to Allah, Lord of the worlds!'"

The closing benediction. The angels in concentric ring (*ḥāffīna*) around the Throne. The final words *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabb al-ʿālamīn* are verbatim Q 1:2. The surah closes by speaking the formula that opens al-Fātiḥa — Q 39's signature self-ring composition. (See MASTER §10.27 and Q039-F-04 in 06-novel-findings.md.)

## Summary — Q 39's Architecture

The surah builds along nine blocks that move from declaration (Block A) → existential framing (B) → qul-cluster + tawḥīd-imperative (C) → rhetorical-theological heart (D, with v. 22 *sharaḥa al-ṣadr* and v. 23 *kitāban mutashābihan mathāniya*) → mortality-of-the-Prophet pivot (E) → confrontation-cycle (F, including v. 42 *yatawaffā al-anfus*) → mercy-apex (G, v. 53) → cosmic-sovereignty (H, v. 67) → eschatological throng-cycle (I, vv. 71-75 *zumaran*).

The surah's tonal arc is from declarative-cosmic → diagnostic-rhetorical → forensic-eschatological → benedictive. The mercy-apex (v. 53) sits at exactly the 70.7% point of the surah (verse 53 of 75 = 70.7%); the eschatological cycle occupies the final 6.7% (vv. 71-75 of 75). The doctrinal anchor (*xlS* attestations vv. 2, 3, 11, 14) front-loads the first 19% (vv. 1-14 of 75); the rhetorical-theological heart (vv. 22-29) sits at the 30-39% range. This is a paradigmatically structured Late Meccan tawḥīd-iʿtiqād surah — declarative front, rhetorical-confrontational middle, eschatological closing.
