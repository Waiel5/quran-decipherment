## 2. Classical names

- **al-Shuʿarāʾ** (الشعراء) — "The Poets" (canonical name; from v 224).
- Also referred to as **Sūrat al-Jāmiʿa** (الجامعة, "The Gathering") in some early traditions, due to its assembled prophet-narratives — but the dominant name is al-Shuʿarāʾ.

## 3. Opening formula — muqaṭṭaʿāt ṬSM (al-ṬS family)

Q 26 opens with **ṬSM** (ṭāʾ-sīn-mīm, طسم) — a 3-letter muqaṭṭaʿ, paired with Q 28 (also ṬSM) and adjacent to Q 27 (ṬS):

- Q 26 al-Shuʿarāʾ — **ṬSM** (ṭ-s-m).
- Q 27 al-Naml — ṬS (ṭ-s).
- Q 28 al-Qaṣaṣ — **ṬSM** (ṭ-s-m).

This is the **ṭ-s/ṭ-s-m letter cluster** (3 contiguous mushaf positions, no other ṬS-family surah elsewhere). Q026-F-02 tests joint cohesion of this triplet across 4 axes; **NULL on all 4 axes** (no-axis pct ≤ 5%; no-axis pct ≥ 95%). ## 4. ⭐ Unique structural feature — the 8-cycle paired refrain

Q 26 contains an 8-cycle paired refrain (Q026-F-01 **CONFIRMED**, p_perm = 0.0083 < α_bon = 0.01):

- **R1**: `إن في ذلك لآية وما كان أكثرهم مؤمنين` — "Indeed in that is a sign, and most of them were not believers"
- **R2**: `وإن ربك لهو العزيز الرحيم` — "And indeed your Lord — He is the Mighty, the Merciful"

R1 occurs at vv **8, 67, 103, 121, 139, 158, 174, 190** in Q 26.
R2 occurs at vv **9, 68, 104, 122, 140, 159, 175, 191** in Q 26.

**Both phrases occur EXCLUSIVELY in Q 26 across the entire corpus** (R1 corpus-total = 8/8 in Q 26; R2 corpus-total = 8/8 in Q 26). No other surah has either phrase.

## 5. ⭐ Other notable features

- **Most-extensive prophet-cycle in the corpus** — 7 sequential prophet-narratives, all closed by identical paired refrain. No other surah has this cycle-architecture.
- **Q 26:214** *wa-andhir ʿashīrataka al-aqrabīn* — the kinship-warning verse, with classical *asbāb al-nuzūl*: revealed in Mecca, prompting the Prophet's public address from al-Ṣafā to Quraysh (al-Bukhārī #2641, Muslim #410 — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`).
- **Q 26:224–227** — the *al-shuʿarāʾ* coda, the foundational anti-poetry-genre statement: *"And the poets — the deviant follow them."* Q026-F-03 tested whether this coda is the *most lexically distinctive* 4-verse window in Q 26: **NULL** — the coda ranks 99 of 224 windows (median pile); the most-distinct window is the Ṣāliḥ-narrative punishment scene at vv 78–81. The coda is rhetorically distinct (it addresses a different subject) but not *lexically* maximally outlying. A re-pre-registered test on a different lexical-feature (e.g. unique-vocabulary-density) could reveal a different result — see §F-03 honest-limits.
- **Anti-poetry classical anchor**: al-Bāqillānī, *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*, builds his foundational iʿjāz argument on this coda — that the Quran *refuses poetic-imitation* and is therefore distinct from genre-poetry.

## 9. Quick content structure

- **vv 1–9**: prologue — muqaṭṭaʿāt ṬSM + book-reference (*tilka āyātu al-kitābi al-mubīn*); theological framing (Prophet's grief at unbelievers); FIRST refrain pair (vv 8–9).
- **vv 10–68**: **Cycle 1 — Mūsā / Pharaoh** (the surah's narrative center, 59 verses). Includes the vocational call (vv 10–17), Mūsā-Pharaoh dialogue (vv 18–37), the magicians' confrontation (vv 38–51), the exodus / Red-Sea crossing (vv 52–67); refrain pair vv 67–68.
- **vv 69–104**: **Cycle 2 — Ibrāhīm**. Includes Ibrāhīm's theological refutation of his father's idols (vv 69–82), his prayer (vv 83–89), and the famous **paradise-hell description** (vv 90–104); refrain pair vv 103–104.
- **vv 105–122**: **Cycle 3 — Nūḥ** (his tribe, ark, judgment); refrain pair vv 121–122.
- **vv 123–140**: **Cycle 4 — Hūd / ʿĀd**; refrain pair vv 139–140.
- **vv 141–159**: **Cycle 5 — Ṣāliḥ / Thamūd** (she-camel, slaughter, punishment); refrain pair vv 158–159.
- **vv 160–175**: **Cycle 6 — Lūṭ** (his people, the rain of stones); refrain pair vv 174–175.
- **vv 176–191**: **Cycle 7 — Shuʿayb / Ahl al-Ayka** (the people of the Thicket, justice in measure); refrain pair vv 190–191.
- **vv 192–227**: **Coda** — the Quran's defense of itself (vv 192–212), Prophet's commission (vv 213–219), the kinship-warning **v 214** *wa-andhir ʿashīrataka al-aqrabīn*, polemics on jinn / poets (vv 221–223), the famous **anti-poetry coda vv 224–227**.
