## 1. Q 26:214 — *wa-andhir ʿashīrataka al-aqrabīn* (the kinship-warning)

This is the most-cited Q 26 verse in the canonical ḥadīth corpus. Multiple isnāds report the Prophet ascending al-Ṣafā mountain immediately after this revelation and calling Quraysh by clan-name.

### al-Bukhārī

- **#2641** (Kitāb al-Waṣāyā) — Abū Hurayra: "When Allah revealed the verse 'Warn your nearest kinsmen' — Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) got up and said: 'O people of Quraish (or said similar words)! Buy [save] yourselves [from the Hellfire] as I cannot save you from Allah's Punishment.'" Goes on to address Banū ʿAbd Manāf, Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, Ṣafiyya bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, and Fāṭima bint Muḥammad. **VERIFIED on disk** at `bukhari.json` idInBook=2641.

- **#3376** (Kitāb al-Manāqib) — Ibn ʿAbbās: "When the Verse: '*wa-andhir ʿashīrataka al-aqrabīn*' (Q 26:214) was revealed, the Prophet (ﷺ) started calling [the Arab tribes], 'O Bani Fihr, O Bani ʿAdī,' mentioning first the various branch tribes of Quraysh." **VERIFIED on disk** at `bukhari.json` idInBook=3376.

- **#3377** (parallel narration of #3376 from a different chain).

- **#4564** (Kitāb al-Tafsīr, Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ) — Ibn ʿAbbās: "When the Verse 'And warn your tribe of near-kindred' was revealed, the Prophet (ﷺ) **ascended al-Ṣafā** (mountain) and started calling, 'O Bani Fihr! O Bani ʿAdī!' addressing various tribes of Quraysh till they were assembled. Those who could not come themselves, sent their messengers to see what was there." **VERIFIED on disk** at `bukhari.json` idInBook=4564.

- **#4565** (Kitāb al-Tafsīr, Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ) — Abū Hurayra (parallel to #2641, in the tafsīr-section context). **VERIFIED on disk**.

### Muslim

- **#407, #409, #410, #412, #414, #415** (Kitāb al-Īmān, bāb on warning the family) — Multiple isnāds:
  - **#407** Abū Hurayra: "When this verse was revealed: 'And warn thy nearest kindred' (Q 26:214), the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) called the Quraysh; so they gathered and he gave them a general warning. Then he made a particular reference to certain tribes, naming Banū Kaʿb b. Luway, Banū Murra b. Kaʿb, Banū Hāshim, Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib." **VERIFIED on disk** at `muslim.json` idInBook=407.
  - **#409** ʿĀʾisha: same content, narrated through Hishām → ʿUrwa → ʿĀʾisha.
  - **#410** Abū Hurayra: same content (parallel to #407 with slightly different wording).
  - **#412** Qabīṣa b. al-Mukhāriq + Zuhayr b. ʿAmr: "When this verse was revealed, 'And warn thy nearest kindred,' the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) **set off towards a rock of the hill and ascended the highest of the rocks** and then called: 'O sons of ʿAbd Manāf! I am a warner.'"
  - **#414** Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr: same content.
  - **#415** through Aʿmash from a different chain (parallel content).

### Asbāb al-nuzūl summary

Across these multiply-attested narrations, the consistent picture is:
1. Q 26:214 was revealed in **Mecca** (consistent with surah being mid-Meccan).
2. The Prophet immediately ascended al-Ṣafā (a hill near the Kaʿba) and addressed the Quraysh clan by clan.
3. He warned each clan that he could not avail them against Allah's punishment if they did not believe.
4. The warning is preserved as a foundational *daʿwa* moment — the public commencement of the Meccan call to faith.

## 2. Q 26:224–227 — the anti-poetry coda + the exception for Muslim poet-defenders

The classical ḥadīth tradition uses Q 26:224–227 as the framework for understanding *what kind of poetry is permitted*. Several ḥadīth report that the exception in v 227 was revealed for the Muslim poets who defended the Prophet against Meccan satirists.

### al-Bukhārī

- **#2237** — ʿAbd Allāh: The Prophet (ﷺ) burned the date palms of Banū al-Naḍīr at al-Buwayra; **Ḥassān b. Thābit** said in a poetic verse [memorialized in this ḥadīth's text]: "*The chiefs of Banū Luʾay found it easy to watch fire spreading at al-Buwayra.*" — context: Hassan as the Prophet's poet, defending against polytheist tribes. **VERIFIED on disk**.

- **#3381** — ʿĀʾisha: "Once Hassan b. Thabit asked the permission of the Prophet (ﷺ) to lampoon (i.e. compose satirical poetry defaming) the infidels. The Prophet (ﷺ) said, 'What about [the fact that I have ancestors among Quraysh]?'" — context: the dual-use of poetry for defense vs offense. The ḥadīth establishes Hassan's permission as the canonical exemplar of v 227's exception. **VERIFIED on disk**.

- **#3678** — Abū Hurayra: "The Prophet (ﷺ) said, 'The most truthful word said by a poet was the word of Labīd: *Verily, everything except Allah is perishable* (*alā kullu shayʾin mā khalā Allāha bāṭilu*).'" — context: distinguishing **truth-bearing poetry** (which is praised) from **vanity-poetry** (which is blamed in Q 26:224–226). **VERIFIED on disk**.

### Muslim

- **#6236** — ʿUrwa via ʿĀʾisha (parallel to al-Bukhārī #3381): "Hassan b. Thabit sought permission from Allah's Apostle (ﷺ) to satirize against the polytheists." **VERIFIED on disk**.

### al-Tirmidhī

- **#3120** — Qatāda b. al-Nuʿmān: A long ḥadīth reporting the household of Banū Ubayriq among whom was Bushayr, "a hypocrite who would recite poetry reviling the companions of the Prophet (ﷺ) then he would attribute it to some of the Arabs." — context: poetry as a tool of anti-Prophetic propaganda; this matches Q 26:226's *yaqūlūna mā lā yafʿalūn*. **VERIFIED on disk**.

## 3. Q 26 — *fadāʾil* (virtues) and recitation

The canonical 9-book corpus does NOT contain a specific *faḍīla* ḥadīth attached to the recitation of Q 26 al-Shuʿarāʾ. Unlike Q 1 al-Fātiḥa (al-Bukhārī #756 *umm al-Kitāb*) or Q 55 al-Raḥmān (al-Tirmidhī #3291 *ʿarūs al-Qurʾān*) or Q 36 Yāsīn (Tirmidhī #2887 *qalb al-Qurʾān*), Q 26 has no dedicated *faḍīla* attestation in the canonical books.

This absence is itself empirical: Q 26 is among the **majority of surahs** without a dedicated *faḍīla* ḥadīth. Per the project's prior analysis ([[h-new-860-hadith-architectural-alignment]]), the *faḍīla*-attested surahs (Q 1, Q 2, Q 9, Q 18, Q 36, Q 55, Q 67, Q 112, Q 113, Q 114, plus a handful) are a non-random subset structurally aligned with corpus outlier-rank. Q 26's lack of a *faḍīla* ḥadīth is consistent with its profile (high UAS but not in the top-10; theological-iʿjāz weight is on its *coda*, not its overall recitation).

## 4. Q 26 in larger ḥadīth narratives

- **al-Bukhārī #3098** — ʿĀʾisha asked the Prophet about a day harder than Uḥud; the Prophet replied "*Your tribes have troubled me a lot*" — this matches the asbāb al-nuzūl context of v 214 (the Prophet's tribe-by-tribe warning).
- **al-Bukhārī #980** — Masrūq via Ibn Masʿūd: the Prophet asks Allah to send famine on the obstinate Quraysh "for seven years like the seven years of (Prophet) Joseph" — this is the Joseph-cycle parallel to the Q 26 prophet-cycle motif (every prophet faces rejection from his own people).
- **al-Bukhārī #758** — Abū Wāʾil via Ibn Masʿūd: "I recited the Mufaṣṣal (Suras) at night in one rakʿa." Ibn Masʿūd said: "This recitation is like the recitation of poetry." — relevant to **anti-poetry-coda interpretation**: Ibn Masʿūd EXPLICITLY uses poetry as a metaphor for *too-fast, undigested* recitation; the Quran is to be heard slowly and thoughtfully, not declaimed-as-poetry. This anchors the classical tradition's reading of the Q 26 anti-poetry-coda as not just genre-distinction but *recitation-pace* distinction. **VERIFIED on disk**.
