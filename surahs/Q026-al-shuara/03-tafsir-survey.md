This survey draws from at least 5 mufassirūn covering Q 26's main interpretive cruxes: the ṬSM muqaṭṭaʿ, the 7-prophet-cycle architecture, the kinship-warning (v 214), and the anti-poetry coda (vv 224–227). ## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

Source: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/26.json`.

al-Ṭabarī's commentary on Q 26 follows his standard verse-by-verse method, with three notable interpretive moves:

1. **ṬSM (v 1)**: al-Ṭabarī reports multiple opinions on the meaning — that they are names God knows best (the agnostic position), that they spell *qasam* (oath-letters), or that they are letters of God's names. He does not endorse a specific interpretation, foregrounding the position later canonized by al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān*, nawʿ 40) that the muqaṭṭaʿāt's meaning is unknowable. This is consistent with the empirical FALSIFICATION of muqaṭṭaʿ-content-cohesion ([[h-new-600-letter-families|H-NEW-600]] and Q026-F-02 NULL extending it to TSM-3).

2. **The Mūsā-Pharaoh dialogue (vv 18–22)**: he gives extensive narrative gloss on Mūsā's killing of the Egyptian and his fleeing to Madyan, drawing the connection to the Exodus narrative of Q 28:14–28 — i.e., he cross-references the *Mūsā cycle in Q 28* without distinguishing the two (Q 26 vs Q 28) as letter-family-correlated. al-Ṭabarī's interpretive instinct treats the same prophet-narratives as one dispersed event-set.

3. **The kinship-warning v 214**: al-Ṭabarī cites the famous ḥadīth of Abū Hurayra (parallel to al-Bukhārī #2641) — when this verse was revealed, the Prophet stood on al-Ṣafā and called out his clan by name, warning each. This anchors the asbāb al-nuzūl rendering.

## 2. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Source: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/26.json` (English translation, Darussalam).

Ibn Kathīr's organization treats Q 26 as **a unified rhetorical block** rather than 7 separate cycles, but he highlights specific themes with explicit section-headers:

1. **"The Qurʾān and the Disbelievers turning away"** (vv 1–9): Ibn Kathīr connects v 1's *tilka āyātu al-kitābi al-mubīn* to the Q 27:1 parallel, noting "we discussed the letters which appear at the beginning of some Surahs" — i.e., he treats ṬSM as a member of the ṬS letter-family without claiming a specific shared content-meaning. This matches the [[h-new-600-letter-families|H-NEW-600]] empirical NULL.

2. **"Nūḥ's preaching to His People, and Their Response"** (vv 105–122): Ibn Kathīr explicitly identifies Nūḥ as "the first Messenger sent by Allah to the people of earth" and frames the Nūḥ-cycle as a TYPOLOGY. The same typological lens is applied across each of the 7 cycles.

3. **"Refutation of the Fabrications of the Idolators"** (vv 221–227, the anti-poetry coda): Ibn Kathīr reads the coda as **anti-pretender** (against false prophets, magicians, fortune-tellers, and poets who claim divine inspiration). His section-header explicitly groups vv 221–227 as one unit — supporting the structural reading that the coda is a self-contained reflexive block.

4. **The paradise-hell description (vv 90–104)**: Ibn Kathīr's gloss is among the most extensive for Q 26 — he treats vv 90–104 as the surah's eschatological climax, with paradise *brought near* and hell *placed in full view*. The formula *brought near* vs *placed in full view* is structurally chiastic, foreshadowing the coda (vv 224–227) where the deviants in hell repeat the same lament.

5. **The kinship-warning (v 214)**: Ibn Kathīr cites ʿĀʾisha and Ibn ʿAbbās for the *al-Ṣafā* ḥadīth (al-Bukhārī #2641, Muslim #410) — the Prophet stood on al-Ṣafā after this verse was revealed and called Quraysh by clan.

## 3. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

Source: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/26.json`.

al-Qurṭubī's juristic interest in Q 26 is concentrated on:

1. **v 214 (the kinship-warning)**: he derives the obligation to warn one's family of Hellfire as a primary religious duty (*al-amr bi-l-maʿrūf wa-l-nahy ʿan al-munkar* applied within the household).

2. **The poets-coda (vv 224–227)**: al-Qurṭubī provides perhaps the richest classical discussion of the *EXCEPTION* clause in v 227 — *illā alladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti wa-dhakarū Allāha kathīran wa-ntaṣarū min baʿdi mā ẓulimū*. He cites the asbāb al-nuzūl tradition that this exception was revealed for the Muslim poets Ḥassān b. Thābit, ʿAbd Allāh b. Rawāḥa, and Kaʿb b. Mālik (the *du'āt* of the Prophet). Their poetry was permitted because it served truth, not vanity. This anchors the **dual-status of poetry** in the canonical reading: anti-poetry-as-imitator + pro-poetry-as-defender-of-truth.

3. **The Mūsā-Pharaoh discussion**: al-Qurṭubī gives extensive coverage to the magicians' confrontation (vv 38–51), reading their conversion as a paradigm of *iltifāt* (the unexpected pivot) — an insight that aligns with Abdel Haleem 1992's catalog of Quranic iltifāt.

## 4. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

Source: OpenITI raw text (referenced via project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/`); also in `razi-99names-extract.md` and `razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md` (excerpts referenced).

al-Rāzī's signature contribution to Q 26 is his **theological reading of the paradise-hell description (vv 90–104)** — he treats this as a structural climax, not a digression. His gloss of v 89 *illā man atā Allāha bi-qalbin salīm* is one of the most cited definitions of the *qalb salīm* (sound heart) in classical Islamic theology — he identifies it as the heart untainted by *shirk* (polytheism) and *nifāq* (hypocrisy).

al-Rāzī also gives important attention to the **iʿjāz dimension of the coda**: he reads vv 224–227 as the Quran's self-defense against the charge of being poetry. He notes that the verb *yahīmūn* in v 225 (poets "wandering in every valley") establishes poetry as a *non-purposive, non-revelatory* mode of speech, while the Quran is its opposite — purposive, revealed, ordered. This is the seed of al-Bāqillānī's later iʿjāz argument.

## 5. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf*

Source: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/zamakhshari-kashshaf/` + spa5k API (covered in tafsir collection).

al-Zamakhsharī's *Kashshāf* on Q 26 is famously rich on **rhetorical structure**:

1. **The refrain function**: al-Zamakhsharī explicitly notes that the paired refrain *inna fī dhālika la-āyatan wa-mā kāna aktharuhum muʾminīn // wa-inna rabbaka la-huwa al-ʿazīzu al-raḥīm* serves as a **rhetorical chorus** (*qarīna*) that **structurally couples** the prophet-stories. Each cycle ends with the same closure, asserting (a) the story is a sign, (b) most rejected, (c) yet God remains Mighty (*ʿazīz* — judging the rejecters) and Merciful (*raḥīm* — providing for the believers).
   - **Empirical confirmation**: this refrain is corpus-unique to Q 26 (Q026-F-01 CONFIRMED, p_perm = 0.0083). No other surah uses this exact paired closure. al-Zamakhsharī's "rhetorical chorus" reading is **empirically locked**.
   - The cycle-length compression (Spearman rho = −0.839) is a **NEW** structural feature that al-Zamakhsharī does NOT discuss explicitly. The classical reading sees the refrain as repetitive (*takrār*) but does not quantify the contracting cycle-lengths.

2. **The coda's anti-poetry argument**: al-Zamakhsharī's reading of *yaqūlūna mā lā yafʿalūn* ("they say what they do not do") frames poetry as the gap between speech and action — i.e., poetry is rhetorically powerful but ethically empty. The Quran, by contrast, joins speech to action (*āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt*).

## 6. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

Source: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf` + raw OpenITI extracts.

al-Biqāʿī's **munāsaba** project is uniquely relevant to Q 26 because:

1. **Q 25 → Q 26 inter-surah munāsaba**: al-Biqāʿī reads the transition as *cleanup-from-poetry-discourse → prophet-cycle*. Q 25 al-Furqān ends with the *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* description (Q 25:63–77), and Q 26 then opens with prophet-cycles culminating in the explicit anti-poetry coda. The thematic sequence is: warn the believer (Q 25) → demonstrate God's punishment of the unbelievers via prophet-cycles (Q 26) → continue Solomon-narrative (Q 27).

2. **Q 26 → Q 27 munāsaba**: al-Biqāʿī notes that Q 27 al-Naml continues the *ṬS letter family* and continues the prophet-narrative arc with Sulaymān-Bilqīs as a new (post-classical-prophet) narrative.

3. **Intra-surah munāsaba** (the 7-cycle architecture): al-Biqāʿī treats the 7 prophet-cycles as a structurally-coherent unit. He does NOT formally count "7" cycles — but his block-by-block exegesis groups them as cycle 1 (Mūsā), cycle 2 (Ibrāhīm), cycle 3-7 (Nūḥ, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lūṭ, Shuʿayb).
   - **Empirical correlate**: al-Biqāʿī's qualitative cycle-grouping matches our paired-refrain cycle-detection (Q026-F-01) at the block boundaries. His munāsaba reading does NOT discuss the cycle-length monotone-decrease.

4. **The anti-poetry coda as theological-iʿjāz**: al-Biqāʿī treats vv 192–227 as the surah's *finale* (a *khātima* that recapitulates the whole), with vv 224–227 specifically as the **anti-genre statement** that the Quran is not poetry, not divination, not lying-fortune-telling. This is the classical anchor for al-Bāqillānī's iʿjāz.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Durr al-manthūr* + *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

Source: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf`, with extracts in `suyuti-itqan-word-counts.md`.

al-Suyūṭī treats Q 26 as **mid-Meccan** in chronology (revelation order ~47, after Q 56 al-Wāqiʿa and before Q 27 al-Naml). His *al-Durr al-manthūr* (the narration-tafsir) provides the rich asbāb al-nuzūl tradition for v 214 (the kinship-warning) and the poets-coda exception.

In *al-Itqān*, al-Suyūṭī's nawʿ 40 (*on the muqaṭṭaʿāt*) and nawʿ 65 (*on the discrete sequence/munāsaba*) both touch Q 26 indirectly — the muqaṭṭaʿāt agnostic position is canonical; and the prophet-cycle-as-rhetorical-trope is documented but not specifically tied to Q 26.

## 8. Convergent and divergent classical positions

**Convergent across all 6 sources**:
- The 7-prophet-cycle structure is recognized.
- The paired refrain functions as a structural chorus.
- The kinship-warning v 214 has the al-Ṣafā asbāb al-nuzūl.
- The coda is the foundational anti-poetry-iʿjāz statement.
- The exception in v 227 protects the Muslim poet-defenders.

**Divergent / under-discussed**:
- Whether the 7 cycles have a quantifiable progression structure (length, content density) — NOT discussed quantitatively in classical sources. **Empirically novel: Q026-F-01 finds Spearman rho(cycle_index, length) = −0.839, p_perm = 0.0083.**
- Whether ṬSM (Q 26, Q 28) and ṬS (Q 27) form a *content-coherent* cluster — al-Biqāʿī implies yes; **empirically tested in Q026-F-02: NULL** (extends prior letter-family NULL streak from H-NEW-600).
- Whether the Q 26 Mūsā-cycle is structurally *closer* to Q 28 (sister-ṬSM) than to Q 20 (ṬH) — implicitly assumed by some letter-family readings; **empirically tested in Q026-F-04: FALSIFIED** (Q 28 is closer to Q 20 than to Q 26 in root-cosine).

These three divergences — quantitative cycle-progression, letter-family content NULL, narrative-twin FALSIFIED — are the project's empirical contributions to the classical tafsir tradition. None of them contradicts the *theological* readings; they refine the *structural* readings.
