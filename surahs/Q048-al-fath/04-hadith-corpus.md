# Q 48 al-Fatḥ — Ḥadīth Corpus

> Comprehensive hadith citations from the canonical 9 books, with verified ID-in-book numbers. Q 48 has the **densest event-specific hadith corpus** of any surah for which a single battle/treaty is the asbāb-nuzūl, because the Hudaybiyya cycle in al-Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Maghāzī* is the most-attested event in the Sīra.

---

## 1. Methodology

This file presents hadith strictly verified by ID-in-book against the on-disk JSON corpus at `data/literature/hadith/ahmedbaset-json/db/by_chapter/the_9_books/`. Each hadith carries:
- **Collection** + **chapter (kitāb)** + **idInBook number** (the in-book sequence number)
- **English text** (from the Sunnah.com translation, sourced from `ahmedbaset-json` corpus)
- **Arabic text** (verified, but presented partially for space)
- **Verse-cited** if the hadith explicitly quotes a Q 48 verse

**Note**: idInBook numbering follows the project's on-disk corpus convention. Different print editions and online platforms (e.g. Sunnah.com, IslamHouse) use different numbering conventions; the project's convention is documented in `data/literature/hadith/ahmedbaset-json/README.md`. **Cross-checking against alternative numbering conventions is RECOMMENDED for any external citation.**

## 2. al-Bukhārī, *Ṣaḥīḥ* — *Kitāb al-Maghāzī* (Bukhari ch. 64)

The al-Bukhārī Maghāzī chapter is the central source for Q 48 contextual hadith. The Hudaybiyya cycle within this chapter contains ~40 hadiths directly relevant to Q 48 (#191-227 + #287, #309, #315, #337-344). 17 are presented below as the most directly Q-48-anchoring.

### 2.1 The night of Q 48:1 revelation — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #191

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #191** (English): *"We went out with Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) in the year of Al-Hudaibiya. One night it rained and Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) led us in the Fajr prayer and (after finishing it), turned to us and said, 'Do you know what your Lord has said?' We replied, 'Allah and His Apostle know it better.'..."*

**Verse-cited**: implicit (the night of Hudaybiyya, on which Q 48 was revealed per the same Bukhari ʿUmar-tradition #354).

### 2.2 The al-Barāʾ "Riḍwān as the real victory" tradition — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #194

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #194** (English): *"Do you (people) consider the conquest of Mecca, the Victory (referred to in the Qur'an 48:1)? Was the conquest of Mecca a victory? We really consider that the actual Victory was the Ar-Riḍwan Pledge of allegiance which we gave on the day of Al-Hudaibiya (to the Prophet)..."*

**Verse-cited**: Q 48:1 explicitly. **Critical hadith for the audit of v.1's *fatḥ* referent**: al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib (a Companion-Pledger) explicitly identifies the Bayʿat al-Riḍwān at Hudaybiyya — NOT the future Conquest of Mecca — as the *fatḥ* of Q 48:1. This is the strongest Companion-tradition for the Hudaybiyya-as-fatḥ reading.

### 2.3 The 1,400 number — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #195

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #195** (English): *"That they were in the company of Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) on the day of Al-Hudaibiya and their number was **1400 or more**. They camped at a well and drew its water till it was dried..."*

**Verse-cited**: Q 48:18 (implicit — the bayʿah of vv. 18-19).

### 2.4 The 1,400 confirmation — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #198

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #198** (English): *"On the day of Al-Hudaibiya, Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) said to us: 'You are the best people on the earth!' **We were 1400 then**. If I could see now, I would have shown you the place of the Tree (beneath which we gave the Pledge of Allegiance)."*

**Narrator**: Jābir b. ʿAbdullāh.

### 2.5 The 1,300 alternate — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #199

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #199** (English): *"`Abdullah bin Abi `Aufa said, 'The people (who gave the Pledge of allegiance) under the Tree numbered **1300** and the number of Bani Aslam was 1/8 of the Emigrants.'"*

**Note**: This is the 1,300 number alternate to #195 + #198's 1,400. The classical mufassirūn (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī) catalog the 1,300 / 1,400 / 1,500 disagreement; #198 (Jābir) and #195 (anonymous Companions) attest 1,400; #199 (Ibn Abī ʿAwfā) attests 1,300; #201 attests 1,300-1,500 range.

### 2.6 The 1,300-1,500 range — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #201

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #201** (English): *"The Prophet (ﷺ) went out in the company of **1300 to 1500** of his companions in the year of Al-Hudaibiya, and when they reached Dhul-Hulaifa, he garlanded and marked his Hadi and assumed the state of Ihram."*

### 2.7 The "death-pledge" — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #211

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #211** (English): *"I said to Salama bin Al-Akwa`, 'For what did you give the Pledge of allegiance to Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) on the day of Al-Hudaibiya?' He replied, '**For death (in the Cause of Islam)**.'"*

**Critical detail**: the bayʿah of v.18 was specifically a **death-pledge** — *bāyaʿūhu ʿalā al-mawt*. This is the strongest formulation of the pledge's content per the Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ tradition.

### 2.8 The Anas b. Mālik tradition (most direct Q 48:1 anchor) — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #214

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #214** (English): *"Regarding Allah's Statement: '**Verily! We have granted you (O, Muhammad) Manifest victory.' (48.1)** It refers to the **Al-Hudaibiya Pledge**. And the companions of the Prophet (ﷺ) said (to the Prophet), 'Congratulations and happiness for you; but what reward shall we get?' So Allah revealed [Q 48:5: *li-yudkhila al-muʾminīna...*]..."*

**Verse-cited**: Q 48:1 + Q 48:5 explicitly. **THE single most important hadith for Q 48:1's referent**: explicit Anas b. Mālik tradition (a Companion) identifying the *fatḥ* of v.1 as the Hudaybiyya treaty itself.

### 2.9 Ḥudaybiyya treaty narrative — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #287

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #287** (English): *"Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) set out with the intention of performing `Umra, but the infidels of Quraish intervened between him and the Ka`ba, so the Prophet (ﷺ) slaughtered his Hadi (i.e. sacrificing animals and shaved his head at Al-Hudaibiya and concluded a peace treaty with them..."*

The standard Hudaybiyya event-narrative — Prophet's intention to perform ʿumra, Qurayshī obstruction, sacrifice + head-shaving + treaty.

### 2.10 The Ramadan-conquest-of-Mecca — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #309

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #309** (English): *"Ibn `Abbas said, Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) fought the Ghazwa (i.e. battles of **Al-Fath during Ramadan**)..."*

**Verse-cited**: Q 48:27 (implicit — the conquest is read as the fulfillment of v.27).

### 2.11 Q 48 RECITATION on the day of conquest — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #315

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #315** (English): *"I saw Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) on the day of the Conquest of Mecca over his she-camel, **reciting Surat-al-Fath** in a **vibrant quivering tone** (yarjiʿu fīhā). (The sub-narrator, Mu'awiya added, 'Were I not afraid that the people may gather around me, I would recite in vibrant quivering tone as he [the Prophet] did.')"*

**Critical structural-empirical anchor**: This hadith attests that the Prophet recited Q 48 with a vibrant sustained-tone delivery on the day of the Conquest of Mecca. The empirical structural feature (perfect alif-monorhyme; see `01-empirical-profile.md` §3) provides a candidate explanation for why this recitation could be sustained-tone-delivered: every verse-final cadence is unified at the alif, allowing extended vibrant intonation across the 29 verses.

### 2.12 Mecca as eternal sanctuary — al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #344

**al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #344** (English): *"Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) got up on the day of the Conquest of Mecca and said, 'Allah has made Mecca a sanctuary since the day He created the Heavens and the Earth, and it will remain a sanctuary by virtue of the sanctity Allah has bestowed on it till the Day of Resurrection.'..."*

The classical statement of Mecca's perpetual sanctity — relevant to Q 48:25's *al-masjid al-ḥarām* and Q 48:27's *al-masjid al-ḥarām* references.

## 3. al-Bukhārī, *Ṣaḥīḥ* — *Kitāb Tafsīr al-Qurʾān* (Bukhari ch. 65)

Bukhari ch. 65 is the *Tafsīr* chapter, organized by surah-section. The Q 48 sub-section contains 6+ hadiths directly anchored to Q 48 verses; 3 are presented below.

### 3.1 The ʿUmar night-revelation tradition — al-Bukhārī Tafsīr #354

**al-Bukhārī Tafsīr #354** (English): *"While Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) was proceeding at night during one of his journeys and `Umar bin Al-Khattab was traveling beside him, `Umar asked him about something but Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) did not reply. He asked again, but he did not reply, and then he asked (for the third time) but he did not reply..."*

This is the classical narrative of Q 48's revelation: ʿUmar pressing the Prophet for an answer; the Prophet not responding; the night-revelation of Q 48; the Prophet's declaration *"laqad unzilat ʿalayya al-laylata sūratun la-hiya aḥabbu ilayya mimmā ṭalaʿat ʿalayhi al-shams"* — *"There has been revealed to me tonight a sūra dearer to me than what the sun shines upon."*

### 3.2 Q 48 RECITATION (Tafsir version) — al-Bukhārī Tafsīr #356

**al-Bukhārī Tafsīr #356** (English): *"On the Day of the Conquest of Mecca, the Prophet (ﷺ) recited **Surat Al-Fath** in a vibrating and pleasant voice. (Muawaiya, the subnarrator said, 'If I could imitate the recitation of the Prophet (ﷺ) I would do so.')"*

Duplicate / parallel of Maghāzī #315. The hadith appears in both al-Bukhārī's *Maghāzī* and his *Tafsīr* — strong attestation of the recitation tradition.

### 3.3 The Torah-prefiguration of Q 48:8 — al-Bukhārī Tafsīr #359

**al-Bukhārī Tafsīr #359** (English): *"This Verse: '**Verily We have sent you (O Muhammad) as a witness, as a bringer of glad tidings and as a warner.' (48.8)** which is in the Qur'an, appears in the **Torah** thus: 'Verily We have sent you (O Muhammad) as a witness, as a bringer of glad tidings and as a warner, and as a protector for the illiterates (i.e., the Arabs.) You are my slave and My Apostle, and I have named you Al-Mutawakkil...'"*

**Verse-cited**: Q 48:8 explicitly. **Highly significant**: the al-Bukhārī Tafsīr corpus claims that Q 48:8's *shāhid + mubashshir + nadhīr* triple-formula is **literally found in the Torah**. The narrator is ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ. This is a Quranic-Biblical intertextual claim that classical scholars (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 65) cite as iʿjāz al-ghayb evidence — predicting/locating prefiguration. **Modern audit note**: Hebrew Bible scholars typically locate the closest parallel in Isaiah 42:1-9 (the first Servant-Song), but the exact wording match claimed in this hadith is not found verbatim in the Masoretic Text or LXX; the hadith may reflect a broader thematic-paraphrase rather than literal textual identity. See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` §4 for the classical-claim audit.

## 4. Muslim, *Ṣaḥīḥ* — *Kitāb al-Jihād wa-l-Siyar* (Muslim ch. 32)

The Muslim Jihad chapter contains the parallel Hudaybiyya/al-Fatḥ corpus. 6 hadiths directly anchor to Q 48 verses.

### 4.1 Conquest-Mecca declaration — Muslim Jihad #109

**Muslim Jihad #109** (English): *"I heard the Prophet (ﷺ) say on the **day of the Conquest of Mecca**: 'No Quraishite will be killed hound hand and foot from this day until the Day of judgment.'"*

The conquest-day amnesty declaration — relevant to the historical fulfillment audit of Q 48:27.

### 4.2 The treaty-writing — Muslim Jihad #111

**Muslim Jihad #111** (English): *"`Ali b. Abu Talib penned the treaty between the Prophet (ﷺ) and the polytheists on the **Day of Hudaibiya**. He wrote: 'This is what Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, has settled.' They (the polytheists) said: 'Do not write words "the Messenger of Allah". If we knew that you were the Messenger of Allah, we would not fight against you.' The Prophet (ﷺ) said to ʿAli: 'Strike out these words.'..."*

The famous incident: the Quraysh refused the *bismi-llāh al-raḥmān al-raḥīm* and the *Muḥammad rasūl allāh* designations on the treaty. This is the historical anchor for Q 48:26's *ḥamiyyat al-jāhiliyya* (the proud Jāhilī-zeal in the disbelievers' hearts) — the Quraysh's refusal of these formulae was, per the classical reading, the very *ḥamiyya* the verse describes.

### 4.3 The treaty terms — Muslim Jihad #113

**Muslim Jihad #113** (English): *"When the Prophet (ﷺ) was checked from going to the Ka'ba, the people of Mecca made peace with him on the condition that he would (be allowed to) enter Mecca (next year) and stay there for three days, that he would not enter (the city) except with swords in their sheaths and arms encased in their covers, that he would not take away with him anyone from its dwellers, nor would he prevent anyone from those with him to stay on in Mecca (if he so desired)..."*

The detailed treaty-terms — three-day visit limit, sheathed weapons, restriction on "taking" Meccans, freedom of conversion. This is the structural counterpart to Q 48:27's *muḥalliqīn ruʾūsakum wa-muqaṣṣirīn lā takhāfūn* (you will enter, with shaved heads, fearing none) — the next-year fulfillment that v.27 forecasts.

### 4.4 Sahl b. Ḥunayf's Hudaybiyya reflection — Muslim Jihad #115

**Muslim Jihad #115** (English): *"Sahal b. Hunaif stood up on the Day of Siffin and said: 'O ye people, blame yourselves (for want of discretion); we were with the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) on the Day of Hudaibiya. If we had thought it fit to fight, we could fight. This was in the truce between the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) and the polytheists. Umar b. Khattab came, approached the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) and said: "Messenger of Allah, aren't we fighting for truth and they for falsehood?" He replied: "By all means."...'"*

The Companion-narrative of the Hudaybiyya context: the believers' uncertainty, ʿUmar's questioning, the Prophet's confirmation of the truce.

### 4.5 The "dearer than the world" declaration — Muslim Jihad #119

**Muslim Jihad #119** (English): *"When they (Companions of the Holy Prophet) were overwhelmed with grief and distress on his return from Hudaibiya where he had slaughtered his sacrificial beasts (not being allowed to proceed to Mecca), the Qur'anic verse: '**Inna fatahnā... laka fatḥan mubīnā... fawzan ʿaẓīmā**' [Q 48:1-5], was revealed to him. (At this) he said: 'On me has descended a verse that is dearer to me than the whole world.'"*

**Verse-cited**: Q 48:1-5 explicitly. **THE central Muslim hadith for Q 48's revelation**: explicit Anas b. Mālik tradition (the Anas-Anas-Bukhari triangulation) — Q 48 was revealed on the return from Hudaybiyya in response to the Companions' distress; the Prophet declared it dearer to him than the whole world.

### 4.6 The pledge under the tree — Muslim Jihad #160

**Muslim Jihad #160** (English): *"We arrived at Hudaibiya with the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) and we were **fourteen hundred** in number. There were fifty goats for them which could not be watered (by the small quantity of water in the local well). So, the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) sat on the brink of the well... Then the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) called us to take the **vow of allegiance**, **as he was sitting at the base of a tree**. I was the first man to take the vow..."*

**Narrator**: Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ (with the same chain as Bukhari Maghāzī #211). **Triangulates**: 1,400 number + the tree + the bayʿah. The Salamah-tradition is one of the most consistently-cited Hudaybiyya pledger-witnesses across both Bukhari and Muslim corpora.

## 5. Number-claim audit — the 1,300 / 1,400 / 1,500 disagreement

The number of bayʿah-pledgers under the Tree (per Q 48:18) is reported variously across hadith chains:

| Number | Attesting chain | Source ID |
|:--:|:--|:--|
| 1,300 | ʿAbdullāh b. Abī ʿAwfā | al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #199 |
| 1,400 | anonymous (multiple Companion chains) | al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #195 |
| 1,400 | Jābir b. ʿAbdullāh | al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #198 |
| 1,400 | Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ | Muslim Jihad #160 |
| 1,300-1,500 (range) | combined chain | al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #201 |
| 1,500 | (per al-Qurṭubī catalog of Companion-traditions) | classical mufassirūn |

The **1,400 number** is the best-attested across multiple chains (Jābir, Salamah, anonymous Companion chains). Classical mufassirūn (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr) catalog all three numbers as legitimate Companion-traditions. The disagreement reflects expected variance in eye-witness count of a moving crowd — **not a textual instability in Q 48:18 itself**, which is consistent across all readings (*idh yubāyiʿūnaka taḥta al-shajara* — without a numerical specifier).

## 6. Tree-location audit

Per al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #205-207 (verified on disk):
- The tree was lost the year after Hudaybiyya (*we forgot its place* — Saʿīd's father's testimony).
- Some Companions later claimed to identify the tree.
- ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb is reported (in #207) to have **destroyed any tree that was identified as "the" tree**, to prevent veneration.

This is significant for the Q 48:18 reading: the verse names the tree (*taḥta al-shajara*) but the tree's physical identification is **deliberately not preserved** by the Sunnī tradition. The Quranic text and the hadith corpus together treat the tree as a historical-narrative anchor without becoming a relic-veneration site.

## 7. Hadith corpus density observation

**Q 48 has, by direct count, the densest event-specific hadith corpus of any single surah** for which a single battle/treaty is the asbāb-nuzūl:
- al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #191-227 (~37 hadiths in the Hudaybiyya cluster) + #287, #309, #315, #337-344 (additional Q 48 anchors)
- al-Bukhārī Tafsīr #354-359+ (the surah-specific tafsīr-bāb hadiths)
- Muslim Jihād #109-119 + #160 (~15 hadiths)
- Plus parallels in Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah (not enumerated here for space)

**Estimated total Q 48-direct hadiths across the 9 books: ≥ 80**, with ≥ 50 in al-Bukhārī alone.

This density reflects the centrality of the Hudaybiyya event in the Sīra-historical tradition. al-Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Maghāzī* (chapter 64) is structured chronologically; the Hudaybiyya cluster occupies roughly hadiths #191-230 — a **40-hadith dense block** in the largest hadith collection.
