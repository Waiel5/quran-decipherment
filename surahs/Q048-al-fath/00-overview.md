## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Fatḥ** ("The Victory / The Manifest Opening") after the opening verse *innā fataḥnā laka fatḥan mubīnan*. The Arabic *fatḥ* (root **f-t-ḥ**) is a verbal noun whose Quranic semantic range covers (i) "opening" (gates, doors, treasuries, knowledge), (ii) "judging / deciding" (between two parties), and (iii) "granting victory / conquest" (the dominant Medinan-context sense). al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* vol. 28) opens the commentary by addressing precisely the exegetical ambiguity: was *fatḥ* in v.1 referring to the Treaty of Ḥudaybiyya (6 AH), the prospective Conquest of Mecca (8 AH), the conquest of Khaybar (7 AH), or a generic "opening" of victory — see §3 below.

## 3. Opening formula — the corpus's only *innā fataḥnā* declarative-victory opener

Q 48:1-3 (no-tashkeel):

| v | Text | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | إنا فتحنا لك فتحا مبينا | *Indeed, We have granted you a manifest victory.* |
| 2 | ليغفر لك الله ما تقدم من ذنبك وما تأخر ويتم نعمته عليك ويهديك صراطا مستقيما | *That Allāh may forgive you what preceded of your fault and what will follow, and complete His favor upon you, and guide you to a straight path.* |
| 3 | وينصرك الله نصرا عزيزا | *And that Allāh may aid you with a mighty victory.* |

The **corpus has no other surah opening with *innā fataḥnā/fataḥnā*** in the perfect-active 1P "We have opened/granted" form. Q 48:1 is **structurally and lexically unique** as a declarative-perfect-victory opener. The closest morphological relatives are:
- Q 110:1 *idhā jāʾa naṣru llāhi wa-l-fatḥ* ("when comes the help of Allāh and the victory") — uses *fatḥ* but in a conditional clause and within an entirely different surah-frame (3-verse retrospective).
- Q 7:89 *rabbanā ftaḥ baynanā wa-bayna qawminā bi-l-ḥaqq* ("our Lord, decide between us and our people in truth") — petitional jussive, not declarative.

Two **classical exegetical disagreements** on the referent of *fatḥ* in v.1 (al-Ṭabarī *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* ad loc.; al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* vol. 28 p. 79-83; al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 18 chronology):

- **The Treaty of Ḥudaybiyya as the *fatḥ*** (al-Bukhārī, al-Zuhrī, Anas b. Mālik via al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #214 — VERIFIED on disk: "*[v. 48:1] refers to the Hudaybiyya Pledge*"). This is the dominant Sunni reading, supported by the majority Companion-tradition (al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib, Anas).
- **The forthcoming Conquest of Mecca as the *fatḥ*** (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Mujāhid in some chains): the perfect tense is rhetorical-prolepsis ("we have granted [as if accomplished]"), reading v.27 *fa-jaʿala min dūni dhālika fatḥan qarīban* as referring to a *different* near victory (Khaybar) and v.1 to Mecca proper.
- **The Conquest of Khaybar** (al-Zamakhsharī mentions but does not endorse): possible referent.
- **Spiritual/exegetical "opening"** (al-Rāzī's option D): metaphorical opening of knowledge/heart — minority, not preferred by classical mufassirūn.

The **dominant reading** (Hudaybiyya = v.1 *fatḥ*; Khaybar or near-future = v.27 *fatḥan qarīban*) is anchored in al-Bukhārī *Maghāzī* #214 (al-Barāʾ tradition: *kāna nazlat fī ṣulḥ al-Ḥudaybiyya*) and Muslim *Jihād* #119 (Anas tradition: when *innā fataḥnā* descended on the return from Hudaybiyya, the Prophet declared *la-qad unzilat ʿalayya āyatun hiya aḥabbu ilayya min al-dunyā jamīʿan*).

## 6. ⭐ Falsifiable forward-prophecy claim — Q 48:27

The verse Q 48:27 (translated: *"Allāh has truly fulfilled His Messenger's vision in truth: you will most certainly enter the Sacred Mosque, if Allāh wills, in safety, with your heads shaved or your hair shortened, fearing none. He knew what you did not know, and granted you, besides this, a near victory."*) is a **rare structural feature in the Quran**: a forward-looking, verifiable temporal prediction. The verse:
- Predicts the Muslim community's safe entry into the Masjid al-Ḥarām (i.e., into Mecca itself), with specific ritual details (head-shaving, *muḥalliqīn ruʾūsakum wa-muqaṣṣirīn*) and a non-fear-state (*lā takhāfūn*).
- Was revealed in 6 AH per the al-Bukhārī Maghāzī chronology (return from Hudaybiyya).
- Was historically fulfilled in 8 AH (the bloodless Conquest of Mecca, *fatḥ Makkah*, per al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #309 *jihād Allāh's Messenger fought the Ghazwa of al-Fatḥ during Ramadan*; al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #344 Mecca-as-sanctuary speech).

Q 48:27 thus joins a narrow structural class of **falsifiable temporal predictions** in the Quran:

| Surah | Verse | Prediction-content | Verifiability | Status |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Q 30:2-4 | al-Rūm | *ghulibati al-Rūm... sa-yaghlibūn fī biḍʿ sinīn* ("the Romans have been defeated... they shall conquer in a few years") | Forward-looking; "few years" = 3-9 in classical Arabic | Reportedly fulfilled at Battle of Nineveh, 627 CE (see §11 below for audit) |
| Q 48:27 | al-Fatḥ | safe entry into Masjid al-Ḥarām | Forward-looking; specific ritual details | Reportedly fulfilled at the Conquest of Mecca, 8 AH (see Q048-F-04 audit) |
| Q 105 | al-Fīl | retrospective on the elephant-army defeat | Retrospective (not forward-looking) | N/A — not a prediction |

Q 48:27 and Q 30:2-4 form an **architectural pair of forward-looking falsifiable predictions**. Both are flagged in classical *iʿjāz al-ghayb* (the iʿjāz of unseen knowledge) sub-tradition (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 65 *iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*; al-Khaṭṭābī *Bayān iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*). ## 7. ⭐ Bayʿat al-Riḍwān — Q 48:18

The verse Q 48:18 (*"laqad raḍiya allāhu ʿan al-muʾminīn idh yubāyiʿūnaka taḥta al-shajara"* — "indeed Allāh was pleased with the believers when they pledged allegiance to you under the tree") is the canonical reference verse for the **Bayʿat al-Riḍwān** ("Pledge of Divine-Pleasure"), one of the most narrated events in the Bukhari Maghāzī cycle. The hadith corpus on this single verse is dense — verified on disk at `data/literature/hadith/ahmedbaset-json/db/by_chapter/the_9_books/bukhari/64.json`:

- **al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #194-218** (~25 hadith): the Hudaybiyya/Tree-pledge cluster, including #194 (al-Barāʾ: *"do you consider the conquest of Mecca to be the victory? we consider the actual victory was the Pledge of al-Riḍwān"*), #195 (Anas: *they were 1400+ at the well*), #198 (Jābir: *we were 1400 then; if I could see now, I would show you the place of the Tree*), #199 (Ibn Abī ʿAwfā: *those under the Tree numbered 1300*).
- **Muslim Jihad #160** (Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ): *"we arrived at Hudaybiya... fourteen hundred in number... [the Prophet] called us to take the vow of allegiance, as he was sitting at the base of a tree. I was the first man to take the vow."*

The Bayʿat al-Riḍwān thus directly anchors Q 48:18 to a specific historical event with multiple independently-attested chains (al-Barāʾ, Anas, Jābir, Salamah b. al-Akwaʿ, Ibn Abī ʿAwfā). See `04-hadith-corpus.md` for the full hadith corpus and `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for the bayʿat-and-tree-location claim audit.

## 11. Quick content structure (3-block taxonomy)

The 29 verses partition naturally into **3 thematic blocks** (per al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar*; al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* vol. 28):

- **vv. 1-10 (RATIFICATION block, 10 verses)**: Divine declaration of *manifest victory* (vv. 1-3), forgiveness and aid; rest-and-tranquility (*sakīna*, v.4) descending on believers; the *muʾminīn / muʾmināt → jannāt* reward (v.5) vs *munāfiqīn / munāfiqāt → jahannam* punishment (v.6); the believers' allegiance-pledge (*innā arsalnāka shāhidan wa-mubashshiran wa-nadhīran*, v.8); the *yubāyiʿūnaka* — *yad allāhi fawqa aydīhim* allegiance-formula (v.10).
- **vv. 11-17 (CONDEMNATION-OF-MUKHALLIFĪN block, 7 verses)**: Direct address to the *al-mukhallifūn min al-aʿrāb* — the Bedouins who stayed behind (vv. 11-12); their hypocrite-rationalization (*shaghalatnā amwālunā wa-ahlūnā*, v.11); the threat of judgment (vv. 13, 16-17); reluctant Bedouin condemnation as the second-largest theme.
- **vv. 18-29 (BAYʿAT-AL-RIḌWĀN + CONQUEST-PROPHECY block, 12 verses)**: The Bayʿat al-Riḍwān verse (v.18) — the most often-cited Q 48 verse in hadith; verbalized divine pleasure with the pledgers under the Tree; the booty-promise (vv. 19-21, *maghānim kathīra*); the dream-fulfillment promise (v.27); the closing universal-prophet description of Muḥammad and his followers (v.29 — "they shall be seen kneeling and prostrating, seeking grace from Allāh and good pleasure; their mark on their faces from the trace of prostration; that is their description in the Torah and the Gospel...").
