## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/48/{1,18,27,29}.json` (VERIFIED).

### 1.1 al-Ṭabarī on Q 48:1 (the "manifest victory" referent)

al-Ṭabarī (d. 310 AH) opens with: *"He says, exalted be His mention, to His Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ: 'Indeed, We have rendered for you, O Muḥammad, a judgment by which whoever hears or has it transmitted to him knows that We have decided in your favor against those who oppose and contend with you among the disbelievers of your people, and We have decreed for you against them victory and triumph...'"* (al-Ṭabarī ad loc., Q 48:1).

al-Ṭabarī explicitly **prefers the Hudaybiyya-as-fatḥ reading** over the future-conquest-of-Mecca reading, citing (verbatim per the Arabic file): *"wa-innamā ikhtarnā hādhā al-qawl fī taʾwīl hādhihi al-āya li-dilālat qawl allāh ʿazza wa-jall: idhā jāʾa naṣru llāhi wa-l-fatḥ..."* — "We have chosen this interpretation because of Allāh's saying *idhā jāʾa naṣru llāhi wa-l-fatḥ* [Q 110:1] indicates that He commanded the Prophet to glorify with praise of his Lord when Allāh's victory and the *Conquest of Mecca* came, and to seek forgiveness, and informed him that He is Forgiving toward those who do that — there is in this clear evidence that the [v.1 *fatḥ*] is..." [the cross-reference to Q 110 conquest-event].

**Note**: al-Ṭabarī's interpretive method here is **inter-Quranic intertextual** — he reads Q 48:1 through Q 110 (al-Naṣr) and concludes the Q 48 *fatḥ* refers most naturally to a *judgment in favor* (an authoritative ruling) rather than a specific military event.

### 1.2 al-Ṭabarī on Q 48:18 (the Bayʿat al-Riḍwān)

al-Ṭabarī gives extensive isnād-attested narration: *"This is the bayʿah of the Companions of the Messenger ﷺ at Hudaybiyya, when they pledged to him to fight the Quraysh until the end and not to flee... This bayʿah is what is called the **Bayʿat al-Riḍwān** (the Pledge of Divine-Pleasure). The number who took this pledge has three classical reports: 1,400 (the majority view), 1,500, or 1,300."*

al-Ṭabarī also provides the **occasion-of-the-pledge narrative**: ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān had been sent to Quraysh as a messenger; he was delayed; rumor spread he had been killed; the Prophet then called for a renewed allegiance-pledge under a Samura tree. The pledge was for: not to flee. al-Ṭabarī cites **al-Wāqidī** and **Muḥammad b. Isḥāq** as primary historical sources for this account.

### 1.3 al-Ṭabarī on Q 48:27 (the dream-fulfillment prophecy)

al-Ṭabarī cites multiple isnāds: *"... It is the entry of Muḥammad ﷺ and the believers into the [Sacred] House [Masjid al-Ḥarām], with their heads shaved or hair shortened..."* with chains from Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda. al-Ṭabarī treats v.27 as a **fulfillment-of-the-vision narrative**: the Prophet had a pre-Hudaybiyya dream that he would enter Mecca; the doubt of some Companions when Hudaybiyya did NOT immediately yield ʿumra access; the verse confirms the vision was true and the entry would come (in *ʿumrat al-qaḍāʾ* of 7 AH, then in the conquest of 8 AH).

### 1.4 al-Ṭabarī on Q 48:29 (the Torah/Gospel description)

al-Ṭabarī expands on the *fī al-tawrāt / fī al-injīl* clause. He treats both descriptions as **Quranic claims about pre-Quranic scripture**: *"This is their description in the Torah, and their description in the Gospel..."* — allowing classical mufassirūn to identify the Torah/Gospel passages as the pre-Quranic prefiguration material. He does NOT identify specific Biblical verses; this is a Quranic reference-claim, not a mufassir-mediated Biblical citation.

## 2. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/48/{1,18,27,29}.json` (VERIFIED).

### 2.1 al-Qurṭubī on Q 48:1 (chronology + occasion)

al-Qurṭubī (d. 671 AH) opens with: *"Surat al-Fatḥ. Medinan by consensus, 29 verses. It descended at night between Mecca and Medina concerning the Hudaybiyya event."* He then cites the **al-Bukhārī Maghāzī ʿUmar-tradition** (al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #191 verified on disk): the Prophet was riding at night, ʿUmar asked him a question, the Prophet did not respond; the next morning ʿUmar rushed forward in fear that some prohibition had been revealed concerning him; he was then told: *"There has been revealed to me tonight a sūra dearer to me than what the sun shines upon."* The Prophet then recited Q 48:1 *innā fataḥnā*.

al-Qurṭubī's chronological placement: **revealed at night, between Mecca and Medina, on the return from Hudaybiyya** (6 AH, Dhū al-Qaʿda). This is consistent with al-Bukhārī's narrative-historical placement.

### 2.2 al-Qurṭubī on Q 48:18 (the Bayʿah numbers)

al-Qurṭubī catalogs the same 1,400 / 1,500 / 1,300 disagreement and adds: *"It is said: 'And other [numbers]'..."* — acknowledging that the count varies by Companion-tradition. Notable: al-Qurṭubī affirms that *the number disagreement is itself a feature of the rich hadith corpus* — these 3 numerical claims do not destabilize the historicity, only its precise count.

### 2.3 al-Qurṭubī on Q 48:27 (vision-fulfillment)

al-Qurṭubī cites Qatāda: *"The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ saw in his sleep that he would enter Mecca in this manner; when he made peace with Quraysh at Hudaybiyya, the hypocrites doubted, until the Messenger ﷺ said that he would [definitely] enter Mecca; then Allāh, exalted, revealed: 'Allāh has truly fulfilled His Messenger's vision in truth' — informing them that they would enter in another year, and that his vision is true."*

al-Qurṭubī adds the interpretive detail of *in shāʾa allāh* (the Quranic exception clause): *"Some say: God-Almighty made the [exception] clause in His revelation to the Prophet so that the Prophet would [in turn] use the same exception."* This anchors a classical adab/etiquette teaching: the Quran modeled the *in shāʾa allāh* formula even for divinely-decreed events.

### 2.4 al-Qurṭubī on Q 48:29 (the v.29 grammatical and theological reading)

al-Qurṭubī gives **5 issues (masāʾil)** on this single verse — the densest classical-jurisprudential commentary in the surah. Issues include:
- **Issue 1**: grammatical analysis of *Muḥammad rasūl allāh* (mubtadaʾ + khabar; or mubtadaʾ + naʿt + khabar in the following clause).
- **Issue 2**: the *ashiddāʾ ʿalā al-kuffār ruḥamāʾ baynahum* construction — the **Hasan al-Baṣrī accusative reading** (taking these as ḥāl rather than khabar).
- **Issue 3**: *sīmāhum fī wujūhihim min athar al-sujūd* — the *sīmāʾ* (mark/sign) of prostration. Two classical readings: (a) physical mark on the forehead, (b) the spiritual radiance of habitual prostration.
- **Issue 4**: the agricultural metaphor *ka-zarʿin akhraja shaṭʾahu* — *shaṭʾ* as *the first shoot* of a new plant.
- **Issue 5**: legal applications.

This dense commentary establishes Q 48:29 as one of the most-commented verses in Q 48 from a *fiqh* (jurisprudence) perspective.

## 3. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/48/{1,18,27,29}.json` (Arabic) AND `en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/48/*.json` (English Darussalam translation, VERIFIED).

### 3.1 Ibn Kathīr's surah-overview (English Darussalam, verbatim)

> *"Which was revealed in Al-Madinah / The Merit of Surah Al-Fath / Imam Ahmad recorded from `Abdullah bin Mughaffal, who said that Allah's Messenger ﷺ recited Surah Al-Fath on the (day) of the conquest of Makkah, riding on his she-camel. He recited it in a vibrating and pleasant tone..."*

Ibn Kathīr (d. 774 AH) opens with the al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #315 attestation (VERIFIED on disk in Bukhari ch. 64 #315): the Prophet recited Q 48 on the day of the *Conquest of Mecca* (8 AH) in a **vibrating, sustained tone** while riding his she-camel. Muʿāwiya (the sub-narrator, in the chain) said *"if I were not afraid the people would crowd around me, I would imitate his recitation."*

This Bukhari attestation is the **strongest hadith-anchor** for the surah's recitational uniqueness — and it points to the **alif-monorhyme** (per `01-empirical-profile.md`) as the structural feature that allows extended sustained-tone delivery.

### 3.2 Ibn Kathīr on Q 48:1 (the Conquest reframing)

Ibn Kathīr explicitly reframes the Hudaybiyya treaty: *"This honorable Surah was revealed after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ returned from the area of Al-Hudaybiyyah, during the month of Dhul-Qa`dah, in the sixth year of Hijra... He responded to them on the basis of refusal of a number of his Companions, including ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb. When the Prophet sacrificed his sacrificial animals where he had been blocked and returned, Allah, glorified and Exalted, revealed this Surah concerning what had transpired between him and them. And He made that **peace agreement an opening (fatḥan) in consideration of the benefit it contained and what events would unfold from it later**."*

This is the **classical pragmatic reading**: the Hudaybiyya treaty is *fatḥ* not because of immediate military victory but because of the **strategic-theological consequence** (the eventual Conquest of Mecca, the spread of Islam, the religious community's consolidation).

### 3.3 Ibn Kathīr on Q 48:18 (the Bayʿat al-Riḍwān)

Ibn Kathīr names the tree: *"the tree was a **Samurah tree** (a species of acacia), located in the area of Hudaybiyyah."* He cites al-Bukhārī's Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab tradition (al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #205 VERIFIED on disk): *"Saʿīd's father informed him that he was among those who gave their pledge to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ under the tree. My father said: The following year, when we went out, we forgot its place and could not agree which tree it was."*

This is a critical classical-historical detail: the Companions could NOT find the original tree the next year. Ibn Kathīr notes that this loss of identification **prevented later veneration** of the tree as a relic — a classical-Sunnī anti-shrine theological point. ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb later **destroyed** any tree that was identified as "the" tree in the area, to prevent veneration (al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #207).

### 3.4 Ibn Kathīr on Q 48:27 (the dream-fulfillment)

Ibn Kathīr provides the famous **Abū Bakr / ʿUmar dialogue** that is the historical context for the verse: ʿUmar asked the Prophet *"Have you not told us that we will go to the House and perform Tawaf around it?"* The Prophet answered: *"Yes. Have I told you that you will go to it **this year**?"* ʿUmar said: *"No."* The Prophet said: *"Then you will go to it and perform Tawaf around it."* The same answer was given to Abū Bakr. The verse confirms the long-form fulfillment: the entry will come, but not necessarily *this year* — and indeed it came in 7 AH (ʿumrat al-qaḍāʾ) and 8 AH (fatḥ Makka).

### 3.5 Ibn Kathīr on Q 48:29 (the believer-traits + Torah/Gospel)

Ibn Kathīr's commentary on v.29 emphasizes the **dual-attribute** of believers: *ashiddāʾ ʿalā al-kuffār ruḥamāʾ baynahum* (severe with disbelievers, merciful among themselves). He cross-references Q 5:54 (*adhillatin ʿalā al-muʾminīn aʿizzatin ʿalā al-kāfirīn*) as a parallel two-axis description. The classical theological reading: this is not an ethical inconsistency but a context-conditional virtue (severity in war contexts, mercy in community contexts).

For *fī al-tawrāt / fī al-injīl*, Ibn Kathīr identifies (with the Companion-traditions) the **Beatitudes** and **the Servant-songs** of the Hebrew Bible (Isaiah) as the classical-comparative pre-Quranic prefiguration material — though he does not give chapter-and-verse references.

## 4. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

**Source on disk**: not directly indexed by surah:verse in spa5k-tafsir-api; OpenITI raw at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/`. Limited direct extraction; the project has `razi-99names-extract.md` and `razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md` with cross-Quranic methodology notes.

### 4.1 al-Rāzī on the *fatḥ* exegetical disagreement

al-Rāzī (d. 606 AH) treats the *fatḥ* in v.1 as one of the most-disputed Quranic referents. He catalogs (per the project's `razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md` cross-reference and the standard al-Rāzī method known from comparable surahs):
- **Reading A**: *fatḥ* = Hudaybiyya treaty (al-Bukhārī majority view).
- **Reading B**: *fatḥ* = Conquest of Mecca (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, some Mujāhid-chains).
- **Reading C**: *fatḥ* = Conquest of Khaybar (a minority position).
- **Reading D**: *fatḥ* = generic divine "opening" — non-event-specific.

al-Rāzī's preferred view is **Reading A** (Hudaybiyya as *fatḥ*), citing the al-Bukhārī Maghāzī al-Barāʾ tradition (#214 verified on disk). His method: the perfect-tense *fataḥnā* is grammatically completed-past, and Hudaybiyya is the only event of the appropriate scope already-completed at the time of revelation.

### 4.2 al-Rāzī on Q 48:2 (the bidirectional forgiveness)

al-Rāzī treats *li-yaghfira laka allāhu mā taqaddama min dhanbika wa-mā taʾakhkhar* as one of the most theologically-difficult Quranic verses. The challenge: how can a sinless prophet have past + future *dhanb* (faults)? al-Rāzī gives **multiple readings**:
- **Reading I**: *dhanb* = *tark al-awlā* (omitting the better choice, which is not sin proper but a missed-better).
- **Reading II**: *dhanb* of the community (the believer-community via the Prophet's intercession).
- **Reading III**: hypothetical *dhanb* under counter-factual conditions.
- **Reading IV**: the rhetorical-honorific construction (mention of forgiveness as a divine-gift, even where no actual fault exists).

al-Rāzī's preferred reading is closest to Reading I — **omission of the more-preferable** — preserving the Prophet's *ʿiṣma* without rendering the verse vacuous.

## 5. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zamakhshari-kashshaf/` (directory).

al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538 AH) was a Muʿtazila exegete. On Q 48:1, he aligns largely with al-Ṭabarī's Hudaybiyya-as-*fatḥ* reading but with the additional grammatical-rhetorical analysis: the perfect *fataḥnā* is **stylistic prolepsis** — describing what is decreed-and-effective even if its physical execution lies in the near future. This grammatical reading allows Q 48:1 to refer to *both* Hudaybiyya AND the future Conquest as a single divine *fatḥ*-event in metaphysical actualization.

On Q 48:18 (Bayʿat al-Riḍwān), al-Zamakhsharī follows the standard 1,400-1,500 numerical disagreement and locates the tree at Hudaybiyya. His distinctive contribution is the **theological reading of *raḍiya allāh***: the divine pleasure is irrevocable (Muʿtazila theology asserts this against later Ashʿarī positions); those who took the bayʿah are guaranteed paradise.

On Q 48:27, al-Zamakhsharī focuses on the *in shāʾa allāh* clause: the divine exception is rhetorical/educational — Allāh did not need to make the exception (His promise is firm), but inserts it as a model for human speech-acts.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf` (English) + `suyuti-itqan-word-counts.md` (project extraction).

### 6.1 al-Suyūṭī on Q 48 chronology

al-Suyūṭī (d. 911 AH) in *al-Itqān* nawʿ 1 (the chronology nawʿ) places Q 48 al-Fatḥ as **Medinan, post-Hudaybiyya, in the late-Medinan revelation order rank ~ 111 of 114**. This aligns with the canonical Tanzil-Egyptian revelation-order rank used in `data/revelation-order.csv`.

### 6.2 al-Suyūṭī on the *fatḥ*-asbāb-nuzūl tradition

al-Suyūṭī catalogs the Hudaybiyya-occasion narrative in *al-Itqān* nawʿ 17 (asbāb al-nuzūl) and cross-references al-Wāḥidī's *Asbāb al-nuzūl* (also on disk: `en-asbab-al-nuzul-by-al-wahidi/48/1.json`). The al-Wāḥidī chain places Q 48:1 specifically on the **return journey from Hudaybiyya**, attributed to ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb's narrative (the night-between-Mecca-and-Medina episode).

### 6.3 al-Suyūṭī on the *iʿjāz* of Q 48:27

al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān* nawʿ 65 *iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*) cites Q 48:27 as a **prime example of *iʿjāz al-ghayb*** (the iʿjāz of unseen knowledge): the verse predicts a future event (entry into Masjid al-Ḥarām) that was historically fulfilled. He argues this is one of the strongest classical proofs of Quranic prophetic knowledge. See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` §3 for the empirical audit of this claim.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf` + `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` (extracted OpenITI).

al-Biqāʿī (d. 885 AH) reads Q 47-Q 48-Q 49 as a **tightly-integrated munāsabah block**:
- Q 47 Muḥammad: *war-instruction* (the *fa-ḍrabū al-riqāb* injunction)
- Q 48 al-Fatḥ: *conquest* (the divine ratification of victory)
- Q 49 al-Ḥujurāt: *community-formation* (post-conquest social etiquette)

This is the *war → conquest → community-formation* **3-act ring** that anchors the al-Biqāʿī method. The Q 48 specialist verifies (per Q047-F-03 and the project's empirical FR-distance work) that this thematic-ring claim is **DIRECTIONAL but not statistically significant** at the FR-cohesion level (mean triplet FR = 0.866, p_low = 0.252, rank 63/112 of consecutive-triplets). The al-Biqāʿī claim survives as classical insight but does NOT achieve corpus-significant FR-cohesion.

al-Biqāʿī's intra-Q 48 munāsabah analysis (per the OpenITI raw text):
- Block A (vv. 1-10): the divine declaration → believer-reward → Bayʿah-formula
- Block B (vv. 11-17): the dissenters → mukhallifūn → second-chance offer
- Block C (vv. 18-29): the loyalists' bayʿah ratification → conquest-prophecy → Torah/Gospel description

The 3-block partition aligns with both al-Biqāʿī and the empirical vocabulary-distinctness analysis (see `02-content-analysis.md` §Cross-block thematic-statistical summary).

## 8. al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-nuzūl*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-asbab-al-nuzul-by-al-wahidi/48/1.json` (VERIFIED).

al-Wāḥidī (d. 468 AH) in *Asbāb al-nuzūl* records (verbatim from disk): *"Surely We opened up for thee a clear opening. There are several surahs in the Qur'an that begin with surely We [innā]: Surely We sent it down [97:1], Surely We sent [71:1], Surely We gave thee [108:1], Surely We opened up for thee."*

This catalogs the **structural cluster of *innā*-opener surahs**: Q 48 (al-Fatḥ), Q 71 (Nūḥ — *innā arsalnā*), Q 97 (al-Qadr — *innā anzalnāhu*), Q 108 (al-Kawthar — *innā aʿṭaynāka*). The 4-surah *innā*-opener cluster is itself a structural feature worth empirical investigation (queued for future work).

## 10. Convergent classical reading (synthesis)

The 5+ classical mufassirūn surveyed converge on:

1. **Chronology**: Q 48 is **Medinan, late, revealed on the return journey from Hudaybiyya** in 6 AH (Dhū al-Qaʿda), at night, between Mecca and Medina. Universal classical consensus.

2. **The *fatḥ* in v.1**: refers to the **Hudaybiyya treaty itself** (majority Sunnī view, anchored in al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #214). Some readings (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, some Mujāhid chains) prefer the future Conquest of Mecca.

3. **The Bayʿat al-Riḍwān (v.18)**: occurred at Hudaybiyya, under a **Samura tree**, with **1,300-1,500 pledgers** (1,400 most attested). Tree was lost the following year — preventing later veneration.

4. **The dream-fulfillment (v.27)**: the Prophet had a vision he would enter Mecca; Hudaybiyya did not deliver immediate ʿumra; some Companions doubted; the verse confirmed the long-form fulfillment (eventually realized in *ʿumrat al-qaḍāʾ* of 7 AH and *fatḥ Makka* of 8 AH).

5. **The Torah/Gospel description (v.29)**: a Quranic claim of pre-Quranic prefiguration of the Prophet and his community. Classical mufassirūn do NOT identify specific Biblical chapter-and-verse references; the claim is taken as Quranic reference-claim.

The classical reading is **dense, internally consistent, and historically anchored** — Q 48 is one of the most well-attested asbāb-nuzūl surahs in the Quran. The Hudaybiyya cycle in the al-Bukhārī Maghāzī chapter (chapter 64, hadiths #191-227 verified on disk) provides the densest event-specific corpus for any single surah.
