# Q 48 al-Fatḥ — Content Analysis

> Verse-by-verse + 3 thematic block analysis.

## Block A — Ratification (vv. 1-10)

The first 10 verses constitute the **Divine ratification** of the Hudaybiyya treaty as a victory. The block is dominated by:
- Allāh as subject (12 of 12 verses' first-person plural verbs are divine)
- *fatḥ* root (4× concentrated here: vv. 1, 18, 27 — but 2 in v.1 alone)
- The believer/disbeliever opposition (v.5 jannāt vs v.6 jahannam)
- The sakīna ("tranquility") gift (v.4)
- The witness-evangelist-warner triple of Muhammad (v.8)
- The Bayʿah-formula declaration (v.10): *al-ladhīna yubāyiʿūnaka innamā yubāyiʿūn allāh*

### v. 1: إنا فتحنا لك فتحا مبينا

*innā fataḥnā laka fatḥan mubīnā* — "Indeed, We have granted you a manifest victory."

**The opening declaration**. In Arabic perfect tense (*fataḥnā*), denoting completed/decisive action. Two f-t-ḥ tokens in one verse (verb + cognate-accusative noun) — the corpus's only instance of f-t-ḥ root used twice in a single verse. The adjective *mubīnan* ("manifest, clear") is a key Quranic descriptor of decisive divine action (also *al-yawm al-ākhar al-mubīn*, *kitāb al-mubīn*, *naṣr al-mubīn*).

**Classical reading** (al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #214 → al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib tradition; al-Bukhārī Tafsir #354): the *fatḥ* refers to the **Treaty of Ḥudaybiyya** itself, NOT a future conquest of Mecca. The Companions initially perceived Hudaybiyya as a setback (the Prophet was barred from entering Mecca for ʿumra, and the treaty's terms were perceived as concession-heavy); v.1 reframes the perception: Hudaybiyya IS the *fatḥ*. al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #214 records al-Barāʾ saying (verbatim): *"Do you (people) consider the conquest of Mecca, the Victory (referred to in the Qur'an 48:1)? Was the conquest of Mecca a victory? We really consider that the actual Victory was the Ar-Riḍwān Pledge..."*

### v. 2: ليغفر لك الله ما تقدم من ذنبك وما تأخر

*li-yaghfira laka allāhu mā taqaddama min dhanbika wa-mā taʾakhkhar* — "That Allāh may forgive you what preceded of your fault and what will follow."

**Theological-rhetorical key**: the divine purpose-clause for the *fatḥ*. The forgiveness is bidirectional in time (*mā taqaddama / mā taʾakhkhar*) — past + future faults of the Prophet are encompassed. al-Rāzī (vol. 28 p. 84-90) treats this as a difficult verse: the Prophet's *ʿiṣma* (sinlessness in the technical theological sense) is preserved by reading *dhanb* as *tark al-awlā* (omission of the better) rather than *maʿṣiya* (sin proper). al-Zamakhsharī gives 4 readings; al-Ṭabarī gives 5.

### v. 3: وينصرك الله نصرا عزيزا

*wa-yanṣuraka allāhu naṣran ʿazīzā* — "And that Allāh may aid you with a mighty victory."

The cognate-accusative *naṣran ʿazīzā* (mighty victory-help) parallels v.1's *fatḥan mubīnā*. Both verses use the *qatl-cognate-accusative* construction (verb + cognate noun + adjective).

### v. 4: هو الذي أنزل السكينة في قلوب المؤمنين ليزدادوا إيمانا مع إيمانهم

*huwa al-ladhī anzala al-sakīnata fī qulūbi al-muʾminīna li-yazdādū īmānan maʿa īmānihim* — "It is He who sent down tranquility into the hearts of the believers, that they may add faith upon their faith."

**The sakīna verse**. *Sakīna* is the rare divine-gift root (s-k-n) for "calmness/tranquility" — used 6 times in the Quran in this technical sense (Q 2:248, Q 9:26, Q 9:40, Q 48:4, Q 48:18, Q 48:26 — 3 of 6 occurrences are in Q 48 itself). The sakīna descends specifically on the believers' hearts, an inner-state grant that **increases** existing faith. The closing *wa-li-llāhi junūdu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* ("Allāh has hosts of the heavens and the earth") is repeated verbatim at v. 7 — a 3-verse-apart frame, structurally distinctive.

### v. 5: ليدخل المؤمنين والمؤمنات جنات تجري من تحتها الأنهار

The believer/believer-feminine pair receives the gardens-of-rivers reward + sin-effacement (*wa-yukaffira ʿanhum sayyiʾātihim*) + *fawzan ʿaẓīmā*. The gendered pair-form (*muʾminīn wa-muʾmināt*) is morphologically marked. Pairs with v.6.

### v. 6: ويعذب المنافقين والمنافقات والمشركين والمشركات

The hypocrite-feminine + hypocrite-masculine + polytheist-feminine + polytheist-masculine four-fold receives jahannam + *dāʾira al-sūʾ* (the ring of evil) + divine wrath. Notable for **two morphologically-marked rare-feminine plurals** *al-munāfiqāt* and *al-mushrikāt*. The verse contains *ẓannin al-sūʾ* (suspicion of evil) — the first of 3 ẓann-tokens in Q 48 (cf. Q048-F-01.5 Bedouin-suspicion theme).

### v. 7: ولله جنود السماوات والأرض وكان الله عزيزا حكيما

Verbatim repetition of v.4's tail clause (*wa-li-llāhi junūdu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ*). The closing *wa-kāna allāhu ʿazīzan ḥakīmā* (echoing v.4's *ʿalīman ḥakīmā*) completes a frame around v.5-6.

### v. 8: إنا أرسلناك شاهدا ومبشرا ونذيرا

*innā arsalnāka shāhidan wa-mubashshiran wa-nadhīrā* — "Indeed, We have sent you as a witness, a bringer of glad tidings, and a warner."

**The triple-role declaration**. The three nouns *shāhid / mubashshir / nadhīr* are also the canonical formula in Q 33:45 (Q 33 al-Aḥzāb, the parallel Medinan-Prophetic-naming surah). Bukhārī Tafsīr #359 (VERIFIED on disk) attests that this verse-formula appears in the Torah verbatim per Companion-tradition.

### v. 9: لتؤمنوا بالله ورسوله

*li-tuʾminū bi-llāhi wa-rasūlihi wa-tuʿazzirūhu wa-tuwaqqirūhu wa-tusabbiḥūhu bukratan wa-aṣīlā* — "That you may believe in Allāh and His Messenger and support him and revere him, and glorify Him in the morning and the evening."

The five-fold devotional verb sequence (*tuʾminū / tuʿazzirū / tuwaqqirū / tusabbiḥū / bukratan-wa-aṣīlā*) — a corpus-distinctive devotional grouping. The pronominal references (some reading as Allāh, some as the Messenger) generate a classical exegetical disagreement (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī).

### v. 10: إن الذين يبايعونك إنما يبايعون الله

*inna al-ladhīna yubāyiʿūnaka innamā yubāyiʿūn allāh, yad allāhi fawqa aydīhim* — "Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you only pledge allegiance to Allāh; the hand of Allāh is over their hands."

**The Bayʿah-equivalence verse**. The pledge to Muhammad IS a pledge to Allāh; the hand of Allāh is metaphorically/theophorically *fawqa aydīhim* (over their hands). This verse provides the theological grounding for the Bayʿat al-Riḍwān (v.18). Both Sunni and Shīʿī classical traditions cite v.10 as a foundation for the bayʿa-as-political-legitimacy doctrine. Closes with *fa-man nakatha fa-innamā yankuthu ʿalā nafsih* (whoever breaks the pledge breaks it against himself).

## Block B — Mukhallifūn Condemnation (vv. 11-17)

Direct address to the *al-mukhallifūn min al-aʿrāb* — the Bedouin tribes who **stayed behind** when the Prophet announced the Hudaybiyya pilgrimage and offered excuses (*shaghalatnā amwālunā wa-ahlūnā*, "our properties and families occupied us"). Block B is morphologically marked by:
- Explicit Bedouin lexicon: *al-mukhallifūn min al-aʿrāb* (vv. 11, 16; corpus-distinctive)
- *qul* (say) imperative — divine address-relay (vv. 11, 16)
- The future-tense *sa-yaqūlu* (they will say) — vv. 11, 15, 16 — a structural anticipation of Bedouin response
- The *ẓann al-sūʾ* (suspicion of evil) repeating from v. 6

### v. 11: سيقول لك المخلفون من الأعراب شغلتنا أموالنا وأهلونا

*"Those of the Bedouins who stayed behind will say to you: 'Our properties and families occupied us, so ask forgiveness for us'..."*

The first explicit *al-mukhallifūn min al-aʿrāb* designation. The Bedouin tribes (Banū Ḥijāz peripheral tribes per al-Wāqidī *Maghāzī*) had been called by the Prophet to accompany the Hudaybiyya pilgrimage but stayed behind, calculating that the Quraysh would defeat the small Muslim contingent. The verse exposes their internal *yaqūlūna bi-alsinatihim mā laysa fī qulūbihim* (they say with their tongues what is not in their hearts) — the canonical hypocrite-inversion (heart-tongue dissociation).

### v. 12: بل ظننتم أن لن ينقلب الرسول والمؤمنون إلى أهليهم

*"Rather, you thought that the Messenger and the believers would never return to their families..."*

Direct revelation of the Bedouin's **internal calculation** — they expected the Quraysh to annihilate the Muslim contingent. The doubled *ẓann al-sūʾ* (suspicion of evil) returns from v.6, but here directed at the believers' fate.

### v. 13: ومن لم يؤمن بالله ورسوله فإنا أعتدنا للكافرين سعيرا

A general theological statement on disbelief, applied to the Bedouin who failed.

### v. 14: ولله ملك السماوات والأرض

A short structural verse asserting divine sovereignty + forgiveness/punishment dichotomy.

### v. 15: سيقول المخلفون إذا انطلقتم إلى مغانم لتأخذوها ذرونا نتبعكم

*"The stayed-behind ones will say when you set out for the booty (you may take): 'Allow us to follow you.'..."*

A second *sa-yaqūlu* future-statement: when **booty** (ġanāʾim) is at stake, the Bedouin will reverse their position and want to join the campaign. This is the **booty-corruption theme** — the same Bedouin who refused the costly pilgrimage will eagerly join the profitable expedition. *Yurīdūna an yubaddilū kalām allāh* (they want to substitute the speech of Allāh) — they want to override the divine order.

### v. 16: قل للمخلفين من الأعراب ستدعون إلى قوم أولي بأس شديد

*"Say to those of the Bedouins who stayed behind: 'You will be called to a people of strong might, against whom you will fight or who will submit (yusliman)...'"*

A second-chance promise: future call to fight a "strong people". Classical identification varies (Banū Ḥanīfa under al-Yamāma, the Persians, the Romans, the Tabūk expedition — al-Ṭabarī catalogs 4 readings). The verse structurally counterbalances the Bedouins' previous refusal with a future-redemption opportunity.

### v. 17: ليس على الأعمى حرج ولا على الأعرج حرج ولا على المريض حرج

*"There is no blame on the blind, nor on the lame, nor on the sick..."*

The **disability-exemption verse** for war-participation. Corpus-rare verse-structure: 3 disjunctive *lā ḥaraj* clauses listing exempt categories. This verse is canonically cross-referenced to Q 9:91 (the parallel Medinan-warfare disability-exemption).

## Block C — Bayʿat al-Riḍwān + Conquest Prophecy (vv. 18-29)

The 12-verse final block contains the surah's most often-cited verses: v.18 (Bayʿat al-Riḍwān), v.27 (the dream-fulfillment / forward-prophecy), v.29 (the Torah/Gospel description of the Prophet and his community).

### v. 18: لقد رضي الله عن المؤمنين إذ يبايعونك تحت الشجرة

*"Indeed, Allāh was pleased with the believers when they pledged allegiance to you under the Tree..."*

**The Bayʿat al-Riḍwān verse**. Two anchors:
- *raḍiya allāh* — divine-pleasure declaration (the basis for the name *al-Riḍwān* "the Pleasure")
- *taḥta al-shajara* — under the Tree (a specific physical-geographic anchor at Hudaybiyya)

The verse continues: *fa-ʿalima mā fī qulūbihim fa-anzala al-sakīnata ʿalayhim wa-athābahum fatḥan qarībā* — "He knew what was in their hearts, and sent down tranquility upon them, and rewarded them with a near victory." This second *fatḥ* token (the third in the surah) refers, per the dominant classical reading, to **the Conquest of Khaybar** (7 AH) — the immediate-future "near victory" granted as reward for the pledge. Some readings identify it with Mecca proper (8 AH).

The hadith corpus on this verse is the densest in Q 48 — see `04-hadith-corpus.md` for the al-Bukhārī Maghāzī #194-218 cluster.

### vv. 19-21: Booty + future conquests promise

- v.19: *wa-maghānim kathīratan yaʾkhudhūnahā* — booty promise
- v.20: *waʿadakum allāhu maghānima kathīratan taʾkhudhūnahā fa-ʿajjala lakum hādhihi* — Allāh's promise; the immediate booty (Khaybar)
- v.21: *wa-ukhrā lam taqdirū ʿalayhā qad aḥāṭa allāhu bihā* — "and another (victory) which you have not been able to (achieve), Allāh has encompassed it" — typically read as a forward-allusion to the Conquest of Mecca

### v. 22: ولو قاتلكم الذين كفروا

A counter-factual hypothetical: had the Quraysh fought, they would have been defeated. This verse anchors the *iʿjāz al-ghayb* claim that the surah forecasts what would have happened in a counter-factual scenario.

### v. 23: سنة الله التي قد خلت من قبل

*sunnatu allāhi al-latī qad khalat min qabl* — "the way of Allāh that has passed before". The classical *sunnat allāh* (divine custom/law) formula — referenced 8× in the Quran.

### v. 24: وهو الذي كف أيديهم عنكم وأيديكم عنهم ببطن مكة

*"It is He who restrained their hands from you, and your hands from them, in the heart of Mecca..."*

A specific event-reference: the moment at Hudaybiyya when both sides pulled back from open warfare. *Ba-ṭn Makka* ("the heart of Mecca") is a specific geographic phrase — read variously as the Hudaybiyya valley itself (~30 km from Mecca, under Meccan sphere) or as a reference to a near-Mecca incident.

### v. 25: هم الذين كفروا وصدوكم عن المسجد الحرام

*"They are those who disbelieved and obstructed you from al-Masjid al-Ḥarām..."*

The Quraysh as obstructors (*ṣaddūkum*) of the Sacred Mosque pilgrimage. The verse continues with a remarkable concession-clause about the **mixed Mecca population**: there were *rijālun muʾminūna wa-nisāʾun muʾmināt lam taʿlamūhum an taṭaʾūhum fa-tuṣībakum minhum maʿarratun bi-ghayri ʿilm* — "men of believers and women of believers whom you did not know, that you might trample them, and incur an unintentional offense from them" — i.e., there were unidentified secret-believers in Mecca whose deaths in a Muslim assault would cause unintentional moral injury. This is read by classical mufassirūn as a divine wisdom-explanation for why the Hudaybiyya peace was preferable to immediate war.

### v. 26: إذ جعل الذين كفروا في قلوبهم الحمية حمية الجاهلية

*"When the disbelievers had put in their hearts the *ḥamiyya* (proud zeal), the *ḥamiyyat al-jāhiliyya*..."*

The Meccan refusal to write *bismi-llāh al-raḥmān al-raḥīm* and *Muḥammad rasūl allāh* on the treaty (Muslim Jihad #111-113 verified on disk) is read as the *ḥamiyyat al-jāhiliyya* (pre-Islamic tribal pride) referenced here. The contrast: *fa-anzala allāh sakīnatahu ʿalā rasūlihi wa-ʿalā al-muʾminīn wa-alzamahum kalimat al-taqwā* — divine tranquility was sent down to the believers; the *kalimat al-taqwā* (word of god-consciousness) was attached to them. This is the **third sakīna-mention** in Q 48 (vv. 4, 18, 26) — the surah is the **densest sakīna-cluster in the corpus**.

### v. 27: لقد صدق الله رسوله الرؤيا بالحق

*"Indeed, Allāh has fulfilled His Messenger's vision in truth: you will surely enter the Sacred Mosque, if Allāh wills, in safety, with your heads shaved or your hair shortened, fearing none. He knew what you did not know, and granted you, besides this, a near victory."*

**THE FORWARD-PROPHECY VERSE**. The Prophet had a dream-vision (*ruʾyā*) before Hudaybiyya in which he saw the Muslims entering Mecca to perform ʿumra. When Hudaybiyya did NOT include immediate ʿumra, some Companions doubted (al-Bukhārī Maghāzī various). The verse confirms:
- The vision will be fulfilled (forward-prophecy: future entry into Masjid al-Ḥarām)
- Specific ritual details: *muḥalliqīn ruʾūsakum wa-muqaṣṣirīn* (heads shaved or hair shortened — the ʿumra/ḥajj completion ritual)
- The completion-state: *lā takhāfūn* (fearing none — i.e., the entry will be peaceful)

This verse was historically fulfilled at the *ʿumrat al-qaḍāʾ* of 7 AH (the make-up ʿumra Hudaybiyya allowed) and at the *fatḥ Makka* of 8 AH (the Conquest of Mecca proper). See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` §3 for the audit of this claim.

The *fatḥan qarīban* (near victory) at the verse's end is the **third and final f-t-ḥ token in Q 48** — the surah's signature root-closure-marker.

### v. 28: هو الذي أرسل رسوله بالهدى ودين الحق

*"It is He who sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth, that He may make it prevail over all religion (li-yuẓhirahu ʿalā al-dīni kullihi)..."*

The **religion-prevalence verse**. Parallel to Q 9:33 and Q 61:9 (the Quran's three *li-yuẓhirahu ʿalā al-dīni kullih* declarations — a corpus-distinctive triple). Q 48:28 forms one corner of this triple. The other two appear in Q 9 (Tawba) and Q 61 (al-Ṣaff, musabbiḥāt — Q 48's structural FR-neighbor at distance 0.79).

### v. 29: محمد رسول الله

*"Muhammad is the Messenger of Allāh, and those with him are forceful against the disbelievers, merciful between themselves; you see them kneeling and prostrating, seeking grace from Allāh and good pleasure; their mark on their faces from the trace of prostration..."*

**The closing description verse**. v.29 contains:
- The explicit *Muḥammad rasūl allāh* declaration — only 4 explicit Muhammad-namings in the corpus (Q 3:144, Q 33:40, Q 47:2, Q 48:29)
- The believer-traits inventory: *ashiddāʾ ʿalā al-kuffār, ruḥamāʾ baynahum, rukkaʿan sujjadan*
- *yabtaghūna faḍlan min allāhi wa-riḍwānā* — the divine-pleasure return (echoing v.18's *raḍiya allāh*)
- The Torah/Gospel description: *dhālika mathaluhum fī al-tawrāt wa-mathaluhum fī al-injīl* — pre-Quranic prophetic prefiguration
- The closing botanical metaphor: *ka-zarʿin akhraja shaṭʾahu fa-azarahu fa-staghlaẓa fa-stawā ʿalā sūqih* — "like a seed that puts forth its shoot, then strengthens it, then it becomes thick, and stands upright on its stem" — a ground-up growth-metaphor for the Muslim community's emergence from a small core.

The corpus-hapax word in this verse is *shaṭʾ* (shoot/sprout) — appearing only in Q 48:29. The agricultural metaphor is morphologically distinctive in Quranic vocabulary.

The verse closes with *waʿada allāhu al-ladhīna āmanū wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāti minhum maghfiratan wa-ajran ʿaẓīman* — the believers' final reward formula, and the surah's closing.

## Cross-block thematic-statistical summary

The 3-block structure is empirically supported by:
- **Vocabulary distinctiveness**: each block has block-specific roots not in other blocks (Block A: *fataḥnā, anzala, sakīna, ʿalīman ḥakīman*; Block B: *al-mukhallifūn, al-aʿrāb, sa-yaqūlū, ġanāʾim*; Block C: *raḍiya, taḥta al-shajara, kalimat al-taqwā, muḥalliqīn ruʾūsakum, ka-zarʿin*)
- **Sakīna-cluster anchoring**: vv. 4, 18, 26 — three sakīna mentions, ONE per block (one in A, one anchoring B-to-C transition at v.18, one closing C at v.26).
- **fatḥ-anchor positions**: vv. 1, 18, 27 — opening of block A, opening of block C, near-closing of block C. The 4 root-tokens span the three thematic blocks asymmetrically (2 in v.1, 1 each in v.18 and v.27).
- **Bayʿa-frame**: Block A's v.10 (*yubāyiʿūnaka* general-formula) anchors the Bayʿah-as-divine-pledge theological framework; Block C's v.18 (*yubāyiʿūnaka taḥta al-shajara* historical-event) instantiates it. The two verses jointly form a frame around Block B.

This is consistent with the classical al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar* analysis of Q 48 as a tightly-integrated 3-act narrative: divine declaration (A) → critique of dissenters (B) → ratification of loyalists (C).
