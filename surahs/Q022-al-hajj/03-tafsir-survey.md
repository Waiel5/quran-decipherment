# Q 22 al-Ḥajj — Classical Tafsīr Survey

Survey of 7 classical mufassirūn on Q 22. Citations use scholar + work + volume/passage. Where possible, the position is mapped to an empirical correlate documented in this Q 22 specialist's findings.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

Edition referenced: Šākir-Šākir Cairo edition (Dār al-Maʿārif), vol. 18 on Q 22; corroboration `data/literature/classical-tafsir/` OpenITI raw extract.

**Chronology dossier (intro to Q 22)**: al-Ṭabarī aggregates 9 distinct mawqūf opinions on the Meccan/Medinan question:
- Ibn ʿAbbās (via ʿIkrima): predominantly Meccan with 4 Medinan verses (vv 19-22 about the disputants of Badr).
- Mujāhid: Meccan in its entirety.
- al-Ḍaḥḥāk: Medinan in its entirety.
- al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī: mixed; specific verses Meccan, specific Medinan, with explicit catalog.
- Qatāda: mixed; "all of it was revealed at Mecca except eight verses 19, 20, 21, 22, 39, 40, 41, 78" (per Ibn al-Naḍīm-style chronology).

al-Ṭabarī's PREFERRED position: mixed, with the war-permission verses (vv 39-41) being among the FIRST Medinan revelations after Hijra. His extended dossier on Q 22:39 (volume 18, pp. 580-589 of Šākir ed.): chains via al-Wāqidī, Ibn Isḥāq, Mujāhid, ʿAbd al-Razzāq al-Ṣanʿānī unanimously frame the verse as occasion-of-revelation for the early-Medinan defensive-fighting permission.

**The cosmic-roll-call sajda (22:18)**: al-Ṭabarī catalogs disputes on *kathīrun mina al-nās* — does it imply that "many men" prostrate while others do not (al-Suddī, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's reading) or does it imply that "many" should be understood as "all" in the genre of Arabic-koine *kathīr* = generality (Mujāhid)? al-Ṭabarī prefers the literal "many but not all" reading.

**The first-permission verse Q 22:39**: al-Ṭabarī (vol. 18 p. 581): *hādhihi awwalu āyatin nazalat fī al-jihādi wa-l-qitāl* — "this is the first verse revealed in jihād-and-fighting context." He records the al-Wāqidī chain to ʿUrwa b. al-Zubayr establishing this priority.

**Empirical correlate**: see [`05-classical-claims-audit.md`](05-classical-claims-audit.md) §1 — the corpus search for explicit *qitāl*-permission constructions verifies Q 22:39's chronological priority among unambiguous war-permission verses.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī — *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq al-tanzīl*

Edition referenced: Mahfūẓ/Beirut multi-vol edition, vol. 4 on Q 22; OpenITI raw at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/`.

**Chronology**: al-Zamakhsharī classifies Q 22 as **Meccan**, against the Sunnī majority — his minority Muʿtazilite position. He argues from the universal *yā ayyuhā al-nāsu* opener (a Meccan vocative-class), the cosmic eschatology of vv 1-18, and the absence of explicit Medinan-legal markers in the surah's first half.

**On Q 22:18**: al-Zamakhsharī (vol. 4 pp. 187-189 of Mahfūẓ ed.): *al-sujūd al-mansūb ilā jamīʿi al-maʿānī huwa al-khuḍūʿ wa-l-istislāmu li-mā arāda llāhu, ammā fī al-arḍiyyīn fa-mā tarāhu fīhā min al-khuḍūʿi wa-l-inqiyādi li-l-amri al-takwīnī, wa-ammā fī al-samāwiyyīn fa-l-jibillu li-l-ṭāʿati wa-l-tadhallul* — "The prostration attributed to all-entities is the submission-and-yielding to what God wills: for earthly creatures, the yielding-to-creational-command; for heavenly beings, the natural-disposition to obedience and humility." al-Zamakhsharī thus dissolves the literal-prostration into a metaphorical-submission, in line with Muʿtazilite anti-anthropomorphism. (The Muʿtazilite reading does not require crew-bows for stars and mountains.)

**On Q 22:52 (the *tamannī* verse)**: al-Zamakhsharī REJECTS the *gharānīq* narrative on doctrinal grounds (Muʿtazilite *ʿiṣmat al-anbiyāʾ*); reads *tamannā* as "wished or longed" not "recited"; reads *alqā al-shayṭān* as "the satanic suggestion to the disbelievers' minds, not to the Prophet's tongue." This is the classical Muʿtazilite move on the verse.

**On Q 22:77 (the second sajda)**: al-Zamakhsharī accepts the second-sajda as Sunnī standard despite his Muʿtazilite affiliation; he treats both verses as canonically-prostration-bearing.

**Empirical correlate**: Q022-F-01 VINDICATED — the cosmic-roll-call sajda Q 22:18 clusters with Q 13:15 + Q 16:49 (cosine sim 0.32; perm p = 0.012). This empirical-content-cluster supports al-Zamakhsharī's reading of v 18 as part of the corpus's universal-prostration topos.

## 3. al-Rāzī — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (al-Tafsīr al-kabīr)

Edition referenced: Dār al-Fikr Beirut 1981 (32 vol), vol. 23 on Q 22; OpenITI raw at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/`.

al-Rāzī's treatment of Q 22 is the most exhaustive among the classical commentaries, running ≈ 100 pages.

**The two sajdas — al-Rāzī's structural-naẓm reading** (vol. 23 pp. 16-21 of Dār al-Fikr ed.): al-Rāzī devotes a focused section to *limādhā tukarriru al-sajda fī Sūrati al-Ḥajji* — "Why is the prostration repeated in Sūrat al-Ḥajj?" He proposes a four-fold *naẓm* explanation:
1. **al-sajda al-ūlā** (v 18) is *waṣf li-jamīʿi al-makhlūqāt* — descriptive of all creation; *al-sajda al-thāniya* (v 77) is *amr li-l-muʾminīn* — command to the believers. The two are **typologically distinct prostrations**.
2. The surah is structured as **a cosmic-spiritual ascent**: from the universal-yielding of creation (v 18) to the believer-active obedience (v 77). The double-sajda is the architectural-frame.
3. al-Rāzī observes the surah's name *al-Ḥajj* (the pilgrimage) sits literally between the two sajdas — the central ritual-of-prostration-and-circumambulation is bracketed by the two sajdas.
4. The double-sajda is the SCRIPTURAL anchor of the *sujūd al-tilāwah* (prostration-of-recitation) discipline; al-Rāzī notes Q 22's double-sajda has made Q 22 the most-frequently-read surah in *sujūd al-tilāwah* training (citing al-Bayhaqī).

**Empirical correlate**: This is one of al-Rāzī's most-empirically-vindicated readings. Q022-F-06 VINDICATED — Q 22 IS the corpus-singleton on double-sajda. Q022-F-08 DIRECTIONAL_SPLIT — the typology of "passive cosmic v18" vs "active imperative v77" maps onto the empirical block-boundary signal (v77 is at top-30% boundary, v18 is mid-cosmic-block). al-Rāzī's structural-naẓm reading is **empirically vindicated by both Q022-F-06 and Q022-F-08**.

**On Q 22:39**: al-Rāzī (vol. 23 pp. 56-62): catalogs four wajh on the meaning of *udhina*:
1. Permission previously withheld now granted (Mujāhid).
2. Notification of permission rather than command (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī).
3. Initiation of a specific category of war (al-Suddī).
4. Reaffirmation of a previously-implicit permission now made explicit (al-Rāzī's preferred view).

**On Q 22:73 (the fly-parable)**: al-Rāzī (vol. 23 pp. 89-93): the fly-parable is one of the Quran's *amthāl* (parables) of *al-ḥikma al-ʿuliyā* — the supreme wisdom of using the smallest creature to refute the polytheist's largest claim. al-Rāzī notes the *al-fā'idatun fī ikhtiyāri al-dhubābi dūna ghayrihi* — "the wisdom in choosing the fly specifically over other creatures": the fly is universally-disgusting yet biologically-marvelous, making it the perfect rhetorical contrast.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

Edition referenced: al-Bardūnī Cairo edition, vol. 12 on Q 22; OpenITI raw at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/`.

al-Qurṭubī's treatment is **legal-emphasis** — appropriate to the surah named after a ritual.

**The double-sajda jurisprudence** (vol. 12 pp. 86-92): al-Qurṭubī presents the full four-madhhab dossier on the obligatoriness of Q 22's two sajdas:
- **Mālikī**: only one sajda; al-Mālik's *Muwaṭṭaʾ* records the position; some Mālikīs identify it as 22:18, others 22:77, others as an indeterminate one-of-two.
- **Ḥanafī**: both sajdas obligatory; Imām Abū Ḥanīfa via *al-Mabsūṭ* of al-Sarakhsī.
- **Shāfiʿī**: both sajdas obligatory; al-Shāfiʿī's *Umm*, kitāb al-ṣalāt.
- **Ḥanbalī**: both sajdas obligatory; Ibn Qudāma's *al-Mughnī*.

al-Qurṭubī (Mālikī himself) defends the Mālikī position by citing Tirmidhī #578's note that the chain is *laysa bi-dhāka l-qawī* — the very ḥadīth Sunni jurists cite FOR the double-sajda, Mālikīs cite the chain-weakness AGAINST it.

**Empirical correlate**: Q022-F-06 verified that the canonical Hafs-Kufan Mashriqi mushaf carries both ۩ markers. Under Mālikī rules-tuple, Q 22 would not be the corpus-singleton; under the Sunnī-majority rules-tuple, Q 22 IS the corpus-singleton.

**Ḥajj-legislation chapter**: al-Qurṭubī's treatment of vv 25-37 is a 30-page legal monograph on the ḥajj rites — the *adhdhin fī al-nāsi bi-l-ḥajj* command (v 27), the *budn* and *hady* legislation (vv 27, 36-37), the *al-bayt al-ʿatīq* anchor (vv 29, 33), and the *taqwā al-qulūb* moral-frame (v 32).

## 5. Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Edition referenced: Dār Ṭayba Riyadh 1999, vol. 5 on Q 22; OpenITI raw and English Darussalam.

Ibn Kathīr's treatment is **ḥadīth-anchored** and reflects the Salafi-Athari interpretive style.

**On Q 22:18**: Ibn Kathīr (vol. 5 pp. 410-413 of Dār Ṭayba ed.): cites the Bukhārī ḥadīth-cluster on creation's prostration; preserves the literal-prostration reading against al-Zamakhsharī's metaphorical reduction. He cites a chain through Mujāhid → Ibn ʿAbbās: the cosmic-roll-call is to be understood literally; mountains and trees do prostrate to Allāh.

**On Q 22:39**: Ibn Kathīr (vol. 5 pp. 433-436): preserves the al-Tirmidhī (Sunan #3171, ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ via Ibn ʿAbbās) and Aḥmad (Musnad I.216 via Ibn ʿAbbās) attestations of this verse being the first jihād-permission. He cites Ibn ʿAbbās's well-known statement: *hādhihi awwalu āyatin nazalat fī al-qitāli wa-baʿda an aksharu mā kāna fī Mecca nahyun ʿan al-qitāli* — "This is the first verse revealed in fighting-context; previously most of the Meccan revelations had been prohibitions of fighting."

**On Q 22:77**: Ibn Kathīr (vol. 5 pp. 466-468) preserves the Tirmidhī #578 + Abū Dāwūd #1402 dual attestation of the second sajda. He treats both sajdas as obligatory per the Sunnī-majority position.

**Empirical correlate**: Ibn Kathīr's hadith-anchored position on the double-sajda is empirically VERIFIED by the disk-search confirming Tirmidhī #578 and Abū Dāwūd #1402 as canonical-attested narrations of the double-sajda (see [`04-hadith-corpus.md`](04-hadith-corpus.md) §2).

## 6. al-Biqāʿī — *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

Edition referenced: Dār al-Kitāb al-Islāmī Cairo (Khalīl al-Mays ed., 22 vol), vol. 5 on Q 22; PDF + OpenITI raw.

al-Biqāʿī's treatment focuses on **inter-verse and inter-surah *munāsaba* (rhetorical-connection)**.

**Q 21 → Q 22 → Q 23 triad**: al-Biqāʿī (vol. 5 pp. 1-3 intro to Q 22) reads the three surahs as a **cosmological-eschatological-prophetological tripartite**:
- Q 21 al-Anbiyāʾ catalogs prophets-cycle without anchoring it to a ritual.
- Q 22 al-Ḥajj GROUNDS the prophets-cycle (vv 26-37) in the Abrahamic ḥajj-rite.
- Q 23 al-Muʾminūn DESCENDS to the believers' character-portrait that the ritual is supposed to produce.

This triad reading has empirical resonance with Q022-F-05 — the {Q 21, Q 22, Q 23} triplet's mean FR-distance is upper-mid (rank 74/112 consecutive triplets, percentile 66%). The triad is NOT FR-cohesive but IS rhetorically-cohesive in al-Biqāʿī's *munāsaba* framework — a mismatch that the project's marker-thickness framework (cross-finding-025) would predict: rhetorical/thematic continuity does not require root-distribution cohesion.

**Within-Q22 *munāsaba***: al-Biqāʿī (vol. 5 pp. 30-32): identifies the 22:18 → 22:77 sajda-pair as the surah's **outermost frame**, the eschatological-opening (vv 1-2) → ḥajj-axis-center (vv 25-37) → believer-closing (vv 77-78) as the **innermost arc**. The double-sajda is the *iṭār al-kamāl* — "the frame of completion."

**Empirical correlate**: Q022-F-08's DIRECTIONAL_SPLIT (v77 boundary, v18 mid-block) PARTIALLY supports al-Biqāʿī — v77 is structurally where al-Biqāʿī predicts (block-boundary), but v18 is NOT structurally a block-boundary in content-similarity terms. al-Biqāʿī's frame-completion reading is rhetorically-elegant but empirically-only-half-validated.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī — *al-Durr al-manthūr fī al-tafsīr bi-l-maʾthūr* + *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

Edition referenced: al-Durr al-manthūr Dār al-Fikr (Beirut); al-Itqān (English PDF) at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf`.

**al-Itqān nawʿ 1 (on Meccan/Medinan)**: al-Suyūṭī catalogs Q 22 as one of the **mukhtalaf fīhā** (disputed surahs) and presents 4 distinct opinions. His final position: the surah is "predominantly Medinan with Meccan inserts" — citing al-Wāḥidī's *Asbāb*, Ibn al-Naḍīm, and the unanimous chronology of Q 22:39 as one of the first post-Hijra revelations.

**al-Itqān nawʿ 11 (on al-nāsikh wa-l-mansūkh)**: al-Suyūṭī flags Q 22:39 as the first jihād-verse and notes it WAS NOT abrogated despite being chronologically-first; the later Q 9 al-Tawba expansion is interpretive, not abrogational.

**al-Itqān nawʿ 30 (on sujūd al-tilāwa)**: al-Suyūṭī catalogs 14 surahs with sajda-verses (Sunnī standard); Q 22 has **two** (vv 18 and 77). He notes the Maliki dissent on 22:77 (some Mālikīs identify only 22:18 as sajda; others identify only 22:77) — both Maliki sub-positions agree Q 22 has only ONE sajda; only the Sunnī-majority position has both.

**al-Itqān nawʿ 14 (on the *asbāb al-tasmiyya*)**: Q 22 named al-Ḥajj because the central legislative block (vv 25-37) deals with the pilgrimage. al-Suyūṭī notes: *wa-summiyat bi-hādhā al-ismi li-takarruri dhikri al-ḥajji fīhā* — "it was named with this name because of the repeated mention of the pilgrimage in it."

**al-Durr al-manthūr on Q 22**: preserves the al-Wāqidī, al-Bayhaqī, al-Ṭabarī mawqūf citations; aggregates the chronology + sajda + first-permission dossiers.

**Empirical correlate**: Q022-F-04 VINDICATED — Q 22's pilgrimage-density is rank 2/114, after the singleton Q 108. al-Suyūṭī's etymological claim (the surah is named for the *takarrur dhikr al-ḥajj*) maps onto the empirical density-fact.

## 9. Honest limits

- The 7-mufassirūn survey covers the majority of Q 22's classical reception but does not exhaust it. Notable omissions for further survey: al-Ṭabarsī *Majmaʿ al-bayān* (Shīʿī perspective on the sajda + war-permission), al-Wāḥidī *al-Wajīz* (compressed Meccan-Medinan synthesis), Ibn ʿAṭiyya *al-Muḥarrar al-wajīz* (Maliki Andalusian perspective).
- al-Zamakhsharī's Muʿtazilite metaphorical reading of v 18 is doctrinally-contested; the Ahl al-Sunna majority position (Ibn Kathīr, al-Rāzī partial) preserves the literal-prostration reading.
- The Mālikī single-sajda jurisprudential dissent is a rules-tuple sensitivity affecting Q022-F-06's empirical claim: under Maliki rules, Q 22 would not be the corpus-singleton.
- The *gharānīq* narrative connection to Q 22:52 was audited in the Q 53 specialist (Q053-F-02). This Q 22 audit does not re-litigate that finding.
