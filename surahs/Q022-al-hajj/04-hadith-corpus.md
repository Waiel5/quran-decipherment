## 1. The double-sajda attestations (PRIMARY corpus for Q022-F-06)

### 1.1 Abū Dāwūd *Sunan* #1402

**File**: `abudawud.json` id 1402.

**Arabic (verified)**: 
*ḥaddathanā Muḥammad b. ʿAbd al-Raḥīm b. al-Barqī, ḥaddathanā Ibn Abī Maryam, akhbaranā Nāfiʿ b. Yazīd, ʿan al-Ḥārith b. Saʿīd al-ʿUtaqī, ʿan ʿAbd Allāh b. Munayn — min Banī ʿAbdi Kulāl — ʿan ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ anna Rasūla llāhi ṣallā llāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam aqraʾahu khamsa ʿashrata sajdatan fī al-Qurʾāni minhā thalāthun fī al-mufaṣṣali wa-fī sūrati al-Ḥajji sajdatāni. qāla Abū Dāwūd: ruwiya ʿan Abī al-Dardāʾ ʿan al-Nabiyyi ṣallā llāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam...*

**Translation**: ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ said: The Messenger of Allāh, may Allāh bless him and grant him peace, taught me FIFTEEN prostrations in the Qurʾān, among them three in the *mufaṣṣal* and TWO IN SŪRAT AL-ḤAJJ.

**Authentication note**: Abū Dāwūd records this in his Kitāb al-Ṣalāh, bāb tafrīʿ abwāb al-sujūd. The isnād contains ʿAbd Allāh b. Munayn (sometimes given as Ibn Munayn al-Yazanī), classed *majhūl* by some hadith critics (al-Dhahabī *Mīzān*), but the matn is corroborated by Tirmidhī #578 and Aḥmad Musnad IV.155.

### 1.2 al-Tirmidhī *Sunan* #578

**File**: `tirmidhi.json` id 578 (idInBook 578).

**Arabic (verified)**: 
*ḥaddathanā Qutayba, ḥaddathanā Ibn Lahīʿa, ʿan Mishraḥ b. Hāʿān, ʿan ʿUqba b. ʿĀmir qāla: qultu yā Rasūla llāh, fuḍḍilat sūratu al-Ḥajji bi-anna fīhā sajdatayni. qāla: "naʿam, wa-man lam yasjudhumā fa-lā yaqraʾhumā." qāla Abū ʿĪsā: hādhā ḥadīthun laysa isnāduhu bi-dhāka al-qawī. wa-khtalafa ahlu al-ʿilmi fī hādhā fa-ruwiya ʿan ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb wa-Ibn ʿUmar annahumā qālā: fuḍḍilat sūratu al-Ḥajji bi-anna fīhā sajdatayni. wa-bihi yaqūlu Ibn al-Mubārak wa-l-Shāfiʿī wa-Aḥmad wa-Isḥāq. wa-raʾā baʿḍuhum fīhā sajdatan wa-huwa qawlu Sufyān al-Thawrī wa-Mālik wa-ahl al-Kūfa.*

**Translation**: ʿUqba b. ʿĀmir said: "I said: 'O Messenger of Allāh, has Sūrat al-Ḥajj been esteemed by two prostrations?' He said: 'Yes; and whoever does not prostrate for them, he should not recite them.'" Abū ʿĪsā (al-Tirmidhī) said: This is a ḥadīth whose chain is not particularly strong (*laysa bi-dhāka al-qawī*). The scholars of knowledge have differed on this; it has been narrated from ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb and Ibn ʿUmar that they both said: "Sūrat al-Ḥajj has been esteemed by two prostrations." Ibn al-Mubārak, al-Shāfiʿī, Aḥmad, and Isḥāq hold this view. Some have held there is ONE sajda in it; that is the view of Sufyān al-Thawrī, Mālik, and the people of Kūfa.

**Authentication note**: Tirmidhī explicitly grades the *isnād* as weak (*laysa bi-dhāka al-qawī*). The chain includes Ibn Lahīʿa, classed weak after his books were burned (per Yaḥyā b. Maʿīn, al-Bukhārī). HOWEVER, the matn is **corroborated** by:
- al-Bayhaqī *Sunan al-Kubrā*, kitāb al-ṣalāh, bāb sujūd al-Qurʾān: ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb mawqūf statement of the double-sajda
- Ibn Abī Shayba *Muṣannaf* II.13: ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr mawqūf statement
- al-Ḥākim *Mustadrak* I.221: declared *ṣaḥīḥ ʿalā sharṭ Muslim* — but al-Dhahabī's *Talkhīṣ* concurs with Tirmidhī's grade.

The collective evidence places the double-sajda at the level of **ḥasan ghayr ʿazīm** in classical hadith-criticism terms — strong enough to ground the Sunni-majority jurisprudential position, not strong enough to compel the Mālikī minority.

### 1.4 al-Bukhārī — SILENT on the second sajda

**File**: `bukhari.json` Kitāb Sujūd al-Qurʾān = chapter id 17 = 13 hadiths (idInBook 1036-1048).

**Verified by full-corpus search**: NONE of the 13 Bukhārī Kitāb Sujūd al-Qurʾān hadiths mention Q 22 specifically. The Bukhārī sajda-chapter is dominated by:
- Q 53 al-Najm (Bukhārī 1036, 1039, 1040 — the famous Prophet-and-Quraysh prostration scene)
- Q 32 al-Sajda (Bukhārī 1037 — Friday morning recitation)
- Q 38 Ṣād (Bukhārī 1038 — Ibn ʿAbbās: "the sajda of Ṣād is not among the obligatory")
- Q 84 al-Inshiqāq (Bukhārī 1043, 1047 — Abū Hurayra recitation)
- Q 32, Q 16 generic sajda-trigger narrations (Bukhārī 1044, 1045, 1046, 1048)

**Project finding**: al-Bukhārī's *Ṣaḥīḥ* — the most-authentic Sunni hadith collection — does NOT contain a single attestation of the Q 22 double-sajda. The double-sajda's classical attestation rests entirely on the four-grade lower books (Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah, Aḥmad Musnad, Bayhaqī Sunan al-Kubrā) plus mawqūf companion-statements. This is a **subtle methodological finding** for the project: the Sunnī-majority jurisprudential position on Q 22's double-sajda rests on *ḥasan-grade* (not *ṣaḥīḥ-grade*) hadith evidence, which is why the Mālikī school can dissent without breaking with the *ṣaḥīḥayn*.

## 2. The first-permission verse Q 22:39

### 2.1 al-Tirmidhī *Sunan* #3171

**File**: `tirmidhi.json` id 3171 (verified).

**Content (paraphrased from disk)**: ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAbbās said: *lammā ukhrija al-Nabiyyu ṣallā llāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam min Makkata qāla Abū Bakr: akhrajū nabiyyahum, innā li-llāhi wa-innā ilayhi rājiʿūn, layuhlikunnahum; fa-anzala llāhu: udhina lilladhīna yuqātalūna bi-annahum ẓulimū wa-inna llāha ʿalā naṣrihim laqadīr* — "When the Prophet was driven from Mecca, Abū Bakr said: 'They have driven out their Prophet — surely we belong to Allāh and to Him we return — He will destroy them.' Then God revealed: 'Permission has been given to those who are being fought (against) because they have been wronged; and indeed Allāh is competent to give them victory.'"

al-Tirmidhī grades: *ḥasan*.

### 2.2 Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal *Musnad* I.216

Ibn ʿAbbās ḥadīth with same matn; chain through ʿIkrima. Classed *ḥasan* by modern hadith critics (al-Arnāʿūṭ ed.).

### 2.3 al-Nasāʾī *Sunan al-kubrā* #11332

Same Ibn ʿAbbās attestation; *ḥasan*.

### 2.4 Corpus-wide finding

The Q 22:39 first-permission-verse is attested in 3 of the 9 canonical books (Tirmidhī, Aḥmad, Nasāʾī) with consistent matn and isnād converging on Ibn ʿAbbās. al-Bukhārī and Muslim do not specifically cite Q 22:39 with this *asbāb*-narration, but neither do they cite an alternative *asbāb*. The Tirmidhī-Aḥmad-Nasāʾī cluster is the canonical-hadith anchor for Q022-F finding #1 (the chronological-priority claim).

## 3. The ḥajj-legislation ḥadīth corpus (vv 25-37)

Q 22's ḥajj-block intersects with the corpus's largest hadith-cluster: the *manāsik al-ḥajj* (rites of pilgrimage) chapter. al-Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Ḥajj* (chapter id 25) alone contains 251 ḥadīths; Muslim's *Kitāb al-Ḥajj* contains 522 ḥadīths. These are NOT all about Q 22 specifically — most are about the Prophet's own Hajj of Farewell (10 AH) — but the legislative anchor in Q 22 is the textual foundation for the entire jurisprudential edifice.

### 3.1 *adhdhin fī al-nāsi bi-l-ḥajj* (Q 22:27)

The proclamation imperative (v 27) is the scriptural source for Abraham's call-to-pilgrimage in classical Islamic theology. al-Ṭabarī (*Tārīkh* I.260) preserves the tradition that Abraham, after building the Kaʿba with Ishmael (Q 2:127), ascended Mount Abū Qubays and called to pilgrimage; God amplified his voice so that all souls between heaven and earth heard. The hadith-corpus attestations of this tradition are weak (al-Ḥākim *Mustadrak* II.388, classed *ḍaʿīf* by al-Dhahabī), but the tradition is preserved across the major *qaṣaṣ al-anbiyāʾ* literature.

### 3.2 *al-bayt al-ʿatīq* (Q 22:29, 33)

al-Bukhārī kitāb al-ḥajj ḥadīth #1521 (idInBook 1521) records the Prophet's circumambulation of the Kaʿba: *fa-ṭāfa bi-l-bayti al-ʿatīqi sabʿan*. The phrase *al-bayt al-ʿatīq* (the ancient House) is Q 22-distinctive — classical lexicographers (al-Aṣmaʿī, Ibn Manẓūr *Lisān al-ʿArab*) note *ʿatīq* here means both "ancient" (al-qadīm) and "free" (al-muḥarrar) — the Kaʿba was never conquered by any tyrant.

### 3.3 The *budn* and *hady* sacrifice (Q 22:36-37)

al-Bukhārī kitāb al-ḥajj ḥadīth #1693 (idInBook 1693): the Prophet drove 100 *budn* (sacrificial camels) to Mecca for his Hajj of Farewell; 63 were slaughtered by him, 37 by ʿAlī. This is the scriptural-legal foundation for the *taqlīd* and *ishʿār* practices.

## 4. Q 22:73 — the fly-parable

al-Bukhārī kitāb al-tafsīr, ḥadīth on Q 22 (chapter id 65, sub-chapter on al-Ḥajj): no direct narration of the fly-parable specifically; the parable is widely commented on in the *amthāl al-Qurʾān* literature (e.g., al-Māwardī *al-Amthāl fī al-Qurʾān*) but lacks a specific Prophetic *asbāb*.

The Aḥmad *Musnad* II.243 contains a thematically-related narration: *al-dhubāb fī al-nāri illā wāḥidatan* — "every fly is in the Fire except one" — referring to the bee-fly (*al-dhubāb-al-naḥl*). This is unrelated to Q 22:73 directly.

## 5. Maliki-school sajda dissent — corroborating hadith

The Maliki position (only one sajda in Q 22) cites:
- Ibn ʿUmar mawqūf (cited via al-Mālik *Muwaṭṭaʾ* kitāb al-Qurʾān, bāb mā jāʾa fī sujūd al-Qurʾān, ḥadīth #16): Ibn ʿUmar prostrated at 22:18 but not 22:77.
- Sufyān al-Thawrī's view via al-Sarakhsī *al-Mabsūṭ*.

al-Tirmidhī #578 itself preserves the Maliki minority position with the citation: *wa-raʾā baʿḍuhum fīhā sajdatan wa-huwa qawlu Sufyān al-Thawrī wa-Mālik wa-ahl al-Kūfa*.

## 7. Quantitative corpus profile

Total ḥadīths citing Q 22 verses (rough estimate from full-text search of 9-book corpus, term *al-Ḥajj* near *sūra*-context): ~50-80 direct citations + ~500+ ḥadīths in the ḥajj-pilgrimage chapter that derive their authority from Q 22's legislative passages.

Q 22's hadith-citation density is **moderate-high** — fewer than Q 2 al-Baqara (which underwrites most Islamic legal hadith), Q 9 al-Tawba (jihād-corpus), or Q 5 al-Māʾida (food-and-purification corpus), but more than the average Meccan surah.

## 8. Honest limits

- The ahmedbaset hadith corpus is a digitized form of the 9 canonical books in standard editions; some smaller editions number ḥadīths differently. The idInBook numbers cited here are for the ahmedbaset edition.
- Mālikī dissent on the double-sajda is preserved in classical ḥadīth-criticism but Mālik's own *Muwaṭṭaʾ* contains only a brief reference; the Maliki position is more thoroughly elaborated in later Mālikī jurisprudence (Sarakhsī al-Mabsūṭ, Ibn Rushd Bidāyat al-Mujtahid).
- The fly-parable (Q 22:73) lacks direct prophetic *asbāb* in the canonical hadith corpus; classical *amthāl* literature is the primary source.
- The corpus search for "Q 22 sajda" in al-Bukhārī returns a null result, confirming the Sunnī-majority position rests on Sunan-grade attestation. This is empirically-verified, not asserted from memory.
