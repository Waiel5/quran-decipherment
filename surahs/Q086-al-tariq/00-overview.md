## 2. Classical name

Named **al-Ṭāriq** (الطَّارِق) after the opening oath in v.1: *wa-l-samāʾi wa-l-ṭāriq*. The Arabic *al-ṭāriq* derives from the root ṭ-r-q ("to knock, to come at night"). Classical reading (Ibn Kathīr ad loc.):

> Qatāda: *summiya al-najm ṭāriqan li-annahu lā yurā illā bi-l-layl wa-yakhfā bi-l-nahār* — "The star is named *ṭāriq* because it is only seen at night and is hidden by day."

Reinforced by the prophetic ḥadīth idiom *yaṭruq ahlahu* ("comes to his family unexpectedly at night" — al-Bukhārī chapter on traveling-home etiquette).

The verse itself supplies the gloss in the famous ✪ tri-verse self-defining structure:

> v.1: *wa-l-samāʾi wa-l-ṭāriq* (By the heaven and the Knocker)
> v.2: *wa-mā adrāka mā al-ṭāriq* (And what made you know what the Knocker is?)
> v.3: *al-najmu al-thāqib* (The piercing-bright star.)

This is one of the **10 corpus-EXACT instances** of the *wa-mā adrāka mā* rhetorical-self-definition formula, locked across 10 surahs of the short-Meccan-tail (per [[h-new-1190-wa-ma-adraka-cluster|H-NEW-1190]] CONFIRMED p=0.00068).

## 5. ⭐ Unique structural feature — guardian-angel doctrine (v.4)

v.4: **إن كل نفس لما عليها حافظ** — *in kullu nafsin lammā ʿalayhā ḥāfiẓ* — "There is no soul but that there is a guardian over it."

The token *ḥāfiẓ* (singular indefinite "guardian") is **CORPUS-HAPAX to Q 86:4** (1 occurrence in the entire corpus as the bare singular indefinite participle; the verse-final fāṣila position completes the *al-ṭāriq / al-thāqib / ḥāfiẓ* opening triad). The plural *ḥāfiẓūn / ḥāfiẓāt / ḥāfiẓīn* and finite-verb forms *yaḥfaẓ / naḥfaẓ* occur elsewhere, but the **bare-singular indefinite *ḥāfiẓ* is corpus-EXACT-UNIQUE** to Q 86:4.

Classical reading (Ibn Kathīr ad loc.):

> *li-kulli nafsin ʿalayhā min al-malāʾikati ḥāfiẓun yaḥfaẓuhā min al-āfāt* — "Every soul has over it from the angels a guardian who protects it from calamities."

Cross-reference: Q 13:11 *lahu muʿaqqibātun min bayni yadayhi wa-min khalfihi yaḥfaẓūnahu min amri-llāh* ("for him are angels in succession, before and behind, guarding him by Allāh's command") — the longest-form parallel of the guardian-angel doctrine. Q 86:4 is the **corpus-EXACT singular-indefinite anchoring** of this doctrine.

## 6. ⭐ Embryological-creation passage (vv. 5-7)

vv. 5-7 contain a tight 3-verse embryology pericope:

> v.5: فلينظر الإنسان مم خلق — *fal-yanẓuri al-insānu mim-ma khuliq* (Let man consider from what he was created)
> v.6: خلق من ماء دافق — *khuliqa min māʾin dāfiq* (He was created from a gushing fluid)
> v.7: يخرج من بين الصلب والترائب — *yakhruju min bayni al-ṣulbi wa-l-tarāʾib* (Issuing from between the backbone and the ribs)

Hapax density: 4 corpus-unique tokens in 3 verses (مم *mim-ma*, دافق *dāfiq*, الصلب *al-ṣulb*, الترائب *al-tarāʾib* — although *al-tarāʾib* is hapax as the article-prefixed token; the bare-stem والترائب is the corpus-unique form). This is one of the densest hapax-clusters in the entire short-Meccan-tail.

Classical readings (Ibn Kathīr, citing Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿIkrimah):

> *al-ṣulb min al-rajul wa-l-tarāʾib min al-marʾa wa-huwa ṣafrāʾ raqīqa wa-lā yakūnu al-walad illā minhumā* — "The backbone of the man and the ribs of the woman; it is yellow and fine; the child cannot come except from both of them."

The verse is a foundational text for classical embryology and a flashpoint in modern *iʿjāz ʿilmī* (scientific-foreknowledge) debates — but the project's H-META-1 / cross-finding-015 framework places *iʿjāz ʿilmī* in the modern-numerology lane (0/12 confirmed). The verse should be read for its rhetorical-cosmic argumentative function (man's lowly origin → resurrection-power proof) rather than for purported anachronistic anatomy.
