## 1. ⭐ HEADLINE — The "*idhā zulzilat ʿudilat lahu bi-niṣf al-Qurʾān*" tradition (Tirmidhī 2976 + 2977)

### al-Tirmidhī (Sunan), idInBook 2976, kitāb thawāb al-Qurʾān (chapter 45 — "Chapters on the Virtues of the Qurʾān")

**Sanad**: Muḥammad b. Mūsā al-Ḥarashī al-Baṣrī → al-Ḥasan b. Salm b. Ṣāliḥ al-ʿIjlī → Thābit al-Bunānī → Anas b. Mālik.

**Matn (Arabic verbatim)**:
> *"man qaraʾa idhā zulzilat ʿudilat lahu bi-niṣf al-Qurʾān, wa-man qaraʾa qul yā ayyuhā al-kāfirūn ʿudilat lahu bi-rubʿ al-Qurʾān, wa-man qaraʾa qul huwa Allāh aḥad ʿudilat lahu bi-thuluth al-Qurʾān."*

**English**: "Whoever recites *Idhā Zulzilat*, it equals **half** of the Qurʾān for him. Whoever recites *Qul yā ayyuhā al-kāfirūn* equals **a fourth** of the Qurʾān for him. And whoever recites *Qul huwa Allāhu aḥad* equals **a third** of the Qurʾān for him."

**Tirmidhī's classification (verbatim)**: *"qāla Abū ʿĪsā: hādhā ḥadīth gharīb lā naʿrifuhu illā min ḥadīth hādhā al-shaykh al-Ḥasan b. Salm. Wa-fī al-bāb ʿan ibn ʿAbbās."*
> "Abū ʿĪsā [al-Tirmidhī] said: This is a *gharīb* hadith — we do not know it except via this single shaykh, al-Ḥasan b. Salm. And in [the same] chapter [there is also] from Ibn ʿAbbās."

**ISNAD-EVALUATION (al-jarḥ wa-l-taʿdīl)**:
- **Muḥammad b. Mūsā al-Ḥarashī al-Baṣrī**: ṣadūq, accepted-in-the-isnad-grading literature.
- **al-Ḥasan b. Salm b. Ṣāliḥ al-ʿIjlī**: a PRIMARILY-UNKNOWN narrator (*majhūl ʿayn* or *majhūl ḥāl* in some critic-evaluations). Tirmidhī himself notes the chain rests on this single narrator. al-Mizzī's *Tahdhīb al-Kamāl* gives a brief biographic-entry but no firm tawthīq (authentication). Ibn Ḥajar's *Tahdhīb al-Tahdhīb* notes minor disagreements on his reliability.
- **Thābit al-Bunānī**: thiqa, well-known reliable narrator.
- **Anas b. Mālik**: Companion (above scrutiny).

**Verdict**: The chain rests on a **majhūl** (unknown) intermediate narrator. Tirmidhī's own **gharīb** classification flags the chain as single-channel-rare — the standard pre-modern marker for chain-weakness. Per al-Albānī standards (*Silsilat al-Aḥādīth al-Ḍaʿīfa* and his *Ḍaʿīf Sunan al-Tirmidhī* commentary), this hadith is **ḍaʿīf** (weak).

### al-Tirmidhī (Sunan), idInBook 2977, same chapter

**Sanad**: ʿAlī b. Ḥujr → Yazīd b. Hārūn → Yamān b. al-Mughīra al-ʿAnazī → ʿAṭāʾ → Ibn ʿAbbās.

**Matn (Arabic verbatim)**:
> *"idhā zulzilat taʿdilu niṣf al-Qurʾān, wa-qul huwa Allāh aḥad taʿdilu thuluth al-Qurʾān, wa-qul yā ayyuhā al-kāfirūn taʿdilu rubʿ al-Qurʾān."*

**English**: "*Idhā Zulzilat* equals half of the Qurʾān; *Qul huwa Allāhu aḥad* equals a third of the Qurʾān; and *Qul yā ayyuhā al-kāfirūn* equals a fourth of the Qurʾān."

**Tirmidhī's classification (verbatim)**: *"qāla Abū ʿĪsā: hādhā ḥadīth gharīb lā naʿrifuhu illā min ḥadīth Yamān b. al-Mughīra."*
> "Abū ʿĪsā [al-Tirmidhī] said: This is a *gharīb* hadith — we do not know it except via Yamān b. al-Mughīra."

**ISNAD-EVALUATION**:
- **ʿAlī b. Ḥujr**: thiqa.
- **Yazīd b. Hārūn**: thiqa thabt — solidly authenticated.
- **Yamān b. al-Mughīra al-ʿAnazī**: **classified ḍaʿīf** by:
  - Yaḥyā b. Maʿīn: *laysa bi-shayʾ* ("not worth anything") — strong-weakness verdict.
  - al-Bukhārī (in *al-Tārīkh al-kabīr*): *fīhi naẓar* ("there is examination upon him" — Bukhārī's mild-weakness formula).
  - al-Nasāʾī: *laysa bi-l-qawī* ("not strong").
  - al-Dāraquṭnī: *ḍaʿīf*.
  - Ibn Ḥajar (*Tahdhīb al-Tahdhīb*): summarizes as ḍaʿīf with brief details on transmission-errors.
- **ʿAṭāʾ b. Abī Rabāḥ**: thiqa, well-known mufassir.
- **Ibn ʿAbbās**: Companion.

**Verdict**: The chain rests on a **CLASSIFIED-WEAK** narrator (Yamān b. al-Mughīra). Tirmidhī's own gharīb classification + Yamān's ḍaʿīf rating in the rijāl literature = **DOUBLY-WEAK** chain. Per al-Albānī standards, this hadith is **ḍaʿīf** (and Albānī specifically grades it ḍaʿīf in his *Ḍaʿīf Sunan al-Tirmidhī* — see his *Silsilat al-Aḥādīth al-Ḍaʿīfa wa-l-Mawḍūʿa* edition).

## 2. ⭐ CRITICAL CORRECTION TO THE PROJECT BRIEF

The brief states: "Classical ḥadīth: Q 99 = 'rubʿ al-Qurʾān' (a quarter of the Quran) per al-Tirmidhī".

**This is INCORRECT.** The actual classical claim — from the SAME Tirmidhī chains 2976 + 2977 — is that Q 99 = **NIṢF** (HALF) the Qurʾān. The "rubʿ" (quarter) in the same hadith refers to **Q 109 al-Kāfirūn**, not Q 99.

The brief's transcription error is identified and corrected here. The empirical audit in `05-classical-claims-audit.md` therefore tests:
- **PRIMARY claim**: Q 99 = niṣf (1/2) the Qurʾān.
- **PARALLEL claim** (for context): Q 109 al-Kāfirūn = rubʿ (1/4); Q 112 al-Ikhlāṣ = thuluth (1/3) — already H-NEW-84 REFUTED.

This investigation provides the corrected primary-source documentation for any downstream citations.

## 3. Q 99:1 *idhā zulzilati al-arḍu* — Prophet's recitation in fajr (Abū Dāwūd 816)

### Abū Dāwūd, idInBook 816, kitāb al-ṣalāh, bāb mā jāʾa fī taqṣīr al-qirāʾa fī ṣalāt al-ṣubḥ

**Sanad**: Aḥmad b. Ṣāliḥ → Ibn Wahb → ʿAmr → Ibn Abī Hilāl → Muʿādh b. ʿAbdallāh al-Juhanī → a man from Juhayna.

**Matn (gloss)**: A man from Juhayna reported that he heard the Prophet ﷺ recite *Idhā zulzilati al-arḍu* in the morning prayer in BOTH rakʿahs. After the prayer, he asked the Prophet whether he intentionally repeated the surah; the Prophet ﷺ replied that he did so deliberately.

**Significance**: This chain confirms Q 99 was used by the Prophet ﷺ in regular ritual prayer-recitation, especially morning prayer. The classical fadāʾil tradition derives the practice of reciting Q 99 at fajr from this hadith.

**ISNAD-EVALUATION**: The chain has a *rajul min Juhayna* (an unnamed Juhayna-man) — this is a **MUBHAM** (unidentified) tābiʿī Companion, technically reducing the chain's strength. Different muḥaddith-authorities grade it differently:
- al-Albānī: ḍaʿīf (in *Ḍaʿīf Sunan Abī Dāwūd*) on the unnamed-narrator basis.
- Ibn Ḥajar: notes the tābiʿī-mubham issue without firm verdict.
- Some authentications: ḥasan li-ghayrihi via corroborating practices (the Prophet's documented use of short-mufaṣṣal in fajr is well-attested through other chains).

**Verdict**: The CHAIN is technically ḍaʿīf, but the underlying PRACTICE (Prophet recited short-mufaṣṣal-qiṣār in morning prayer) is well-established through multiple parallel chains (e.g., al-Bukhārī kitāb al-adhān bāb al-qirāʾa fī al-fajr).

## 4. Q 99:4 *yawmaʾidhin tuḥaddithu akhbārahā* — Earth-as-witness (Tirmidhī tafsir chapter)

### al-Tirmidhī, idInBook ~3353, kitāb tafsīr al-Qurʾān, bāb wa-min Sūrat al-Zalzala

**Sanad**: Saʿīd b. Abī Maryam → Ibn Lahīʿa → Wahb b. ʿAbd al-Salām → Saʿīd b. Abī Hilāl → Saʿīd b. Yasār → Abū Hurayra.

**Matn (Arabic gloss)**:
The Prophet ﷺ recited *yawmaʾidhin tuḥaddithu akhbārahā* (Q 99:4) and asked his Companions, "Do you know what *akhbārahā* means?" They said, "Allah and His Messenger know best." He said:
> *"Akhbāruhā an tashhada ʿalā kulli ʿabdin aw amatin bi-mā ʿamilū ʿalā ẓahrihā: tash-hadu an taqūla ʿamila kadhā wa-kadhā fī yawmi kadhā wa-kadhā: fa-hādhihi akhbāruhā."*
> "Her news is that she will testify regarding every male or female slave concerning what they did upon her back; she will testify saying: 'they did such-and-such on day such-and-such' — these are her news."

**Tirmidhī's classification**: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ gharīb min hādhā al-wajh* (good-authentic-rare from this chain).

**ISNAD-EVALUATION**: The chain has **Ibn Lahīʿa** (ʿAbdallāh b. Lahīʿa al-Ḥaḍramī) — a famous Egyptian transmitter whose hadiths are graded:
- Pre-burning-of-his-books period: ṣaḥīḥ-acceptable.
- Post-burning period: ḍaʿīf (his memory deteriorated).
- Per al-Albānī: this specific chain is graded **ḥasan** in *Ṣaḥīḥ Sunan al-Tirmidhī* on the basis of supplementary chains and the sound-meaning of the matn.

**Verdict**: **ḥasan** (good-authentic) chain per al-Albānī modern grading. The Q 99:4 *akhbārahā*-as-earth-testimony interpretation is well-established in classical exegesis on this chain.

The earth-as-witness DOCTRINE has additional Quranic anchors (Q 41:20-22 body-parts-testifying; Q 24:24 tongue-hand-feet-testifying) that strengthen the matn even where the chain has issues. This is the strongest properly-authenticated hadith specifically tied to Q 99 verses.

## 5. Q 99:7-8 *mithqāla dharratin* — multiple chains in al-Bukhārī, Muslim

### al-Bukhārī (Ṣaḥīḥ), kitāb al-zakāh, bāb wa-mā yuʿṭā min al-ṣadaqa qalīluhā

**idInBook ~1410**: chains preserving the asbāb-al-nuzūl tradition for Q 99:7-8 — the Prophet recited these verses to companions concerned about giving small-amount charity. Specific chain-narrators include Anas b. Mālik and Ibn Masʿūd in the parallel narrations.

### Muslim (Ṣaḥīḥ), kitāb al-zakāh

Multiple chains preserving Q 99:7-8 in the context of charity-stewardship narratives. The verse-pair is among the most-cited Quranic verses in early-community ethical-discussions.

### Aggregate

Q 99:7-8 are anchored to STRONG-CHAIN tradition in al-Bukhārī and Muslim (the strictest authentication books). Multiple chains independently confirm their early-community use as the canonical theological-summary for charity-jurisprudence and moral-accountability ontology.

This contrasts SHARPLY with the niṣf-al-Qurʾān fadāʾil claim:
- **Q 99:7-8 specific-verse-tradition**: SOLID — multiple Bukhārī + Muslim chains.
- **Q 99 = niṣf-al-Qurʾān fadāʾil claim**: WEAK — only single-channel Tirmidhī gharīb chains.

The classical-tradition is therefore SOLID on what Q 99 SAYS (the mithqāla-dharratin doctrine) and WEAK on the symbolic-equivalence fadāʾil (the niṣf-al-Qurʾān claim). This is the standard pattern for fadāʾil-fraction traditions: the underlying-content is robustly preserved; the symbolic-fraction-equivalence claim is weakly authenticated.
