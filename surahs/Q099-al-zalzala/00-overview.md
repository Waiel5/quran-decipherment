## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Zalzala** (or *al-Zilzāl*) after its opening *zilzālahā* in v. 1. The Arabic *zalzala* is the reduplicated quadriliteral form (z-l-z-l) derived from the geminate root z-l-l ("to slip/fall"); the reduplication intensifies to denote violent shaking / quaking / convulsion. The form *zilzāl* is a verbal-noun (maṣdar) meaning "the [absolute] shaking [of itself]." This morphology — root-reduplication producing intensive cosmic-violence vocabulary — is characteristic of the short-Meccan-tail eschatology surahs (cf. *qāriʿa* Q 101, *ṭāmma* Q 79, *ṣākhkha* Q 80).

Classical naming-tradition is unanimous on the surah-title: al-Bukhārī, Muslim, al-Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah, al-Nasāʾī, al-Dārimī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī all use either *Sūrat al-Zalzala* or *Sūrat Idhā Zulzilat* (the opening-incipit name).

## 3. Classical chronology debate (Meccan vs Medinan)

The chronology of Q 99 is one of the corpus's most-disputed cases:

**Meccan position** (Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Ṭabarī; al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 1; al-Wāḥidī *Asbāb al-nuzūl*):
- Eschatological cosmic-event content matches the Late-Meccan idhā-opener cluster (Q 56, 81, 82, 84).
- Short-verse rhythm + dense divine-pronoun economy fit Late-Meccan stylistic register.
- Hostile addressee ("*wa-qāla al-insānu mā lahā*") = generic-mankind, not specific Medinan community.

**Medinan position** (Ibn Masʿūd; al-Ḍaḥḥāk via Ibn Kathīr; al-Qurṭubī also notes this view; QAC `revelation-order.csv` tags this surah `medinan`):
- The pairing of Q 99:7-8 (*mithqāla dharratin khayran yarah / sharran yarah*) with the parallel-of-individual-accountability theological tone matches Medinan legal-eschatology development.
- Several traditions place the revelation occasion at Madīna in connection with the *aṣḥāb al-ṣadaqāt* (those who quibbled over giving even small amounts in charity).
- Mukāwala-tradition: a group of Companions complained about giving small amounts; v. 7-8 revealed assuring them no amount is too small.

**Modern verdict** (per al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān*, Nöldeke *Geschichte des Qorāns*, al-Zarkashī *al-Burhān*): consensus mid-tradition is **Late Meccan** with a Medinan-revelation-occasion possibly attached to vv. 7-8 specifically. Nöldeke ranks Q 99 in his Period III (Late Meccan, 86-99 in his sequence). Theodor Nöldeke's chronological position: rank ~93/114.

**Empirical anchor (this project)**: H-NEW-1200 confirms Q 99 in the 14-surah short-Meccan-tail eschatology cluster (FR-cohesive at p=0.00030) and specifically as one of the 5 *idhā*-cosmic-event-openers. ## 11. The *fadāʾil* tradition — Tirmidhī "ḥalf the Quran" (al-Tirmidhī 2976 + 2977)

al-Tirmidhī *Sunan*, kitāb thawāb al-Qurʾān, idInBook 2976 + 2977 preserves the famous claim:

> *man qaraʾa Idhā zulzilat ʿudilat lahu bi-niṣf al-Qurʾān* — "Whoever recites Idhā Zulzilat, it equals **HALF** the Qurʾān for him."

In the same hadith (BOTH chains), the recitation of Q 109 al-Kāfirūn is said to equal a quarter, and Q 112 al-Ikhlāṣ a third.

**HEADLINE EMPIRICAL VERIFICATION RESULT**: Both Tirmidhī chains (2976 and 2977) are classified by Imām Tirmidhī himself as **gharīb** (single-channel rare hadith — a marker for weak isnād authentication):
- Tirmidhī 2976: *"hādhā ḥadīth gharīb lā naʿrifuhu illā min ḥadīth hādhā al-shaykh al-Ḥasan b. Salm"* — "this is gharīb; we know it only via this single shaykh."
- Tirmidhī 2977: *"hādhā ḥadīth gharīb lā naʿrifuhu illā min ḥadīth Yamān b. al-Mughīra"* — "this is gharīb; we know it only via Yamān b. al-Mughīra" (Yamān b. al-Mughīra is classified ḍaʿīf by Ibn Maʿīn, al-Bukhārī, al-Nasāʾī, and others — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`).

Per al-Albānī standards (*Silsilat al-Aḥādīth al-Ḍaʿīfa*), both chains are **ḍaʿīf** (weak). NO ṣaḥīḥ-classified parallel chain exists in the 9 books for the niṣf-al-Qurʾān equivalence. The hadith is part of the same *ʿadlat thawāb* (recitation-reward equivalence) cluster as the Q 36 = "qalb al-Qurʾān" hadith (refuted as ḍaʿīf-jiddan / mawḍūʿ via H-NEW-82) and the Q 112 = "thuluth al-Qurʾān" hadith (literally refuted at quantitative level via H-NEW-84).

**CRITICAL CORRECTION TO PROJECT BRIEF**: The brief stated Q 99 = "rubʿ al-Qurʾān" (a quarter). This is INCORRECT. The actual classical claim — from the same Tirmidhī hadith — is that Q 99 = **NIṢF** (HALF) the Qurʾān; the "RUBʿ" (quarter) in the same hadith refers to Q 109 al-Kāfirūn. The brief's transcription error is corrected in this investigation.
