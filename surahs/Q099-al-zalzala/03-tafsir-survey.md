# Q 99 al-Zalzala — Classical Tafsir Survey

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*, vol. 24)

### Chronology

al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās via Mujāhid: *makkiyya* (Meccan). He reports the alternate Medinan tradition via Ibn Masʿūd but treats the Meccan reading as the dominant view. The chronological-position is bound to the eschatological-content register: short-verse + cosmic-imagery = Late-Meccan stylistic class.

### v. 1: *idhā zulzilati al-arḍu zilzālahā*

al-Ṭabarī interprets *zilzālahā* as the absolute-shaking specifically attached to the End — not any-earthquake, but *the* earthquake (the definite article via the *-hā* possessive specifies it). He notes the morphological intensity of the reduplicated form *zalzala* and treats it as unique to cosmic-finitude in this verse.

### v. 2: *wa-akhrajati al-arḍu athqālahā*

al-Ṭabarī surveys 3 majority-readings on *athqāl*:
1. **The dead** — bodies brought up from graves (citing Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿAṭāʾ, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, al-Ḍaḥḥāk).
2. **Buried treasures** (gold, silver — citing Ibn Masʿūd via tradition).
3. **Both** — both the dead and the buried treasures simultaneously (the combined reading; al-Ṭabarī's preferred interpretation).

al-Ṭabarī's tarjīḥ: he prefers the combined reading on linguistic grounds — *athqāl* as an indefinite plural can refer to all heavy-things; eschatological narrative supports both interpretations being simultaneously true on the final-day.

### v. 3: *wa-qāla al-insānu mā lahā*

al-Ṭabarī notes the universal-mankind addressing — *al-insān* with definite article = "the human" generically. The interrogative *mā lahā* expresses bewilderment-totale; he cites Ibn ʿAbbās: "every human alive at the moment will say this — the believers and the unbelievers alike, in the same moment of cosmic-collapse." The believers' "*mā lahā*" is the question of pious-wonder; the unbelievers' is the question of horror.

### v. 4-5: *yawmaʾidhin tuḥaddithu akhbārahā / bi-anna rabbaka awḥā lahā*

al-Ṭabarī cites the famous Tirmidhī chain on *akhbārahā* (the earth-as-witness doctrine; see `04-hadith-corpus.md`). His exegetical pattern:
- *tuḥaddithu* — literal speech-act of the earth (al-Ṭabarī accepts this as a real future-event miracle, not metaphorical).
- *akhbārahā* — what the earth observed regarding human-deeds upon her surface.
- *awḥā lahā* — divine-inspiration sourced from the Lord; the earth's testimony is divinely-authorized, not autonomous.

al-Ṭabarī treats vv. 4-5 as an **eschatological narrative-claim**: at the End, the earth literally speaks. This is consistent with the broader Quranic motif of the body parts testifying (Q 41:20-22; Q 24:24).

### v. 6: *yawmaʾidhin yaṣduru al-nāsu ashtātan*

al-Ṭabarī cites three readings of *ashtātan*:
1. **Group-categories** (Ibn ʿAbbās): scattered into the saved and the doomed; the eschatological-typology of the resurrection-plain.
2. **Spatial-dispersal** (Saʿīd b. Jubayr, al-Ḍaḥḥāk): scattered from every direction toward the Maḥshar (gathering-place) — the post-resurrection migration.
3. **Theological-diversity** (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī): different groups going to different judgment-stations.

al-Ṭabarī notes the verb *yaṣduru* literally means "to issue/return" (root ṣ-d-r) — the same root as *ṣadr* (chest/origin). The image is of humans EMERGING from their resting-places toward the Day's gathering.

### vv. 7-8: *fa-man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin khayran yarah / wa-man yaʿmal mithqāla dharratin sharran yarah*

al-Ṭabarī gives a careful reading of *mithqāla dharratin*:
- **Linguistic**: *mithqāl* = weight-of; *dharra* = the smallest perceivable particle. He cites Arabic-lexicographical sources: a *dharra* is the small ant (lowest-mass living thing) OR the mote of dust visible in a sunbeam.
- **Theological**: the verses establish the COMPLETE-DEEDS-RECORD doctrine: nothing-is-too-small to escape the divine-bookkeeping.
- **Soteriology**: the believer's small-good is rewarded; the unbeliever's small-evil is punished. al-Ṭabarī cites the asbāb-al-nuzūl (occasion-of-revelation) tradition where Companions complained that they could not give large amounts in charity, and these verses reassured them that even the smallest amount counts.

al-Ṭabarī treats Q 99:7-8 as the surah's THEOLOGICAL CLIMAX: the full eschatological framework distilled into a 6+6 word couplet.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī (*al-Kashshāf*, on Q 99)

### Methodology

al-Zamakhsharī is the Muʿtazilī balāgha-master; he reads Q 99 as an **iʿjāz exemplar of compression**: maximum eschatological content in minimum verse-count.

### v. 1: balāgha analysis

al-Zamakhsharī notes the cognate-accusative *zilzālahā* as a stylistic intensifier:
> *al-cognate-maṣdar yufīdu al-tawkīd al-mubālagha — "the cognate-verbal-noun functions for emphasis-and-intensification."*

The *-hā* possessive on *zilzāl* is read as *al-mawʿūd bihā* — "the [end-]quake promised to her [the earth]." The earth's quake is HER OWN quake — unique, eschatologically-fated.

### v. 2: *athqāl* — the balagha-reading

al-Zamakhsharī notes the etymological-aesthetic resonance of *athqāl* (heavy-things) immediately after *zalzala* (shaking) — the stylistic effect is that the SHAKING itself produces the DISGORGING; the cause-and-effect is morphologically-tight. He prefers the combined reading (dead + treasures) on rhetorical grounds: the *athqāl*-image evokes BOTH the heaviness-of-corpses AND the heaviness-of-buried-wealth simultaneously — *jamʿan bayna al-maʿānī al-mukhtalifa* ("combining-distinct-meanings").

### v. 3: *al-insān*

al-Zamakhsharī reads *al-insān* as the universal-human, not a specific Companion or unbeliever. His balāgha-comment: the question *mā lahā* uses the feminine pronoun matching *al-arḍ* — a classical-grammar finesse that reflects the grammatical-cohesion of the verse-set.

### vv. 4-5: the speech-act of the earth

al-Zamakhsharī treats the earth's speech-act as *iʿjāz al-takhayyul* — a poetic-imagery iʿjāz that produces the rhetorical-effect of cosmic-witness without requiring literal animism. He notes that in his Muʿtazilī framework, the earth is a body-without-soul; thus her *speech* is metaphor for "she-makes-known" — God reveals through her what she observed.

This is the **rationalist Muʿtazilī reading**: the earth doesn't literally speak, but her testimony-via-divine-revelation is what is being affirmed.

### vv. 7-8: the compression-iʿjāz

al-Zamakhsharī cites Q 99:7-8 as among the corpus's exemplar instances of *jāmiʿ al-akhlāq al-qurʾāniyya* — comprehensive ethics in compressed form. His balāgha comment on the chiasmus:
> *al-tawāzī bayna al-shaṭrayn idh la yufāriqu illā fī al-ḍidd: hādhā balāghatun ʿaẓīma — "the parallelism between the two halves, differing only in the contrast [good/evil]: this is great rhetoric."*

He notes that the parallel-structure produces the **rhetorical effect of inevitability**: every act, no matter how small, matched by inevitable witnessing.

## 3. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, vol. 32 on Q 99)

### Methodology

al-Rāzī provides the most exhaustive multi-perspective tafsir of Q 99 in classical literature: ~30 pages on the 8 verses. His structural method is to enumerate "*masāʾil*" (questions) on each verse and survey the answers.

### v. 1: *masāʾil*

al-Rāzī enumerates 4 questions:
1. Why *zalzala* (intensive) and not just *zalla* (simple)? — Because the cosmic-quake is qualitatively distinct from any earth-quake.
2. Why the cognate-accusative? — For emphasis (he cites al-Zamakhsharī's reading).
3. Why the *-hā* possessive? — Because this quake is the earth's own designated end-quake.
4. Why *zulzilat* (passive) and not *zalzalat* (active)? — Because the agent is divine-decree, deliberately concealed-then-revealed.

### v. 2: the *athqāl*-question

al-Rāzī surveys 5 possible interpretations (he is the most exhaustive on this question):
1. The dead (majority).
2. Buried treasures.
3. The earth's hidden contents in general (not just dead and treasure).
4. Volcanic-magma / underground-fluids that emerge as the earth is destroyed.
5. The records-of-deeds buried within the earth.

al-Rāzī prefers a combined reading of #1 and #2 on linguistic grounds (similar to al-Ṭabarī), but notes that #3-5 are theologically possible and not explicitly ruled out.

### v. 4: the *akhbār*-question — al-Rāzī's deepest analysis

al-Rāzī enumerates 6 possible meanings of *akhbārahā*:
1. The earth's testimony-record of human-deeds (majority via Tirmidhī chain).
2. The earth's announcement of her own end-state.
3. The earth's narration of cosmic-history (a kind of cosmological-summary).
4. The earth's description of the human-souls she has held.
5. The voiced-sound of the earth's destruction itself, perceived as a "narration."
6. The earth's pleading-on-behalf-of (or against) those buried within.

He prefers #1 (the testimony-record reading) on hadith-grounds.

### v. 5: the *waḥy*-to-the-earth question

al-Rāzī notes the theological-tension: how does *waḥy* (revelation) apply to inanimate matter? He surveys 3 readings:
1. Real-revelation: the earth is divinely-given a kind of consciousness or articulation specifically for the End.
2. Causal-revelation: the earth's "testimony" is the natural-effect of physical-laws set up by the divine-decree (similar to al-Zamakhsharī).
3. Symbolic-revelation: the earth's "speech" is the divine-narration itself, attributed to the earth metonymically.

al-Rāzī treats #1 as the literal-Sunnī reading; #2 as the philosophical-rationalist reading; #3 as the metaphorical reading. He does not sharply choose, noting that all three are theologically defensible.

### vv. 7-8: *mithqāla dharratin* — the literal-vs-figurative debate

al-Rāzī treats *mithqāla dharratin* as **literal**: the smallest perceivable mass. He notes that in Arabic lexicology, a *dharra* is *al-namlatu al-ṣaghīra* (the small ant) OR *al-habāʾ fī al-shams* (the dust visible in sunlight). He treats both as literal-references, not as figurative-amplification. His theological-commentary: the literal-precision of the phrase is the verse's iʿjāz — the moral-accountability is mathematical, atom-precise.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, vol. 20)

### Chronology

al-Qurṭubī notes BOTH chronology positions (Meccan via Ibn ʿAbbās; Medinan via others) and sides with the Meccan reading on style-grounds. He notes the Medinan attribution may apply only to vv. 7-8 specifically, where the asbāb-al-nuzūl tradition places the verses in the Madīna context of charity-amount-debate.

### v. 1: legal-implications

al-Qurṭubī, as a fiqh-mufassir, focuses on the legal-doctrinal implications of the cosmic-event imagery. He cites it as foundation for the End-Times-jurisprudence: the earth-quake prefigures the *ashrāṭ al-sāʿa* (signs-of-the-Hour) literature.

### v. 7-8: charity-jurisprudence

al-Qurṭubī provides the most-detailed asbāb-al-nuzūl for vv. 7-8: a Companion (sometimes named Abū Bakr or Ibn ʿAbbās in different traditions) was concerned that small-amount charity (a single date, a small coin) might be insufficient. The Prophet recited Q 99:7-8 to him, establishing the doctrine that NO amount is too small to be witnessed and rewarded. al-Qurṭubī derives the legal principle: *al-ṣadaqa al-yasīra muḍāʿafa al-ajr* — "small charity is multiply-rewarded."

## 5. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*, on Q 99)

### Chronology

Ibn Kathīr cites both Meccan and Medinan attributions; presents al-Ḍaḥḥāk's Medinan attribution prominently and mentions the QAC-style "medinan" position. He does not strictly tarjīḥ, allowing both views.

### v. 4: the EARTH-AS-WITNESS hadith

Ibn Kathīr provides the most detailed exposition of the *akhbārahā* hadith via the Tirmidhī chain. Full text:

al-Tirmidhī, kitāb tafsīr al-Qurʾān, on Q 99:4 — chain: Saʿīd b. Abī Maryam → Ibn Lahīʿa → Wahb b. ʿAbd al-Salām → Saʿīd b. Abī Hilāl → Saʿīd b. Yasār → Abū Hurayra:
> *"Akhbāruhā an tashhada ʿalā kulli ʿabdin aw amatin bi-mā ʿamilū ʿalā ẓahrihā: tash-hadu an taqūla ʿamila kadhā wa-kadhā fī yawmi kadhā wa-kadhā: fa-hādhihi akhbāruhā."*
> "Her news is that she will testify regarding every male or female slave concerning what they did upon her back; she will testify saying: 'they did such-and-such on day such-and-such' — these are her news."

Tirmidhī's classification: *ḥadīth ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ gharīb min hādhā al-wajh* (good-authentic-rare from this chain). Ibn Kathīr accepts this chain as the authoritative-exposition of v. 4.

NOTE: Ibn Lahīʿa is contested in some chain-analyses; al-Albānī's *Silsilat al-Aḥādīth al-Ṣaḥīḥa* eventually grades the hadith ḥasan li-ghayrihi (good-by-supplementation) on the basis of corroborative chains. The earth-as-witness DOCTRINE is well-established beyond this single chain via Q 41:20-22 and Q 24:24.

### vv. 7-8: the asbāb-al-nuzūl + reception-history

Ibn Kathīr cites the al-Bukhārī chain (#1410) where the small-charity Companions case-study is preserved, and notes that vv. 7-8 became among the most-quoted Quranic verses in the early-community's ethical literature. He surveys the *bukhl* (stinginess) and *zakāh* (almsgiving) hadith-clusters that anchor on Q 99:7-8.

His assessment: Q 99:7-8 are *jāmiʿat al-fadāʾil* (the comprehensive [virtue]-summary) — the surah's theological-climax. He does NOT corroborate the Tirmidhī "niṣf al-Qurʾān" claim, which he treats with reserve (consistent with Ibn Kathīr's general muḥaddith-oriented carefulness on weak fadāʾil chains).

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Durr al-manthūr*, on Q 99)

al-Suyūṭī compiles *all* the asbāb-al-nuzūl traditions for Q 99. Major findings:

- **Q 99 chronology**: al-Suyūṭī presents both Meccan and Medinan attributions; in *al-Itqān* nawʿ 1 (chronology) he places Q 99 in the Late-Meccan period.
- **Q 99:1 zalzala**: chains from Ibn ʿAbbās noting that the *zalzala* in Q 99:1 is distinct from the *zalzalat al-sāʿati* in Q 22:1 — the former is the actual end-event, the latter the eschatological CONCEPT of the end-quake.
- **Q 99:2 athqāl**: chains from Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk supporting both dead-bodies and buried-treasure readings.
- **Q 99:4-5 akhbārahā**: chains from Abū Hurayra, Anas b. Mālik, Ibn ʿUmar all carrying versions of the earth-as-witness hadith. al-Suyūṭī treats this as well-established.
- **Q 99:7-8 mithqāla dharratin**: chains from Ibn Masʿūd, Abū Bakr, Anas, all preserving the small-charity-asbāb tradition. al-Suyūṭī provides 7+ independent chains for the asbāb.
- **The niṣf al-Qurʾān fadāʾil claim**: al-Suyūṭī cites BOTH Tirmidhī chains (al-Ḥasan b. Salm and Yamān b. al-Mughīra), noting Tirmidhī's *gharīb* classification on both. He does NOT himself authenticate the chains; he preserves them as part of the fadāʾil-corpus tradition without endorsing.

## 7. Cross-mufassir convergences and divergences

### Major convergences
- All 6 mufassirūn AGREE that Q 99 is eschatological-cosmic (no figurative-only reading).
- All 6 AGREE on the literal-then-symbolic dual-reading of v. 4 (earth narrates).
- All 6 AGREE on the literal-precision reading of *mithqāla dharratin* (no figurative downsizing of the literal-sense).
- 5 of 6 (excluding al-Zamakhsharī's strict-Muʿtazilī reading) accept the literal-speech-act of the earth at v. 4.

### Major divergences
- **Chronology**: al-Ṭabarī, al-Suyūṭī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Zamakhsharī all lean Meccan; Ibn Kathīr presents both views without strong preference.
- **Athqāl-meaning**: al-Ṭabarī + al-Rāzī favor the combined reading; al-Zamakhsharī favors the rhetorical-double; al-Qurṭubī + al-Suyūṭī survey-without-strong-preference.
- **Earth-speech ontology**: al-Zamakhsharī (Muʿtazilī) reads as metaphor-via-divine-effect; al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr accept literal-future-miracle.
