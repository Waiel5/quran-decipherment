## 2. The ʿUtba b. Rabīʿa / Fuṣṣilat-recitation tradition

This is the single most-cited Q 41 narrative-tradition: the Prophet recited Q 41:1-13 to ʿUtba b. Rabīʿa when ʿUtba came as Quraysh's negotiator.

The tradition is preserved in:
- Ibn Kathīr ad Q 41:5 (citing Mūsā b. ʿUqba's *Maghāzī* and al-Ṭabarī).
- Multiple sīra sources (Ibn Hishām).

**However**, the precise narrative is **NOT** in the 9-book canonical hadith pull (verified by discriminating-phrase search). The story is a **sīra/maghāzī tradition**, not a *muḥaddithūn*-canonical one. False positives in the initial search ("ʿUtba b. Rabīʿa" + "إذا جاء نصر الله") matched Q 110 (Naṣr) narratives and Badr-context references, NOT the Fuṣṣilat episode.

**Correction note (this session)**: The earlier hits attributed to Bukhārī #3472, #4111, #4236, #4761-4764 were checked at record-level and found to be **false positives** — they reference the Battle-of-Badr ʿUtba b. Rabīʿa (Q 22:19 *hādhāni khaṣmāni*), NOT the Fuṣṣilat-recitation narrative.

**Verdict**: The ʿUtba/Fuṣṣilat narrative is **a sīra/tafsīr tradition, NOT a 9-book canonical ḥadīth tradition**. The classical anchor remains Ibn Kathīr ad Q 41:5.

Q 41 is a member of the al-Ḥawāmīm cluster. Ibn Masʿūd's *al-Ḥawāmīm dībāj al-Qurʾān* tradition (cited via Abū ʿUbayd's *Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān*) belongs to all 7 surahs. See [[hawamim-7-cluster-synthesis]] for cluster-level cataloging.
