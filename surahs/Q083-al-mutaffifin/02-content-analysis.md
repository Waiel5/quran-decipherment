# Q 83 al-Muṭaffifīn — Content Analysis

Arabic text quoted from `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json` (Q 83, 36 verses). Glosses are the
investigator's working translation, cross-checked against `data/translations/en.sahih.txt` and the
tafsir survey (03).

## 1. Content register

Q 83 is **eschatological-ethical**: it opens with a concrete commercial-ethics indictment (taṭfīf),
pivots immediately to resurrection and the standing-before-God, and then unfolds a two-panel afterlife
diptych (fujjār-record / abrār-record) before closing on an eschatological reversal of worldly scorn.
The unifying logic is **measure and recompense**: those who give short measure in this world will be
measured out in full in the next; those who mocked will be mocked.

## 2. Four-block architecture (verse-by-verse)

### Block A — TAṬFĪF indictment + resurrection (vv. 1–6)

| v | Arabic | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | ويل للمطففين | Woe to the defrauders (short-measurers)! |
| 2 | الذين إذا اكتالوا على الناس يستوفون | who, when they take measure from people, take in full, |
| 3 | وإذا كالوهم أو وزنوهم يخسرون | but when they measure or weigh for them, give less. |
| 4 | ألا يظن أولئك أنهم مبعوثون | Do those not think that they will be resurrected — |
| 5 | ليوم عظيم | for a tremendous Day, |
| 6 | يوم يقوم الناس لرب العالمين | the Day mankind will stand before the Lord of the worlds? |

The block moves from a **specific economic sin** (taṭfīf, the double-standard of demanding full measure
while giving short) to the **resurrection ground** that makes it accountable. v.6 *yawma yaqūmu l-nāsu
li-rabbi l-ʿālamīn* is the surah's eschatological anchor (and the locus of the ṣaḥīḥ Ibn-ʿUmar
sweat-of-Judgment hadith — 04-hadith-corpus). al-Qurṭubī (ad v.6) details four grammatical analyses of
the *yawma* governor; the sense is "they will be raised on the Day mankind stands before the Lord."

### Block B — FUJJĀR scene: SIJJĪN (vv. 7–17)

| v | Arabic | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 7 | كلا إن كتاب الفجار لفي سجين | **Kallā!** Indeed the record of the wicked is in Sijjīn. |
| 8 | وما أدراك ما سجين | And what can make you know what Sijjīn is? |
| 9 | كتاب مرقوم | A written (inscribed) record. |
| 10 | ويل يومئذ للمكذبين | Woe that Day to the deniers — |
| 11 | الذين يكذبون بيوم الدين | those who deny the Day of Judgment. |
| 12 | وما يكذب به إلا كل معتد أثيم | None denies it but every sinful transgressor. |
| 13 | إذا تتلى عليه آياتنا قال أساطير الأولين | When Our verses are recited to him he says, "Legends of the ancients!" |
| 14 | كلا ۖ بل ۜ ران على قلوبهم ما كانوا يكسبون | **Kallā!** Rather, what they earned has rusted over their hearts. |
| 15 | كلا إنهم عن ربهم يومئذ لمحجوبون | **Kallā!** Indeed from their Lord, that Day, they are veiled. |
| 16 | ثم إنهم لصالو الجحيم | Then indeed they will [enter and] burn in the Blaze. |
| 17 | ثم يقال هذا الذي كنتم به تكذبون | Then it will be said, "This is what you used to deny." |

The block contains **three of the surah's four rebuke-*kallā*** (vv. 7, 14, 15). v.13 *asāṭīr
al-awwalīn* ("legends of the ancients") is the Meccan-polemic motif Ibn al-Faras used to argue the surah
is Meccan (05, Claim 1). v.14 *rāna ʿalā qulūbihim* is the **rān (heart-rust) verse** — the locus
classicus explicated by the Abū-Hurayra black-spot hadith (04). v.15 *maḥjūbūn* (veiled from the Lord)
is read by al-Shāfiʿī (via al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr) as a proof-text that the believers WILL see God (the
wicked's veiling implies the righteous' unveiling) — see 03.

### Block C — ABRĀR scene: ʿILLIYYĪN (vv. 18–28)

| v | Arabic | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 18 | كلا إن كتاب الأبرار لفي عليين | **Kallā!** Indeed the record of the righteous is in ʿIlliyyīn. |
| 19 | وما أدراك ما عليون | And what can make you know what ʿIlliyyūn is? |
| 20 | كتاب مرقوم | A written (inscribed) record, |
| 21 | يشهده المقربون | witnessed by those brought near (the angels). |
| 22 | إن الأبرار لفي نعيم | Indeed the righteous will be in bliss, |
| 23 | على الأرائك ينظرون | on couches, gazing. |
| 24 | تعرف في وجوههم نضرة النعيم | You will recognize on their faces the radiance of bliss. |
| 25 | يسقون من رحيق مختوم | They are given to drink of a sealed nectar, |
| 26 | ختامه مسك ۚ وفي ذلك فليتنافس المتنافسون | whose seal is musk — for that let the competitors compete — |
| 27 | ومزاجه من تسنيم | and its mixture is of Tasnīm, |
| 28 | عينا يشرب بها المقربون | a spring from which those brought near will drink. |

This block opens with the **fourth rebuke-*kallā*** (v.18). It is the structural mirror of Block B,
sharing the EXACT frame (*kallā inna kitāba …, wa-mā adrāka mā …, kitābun marqūm*, vv. 18–20 ≅ vv. 7–9)
but inverting the destiny: ʿilliyyīn (not sijjīn), bliss (not blaze), gazing on couches (not veiled),
sealed musk-nectar (not the record of doom). v.26 *fal-yatanāfas il-mutanāfisūn* ("for that, let the
competitors compete") is a famous exhortation-aside breaking the descriptive frame.

### Block D — ESCHATOLOGICAL REVERSAL of scorn (vv. 29–36)

| v | Arabic | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 29 | إن الذين أجرموا كانوا من الذين آمنوا يضحكون | Indeed the criminals used to laugh at those who believed; |
| 30 | وإذا مروا بهم يتغامزون | and when they passed by them, they would wink at one another; |
| 31 | وإذا انقلبوا إلى أهلهم انقلبوا فكهين | and when they returned to their people, they returned jesting; |
| 32 | وإذا رأوهم قالوا إن هؤلاء لضالون | and when they saw them they said, "Indeed these are astray!" |
| 33 | وما أرسلوا عليهم حافظين | — though they had not been sent as guardians over them. |
| 34 | فاليوم الذين آمنوا من الكفار يضحكون | So Today the believers laugh at the disbelievers, |
| 35 | على الأرائك ينظرون | on couches, gazing. |
| 36 | هل ثوب الكفار ما كانوا يفعلون | Have the disbelievers [not] been repaid for what they used to do? |

al-Qurṭubī (ad v.29) records the *asbāb* identifying the scorners as Quraysh chiefs (al-Walīd b.
al-Mughīra, ʿUqba b. Abī Muʿayṭ, al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil, Abū Jahl, al-Naḍr b. al-Ḥārith …) mocking poor
believers (ʿAmmār, Khabbāb, Ṣuhayb, Bilāl). v.34 *fal-yawma lladhīna āmanū min al-kuffāri yaḍḥakūn* is
the **exact reversal** of v.29: the laughed-at become the laughers. al-Qurṭubī (ad v.34) ties it to the
end of Sūrat al-Muʾminūn and cites Qatāda's image of a window between Paradise and Hell through which
the believers look down on their former tormentors. v.35 *ʿalā l-arāʾiki yanẓurūn* verbatim repeats
v.23 — binding Block D back to Block C (the abrār's couches).

## 3. The chiastic-justice macro-structure

The surah's outer frame is a **justice-inversion ring**:
- **A (vv. 1–3):** the defrauders take FULL, give SHORT (worldly imbalance).
- **D (vv. 34–36):** the mockers are repaid in FULL (*hal thuwwiba …* — "have they been repaid?"); the
  scorned are vindicated (eschatological rebalancing).

The center is the two-panel record-diptych (B/C). The *taṭfīf* of the opening — giving less than is
due — is answered by a God who gives EXACTLY what is due (*hal thuwwiba l-kuffāru mā kānū yafʿalūn*):
the short-measurer meets the full-measurer. This is the surah's deep coherence and the reason classical
*munāsaba* scholars (al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar*) treat its 36 verses as a single argument.

## 4. The SIJJĪN ↔ ʿILLIYYĪN antithesis (empirical close-reading)

The B/C diptych is the corpus's clearest *muqābala* of contrasted destinies. Empirically (Q083-F-01,
06-novel-findings):
- The two 11-verse blocks (vv. 7–17, 18–28) share only **3 QAC roots** — `ktb` (kitāb), `rqm` (marqūm),
  `dry` (adrāka) — i.e. ONLY the announcement frame *kallā inna kitāba …, wa-mā adrāka mā …, kitābun
  marqūm*. Against a null of random 11-verse block pairs (mean 12.7 shared roots), the two blocks are
  FAR MORE lexically disjoint than chance.
- The destiny-vocabularies are **perfectly disjoint** (zero leakage): sijjīn / jaḥīm / maḥjūb confined
  to B; ʿilliyyīn / naʿīm / raḥīq / misk / tasnīm / arāʾik confined to C.

So the antithesis is achieved by a **minimal 3-root shared scaffold + total destiny-disjunction** — the
opposite of "lexical mirroring." The blocks are deliberately NON-overlapping except for the bare
announcement formula that signals "here is another record." This is a more austere, more rhetorically
disciplined antithesis than the hypothesis anticipated.

## 5. Vocabulary distinctness

- **Corpus-distinctive vocabulary:** *sijjīn* (Q 83:7, 8 — only occurrences in the corpus), *ʿilliyyīn /
  ʿilliyyūn* (Q 83:18, 19 — only occurrences), *muṭaffifīn / taṭfīf*-sense (the title; root ṭ-f-f
  appears here), *raḥīq* (Q 83:25 — only occurrence), *tasnīm* (Q 83:27 — only occurrence). The surah is
  unusually rich in **corpus-singleton afterlife nouns**, which is part of why its FR-roots distance is
  short to other eschatological short-tail surahs yet its specific vocabulary is locally unique.
- **Repeated frame:** *kitābun marqūm* (vv. 9, 20 — verbatim), *wa-mā adrāka mā …* (vv. 8, 19), *ʿalā
  l-arāʾiki yanẓurūn* (vv. 23, 35 — verbatim), *kallā* ×4 (vv. 7, 14, 15, 18). The internal verbatim
  repetition is what lowers the surah's TTR (0.698) below the extreme short-tail surahs.

  *fal-yatanāfas il-mutanāfisūn* (the only legitimate competition is for ʿilliyyīn, v.26).
- **end of Sūrat al-Muʾminūn (Q 23:110)** — al-Qurṭubī (ad Q 83:34) explicitly cross-references the
  believers-laughing-at-disbelievers reversal there.
