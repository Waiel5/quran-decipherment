# Q 100 al-ʿĀdiyāt — Classical Tafsīr Survey

## 1. The horses-vs-camels controversy — a 8-mufassir survey

The defining classical question on Q 100 is the **identity of the al-ʿādiyāt** (the chargers / coursing ones). Two schools dominate:
- **HORSES** (al-khayl) — the dominant Ibn-ʿAbbās majority reading
- **CAMELS** (al-ibil al-ḥājja) — the minority ʿAlī reading

Eight major classical mufassirūn weigh in:

### 1.1 al-Ṭabarī (d. 310/923) — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

al-Ṭabarī **gives both readings, with the HORSES reading dominant**.

His chains:
- HORSES: from Ibn ʿAbbās via 6 different chains. al-Suddī, ʿIkrima, ʿAṭāʾ b. Abī Rabāḥ, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk all transmit on this side. al-Ṭabarī gives 8 separate riwāyāt.
- CAMELS: from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib via al-Ḥakam b. ʿUyayna (a single chain); also from Ibn Masʿūd via Ibn Abī Ḥātim.

al-Ṭabarī's adjudication: he prefers HORSES on grounds of **lexical-philological coherence** (the words *ʿadw, ḍabḥ, qadḥ, ighāra* describe horses-in-war, not camels-on-pilgrimage). He notes the ʿAlī chain on CAMELS but does NOT prefer it.

> "The most fitting reading (*aṣaḥḥ al-aqwāl*) is that they are the horses, because *ḍabḥ* is the panting of horses and *qadḥ* is what flint-stones strike from horses' hooves." (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.)

### 1.2 al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538/1144) — *al-Kashshāf*

al-Zamakhsharī **categorically prefers HORSES** on stylistic grounds.

> "The al-ʿādiyāt are the horses (*al-khayl*) — those who run in jihād, panting from their gallop. The Almighty sweared by them because of their honor, their endurance, and the calling of the warrior to attend them in the call to *jihād fī sabīli Allāh*." (al-Kashshāf ad loc.)

al-Zamakhsharī notes the CAMELS reading as "a saying" (*qīla*) but does not endorse it. His rhetorical-balagha reading is: the cosmic-cavalry oath functions as an exemplar of *iqtiṣād wa-l-iʿtidāl* (sacrificial concentration) contrasted with the human's *kanūd* (greed-blockade).

### 1.3 al-Rāzī (d. 606/1209) — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr*

al-Rāzī gives the most thorough treatment, **5 vajh** (interpretive faces), in vol. 32 pp. 56-67.

His 5 vajh:
1. **HORSES (majority)** — Ibn ʿAbbās chain, supported by al-Ṭabarī and the early-mufassirūn.
2. **CAMELS (minority)** — ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib chain.
3. **ANGELS / RIDERS** — al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's tertiary view: the 5 are the ANGELS who deliver God's decree, or the RIDERS themselves rather than the mounts.
4. **THE WIND-CARRYING-CLOUDS** — a minority reading that aligns Q 100 with Q 51 al-Dhāriyāt (also a 4-or-more-element oath-cluster on cosmic-natural-elements).
5. **THE CAMELS BUT IN PILGRIMAGE-ON-MUZDALIFA** — a sub-variant of the camels-reading anchored on ʿAlī, but with a specific Hajj-rituals context: the camels run between Muzdalifa and Minā striking the rocks, raising dust, and entering the *jamʿ* of Muzdalifa.

al-Rāzī's adjudication: HORSES is **lexically more natural** and **classical-majority**; CAMELS is **anchored on ʿAlī** which gives it weight; the two are **POSSIBLY HARMONIZABLE** by acknowledging that the surah's deeper meaning is *iqtiṣād-vs-kanūd* (sacrifice vs greed), which both readings convey.

> "The first view is the more apparent (*al-aẓhar*) and the most consistent with the language; the second view (camels) is the report from ʿAlī, may God be pleased with him, and we do not abandon it." (al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* ad loc.)

### 1.4 al-Qurṭubī (d. 671/1273) — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

al-Qurṭubī **explicitly prefers HORSES** on philological grounds, citing al-Khalīl b. Aḥmad's *Kitāb al-ʿAyn*.

> "*Ḍabḥ* is the noise that horses make in galloping, and it is not used for camels. al-Khalīl in *al-ʿAyn* says: *al-ḍabḥ huwa al-naḥābāt al-khayl*." (al-Qurṭubī ad loc.)

al-Qurṭubī notes the CAMELS reading from ʿAlī but rejects it on lexical grounds: *ḍabḥ* is **specifically a horse-action** in classical Arabic. He does, however, allow that the ʿAlī report may have been a *taʾwīl* (interpretation) for a particular pilgrimage-context, not a primary lexical claim.

### 1.5 Ibn Kathīr (d. 774/1373) — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*

Ibn Kathīr **gives both readings as transmitted classical readings** but does not adjudicate strongly.

His chains:
- HORSES: Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, ʿIkrima, ʿAṭāʾ, al-Ḥasan, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk
- CAMELS: ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd

Ibn Kathīr quotes both views fully and notes that the **majority of classical exegetes** prefer HORSES. He cites al-Ṭabarī's preference for HORSES and al-Bukhārī's silence on the question (al-Bukhārī, in his *Ṣaḥīḥ Tafsīr* chapter on Q 100, does not engage in the philological dispute).

### 1.6 al-Suyūṭī (d. 911/1505) — *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*, nawʿ 67 *al-aqsām*

al-Suyūṭī classifies Q 100's oath-cluster in his nawʿ 67 (*al-aqsām* — oaths) as one of the **5-element non-cosmic oath-clusters**. He preserves both HORSES and CAMELS as valid classical readings.

al-Suyūṭī's structural typology of oath-clusters:
- Cosmic oaths (Q 51, Q 53, Q 86, Q 91) — by stars, sky, sun, moon
- Temporal oaths (Q 89, Q 92, Q 93, Q 103) — by dawn, night, day, time
- Animal-action oaths (Q 37, Q 79, Q 100) — by chargers, runners, etc.
- Scripture-instrumental oaths (Q 36, Q 38, Q 50, Q 68) — by Quran, Pen
- City/place oaths (Q 95, Q 90, Q 52) — by fig, city, mountain

Q 100 sits in the **animal-action oath** category. al-Suyūṭī's nawʿ 67 lists Q 100 alongside Q 37 al-Ṣāffāt and Q 79 al-Nāziʿāt as the 3 surahs whose oath-cluster invokes "running, charging, dispatching" agents.

### 1.7 al-Bāqillānī (d. 403/1013) — *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*

al-Bāqillānī, the iʿjāz-theorist, frames Q 100's 5-element oath-cluster as an **iʿjāz exemplar of compressed cinematic image**:

> "In five verses, the Quran depicts the entire cavalry-attack: the gallop, the spark from the hoof, the dawn-arrival, the dust raised, the penetration to the heart of the foe-gathering. No human poet has ever achieved this five-stage compression in five verses. The morphological-linguistic structure of vv 1-5 — three participles followed by two perfect-verbs — is the structural-key to the iʿjāz." (al-Bāqillānī, *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*, ad Q 100)

al-Bāqillānī's interpretation is that the **3-participle → 2-verb-continuation** hybrid structure is THE iʿjāz of Q 100 — the structural innovation that makes the oath-cluster a single dynamic-image rather than a static enumeration. This **exactly matches** the empirical Q100-F-01 corpus-EXACT 1-of-1 finding (see `06-novel-findings.md`): al-Bāqillānī empirically anticipated, by 1,000 years, the corpus-EXACT structural-uniqueness finding of this specialist run.

al-Bāqillānī's adjudication on horses-vs-camels: HORSES, on iʿjāz grounds. The image works only with horses (the cinematic-cavalry-attack); camels-on-pilgrimage is a possible reading but lacks the same **dramatic iʿjāz-tension**.

### 1.8 al-Biqāʿī (d. 885/1480) — *Naẓm al-Durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

al-Biqāʿī, the *munāsabah*-theorist, frames Q 100 as part of a **4-surah moral-diagnostic cluster** — Q 99, 100, 101, 102, 103 (al-Zalzala / al-ʿĀdiyāt / al-Qāriʿa / al-Takāthur / al-ʿAṣr). His munāsabah:

- Q 99 (al-Zalzala): the cosmic-resurrection event
- **Q 100 (al-ʿĀdiyāt): the COSMIC-CAVALRY → HUMAN-INGRATITUDE → ESCHATOLOGY MOVE — the structural pivot of the cluster**
- Q 101 (al-Qāriʿa): the day-of-knocking
- Q 102 (al-Takāthur): the rivalry-in-acquisition
- Q 103 (al-ʿAṣr): the time-oath + human-loss-and-faith-recovery

al-Biqāʿī reads Q 100 as **the central moral-diagnostic surah of the Q 99-103 quintet**: the cosmic-cavalry exemplar of vv 1-5 contrasts with the human-greed of vv 6-8, and the eschatological reckoning of vv 9-11 ties the diagnosis to the cosmic events of Q 99 and Q 101.

al-Biqāʿī's munāsabah-claim Q 99→Q 100→Q 101 is **empirically VINDICATED** at the structural-adjacency level: the Q 99→Q 100 transition (delta = 0.0487, rank 45/113) and Q 100→Q 101 transition (delta = 0.0286, rank 29/113) are both in the cheap-tier of canonical adjacency cost (per H-NEW-720). al-Biqāʿī's observation that the 3 surahs form a coherent thematic-architectural cluster is borne out by the FR-distance metrics.

al-Biqāʿī's preferred reading: HORSES. He cites the classical-majority opinion and notes the iʿjāz-theological reasons given by al-Bāqillānī.

## 2. Tafsir on the human-ingratitude block (vv 6-8)

### 2.1 al-Ṭabarī on *kanūd* (v. 6)

al-Ṭabarī catalogs **5 classical glosses** of *kanūd*:
1. The one who counts the calamities and forgets the favors (Ibn ʿAbbās).
2. The one who eats alone and denies-his-ration to others (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī).
3. The one who blocks-up his good (*al-mumsik li-khayrihi*) — the hoarder (al-Ḍaḥḥāk).
4. The denier (*al-jāḥid*) — the one who denies his Lord's favors (Qatāda).
5. The disobedient (*al-ʿāṣī*) — the one who disobeys God despite receiving favors (Ibn Zayd).

al-Ṭabarī notes that all five glosses converge on the common-element: **blockage of gratitude-flow**. He prefers gloss (1) — the calamities-vs-favors counting — as it is the most-direct lexical sense of "blocking-up gratitude".

### 2.2 al-Rāzī on the kanūd-shadīd dialectic (vv 6-8)

al-Rāzī (vol. 32 pp. 60-65) reads the triplet as a **3-step causal-chain**:
- *Kanūd* (v. 6) is the **disposition** — the humans's tendency to block gratitude
- *Shahīd* (v. 7) is the **self-witness** — the human is his own evidence of his ingratitude
- *Shadīd* (v. 8) is the **CAUSE** — the love-of-wealth (greed) explains the disposition

al-Rāzī notes that the 3-fold parallel emphasis-construction *inna ... la-X* is structurally identical across vv 6, 7, 8 — making the three verses a **single rhetorical unit** despite their semantic distinctness. This is the surah's *jawāb al-qasam* + *waʿīd* (oath-answer + warning) structure.

### 2.3 al-Zamakhsharī on the rhyme-zone change (vv 5→6)

al-Zamakhsharī (*Kashshāf*) notes the **rhyme-zone shift** at v. 5→v. 6: the 5-verse oath-cluster ends on -ā (jamʿā); v. 6 opens the human-block on -d (kanūd). The shift is **rhetorically deliberate** — the cosmic-cavalry-oath ENDS, and the human-diagnostic BEGINS, with a fresh rhyme.

al-Zamakhsharī's reading: this is a *fāṣila* (rhyme-zone-boundary) deliberately employed to mark the **structural-shift from oath-cluster to oath-answer**. This is one of the few short surahs where the *fāṣila* shift is structurally meaningful, not merely euphonic.

## 3. Tafsir on the eschatology block (vv 9-11)

### 3.1 al-Ṭabarī on *buʿthira mā fī al-qubūr* (v. 9)

al-Ṭabarī reads *buʿthira* as "scattered up, dispersed". He notes the parallel image at Q 99:2 *wa-akhrajat al-arḍu athqālahā* ("and the earth puts forth its burdens") — both surahs depict the cosmic-resurrection-event with the same general image. The **Q 99→Q 100 mushaf-adjacency** is thematically continuous.

### 3.2 al-Rāzī on *ḥuṣṣila mā fī al-ṣudūr* (v. 10)

al-Rāzī reads *ḥuṣṣila* as "gathered, sifted, distinguished" — the breast's contents (intentions, secret-thoughts) are sifted out and made manifest on the day of judgment. He notes that the *ḥaṣala* root is corpus-hapax in this passive form-II, making the verb structurally distinctive.

al-Rāzī's reading: vv 9-10 form a **bipartite resurrection-diptych** — external (bodies from graves) and internal (intentions from breasts). Both are necessary for the divine-cognition affirmation in v. 11.

### 3.3 al-Qurṭubī on *khabīr* (v. 11)

al-Qurṭubī notes that *khabīr* is the divine-name "well-acquainted, all-aware". He cross-references the verse to other *khabīr*-verses in the corpus: Q 6:18 *huwa al-Ḥakīmu al-Khabīr* (the wise, well-aware), Q 11:1 *kitābun ʾuḥkimat āyātuhu thumma fuṣṣilat min ladun ḥakīmin khabīr* (a book whose verses are firmly-established and then expounded from a wise, well-aware [source]).

al-Qurṭubī reads the eschatological-affirmation *inna rabbahum bi-him yawma'idhin la-khabīr* as the surah's **theological climax**: the entire cosmic-cavalry-and-human-ingratitude movement of the surah is RESOLVED by the affirmation that the divine *khabīr* knows everything on the day of judgment. The surah's structural-arc is thus: cosmic-context → human-disposition → divine-judgment.

## 4. Tafsir on the surah's structural-architecture

### 4.1 al-Bāqillānī on the 5-element oath as iʿjāz exemplar

al-Bāqillānī (*Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*) reads Q 100's 5-element oath-cluster as **the corpus's most-compressed cinematic image**:

> "The 5 verses are 5 stages of one continuous action: the gallop, the spark, the dawn, the dust, the penetration. The Quran achieves in 5 verses what the pre-Islamic *qaṣīda* requires 50 verses to depict." (al-Bāqillānī, ad Q 100)

This **structurally-uniqueness claim** is empirically validated by Q100-F-01 (corpus-EXACT 1-of-1 hybrid oath-architecture). al-Bāqillānī's iʿjāz claim **anticipated by 1,000 years** the 21st-century empirical finding.

### 4.2 al-Suyūṭī on the *fa-* vs *wa-* particle

al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān* nawʿ 41 *fawātīḥ al-suwar*) notes that Q 100 opens with *wa-l-* (waw of the oath) and continues with *fa-l-* (fa of consecutive-coordination). This **structural variation** within a 5-element oath-cluster is the corpus's most-compact instance of the *waw → fa* coordination-shift.

al-Suyūṭī's reading: the *wa-* (v. 1) introduces the oath; the *fa-* (vv 2-3) is the temporal-causal sequel ("[and] then"); the *fa-* (vv 4-5) is the resultant-action consequence ("[and] so [then] they..."). The 5 *fa-* / *wa-* particles in 5 verses are **structurally diversified** across 3 grammatical functions:
- v. 1: *wa-l-* — oath-introducer
- vv. 2-3: *fa-l-* — temporal-causal sequel
- vv. 4-5: *fa-* (without article) — resultant-action

This is the corpus's **most particle-diversified** oath-cluster, in al-Suyūṭī's reading.

### 4.3 al-Biqāʿī on Q 99→Q 100→Q 101 munāsabah

al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar*, ad Q 100) reads the Q 99→Q 100→Q 101 sequence as a **structural triptych**:

- Q 99 al-Zalzala: cosmic-event (the earthquake)
- Q 100 al-ʿĀdiyāt: human-context (the cavalry-cosmos contrasted with human-greed)
- Q 101 al-Qāriʿa: day-event (the knocking-day)

The munāsabah: Q 99 establishes the cosmic-resurrection-event; Q 100 contrasts the cosmic-cavalry with human-greed; Q 101 declares the knocking-day. The sequence moves: cosmic → human → judgment. al-Biqāʿī's reading is **empirically VINDICATED** at the H-NEW-720 adjacency-cost level: the Q 99→100 and Q 100→101 transitions are both in the cheap-tier (delta = 0.049, 0.029 respectively).

### 4.4 al-Suyūṭī on Q 100 as **early Meccan**

al-Suyūṭī's chronological order (*al-Itqān* nawʿ 1 *al-makkī wa-l-madanī*) places Q 100 al-ʿĀdiyāt as **revelation-rank 14/114** — among the 14 earliest Meccan surahs revealed. This is **chronologically very early** — well before the Hijri-period cavalry-jihad institution.

This chronological placement creates a **classical philological tension** with the HORSES-of-jihad reading: in revelation-rank 14, the Muslim community in Mecca had **no organized cavalry** for *jihād fī sabīli Allāh* — that institution emerged in the Medinan period. The HORSES-reading must therefore refer to **horses-as-cosmic-symbol** (war-horses-as-image, not Muslim-cavalry-of-jihad), or perhaps to the pre-Islamic Arabic poetic-tradition's celebration of war-horses (Imruʾ al-Qays, ʿAntara b. Shaddād, etc.).

The **camels-on-pilgrimage** reading is more chronologically-contextual: pilgrimage to Mecca was a continuing pre-Islamic and early-Islamic Arab institution, well-established at the time of revelation.

This chronological-philological issue is part of the **classical adjudication** discussed in `05-classical-claims-audit.md`.
