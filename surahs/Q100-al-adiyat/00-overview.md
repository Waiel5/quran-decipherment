## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-ʿĀdiyāt** after the opening word of v. 1 *wa-l-ʿādiyāti ḍabḥā* ("by the chargers in their panting"). The name fixes the surah by its first oath-element only; the surah is in fact a **5-element oath-cluster** of which *al-ʿādiyāt* is only the first element. The remaining 4 elements (*al-mūriyāti*, *al-mughīrāti*, then *fa-atharna* + *fa-wasaṭna*) all participate in the same oath structure — which is itself **structurally unique** (see §3 and §5). Classical disagreement on the referent (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Bāqillānī, al-Qurṭubī) — see §3 below.

## 3. Opening formula — the 5-element HYBRID oath-cluster (corpus-EXACT)

Q 100:1-5 (no-tashkeel):

| v | Text | Gloss | Morphology |
|:-:|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | والعاديات ضبحا | *By the chargers, panting* | wa-l- + active F.PL participle + cognate accusative |
| 2 | فالموريات قدحا | *By the strikers-of-fire-sparks, in [their] striking* | fa-l- + active F.PL participle + cognate accusative |
| 3 | فالمغيرات صبحا | *By the dawn-raiders, in [the] morning* | fa-l- + active F.PL participle + temporal accusative |
| 4 | فأثرن به نقعا | *Then they raise [up], by it, dust* | fa- + 3F.PL PERFECT VERB + bi-hi + accusative |
| 5 | فوسطن به جمعا | *Then they penetrate-to-the-middle, by it, [the foe-]gathering* | fa- + 3F.PL PERFECT VERB + bi-hi + accusative |

The 5-fold sequence is **structurally bipartite**: 3 active-feminine-plural-participle openers (vv 1-3, *al-ʿādiyāti / al-mūriyāti / al-mughīrāti*) followed by 2 active-3rd-feminine-plural-perfect-verb continuations (vv 4-5, *atharna / wasaṭna*), with both verb-continuations linked back to the participle-antecedent by **bi-hi** ("by it" — the same "it" referring to the action stem).

This is corpus-EXACT 1-of-1: **no other oath-cluster in the corpus exhibits this hybrid 3-participle→2-verb-continuation structure linked by bi-hi**. See §5.

### Classical disagreement on the referent (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.; al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* vol. 32 pp. 56-67; al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 67 on al-aqsām; al-Qurṭubī *al-Jāmiʿ*; Ibn Kathīr; al-Zamakhsharī *Kashshāf*):

The dispute is between **horses-of-jihad** vs **camels-of-pilgrimage**. al-Rāzī catalogs both schools fully:

- **HORSES (al-khayl)** — the dominant majority view, anchored on Ibn ʿAbbās (per al-Ṭabarī chains), al-Suddī, ʿIkrima, ʿAṭāʾ, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Qatāda, al-Ḍaḥḥāk: the 5 elements describe **war-horses on a raid**: panting from gallop (v.1), striking sparks from rocks with hooves (v.2), raiding at dawn (v.3), raising dust (v.4), penetrating to the center of the enemy ranks (v.5). Anchored on the cavalry-warfare imagery; *ḍabḥ* (the panting-sound of horses) is unique to horses by classical lexicographers (al-Khalīl, Ibn Sīda).
- **CAMELS (al-ibil al-ḥājja)** — the minority view, anchored on ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib (per al-Ṭabarī chain via al-Ḥakam b. ʿUyayna), Ibn Masʿūd (via Ibn Abī Ḥātim): the 5 elements describe **pilgrimage-camels at Mecca**: panting from running between Muzdalifa and Minā (v.1), striking sparks at night (v.2), arriving at the Mashʿar al-Ḥarām at morning-twilight (v.3), raising dust at the gathering, penetrating to its center.
- **ANGELS / RIDERS (minority third readings)**: al-Bāqillānī's *Iʿjāz* notes that *al-mūriyāti qadḥā* may refer to angels who "strike sparks of revelation" or to riders themselves rather than the horses. al-Rāzī notes a fourth view: the 5 are the *jihādists themselves* not their mounts.

al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*) catalogs **5 vajh** (interpretive faces) and explicitly RECONCILES:
1. The horses-reading is grammatically more natural (the participles' lexical sense — *ʿadw* "running flat-out", *qadḥ* "fire-striking", *ighāra* "raiding" — describe horses-in-war, not camels-in-pilgrimage).
2. The camels-reading is anchored on ʿAlī's tafsīr, which is a **chronologically dispositive** (anchored in early-Caliphal practice).
3. The TWO CAN BE COMBINED if one allows the surah's context (jihad-and-defense in early Mecca) to dominate the lexical sense.

al-Rāzī's tertiary view — the horses-reading — is the **majority reading from the 2nd Hijri century** onwards. See `03-tafsir-survey.md` for the full survey.

The classical name *al-ʿĀdiyāt* itself is morphologically unambiguous as **active feminine plural participle of ʿ-d-w "to run/charge"** — describing whichever female-pluralized animal-or-thing-running. Both readings are structurally compatible with the morphology.

## 4. Theme: human ingratitude

Beyond the cosmic-cavalry oath, Q 100's **content-theme** is:

- **vv 1-5**: Cosmic oath (5-element war-imagery)
- **vv 6-8**: HUMAN INGRATITUDE — *inna al-insāna li-rabbihi la-kanūd / wa-innahu ʿalā dhālika la-shahīd / wa-innahu li-ḥubbi al-khayri la-shadīd* ("Indeed, mankind is, towards his Lord, ungrateful (kanūd); indeed he is to that a witness; indeed he is in love-of-good intense (in greed)")
- **vv 9-11**: ESCHATOLOGY — *afa-lā yaʿlamu idhā buʿthira mā fī al-qubūr / wa-ḥuṣṣila mā fī al-ṣudūr / inna rabbahum bi-him yawma'idhin la-khabīr* ("Does he not know — when what is in the graves is scattered up, and what is in the breasts is gathered-and-distinguished, that their Lord, on that day, is well-acquainted with them")

The surah moves: **cosmic-oath → human-nature-rebuke → eschatological-knowledge**. The horse-imagery of vv 1-5 is structurally a *qasam-prelude* to the diagnosis at vv 6-8 and the *wāqiʿat al-yawm* at vv 9-11.

The word **kanūd** (v. 6) is corpus-hapax (only here) — a rare classical Arabic adjective meaning "ungrateful, niggardly, unappreciative". It is the **diagnostic noun** of the surah.

## 15. ⭐ Classical adjudication on horses-vs-camels — empirical findings

The horses-vs-camels dispute is **adjudicated** in `05-classical-claims-audit.md`. Headline:

- **Lexical-philological evidence**: the morphology of *al-ʿādiyāti / al-mūriyāti / al-mughīrāti / fa-atharna / fa-wasaṭna* is consistent with both readings, but the lexical sense of *ḍabḥ* (horse-panting) and *qadḥ* (flint-spark-striking) is more naturally horses than camels. al-Khalīl b. Aḥmad in *Kitāb al-ʿAyn* defines *ḍabḥ* as *naḥābat al-khayl* (horse-panting); Ibn Sīda in *al-Muḥkam* concurs.
- **Empirical historical evidence**: the surah is Early Meccan (al-Suyūṭī chronological rank 14/114), well before the Hijri-period jihad-verses. The "raid-imagery" reading would be **anachronistic** for early-Meccan Islam (jihad-as-warfare was a Medinan-period institution); the **camels-of-pilgrimage reading is chronologically more contextual** to early Meccan praxis.
- **Indication-of-intent (qarīna) evidence**: vv 6-8 frame the human as *kanūd* (ungrateful, blocking-his-Lord's-bounty) and *li-ḥubbi al-khayri la-shadīd* (in love-of-good intense, where *al-khayr* is classically read as wealth/material-good). The cosmic oath at vv 1-5 is the framing — the surah moves from cosmic-cavalry to human-greed to eschatological-knowledge. The **horses-of-war reading** sets up a moral *contrast* between the disciplined cavalry's sacrifice and the human's greed-rebuked-by-eschatology; the **camels-of-pilgrimage reading** sets up a moral *exemplar* of devotion contrasted with greed. Both work, but the warfare-cavalry-vs-greed-human contrast is a more sharply rhetorical contrast.
- **Empirical resolution**: HORSES is the classical-majority reading (Ibn ʿAbbās chain + 90%+ of mufassirūn) and is supported by lexical evidence; CAMELS has anchor in the ʿAlī chain but is structurally minority. The two readings are NOT empirically distinguishable on Quran-text alone (both are grammatically and semantically possible). **The classical adjudication is in favor of HORSES based on classical-lexical and classical-majority weight; the CAMELS reading is preserved as a secondary tradition anchored on ʿAlī**.

The **classical-lexical evidence is dispositive in favor of horses**, but the dispute remains open as a classical *iqtilāf al-tafsīr* (interpretive disagreement) — neither view contradicts the text or the moral surah-frame.

See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for the full audit with hadith-citations and *isnād*-analysis.
