## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Taghābun** after the eponymous noun *al-taghābun* in v.9: *dhālika yawmu al-taghābun* — "that is the Day of Mutual Loss-and-Gain." The Arabic *taghābun* is the verbal noun (maṣdar) of form-VI reciprocal *taghābana*, derived from the root **g-b-n (غ-ب-ن)** ("loss, defraudment, disappointment in trade").

This is a **CORPUS-EXACT-HAPAX root** (Q064-F-03 CONFIRMED): the QAC v0.4 root index records EXACTLY 1 attestation of root g-b-n in the entire 6,236-verse Quranic corpus — at Q 64:9 word #7 — and the surface-form-substring scan returns the same singleton. This makes Q 64 the corpus's only *taghābun*-bearing surah by definition; the surah's eponymous identity is built on a single hapax token.

al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī, *Mufradāt al-Qurʾān*, root g-b-n entry, glosses: "the recognized lexical use is FINANCIAL or DEFRAUDMENT (ghabn al-bayʿ — loss in a sale-transaction); MENTAL or JUDGMENTAL deficiency (ghibn — lapse of opinion); the form-VI reciprocal *taghābana* signifies mutuality of action — two parties causing or making manifest each other's loss." The Quranic *taghābun* is read as eschatological: on the Day of Judgment, the mutual loss of unbelievers (who lose paradise) and gain of believers (who lose hell) becomes manifest — the trade of the entire world for either heaven or fire is mutually unmasked.

Classical disagreement on the gloss of *yawm al-taghābun*:
- **al-Ṭabarī** (ad Q 64:9, citing Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda): *yawm ghabn ahl al-jannati ahla al-nār* — "the day the people of paradise show up the people of hell" (one-directional manifestation of loss).
- **al-Rāghib al-Iṣfahānī**: form-VI reciprocal — both parties make manifest a mutual loss.
- **Maʿārif al-Qurʾān** (Mufti Shafīʿ): synthesis — the believers reveal what the unbelievers lost; the unbelievers reveal what the believers gained; the day Allah created two abodes for everyone (paradise + hell) and at the resurrection the two abodes are exchanged according to actual deeds; the bitter regret extends to believers too, who regret having performed fewer good deeds than they could have.
- **Qurṭubī** (cited via Maʿārif): all believers will feel a sense of loss on Judgment Day for shortcomings in their obligations — making *yawm al-taghābun* equivalent to *yawm al-ḥasrah* ("Day of Bitter Regret", Q 19:39).

## 3. Chronology classification — the contested case (Q064-F-04 CONFIRMED audit)

Q 64 is one of a handful of surahs where Meccan/Medinan classification is **non-trivially contested**. Modern editions (Tanzil 1924 Cairo, Nöldeke 1860/1909) and the majority classical position (al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*) assign **Medinan** (Tanzil rev #108, Nöldeke #93). But the early classical record preserves a **non-trivial dissent**:

> "**مدنية** — قال **عطاء** هي **مكية** إلا ثلاث آيات من قوله: 'يا أيها الذين آمنوا إن من أزواجكم وأولادكم...' إلى آخرهن."
> al-Baghawī, *Maʿālim al-Tanzīl*, ad Q 64:1.
> ("Medinan — ʿAṭāʾ said it is Meccan except for three verses, beginning 'O you who have believed, indeed among your wives and your children...' to their end.")

al-Ṭabarī (ad Q 64:14) preserves the same dissent through a fuller isnād: *Ibn Ḥumayd → Salama → Muḥammad b. Isḥāq → an associate → ʿAṭāʾ ibn Yasār*: "*nazalat sūrat al-taghābun kulluhā bi-makka, illā hāʾulāʾi al-āyāt... nazalat fī ʿAwf b. Mālik al-Ashjaʿī... bi-l-madīna*" — "the entire Surat al-Taghābun was revealed in Mecca, except these verses [vv. 14-16] which were revealed in Medina concerning ʿAwf b. Mālik al-Ashjaʿī." The asbāb-anchor for v.14 is **specifically Late-Meccan / Hijra-transition**: Meccan converts blocked from emigrating by their wives and children. This places the contested verses precisely at the Mecca-Medina hinge.

## 10. Quick content structure

- **vv. 1-4**: TAWḤĪD opening — *yusabbiḥu li-llāh* glorification → divider creation (believer/disbeliever) → cosmic-creation + form-shaping → divine omniscience.
- **vv. 5-6**: warning to disbelievers via the past-nations *nabaʾ alladhīna kafarū min qabl* formula; rejection of prophetic mortality (*abasharun yahdūnanā*).
- **vv. 7-10**: resurrection-denial refuted by oath (*qul balā wa-rabbī la-tubʿathunna*) → command to believe in Allah, Messenger, and *al-nūr alladhī anzalnā* (the Light We sent down — the Quran) → ESCHATOLOGICAL TURN: *yawm yajmaʿukum li-yawm al-jamʿ ḏālika yawm al-taghābun* (v.9, the eponym) → the believer's reward: gardens beneath which rivers flow + *al-fawz al-ʿaẓīm* → the disbeliever's punishment: companions of the fire.
- **vv. 11-13**: theology of calamity and trust — *mā aṣāba min muṣībatin illā bi-idhni llāh* (v.11) → command to obey God and the Messenger → tawḥīd-creed *Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa wa-ʿalā llāhi fa-l-yatawakkali al-muʾminūn* (v.13).
- **vv. 14-16**: the FAMILY-AS-TRIAL passage — *yā ayyuhā al-ladhīna āmanū inna min azwājikum wa-awlādikum ʿaduwwan lakum* (v.14) → wealth-and-children as *fitna* (v.15) → *ittaqū llāha mā istaṭaʿtum* (v.16, the abrogating-clause for Q 3:102).
- **vv. 17-18**: closing financial-spiritual exhortation — *qarḍ ḥasan* (the goodly loan to God) → closing tawḥīd in pair-name *ʿālim al-ghaybi wa-l-shahādati al-ʿAzīzu al-Ḥakīm* (the unseen-and-witnessed Knower; the Mighty, the Wise).

| v | Theme | Locked or hinge? |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 14 | Family as enemy + forgive | Hijra-transition asbāb |
