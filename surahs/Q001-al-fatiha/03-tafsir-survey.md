# Q 1 al-Fātiḥa — Classical Tafsir Survey

## 1. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

**Source on disk**:
- English translation: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf`
- Arabic raw (OpenITI): `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt` (25,667 lines)

al-Suyūṭī's *Itqān* is a corpus of *ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* topics rather than a verse-by-verse tafsir; he treats al-Fātiḥa across multiple *anwāʿ* (sections).

### 1.1 Asbāb al-Nuzūl & status (Itqān, *nawʿ* 1: Meccan vs Medinan)

Raw lines 806-829: al-Suyūṭī surveys the Meccan-vs-Medinan disagreement on Q 1. He lists **four positions**:

> "سورة الفاتحة: الأكثرون على أنها مكية بل ورد أنها أول ما نزل ... وقد روى الواحدي والثعلبي ... عن علي بن أبي طالب قال نزلت فاتحة الكتاب بمكة من كنز تحت العرش. واشتهر عن مجاهد القول بأنها مدنية ... وذهب بعضهم إلى أنها نزلت مرتين مرة بمكة ومرة بالمدينة مبالغة في تشريفها. وفيها قول رابع أنها نزلت نصفين نصفها بمكة ونصفها بالمدينة حكاه أبو الليث السمرقندي."

(Source: `suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt` lines 806-829.) Translation summary: (1) the majority hold Q 1 is Meccan, with a tradition from ʿAlī that it was revealed *min kanzin taḥta l-ʿarsh* ("from a treasure beneath the Throne") in Mecca; (2) Mujāhid (citing al-Wāḥidī and al-Thaʿlabī as transmitters) held it Medinan; (3) some held it was revealed *twice* (once in Mecca, once in Medina), as honour-emphasis; (4) some held that *half* was revealed in Mecca and *half* in Medina (ascribed to Abū l-Layth al-Samarqandī). al-Suyūṭī favours the Meccan-priority view, citing Q 15:87 (*sabʿan min al-mathānī*) — Sūrat al-Ḥijr is unanimously Meccan, so a citation of *al-mathānī* there pre-supposes the prior revelation of al-Fātiḥa.

**Project locked position**: per `data/revelation-order.csv`, Q 1 = revelation order 5 (Tanzil Egyptian Standard, after al-ʿAlaq, al-Qalam, al-Muzzammil, al-Muddaththir). Nöldeke's reconstruction places it at order 48 (mid-Early-Meccan). Both place Q 1 as Meccan. The half-Meccan/half-Medinan position has no scholarly defenders today.

### 1.2 Fadāʾil al-Fātiḥa (Itqān, *nawʿ* 14 and *nawʿ* on khawāṣṣ al-suwar)

Raw lines 2255-2470: al-Suyūṭī compiles ḥadīth on Q 1's virtues, including:

- *Hiya umm al-Qurʾān wa-hiya fātiḥat al-Kitāb wa-hiya al-sabʿu al-mathānī* — ḥadīth attributed to the Prophet, transmitted via Abū Hurayra, with Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī cited as collector. (`suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt` line 3300-3301.)
- The healing/protection chain (*shifāʾ min al-summ* — "cure from poison") — al-Bayhaqī in al-*Shuʿab* via Anas, raw line 2417.
- al-Fātiḥa's status as the last of three texts of supreme power: al-Fātiḥa + Āyat al-Kursī (Q 2:255) + the closing of al-Baqara (Q 2:285-286). Raw line 2443-2444.

## 2. al-Zarkashī, *al-Burhān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zarkashi-al-burhan-fi-ulum-al-quran.pdf` (29 MB PDF, no extracted Arabic raw on disk yet).

al-Zarkashī's *al-Burhān* is the structural-rhetorical predecessor of al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān*; many of al-Suyūṭī's chapters are condensations of al-Zarkashī. al-Zarkashī's signature contribution to Q 1 reading is in the **iltifāt** chapter and the **munāsabāt al-ʾāyāt** chapter:

- **Iltifāt** (al-Burhān, *nawʿ al-iltifāt*, vol. 3, ch. 39 in standard editions; not extracted to OpenITI raw on disk — **AWAITING TXT EXTRACTION**). al-Zarkashī treats Q 1:5 as the paradigmatic 3rd→2nd-person shift. Extracted catalog at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/abdel-haleem-iltifat-catalog.md` includes Q 1:5 in Category I-3 (3rd→2nd) per Abdel Haleem's rendition of al-Zarkashī.
- **Munāsabāt** (al-Burhān, *nawʿ al-munāsaba*). al-Zarkashī is one of the key carriers of the *ʿilm al-munāsabāt* chain (al-Naysābūrī → al-Rāzī → al-Zarkashī → al-Biqāʿī). His treatment of Q 1's internal *munāsaba* (verse-to-verse coherence) and Q 1's *munāsaba* with Q 2 is the seed of al-Biqāʿī's full elaboration. See `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md` for the chain history.

## 3. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

**Source on disk**:
- PDF: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf` (129 MB, 738 pp.)
- Arabic raw (OpenITI): `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` (158,479 lines)
- Alt raw (ShamAY): `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.ShamAY.raw.txt` (162,262 lines)

al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar* is the magnum opus of the *ʿilm al-munāsabāt* tradition. The Q 1 section runs from raw line 40 to roughly line 970.

### 3.1 al-Biqāʿī's controlling principle (raw lines 47-65)

al-Biqāʿī cites his teacher Abū l-Faḍl al-Mashdālī al-Maghribī al-Bijāʾī al-Mālikī. The principle:

> "الأمر الكلي المفيد لعرفان مناسبات الآيات في جميع القرآن هو أنك تنظر الغرض الذي سبقت له السورة، وتنظر ما يحتاج إليه ذلك الغرض من المقدمات وتنظر إلى مراتب تلك المقدمات في القرب والبعد من المطلوب ..."

("The universal principle for understanding inter-verse coherence in the entire Qurʾān is that you look at the *gharaḍ* — the purpose for which the surah was sent — and you look at what that purpose requires of preliminary premises, and at the ordering of those premises in proximity to the goal ...") This is al-Biqāʿī's *gharaḍ-method*: every surah has a single dominant purpose, and verse-to-verse *munāsaba* is read as preliminary→goal sequencing.

### 3.2 al-Biqāʿī on Q 1's *gharaḍ*

Raw lines 74-95: al-Biqāʿī states that al-Fātiḥa's *gharaḍ* is:

> "إثبات استحقاق الله تعالى لجميع المحامد وصفا الكمال، واختصاصه بملك الدنيا والآخرة، وباستحقاق العبادة والاستعانة، بالسؤال في المن بإلزام صراط الفائزين والإنقاذ من طريق الهالكين مختصا بذلك كله، ومدار ذلك كله مراقبة العباد لربهم، لإفراده بالعبادة، فهو مقصود الفاتحة بالذات وغيره وسائل إليه."

("Establishing God's exclusive entitlement to all praise and attributes of perfection; His exclusivity in the dominion of this world and the next; His exclusive entitlement to worship and to be sought-from-aid; the petition for the path of the victorious and rescue from the path of the perished. The pivot of all this is the servants' watchfulness over their Lord and His exclusivity in worship — that is the *maqṣūd* of al-Fātiḥa by-essence, and all else is means to it.")

This is al-Biqāʿī's compressed reading: Q 1 = *ʿibāda + tawḥīd al-rubūbiyya + duʿāʾ*. He treats every verse as a sub-goal serving this central *gharaḍ*.

### 3.3 al-Biqāʿī's basmala numerology (raw line 122)

> "وكونها تسعة عشر حرفا خطية وثمانية عشر لفظية إشارة إلى أنها دوافع النقمة من النار التي أصحابها تسعة عشر، وجوالب للرحمة بركعات الصلوات الخمس وركعة الوتر اللاتي من أعظم العبادات الكبرى."

("Its being 19 letters orthographically and 18 phonetically points to its repelling the punishment of the Fire whose keepers are 19 [Q 74:30 *ʿalayhā tisʿat ʿashar*], and to its drawing-down mercy through the rak'as of the five prayers plus the witr.") This is the **classical 19-letters-of-the-basmala observation**, well-attested across pre-modern tafsir, centuries before any Code-19 movement. **Empirical status**: the orthographic 19-count is rules-tuple-stable (Mashriqi Uthmani, hamza-on-alif counted as one letter, *Allāh* + ligatures handled per QAC). It is not a "Code-19" claim per se — al-Biqāʿī does not propose any divisibility-of-the-Quran-by-19 thesis. The connection to Q 74:30's *tisʿat ʿashar* (the 19 angelic guardians of Hell) is an **interpretive overlay**, not a numerical proof. See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` and Khalifa-1989 for the modern Code-19 trajectory and its falsification.

### 3.4 al-Biqāʿī on the basmala→Q 1→Quran nesting (raw lines 117-125)

> "نسبة البسملة من الفاتحة نسبة الفاتحة من القرآن صدرت بها الفاتحة كما صدر القرآن بالفاتحة... هو إجمال لتفصيل الفاتحة كما أن الفاتحة إجمال تفصيل القرآن."

("The relation of the basmala to al-Fātiḥa is [parallel to] the relation of al-Fātiḥa to the Qurʾān: al-Fātiḥa is opened by it as the Qurʾān is opened by al-Fātiḥa; ... [the basmala] is the summary of the detail of al-Fātiḥa as al-Fātiḥa is the summary of the detail of the Qurʾān.") This is the **fractal-nesting argument** — three nested levels: basmala ⊂ Q 1 ⊂ Qurʾān, each layer a compression of the next. al-Biqāʿī gives this as a structural-theological observation; the project's [[h-new-111-fisher-rao-mushaf|H-NEW-111]] root-distribution analysis provides an empirical correlate (Q 1 is closer to the corpus centroid than typical, see §1 of `02-content-analysis.md`).

### 3.5 al-Biqāʿī on Q 1's *munāsaba* with the muʿawwidhatān (raw lines 89-100)

> "ومن هنا تعرف مناسبة المعوذتين بالفاتحة. ولما افتتح التعوذ بالهمزة أشارة إلى ابتداء الخلق وختم بالميم إيماء إلى المعاد جعلت البسملة كلها للمعاد لأبتدائها بحرف شفوي، وختام أول كلماتها ومآخرها بآخر إشارة إلى أن الرجوع إليه..."

al-Biqāʿī links Q 1 to Q 113-114 (the muʿawwidhatān) via a letter-symbolic argument — *taʿawwudh* opens with hamza and closes with mīm; the basmala plays a counterpart role at the head. **Empirical correlate**: the [[h-new-111-fisher-rao-mushaf|H-NEW-111]] FR-distance row places Q 112 and Q 113 / Q 114 / Q 110 / Q 108 as Q 1's nearest neighbours in root-distribution; the al-Biqāʿī *munāsaba* of "Fātiḥa-bracket-mu'awwidhatayn" is at law-strength a **lexical-doxological pole**, distance 0.34-0.36 vs Q 1's mean distance to the corpus 0.78. al-Biqāʿī did not have the matrix; he had the structural intuition. Both converge.

### 3.6 al-Biqāʿī on Q 1 → Q 2 *munāsaba* (raw lines 950-980)

al-Biqāʿī's transition from Q 1 to Q 2 (the standard *fātiḥa→baqara* munāsaba): Q 1 ends in *ihdinā al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm* / *anʿamta ʿalayhim*, asking for guidance; Q 2 opens *Alif Lām Mīm. Dhālika al-kitābu lā rayba fīhi hudan li-l-muttaqīn* — "this is the Book in which there is no doubt, a guidance for the godfearing." al-Biqāʿī reads this as direct answer-to-petition: "you asked for guidance, here is the Book of guidance." This is the strongest classical *munāsaba* for the Q 1→Q 2 transition.

**Empirical paradox**: this is *the most expensive single canonical adjacency in the entire corpus* (7.5 % of the FR-residual, [[h-new-720-canonical-adjacency-cost|H-NEW-720]]). Root-distribution-wise Q 1 and Q 2 are *far apart* (FR distance 1.18, in Q 1's 110th-rank-distance-out-of-113). al-Biqāʿī's *munāsaba* is **semantic-thematic**, not lexical — the Q 1→Q 2 link is the petition→book link, not a vocabulary continuation. The 7.5 % cost is the *price the mushaf pays* to make this thematic *munāsaba* canonical. Both al-Biqāʿī and the empirical decomposition agree: Q 1→Q 2 is held together by purpose, not by lexicon.

## 4. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr)

**Source on disk**:
- Arabic raw (OpenITI): `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` (267,943 lines)
- Extracts: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-99names-extract.md`; `razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md`; `razi-muqattaat-surah-qaf.md`.

al-Rāzī's commentary on Q 1 is **his most exuberant performance**. He famously claims the surah can yield "ten thousand questions" (raw line 65: *yumkinu an yustanbaṭa min fawāʾidihā wa-nafāʾisihā ʿasharatu ālāfi masʾalatin* — "from its benefits and gems, ten thousand questions can be derived"). What follows is over 200 pages of philosophical-rationalist commentary. Highlights:

### 4.1 al-Rāzī on the asmāʾ of al-Fātiḥa (raw lines 5625-5800)

al-Rāzī enumerates the names with multi-layered etymological-philosophical explanations. The most substantively novel section is **the third reason for the title *Umm al-Kitāb***:

> "أن المقصود من جميع العلوم: إما معرفة عزة الربوبية، أو معرفة ذلة العبودية. فقوله: الحمد لله رب العالمين الرحمن الرحيم مالك يوم الدين يدل على أنه هو الإله المستولي على كل أحوال الدنيا والآخرة، ثم من قوله: إياك نعبد وإياك نستعين إلى آخر السورة يدل على ذل العبودية..."

(Raw lines 5650-5660.) al-Rāzī divides Q 1 into two halves on the *ʿizzat al-rubūbiyya / dhillat al-ʿubūdiyya* axis: verses 1-4 = lordship-glory (third-person), verses 5-7 = servant-humility (second-person). This is **al-Rāzī's most influential structural division of Q 1**. Note: it does **not coincide** with the popular "1-3 God's half / 4-7 servant's half" divider that goes back to the *qasamtu al-ṣalāh* hadith (Muslim 395; Tirmidhī 2953); al-Rāzī's pivot is at the v 4 / v 5 boundary (the iltifāt), Muslim's pivot is at the v 3 / v 4 boundary.

**Both pivots have empirical correlates**:
- v 3 / v 4 pivot = thematic-pivot (lordship-attributes → eschatological-judgement → petition).
- v 4 / v 5 pivot = grammatical-iltifāt pivot (3rd→2nd person).

These are two real but *off-set* pivots of the surah's structure (see `02-content-analysis.md` §2). The classical disagreement reflects a real bi-axial pivot, not an interpretive arbitrariness.

### 4.2 al-Rāzī's missing-7-letters claim (raw lines 5784-5810)

> "هذه السورة لم يحصل فيها سبعة من الحروف، وهي الثاء، والجيم والخاء، والزاي، والشين، والظاء، والفاء، والسبب فيه أن هذه الحروف السبعة مشعرة بالعذاب..."

al-Rāzī asserts 7 letters are absent from Q 1 — **ث ج خ ز ش ظ ف** — and reads this as a sign-cluster pointing to seven gates of Hell (Q 15:43-44). He gives a thematic etymology for each missing letter: ث → *thubūr* (perdition, Q 25:14); ج → *jahannam*; خ → *khizy* (humiliation); ز → *zaqqūm*; ش → *shaqāwa* (wretchedness, Q 11:106); ظ → *ẓulluhū* + *laẓā* (Q 70:15); ف → *firāq*.

**Computational verification** (`quran-no-tashkeel.json`, basic 28-letter alphabet, Q 1 letters present = 23 of 28): the missing letters are **exactly** ث ج خ ز ش ظ ف — al-Rāzī's list with no exceptions. This is rules-tuple-stable across no-tashkeel, min-tashkeel, full-tashkeel, and Uthmani-consonantal variants. See `01-empirical-profile.md` §7 and the audit in `05-classical-claims-audit.md`.

This is one of the cleanest cases of a classical letter-counting claim that **survives mechanical verification at every rules-tuple cell**. The thematic-etymological overlay is post-hoc and untestable; the empirical letter-absence is testable and confirmed.

### 4.3 al-Rāzī on the *iltifāt* and the dual structure (raw lines 3204-3290)

al-Rāzī repeatedly invokes the v 5 *iltifāt* as the surah's hinge. His treatment overlaps with and extends al-Zarkashī's *Burhān* iltifāt-chapter. al-Rāzī adds a **logical-rationalist** read: the third-person addresses build a knowledge-base about God (*existence, lordship, mercy, judgement*); the second-person address marks the moment when that knowledge produces an addressable You. Verse 5 is the *epistemic-to-pragmatic* turn.

### 4.4 al-Rāzī on the 4 *al-ḥamd* opener-cluster (raw lines 5860-5890)

> "ثم إنه تعالى افتتح سورا أربعة بعد هذه السورة بقوله: الحمد لله ... فالمذكور في أول سورة الأنعام كأنه قسم من أقسام ما هو مذكور في أول سورة الفاتحة..."

al-Rāzī treats the four post-Q1 *al-ḥamd*-openers (Q 6 al-Anʿām, Q 18 al-Kahf, Q 34 Sabaʾ, Q 35 Fāṭir) as **sub-cases (*aqsām*) of Q 1's *rabb al-ʿālamīn***. Each post-Q1 opener narrows from "Lord of the worlds" to a specific lordship: Q 6 = Lord of heavens-earth-light-darkness; Q 18 = Lord-who-sent-down-the-Book; Q 34 = Lord of what-is-in-heavens-and-earth; Q 35 = Lord of the angels (*Fāṭir al-samawāt*). This is one of al-Rāzī's clearest **al-ḥāmidāt-cluster** structural arguments and an early classical correlate of the modern cluster-analysis of Quranic surahs.

## 5. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

**Source on disk**:
- Arabic JSONs (per-verse): `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/1/{1,2,3,4,5,6,7}.json`
- Each verse has its own JSON with full Arabic text. Verse 1 = 21,881 chars; Verse 2 = 21,346 chars; Verse 4 = 23,808 chars; Verse 6 = 22,300 chars; Verse 7 = 25,433 chars. (Verse 3 is short — 3,318 chars — because al-Ṭabarī defers to his treatment in v 1.)

Highlights:

- **Verse 1** (`1.json`): al-Ṭabarī opens with the sunna-of-divine-naming reading: *adda allāhu nabiyyahu Muḥammadan bi-taʿlīmihi taqdīma dhikri asmāʾihi al-ḥusnā amāma jamīʿi afʿālihi* — "God taught his Prophet Muhammad to place mention of His names before all his actions, and made what He taught him into a *sunna* for all creatures to follow in opening their speech."
- **Verse 2** (`2.json`): al-Ṭabarī treats *al-ḥamd li-llāh* as **shukr** (gratitude) restricted to God — he glosses *al-ḥamd* as "thanks exclusive to God, exclusive of every-thing-else-worshipped, and of every-thing-He-created."
- **Verse 4** (`4.json`): the **qirāʾāt diversity** is opened up in detail. al-Ṭabarī enumerates: *Mālik* (Hafs), *Malik* (Warsh / madanī), and *Mālika* (with fatḥa on the kāf, an outlier reading). He surveys the chains for each.
- **Verse 6** (`6.json`): al-Ṭabarī glosses *ihdinā al-ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm* as *waffiqnā li-l-thabāt ʿalayhi* — "grant us the steadfastness to remain on it" — invoking Ibn ʿAbbās via Abū Rawq al-Ḍaḥḥāk. This is the **mainstream Sunnī gloss**: Q 1:6 is not "guide [us who are unguided]" but "keep [us, who are already on the path] steadfast."
- **Verse 7** (`7.json`): the *al-maghḍūb ʿalayhim* and *al-ḍāllīn* identifications. al-Ṭabarī cites the dominant reading (Yahūd = wrath-incurring, Naṣārā = astray) via multiple Companion-chains (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAdī b. Ḥātim).

al-Ṭabarī's commentary on Q 1 is **massive in volume** (≈124 KB across 7 verses) and serves as the foundational gloss-archive for almost every later commentator.

## 6. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**Source on disk**:
- Arabic JSONs (per-verse): `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/1/{1-7}.json`
- English: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/1/{1-7}.json`

Ibn Kathīr's Q 1:1 entry (`1.json`) opens with a comprehensive **asmāʾ + status survey** that overlaps al-Suyūṭī's:

- Lists 12 names with hadith attestation.
- Records the disagreement on Meccan/Medinan: *hiya makkiyya, qālahu Ibn ʿAbbās wa-Qatāda wa-Abū l-ʿĀliya, wa-qīla madaniyya, qālahu Abū Hurayra wa-Mujāhid wa-ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasār wa-l-Zuhrī*.
- Ibn Kathīr's own preferred view: *al-awwalu ashbahu* — "the first [Meccan] is more likely" — citing Q 15:87.
- States Q 1 = 7 verses *bilā khilāf* (without disagreement on count), but notes the **basmala** controversy (whether it counts as v1).
- Gives *kalimātuhā khams wa-ʿishrūna kalima wa-ḥurūfuhā miʾatun wa-thalāthata ʿashara ḥarfan* — "its words are 25 and its letters 113." **Note**: this is the *non-basmala-counted* count. Under the project's basmala-counted-only-in-Q1 default, Q 1 = 29 words / 143 letters.
- Records **Ibn Masʿūd's mushaf** controversy (Q 1 absent from his copy): *qīla li-Ibn Masʿūd: limā lam taktub al-Fātiḥa fī muṣḥafika? Fa-qāla: law katabtuhā la-katabtuhā fī awwali kulli sūra* — "It was said to Ibn Masʿūd: why did you not write al-Fātiḥa in your codex? He said: had I written it, I would have written it at the head of every surah." This is preserved in Ibn Kathīr from al-Aʿmash via Ibrāhīm. (The later Suyūṭī treatment, raw line 4876-4920, agrees that Ibn Masʿūd's omission was *liturgical-frame* reasoning, not a denial of canonical status.)

### 6.1 Ibn Kathīr on Q 1:5 (`5.json`)

Ibn Kathīr surveys the seven canonical readings of *iyyāka*: classical (with shadda on yāʾ); ʿAmr b. Fāʾid's *iyāka* (without shadda, *shādhdha*; rejected because *iyā* would mean "sunlight"); the *ayyāka* variant (with fatḥa on hamza); *hayyāka* with hāʾ-substitution. He concludes the *iyyāka* (with shadda) is the only canonical reading.

### 6.2 Ibn Kathīr on Q 1:7 (`7.json`)

The *al-maghḍūb ʿalayhim* / *al-ḍāllīn* identifications. Ibn Kathīr provides hadith chains for the Yahūd/Naṣārā mapping (most prominently from Tirmidhī #2953, Aḥmad in *Musnad* via ʿAdī b. Ḥātim), then notes the **alternative** reading (al-Sulamī, Ibn ʿArabī): the two phrases as *generic categories* — "those who knew but went against" (Yahūd-paradigm) and "those who erred without knowing" (Naṣārā-paradigm) — where the surface mapping to specific religious communities is one historical instance of a more general schema.

## 7. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**Source on disk**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/1/{1-7}.json`

al-Qurṭubī's strength is **fiqhī implications**. His Q 1:1 (`1.json`) treats the *istiʿādha* (taking-refuge formula) before the basmala in 12 *masāʾil*: when to say it, whether to say it aloud, whether the basmala itself is a verse of every surah or only of Q 1, etc.

His Q 1:5 (`5.json`) opens with the iltifāt analysis:

> "إياك نعبد رجع من الغيبة إلى الخطاب على التلوين ; لأن من أول السورة إلى هاهنا خبرا عن الله تعالى وثناء عليه..."

("*Iyyāka naʿbudu* — a return from third-person to second-person, on the principle of *talwīn* [variation]; for from the beginning of the surah to this point has been third-person predication-about-God and praise-of-Him.") al-Qurṭubī uses the term *talwīn* (lit. "colouring") rather than *iltifāt* — a marginal terminological variant within the same balāgha tradition.

His Q 1:7 (`7.json`) treats *ṣirāṭ al-mustaqīm* in legal terms (*adim hidāyatanā*, "perpetuate our guidance") and surveys eight different *al-mun'am ʿalayhim* glosses, including the prophets-and-righteous gloss tied to Q 4:69.

## 12. Synthesis

The classical exegetical tradition on Q 1 is *uniformly convergent* on five claims:

1. **Q 1 is *umm al-Kitāb*** — recurring across al-Bukhārī (#4474), al-Tirmidhī (#3124), Aḥmad's *Musnad*, and read into the surah by al-Suyūṭī, al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī, Ibn Kathīr.
2. **Q 1 is the *ʿṣirat-al-Qurʾān* in compression** — al-Rāzī's "ten thousand questions," al-Biqāʿī's "Q 1 = compression of the Quran as basmala = compression of Q 1," al-Suyūṭī's 25-name density. All converge on the same structural claim.
3. **Q 1:5 is the iltifāt-pivot** — al-Zarkashī (*Burhān*), al-Suyūṭī (*Itqān*), al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Zamakhsharī all flag the 3rd→2nd shift; Abdel Haleem 1992 systematizes.
4. **Q 1:6 is the surah's only imperative and the seed of the *duʿāʾ* function** — al-Rāzī names Q 1 *Sūrat al-Duʿāʾ*; al-Suyūṭī accepts the name.
5. **Q 1:7 trifurcates humanity** — al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī all read v 7 as a three-class typology.

These five claims are all **empirically confirmed** at law-strength by the project's quantitative artifacts ([[h-new-840-unified-architectural-score|H-NEW-840]], [[h-new-720-canonical-adjacency-cost|H-NEW-720]], [[h-new-590-outlier-spectrum|H-NEW-590]], [[h-new-111-fisher-rao-mushaf|H-NEW-111]]).

The two claims that require *more careful* audit:
- The basmala-letter-numerology (al-Biqāʿī, line 122) — rules-tuple-stable as a count, but the *mathematical-significance* claim is post-hoc and untestable; the modern Code-19 trajectory falsifies any divisibility-thesis built on it.
- The Q 1 internal ring-composition (Farrin/Cuypers school, 114-chambers blog) — pre-registered audit queued.

These are addressed in `05-classical-claims-audit.md` and `06-novel-findings.md`.
