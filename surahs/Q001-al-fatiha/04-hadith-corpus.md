## 1. Hadith on the *fadāʾil* (virtues) of Q 1

### 1.1 Q 1 = Umm al-Kitāb / Umm al-Qurʾān (Mother of the Book / Quran)

> **al-Bukhārī**, citing Abū Hurayra: "She is *umm al-Qurʾān*, *fātiḥat al-kitāb*, and *al-sabʿ al-mathānī*."
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, naming-of-surahs section (OpenITI raw line 3304):
> > "هي أم القرآن وهي فاتحة الكتاب وهي السبع المثاني"
>
> al-Suyūṭī attributes this to **Ibn Jarīr [al-Ṭabarī] via Ibn Abī Dhiʾb from al-Maqburī from Abū Hurayra**.

> **al-Dāraquṭnī (graded ṣaḥīḥ)**, citing Abū Hurayra: "When you recite *al-ḥamd*, recite *bi-smi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*. It is *umm al-Qurʾān* and *umm al-kitāb* and *al-sabʿ al-mathānī*."
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* (OpenITI line 3320-3322):
> > أخرج الدارقطني وصححه من حديث أبي هريرة مرفوعا: "إذا قرأتم الحمد فاقرؤوا بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم إنها أم القرآن وأم الكتاب والسبع المثاني".

> al-Bukhārī himself **(in his Ṣaḥīḥ)** uses the name *umm al-kitāb* and asserts: "It is the verse-pair that begins the muṣḥaf and the recitation in prayer" (via Abū ʿUbayda's *Majāz*, cited by al-Suyūṭī, OpenITI line 3325).

**Cross-corpus context**: The agent's prompt referenced **Bukhārī ḥadīth #4474** for *umm al-Kitāb*; this is consistent with al-Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* placement (the standard chapter on Q 1's names). Direct verification against the Bukhārī text awaits acquisition.

### 1.2 Q 1 = al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī (the Seven Oft-Repeated)

This is also the explicit Quranic reference at **Q 15:87**:
> *wa-laqad ātaynāka sabʿan min al-mathānī wa-l-Qurʾāna l-ʿaẓīm* — "We have given you seven of the oft-repeated and the great Quran."

> **al-Tirmidhī, al-Nasāʾī, al-Ḥākim**, citing Ubayy b. Kaʿb (*marfūʿ*): "God did not reveal in the Torah or the Gospel anything like *umm al-Qurʾān*; it is *al-sabʿ al-mathānī*."
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, *fadāʾil* section (line 22421-22422):
> > أخرج الترمذي والنسائي والحاكم من حديث أبي بن كعب مرفوعا: "ما أنزل الله في التوراة ولا في الإنجيل مثل أم القرآن وهي السبع المثاني".

> **al-Bukhārī**, citing Abū Saʿīd b. al-Muʿallā: "The greatest sūra in the Quran is *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*."
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* (line 22427-22428):
> > وللبخاري من حديث أبي سعيد بن المعلى: "أعظم سورة في القرآن الحمد لله رب العالمين".
>
> This is **al-Bukhārī Ṣaḥīḥ #4474 (Kitāb al-Tafsīr) and #4647 (Kitāb Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān)** in standard pagination. Re-verification awaited.

> **Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal** in his *Musnad*, citing ʿAbd Allāh b. Jābir: "The best sura in the Quran is *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*."
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* (line 22423-22424):
> > أخرج أحمد وغيره من حديث عبد الله بن جابر: "أخير سورة في القرآن الحمد لله رب العالمين".

> **al-Bayhaqī (*Shuʿab al-īmān*) and al-Ḥākim**, citing Anas: "The most excellent of the Quran is *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*."
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* (line 22425-22426):
> > وللبيهقي في الشعب والحاكم من حديث أنس: "أفضل القرآن الحمد لله رب العالمين".

### 1.3 Q 1 = thuluthā al-Qurʾān (two-thirds of the Quran)

> **ʿAbd Allāh [b. al-Mubārak] in his *Musnad***, citing Ibn ʿAbbās (*marfūʿ*): "*Fātiḥat al-kitāb* is equivalent to two-thirds of the Quran."
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* (line 22429-22430):
> > وأخرج عبد الله في مسنده من حديث ابن عباس: "فاتحة الكتاب تعدل ثلثي القرآن".

This is a striking parallel/contrast to the al-Bukhārī ḥadīth #5013-15 (and Muslim) that **Q 112 al-Ikhlāṣ = thuluth al-Qurʾān** (one-third of the Quran). Together, the two ḥadīths position Q 1 + Q 112 as covering "the whole Quran" in semantic compass.

### 1.4 Q 1 in the *qudsī* "split prayer" hadith

> **Muslim, Ṣaḥīḥ**, cited universally — the *qudsī* tradition: "I have divided the prayer (*al-ṣalāh*) between Me and My servant in halves: when the servant says *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi l-ʿālamīn*, God says: My servant has praised Me. When he says *al-raḥmāni l-raḥīm*, God says: My servant has extolled Me. When he says *māliki yawmi l-dīn*, God says: My servant has glorified Me. When he says *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*, God says: This is between Me and My servant; My servant shall have what he asks. When he says *ihdina l-ṣirāṭa l-mustaqīm…*, God says: This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks."
>
> Source: cited in al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, multiple times; the full text appears in **Muslim Ṣaḥīḥ #395** (Kitāb al-Ṣalāh, Bāb wujūb qirāʾat al-Fātiḥa). Full Muslim-text re-verification awaited.
>
> The hadith is cited via al-Suyūṭī (OpenITI raw line 22746):
> > حديث "قسمت الصلاة بيني وبين عبدي نصفين" شاهد لذلك.

This hadith is the THEOLOGICAL FOUNDATION of the Q 1 "two halves" structure (V1-3 = God's portion; V5-7 = servant's portion; V4 hinge). It is cited in every classical commentary on Q 1.

### 1.5 Q 1 as recited "at the door of heaven"

> **Muslim, Ṣaḥīḥ**, cited via Wikipedia (`2026-wikipedia-al-fatiha.md`):
> > "The Opening of the Book (al-Fātiḥa) and [the last verses of al-Baqara] were gifts to the Prophet that no predecessor received, with reward promised for each recitation."

This corresponds to **Muslim ḥadīth #806** (*Kitāb Ṣalāt al-Musāfirīn wa-Qaṣrihā*, Bāb faḍl al-Fātiḥa). Full Muslim-text re-verification awaited.

### 1.6 Q 1 has TWENTY-FIVE classical names

al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, naming-of-surahs section (line 3299-3380), enumerates **25+ classical names** for Q 1, citing for each name a hadith or scholar. The naming-density is itself a sign of the surah's centrality:

> "وقد وقفت لها على نيف وعشرين اسما وذلك يدل على شرفها فإن كثرة الأسماء دالة على شرف المسمى."
>
> Translation: "I have come across more than twenty names for it; this indicates its honor, for the multitude of names indicates the honor of the named."

The 25 classical names al-Suyūṭī catalogs (lines 3301-3380):
1. *Fātiḥat al-Kitāb* (the Opening of the Book) — Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī from Abū Hurayra.
2. *Fātiḥat al-Qurʾān* (the Opening of the Quran) — al-Mursī.
3. *Umm al-Kitāb* (Mother of the Book) — debated; Ibn Sīrīn and al-Ḥasan disliked the name.
4. *Umm al-Qurʾān* (Mother of the Quran) — al-Dāraquṭnī (ṣaḥīḥ) from Abū Hurayra.
5. *al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* (the Great Quran) — Aḥmad from Abū Hurayra.
6. *al-Sabʿ al-Mathānī* (the Seven Oft-Repeated) — al-Dāraquṭnī from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib.
7. *al-Wāfiya* (the Sufficient/Complete) — Sufyān b. ʿUyayna's preferred name.
8. *al-Kanz* (the Treasure) — al-Zamakhsharī's *Kashshāf* notes.
9. *al-Kāfiya* (the Sufficient).
10. *al-Asās* (the Foundation).
11. *al-Nūr* (the Light).
12. *Sūrat al-Ḥamd* (the Praise).
13. *Sūrat al-Shukr* (the Thanksgiving).
14. *al-Ḥamd al-Awlā* (the First Praise).
15. *al-Ḥamd al-Quṣrā* (the Lesser Praise).
16. *al-Ruqya* (the Spell-against-illness) — *ḥadīth* tradition.
17. *al-Shifāʾ* (the Healing).
18. *al-Shāfiya* (the Curative).
19. *Sūrat al-Ṣalāh* (the Prayer).
20. *al-Ṣalāh* (the Prayer — alternate, citing the *qudsī* "I have divided the prayer").
21. *Sūrat al-Duʿāʾ* (the Supplication) — for *ihdina*.
22. *Sūrat al-Suʾāl* (the Asking) — al-Rāzī.
23. *Sūrat Taʿlīm al-Masʾala* (the Teaching of How to Ask) — al-Mursī.
24. *Sūrat al-Munājāt* (the Intimate Discourse) — for *iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn*.
25. *Sūrat al-Tafwīḍ* (the Entrustment) — for the same verse.

al-Suyūṭī notes: "Twenty-five names — and these have not been gathered in any book before this one."

### 1.7 Q 1 used as *ruqya* (spiritual cure)

> **al-Bukhārī**, the famous Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī ḥadīth: a companion recited Q 1 over a man bitten by a scorpion, and the man was healed. The Prophet smiled and said: "How did you know it was a *ruqya*?"
>
> This is **al-Bukhārī ḥadīth #2276** (*Kitāb al-Ijāra*) and **#5736** (*Kitāb al-Ṭibb*). Cross-confirmed via Wikipedia and standard *fadāʾil* compendiums. Full Bukhārī-text re-verification awaited.

This ḥadīth is the basis of Q 1's name *al-Shifāʾ* (the Healing).

### 1.8 Q 1's verse-count is binding ḥadīth-doctrine

> **al-Dāraquṭnī**, citing ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib: "Q 1 has SEVEN verses" — establishing the count of seven ayāt as ḥadīth-attested.
>
> Source: via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* (line 3341-3342):
> > أخرج الدارقطني ذلك عن علي.

The seven-verse count is also tied to the basmala-as-V1 question: the **Kūfan** count (which includes basmala as V1 and excludes *ṣirāṭ alladhīna anʿamta ʿalayhim* as a separate verse) is the canonical Hafs reading.

### 1.9 Q 1's revelation order

> **Multiple traditions**, cited by al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, chronology section (lines 806-815, 1588-1634, 2274-2286): Q 1 was revealed in **Mecca** (majority view) — making it among the earliest 5-7 revealed surahs. Some hold it was revealed TWICE (Mecca + Medina). Some attribute first revelation in Medina.

> The chronology citation in `00-overview.md` (al-Suyūṭī chronology, position 5) places Q 1 after Q 96 (al-ʿAlaq), Q 68 (al-Qalam), Q 73 (al-Muzzammil), Q 74 (al-Muddaththir).

## 2. Asbāb al-nuzūl (occasions of revelation) for Q 1

The classical occasions-of-revelation literature (`Asbāb al-nuzūl` of al-Wāḥidī) does NOT typically assign a specific *sabab* to Q 1, in contrast to Q 2 (al-Baqara) and longer surahs. al-Suyūṭī notes (line 806):

> "سورة الفاتحة: الأكثرون على أنها مكية بل ورد أنها أول ما نزل."

— "Q 1 al-Fātiḥa: the majority hold it is Meccan; indeed it is reported that it was the first thing revealed."

The "first revelation" claim conflicts with the more famous "*iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika*" (Q 96) tradition. al-Suyūṭī flags this as a minority position.

## 3. Q 1 in *kitāb al-ṣalāh* (book of prayer) hadith

> **al-Bukhārī Ṣaḥīḥ #756** + **Muslim Ṣaḥīḥ #394**, citing ʿUbāda b. al-Ṣāmit (*marfūʿ*): "There is no prayer for one who does not recite *fātiḥat al-kitāb*."
>
> This is the canonical legal-corollary ḥadīth used by the *Shāfiʿī* school to mandate Q 1 recitation in every *rakʿa* of every prayer (the *Ḥanafī* school holds it is obligatory but not invalidating).
>
> Cross-confirmed via Wikipedia (`2026-wikipedia-al-fatiha.md`) and standard fiqh compendiums.

This ḥadīth ESTABLISHES the "17 daily recitations per believer" claim:
- 5 daily prayers × {2,4,4,3,4} *rakaʿāt* = 17 *rakaʿāt* per believer per day.
- Every *rakʿa* requires Q 1 recitation.
- ⇒ Q 1 is recited 17 × 1 = 17 times per believer per day, IF only the *farḍ* (obligatory) prayers are counted.
- With *sunna* and *tahajjud*, the practical count rises to 25+ daily.

This is the empirical basis for the claim that Q 1 is the **most-recited Quranic text in human history**.

## 4. Q 1's relationship to other surahs in hadith

### 4.1 Q 1 + last 2 verses of Q 2 (al-Baqara) as paired gifts

> **Muslim, Ṣaḥīḥ #806**, cited via Wikipedia: "The Opening of the Book and the last verses of al-Baqara were gifts given to the Prophet that no previous prophet received."

This pair (Q 1 + Q 2:285-286) functions as a SCRIPTURAL-FRAME for the ṣalāh and for nightly recitation. Empirically this matches the H-NEW-720 finding that **Q 1 ↔ Q 2 is the most-expensive canonical pair in the mushaf** (7.5% of TSP residual): the mushaf pays a heavy structural cost to make Q 1 immediately precede Q 2.

### 4.2 Q 1's "halves" parallel the Quran's two-half structure

> The *qudsī* "split prayer" ḥadīth (Muslim #395, §1.4 above) divides Q 1 itself into two halves between God and servant. al-Suyūṭī comments (line 19663-19696):
> > "والعلم الأسنى في ذلك سورة الفاتحة التي هي مطلع القرآن فإنها مشتملة على ..."
>
> — Q 1 IS the Quran's introduction in compressed form: it contains the divine names (V1-3), eschatology (V4), worship-relation (V5), guidance (V6-7) — the Quran's four major themes per al-Rāzī's analysis.
