# Q 103 al-ʿAṣr — Tafsīr Survey

Six classical commentaries surveyed from the on-disk corpus; each passage cited to its source file. I read
the Arabic of al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī and al-Baghawī directly, and the English Ibn Kathīr + al-Jalālayn.
The three recurring exegetical hubs are: (i) **what does *ʿaṣr* swear by?** (the four/five readings); (ii)
**how general is *al-insān*?** (the generic-vs-specific debate, resolved by the v 3 exception); (iii) **the
iʿjāz/taḥaddī status of the surah** (al-Shāfiʿī, al-Rāzī, the Musaylima anecdote).

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (on Q 103:1-3)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/103/{1,2,3}.json`.

**v 1.** al-Ṭabarī reports the disagreement (*ikhtalafa ahl al-taʾwīl*) over *ʿaṣr*: some say it is **al-dahr**
(time/the age) — via ʿAlī ← Ibn ʿAbbās, *"al-ʿaṣr: sāʿa min sāʿāt al-nahār"* (a portion of the day's hours);
al-Ḥasan (via Maʿmar ← Ibn Thawr): *"huwa al-ʿashī"* (the late afternoon). **His own preferred reading** (*al-ṣawāb*):
*"that our Lord swore by* al-ʿaṣr*, a name for* al-dahr*, which is the evening, the night and the day, with no
restriction of one sense over another"* — i.e. the oath is on **time-in-general**, unrestricted.

**v 2.** *inna al-insāna la-fī khusr* = *"inna ibn Ādam la-fī halaka wa-nuqṣān"* (the son of Adam is in ruin and
deficiency). He preserves the **ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib variant recitation** *wa-l-ʿaṣr wa-nawāʾib al-dahr, inna
al-insāna la-fī khusr, wa-innahu fīhi ilā ākhir al-dahr* (via Abū Isḥāq ← ʿAmr Dhū Marr), explicitly framing
it as *tafsīr*, not Qurʾān (an interpolated gloss). Mujāhid: *"illā man āmana"* (except whoever believed).

**v 3.** *illā alladhīna āmanū* = *"those who affirmed God, declared His oneness, professed obedience, did
righteous deeds, discharged the obligations laid on them, and shunned His prohibitions."* **Key grammatical
argument:** *"He excepted the believers from* al-insān*, because* al-insān *here means the collective (jamʿ),
not the single individual."* — the exception proves the generic reading. *wa-tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqq*: *al-ḥaqq* =
**kitāb Allāh** (the Book of God, via Qatāda and al-Ḥasan, with the al-Ḥasan chain repeated three times).
*wa-tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabr*: *al-ṣabr* = **ṭāʿat Allāh** (patience in obeying God, Qatāda + al-Ḥasan). Closes:
*"ākhir tafsīr sūrat wa-l-ʿaṣr."*

## 2. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (on Q 103:1, 3)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/103/{1,3}.json`.

**v 1 (two masāʾil).** Opens: *"Meccan; Qatāda said Medinan, and it is reported from Ibn ʿAbbās; it is three
verses"* (*wa-hiya makkiyya. wa-qāla Qatāda madaniyya … wa-hiya thalāth āyāt*). He gives **five readings** of
*ʿaṣr*: (a) *al-dahr* (Ibn ʿAbbās), with the rationale that God swore by it *"li-mā fīhi min al-tanbīh bi-taṣarruf
al-aḥwāl wa-tabaddulihā, wa-mā fīhā min al-dalāla ʿalā al-ṣāniʿ"* (for the alert it gives, through the turning
of states, to [the existence of] the Maker); (b) *al-layl wa-l-nahār* (night and day — *al-ʿaṣrān*, Ḥumayd b.
Thawr's verse); (c) *al-ghadāt wa-l-ʿashī* (morning and evening); (d) *al-ʿashī*, the time from the sun's decline
to its setting (al-Ḥasan, Qatāda); (e) **ṣalāt al-ʿaṣr**, the middle prayer (*al-wusṭā*), per Muqātil, cross-linked
to Q 2:238 and the ḥadīth *al-ṣalāt al-wusṭā ṣalāt al-ʿaṣr*; (f) the **age of the Prophet ﷺ** *"li-faḍlihi
bi-tajdīd al-nubuwwa fīhi"*; or *"wa-Rabb al-ʿaṣr."* The **second masʾala** is legal: *"Mālik said: whoever swears
not to speak to a man for an* ʿaṣr *— he shall not speak to him for a year (sana); al-Shāfiʿī said: he is freed of
the oath after an hour (sāʿa), unless he had a [contrary] intention."* (The oath-interpretation masʾala, Ibn al-ʿArabī's
note that Mālik takes the maximal sense.)

**v 3.** *illā alladhīna āmanū* is an *istithnāʾ* from *al-insān*, *"since it means 'the people' on the sound view"*
(confirming the generic reading). *wa-ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt*: discharged the obligatory duties — *"and they are the
Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ."* He preserves the **Ubayy b. Kaʿb prophetic-tafsīr**: Ubayy recited
*wa-l-ʿaṣr* to the Prophet and asked its meaning; the Prophet glossed *al-insān* = **Abū Jahl**, *alladhīna
āmanū* = **Abū Bakr**, *ʿamilū al-ṣāliḥāt* = **ʿUmar**, *tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqq* = **ʿUthmān**, *tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabr*
= **ʿAlī** — *"and thus Ibn ʿAbbās preached it from the pulpit, mawqūfan ʿalayhi."* (al-Qurṭubī flags this as
*mawqūf* on Ibn ʿAbbās.) *al-ḥaqq* = al-tawḥīd (al-Ḍaḥḥāk ← Ibn ʿAbbās) / al-Qurʾān (Qatāda) / God Himself (al-Suddī).

## 3. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (on Q 103, and on the taḥaddī of Q 2:23)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`, surah-ʿAṣr block at
**L263739-263868**; taḥaddī passage at **L13989-14006**.

**The four "aqwāl" on *ʿaṣr* (L263746-263839).** al-Rāzī organizes the readings as four positions, each with
sub-arguments: (1) **al-dahr** — six rationales, including that *dahr* is full of marvels and that *"the
remainder of a man's life has no price"* (a contrast that motivates the *khusr* of v 2); he notes the ʿAlī
recitation *wa-l-ʿaṣr wa-nawāʾib al-dahr* is *tafsīr*, not Qurʾān (*"this would void the prayer, so we do not say
he recited it as Qurʾān but as exegesis"*, L263750). (2) **one of the two ends of the day** (Abū Muslim) — paired
explicitly with **al-ḍuḥā**: *"He swore by* al-ʿaṣr *as He swore by* al-ḍuḥā*, for in both are signs of [His]
power"* (L263779), and the **profit/loss diptych**: *"as He swore by* al-ḍuḥā *in the matter of profit and gave
the Messenger glad tidings of advance, so here in the matter of the loser He warns of decline"* (L263793-263795).
Here sits the famous **ice-seller / merchant-of-loss anecdote**: *"a salaf said: I learned the meaning of the surah
from a seller of ice who was crying out 'Have mercy on one whose capital is melting (irḥamū man yadhūbu raʾs
māluhu)!' — and I said: this is the meaning of* inna al-insāna la-fī khusr*: the* ʿaṣr *(age) passes over a man,
his life goes by and he earns nothing, so he is a loser"* (L263796-263799). (3) **ṣalāt al-ʿaṣr** (Muqātil), with
six supports. (4) **the age of the Prophet ﷺ** (L263826-263839): *"and He swore by* al-ʿaṣr *meaning the age you
are in"* — the *ʿaṣr*/*balad* (Q 90:2)/*ʿumr* (Q 15:72) trio of oaths on the Prophet's time, place, and life.

**v 2 (four masāʾil, L263844-263868).** On the *alif-lām* of *al-insān*: either generic (*li-l-jins*, the sound
view, supported by *"the exception of the believers"*) or specific (a named disbeliever — al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra,
al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil, al-Aswad per Ibn ʿAbbās; Abū Lahab per Muqātil; Abū Jahl in a *marfūʿ* report). On the indefinite
*khusr-in* (not *al-khusr*): *al-tankīr* signals either **enormity** (*tahwīl* — "a loss whose extent only God
knows") or **belittlement** (*taḥqīr*); al-Rāzī prefers the first.

**The taḥaddī (on Q 2:23, *faʾtū bi-sūratin min mithlihi*, L13989-14006).** al-Rāzī's worked example of "a surah
like it" is precisely **sūrat al-Kawthar, sūrat al-ʿAṣr, and qul yā ayyuhā al-kāfirūn** (L14000) — the shortest
surahs — and he addresses the objection that imitating these *seems* humanly possible by arguing that either they
reach the threshold of *iʿjāz* in eloquence, or the Arabs' failure to meet the challenge (despite every incentive)
is itself the miracle. **Q 103 is thus one of al-Rāzī's three named test cases for inimitability** — directly
relevant to Q103-F-01's "minimal-form, maximal-content" framing.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (on Q 103)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/103/{1,3}.json`
(Arabic parallel at `.../ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/103/`).

Ibn Kathīr's section title: *"Which was revealed in Makkah"* and *"How ʿAmr bin al-ʿĀṣ was aware of the Qurʾān's
Miracle due to this Surah."* He leads with the **Musaylima failed-imitation anecdote**: ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ (pre-Islam)
visits Musaylima, who asks what was revealed to Muḥammad; ʿAmr recites al-ʿAṣr in full; Musaylima offers his
parody (*"yā wabr yā wabr, innamā anta udhunān wa-ṣadr…"* — "O hyrax, you are but two ears and a chest…") and
ʿAmr replies *"by Allāh, you know that I know you are lying."* Ibn Kathīr sources part of the chain to Abū Bakr
al-Kharāʾiṭī, *Masāwiʾ al-Akhlāq*, vol. 2.

He then gives the **two-Companions tradition**: *"At-Tabarani recorded from ʿAbdullāh bin Hisn Abi Madinah that
he said: 'Whenever two men from the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to meet, they would not part
until one of them had recited Sūrat al-ʿAṣr in its entirety to the other, and one of them had given the greetings
of peace to the other.'"* — attributed to **al-Ṭabarānī** (not the 9 books; see `04-hadith-corpus.md` for the
verification status). Immediately after: **al-Shāfiʿī's verdict** — *"Ash-Shafiʿi said: 'If the people were to
ponder on this Surah, it would be sufficient for them.'"* On the verses: *al-ʿaṣr* = "the time in which the
movements of the Children of Adam occur"; *khusr* = "loss and destruction"; *tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqq* = obedience and
avoidance of the forbidden; *tawāṣaw bi-l-ṣabr* = patience against the harms of those who oppose enjoining good.

## 5. al-Baghawī, *Maʿālim al-tanzīl* (on Q 103:1, 3)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-baghawi/103/{1,3}.json`.

*"Makkiyya."* **v 1:** *ʿaṣr* = al-dahr (Ibn ʿAbbās); *"He swore by it because there is in it a lesson for the
beholder"*; or *"wa-Rabb al-ʿaṣr"*; Ibn Kaysān: night and day (*al-ʿaṣrān*); al-Ḥasan: from the sun's decline to
setting; Qatāda: the last hour of the day; Muqātil: ṣalāt al-ʿaṣr, the middle prayer. **v 3:** *al-ḥaqq* = the
Qurʾān (al-Ḥasan, Qatāda) / faith and tawḥīd (Muqātil); *al-ṣabr* = "patience in discharging the obligations and
establishing God's command." al-Baghawī adds the **Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī gloss** (via Ibn ʿAwn): *"man, when he
lives long in this world and grows old, is in decrease and decline — except the believers, for their wages and
the good deeds of their youth and health are written for them"* — and explicitly cross-references **Q 95:4-6**
(*laqad khalaqnā al-insāna fī aḥsani taqwīm … thumma radadnāhu asfala sāfilīn illā alladhīna āmanū*), the
structural parallel to Q 103's loss/exception frame.

## 6. al-Jalālayn (on Q 103:1-3)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-al-jalalayn/103/{1,2,3}.json`.

Terse: **v 1** *"By Time! — or* ʿaṣr *can mean the period from the declining of the sun to sunset, or it may
denote the afternoon prayer."* **v 2** *"Verily man (*al-insān*, the generic) is in a state of loss in all his
bargaining"* — the **mercantile reading of *khusr*** stated outright. **v 3** *"except those who believe and
perform righteous deeds — they are not in a state of loss — and enjoin one another to follow the truth (faith)
and to patience (in maintaining obedience and refraining from disobedience)."*

## 7. Points of agreement / disagreement

| Question | al-Ṭabarī | al-Qurṭubī | al-Rāzī | Ibn Kathīr | al-Baghawī | al-Jalālayn |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Meccan? | (implicit) | **Meccan** (Qatāda: Medinan) | Meccan | **Meccan** | Meccan | — |
| *ʿaṣr* preferred sense | **al-dahr** (unrestricted) | al-dahr (lists 5+) | lists 4 aqwāl; leans dahr | "time of human action" | al-dahr | afternoon/prayer |
| ʿAlī *nawāʾib al-dahr* recitation | reports as *tafsīr* | — | reports as *tafsīr* (not Qurʾān) | — | — | — |
| *al-insān* | **generic** (exception proves it) | **generic** | generic (sound) or specific | "species of man" | generic | **generic** |
| *khusr* | ruin/deficiency | bad bargain (*ghabn*) | loss of capital/life | loss/destruction | decline of old age | "his bargaining" |
| iʿjāz hook | — | — | **taḥaddī test-case (Q2:23)** | **al-Shāfiʿī + Musaylima** | Q 95 parallel | — |

The strongest cross-mufassir consensus: (i) *ʿaṣr* most plausibly = **time-in-general (*al-dahr*)**, the oath
being a *tanbīh* on time as a sign of the Maker and as the capital humankind squanders; (ii) *al-insān* is
**generic** (proven by the v 3 plural exception — al-Ṭabarī's grammatical argument, echoed by al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī,
al-Jalālayn); (iii) the surah is a recognized **iʿjāz/taḥaddī locus** — al-Rāzī's named test-case, al-Shāfiʿī's
"would suffice them," and the Musaylima parody all treat its brevity-with-completeness as the point. This is the
qualitative claim Q103-F-01 operationalizes (minimal-surah architecture; `06-novel-findings.md`).

## 8. Honest limits

- al-Rāzī is read from a full OpenITI raw concatenation; the surah block (L263739-263868) and the taḥaddī passage
  (L13989-14006) were located by section headers and verse-text and read in context.
- The Ubayy b. Kaʿb prophetic-tafsīr (al-Qurṭubī, v 3) is graded *mawqūf on Ibn ʿAbbās* by al-Qurṭubī himself; it
  is reported as an exegetical tradition, not adjudicated for authenticity here (see `04-hadith-corpus.md`).
- al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* and al-Ṭabarsī's *Majmaʿ al-bayān* on Q 103 are on disk but not surveyed in
  depth here; the six above already exceed the protocol's ≥5-mufassirūn requirement and cover the full position-space.
- The four/five readings of *ʿaṣr* are not empirically decidable from the text; the H-NEW-2210 "temporal" tag
  records only that the sworn object is a time-word.
