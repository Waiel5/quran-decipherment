## 2. Classical name and naming-tradition

The surah is named **al-Mumtaḥana** ("The Examined Woman") after the imperative form *imtaḥinūhunna* ("examine them") at v. 10, addressing the Muslim community on how to receive women emigrants from Mecca after the Treaty of Hudaybiyya. The passive-participle naming choice is significant: it foregrounds **the feminine subject** of the procedural instruction. Classical sources (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 17 *fī asmāʾ al-suwar*; al-Zarkashī *al-Burhān* 1:269-270) record an alternate name **al-Imtiḥān** ("The Examination") and **Sūrat al-Mawadda** ("The Surah of Affection," after the *al-mawadda* in vv. 1, 7); the canonical *al-Mumtaḥana* dominates the manuscript tradition (al-Sakhāwī, *al-Tibyān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*).

## 3. Opening formula — *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā tattakhidhū*

Q 60:1 opens with the corpus's most common high-density Medinan vocative-prohibition pattern: *yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* + *lā tattakhidhū* + an antagonistic group + *awliyāʾ*. This double-clause anti-alliance formula appears 6 corpus times and is dominant in Q 5 + Q 60:

| v | Verse | Antagonist |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| Q 3:118 | *lā tattakhidhū biṭānatan min dūnikum* | non-Muslim confidants |
| Q 4:144 | *lā tattakhidhū al-kāfirīna awliyāʾ* | unbelievers |
| Q 5:51 | *lā tattakhidhū al-yahūda wa-l-naṣārā awliyāʾ* | Jews and Christians |
| Q 5:57 | *lā tattakhidhū alladhīna ittakhadhū dīnakum huzuwan* | religion-mockers |
| Q 9:23 | *lā tattakhidhū ābāʾakum wa-ikhwānakum awliyāʾ* | unbelieving kin |
| **Q 60:1** | *lā tattakhidhū ʿaduwwī wa-ʿaduwwakum awliyāʾ* | "My enemy and your enemy" |

Q 60:1's form is **uniquely framed in the first-person divine-voice** ("My enemy AND your enemy") — not "the unbelievers" or "your kin," but a divine-and-communal joint enemy. This is the only corpus instance of the *ʿaduwwī wa-ʿaduwwakum* dual pronominalization in the anti-alliance class.

### 5c. Ibrahim-disowning-pagan-kin formula

The *barāʾ + min* + Ibrahim-context formula (Ibrahim disowning his pagan kin) is corpus-EXACT to **2 verses**: Q 43:26 and Q 60:4. Q 60:4 is the **only Medinan instance** and the **only collective-disowning instance** ("Ibrāhīm AND those with him said to their people 'we are dissociated from you'"). Q 43:26 is the Late-Meccan personal disowning ("Ibrāhīm said to his father and his people 'I am dissociated…'").

## 11. Quick content structure

| Block | vv. | Theme |
|:--|:-:|:--|
| 1 | 1-3 | **Anti-alliance directive**: do not befriend "My enemy and your enemy"; warning of betrayal of intelligence; eschatological irrelevance of kinship. |
| 2 | 4-6 | **Ibrāhīm-as-paradigm**: dissociation from idol-worshipping kin (with the exception of his prayer for his father — historical caveat); *uswa ḥasana* mirror-formula echoing Q 33:21. |
| 3 | 7-9 | **Conditional reconciliation**: God may yet effect mawadda between you and former enemies; the differentiating principle (do not fight = do befriend; do fight = do not befriend). |
| 4 | 10-11 | **Women emigrants protocol**: examine them on imān; do not return to unbelievers; financial settlements with former Meccan husbands; reciprocal compensation when Muslim wives defect. |
| 5 | 12 | **Women's bayʿa formula**: 6-clause prohibition list for the feminine-plural pledge of allegiance. |
| 6 | 13 | **Closing prohibition** (mirroring v. 1): do not befriend a people God has cursed, who have despaired of the Hereafter. |

The surah has a **ring-frame composition**: v. 1 and v. 13 are anti-alliance directives (the inclusio); the inner content (vv. 4-12) is paradigm + protocols. al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar* describes this as *naẓm marbūṭ* ("a tied composition") with the disownment-of-pagans theme spanning the entire surah.
