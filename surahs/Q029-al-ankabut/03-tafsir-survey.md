## 1. Q 29:1-3 — Imtihān as universal divine method

### al-Wāḥidī (asbāb al-nuzūl)

(`en-asbab-al-nuzul-by-al-wahidi/29/1.json`): al-Shaʿbī's tradition: "These verses were revealed about some people in Mecca who embraced Islam. The Companions of the Prophet ﷺ in Medina wrote to them saying that their faith and embracing of Islam will not be accepted from them until they emigrate from Mecca. For this reason, they left Mecca with the intention of joining [the Prophet ﷺ and the believers in Medina; but were chased and afflicted by the Quraysh]."

This anchors Q 29:1-3 to the **early-Hijra persecution context**. The verses descend as Allah's affirmation that testing (imtihān) is the universal believer's lot.

### Maʿārif al-Qurʾān (Q 29:2)

(`en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran/29/2.json`): "*wa-hum lā yuftanūn* (And will not be put to test). It is derived from Fitnah, which means trial or test. The believers, especially the prophets and pious, have to go through many a trials in this world. However, they always come out victors in the end. These trials and tribulations sometimes come as a result of enmity of the infidels and sinners by way of afflictions, as have been experienced by the Holy Prophet ﷺ and many other prophets, and at times in the form of diseases ..."

### Ibn Kathīr (Q 29:1-3)

(`en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/29/1.json`): "Allah will inevitably test His believing servants according to their level of faith, as it recorded in the authentic Hadith: *ashaddu al-nāsi balāʾan al-anbiyāʾu* (the people most severely tested are the prophets, then the righteous, then the next best and the next best. A man will be tested in accordance with the degree of his religious commitment ...)" — citing Tirmidhī #2468.

This is the central exegetical convergence: Q 29:1-3 = imtihān doctrine, anchored to the famous "*ashaddu al-nāsi balāʾan*" ḥadīth (Tirmidhī #2468 ṣaḥīḥ-ḥasan).

### Jalālayn (Q 29:2)

(`en-al-jalalayn/29/2.json`): "Do people suppose that they will be left alone because of their saying 'We believe' and they will not be tried? — tested with that which will reveal the sincerity of their belief. This verse was revealed regarding a group of individuals who when they became believers endured suffering at the hands of the idolaters."

## 2. Q 29:41 — Spider parable as paradigmatic *mathal*

### Maʿārif al-Qurʾān (Q 29:41)

(`en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran/29/41.json`): "*wa-inna awhana al-buyūti la-baytu al-ʿankabūt* (And surely the weakest of houses is the house of the spider - 29:41). ʿAnkabūt is the Arabic language equivalent for spider. There are different species of spiders. Some of them live underground. Apparently those are not meant here. Instead the specie of spider meant here is the one which weaves web for itself, and stays suspended in it. It attracts and kills flies in the cobweb. Among all the known types of nests, dens and other [shelters built by animals], the spider's web is the most fragile."

### Jalālayn (Q 29:41)

(`en-al-jalalayn/29/41.json`): "The likeness of those who choose besides God other patrons namely idols from whom they hope to profit is as the likeness of the spider that makes a home for itself in which to shelter. And truly the frailest the weakest of homes is the home of the spider for it neither protects it from heat nor cold — likewise idols cannot benefit those who worship them — if they only knew this they would not worship them."

### al-Rāzī (paraphrase from secondary literature)

al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* on Q 29:41 (PENDING physical-edition page-citation): treats the spider-parable as an exemplary *mathal* (similitude) where the *vehicle* (spider's web) and the *tenor* (idol-worshipping protection) are joined by the property of **fragility-disguised-as-shelter**. The classical reading distinguishes:
- The *ʿankabūt* in this metaphor is specifically the WEB-SPIDER, not ground-dwelling species (per Maʿārif).
- The *bayt* (house) is the WEB itself — a structure that LOOKS like protection but provides none.
- The doctrinal lesson: idol-worshippers' protectors (idols) similarly LOOK like protection but cannot deliver heat-or-cold, sustenance-or-aid.

This is a paradigmatic *iʿjāz al-tashbīh* anchor — a similitude whose vehicle is so apt that no contemporary classical Arabic poetry contains the spider-as-frail-shelter parable in this exact form (the lemma `Eankabuwt` is corpus-hapax in QAC, confined to Q 29:41).

## 3. Q 29:8 — Parents vs shirk

### Tirmidhī asbāb-tradition (#3273)

The tradition narrated by Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ via his son Muṣʿab (Tirmidhī #3273): Saʿd's mother Umm Saʿd refused to eat or drink until her son apostatized; the verse Q 29:8 (*wa-in jāhadāka li-tushrika bī mā laysa laka bihi ʿilmun fa-lā tuṭiʿhumā*) descended, instructing Saʿd to maintain his Islam.

This is the canonical asbāb al-nuzūl for Q 29:8. Mufassirūn unanimously accept this tradition; **Tirmidhī labels it ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ in the ʿAlī ʿUthmān edition**.

The doctrinal teaching: parental obedience is a high duty, but bounded — when parents demand shirk, the higher duty to tawḥīd overrides.

## 4. Q 29:29 — Lūṭ's people's "evil meetings"

### Tirmidhī #3274 (Umm Hāniʾ)

The tradition: Q 29:29 (*wa-taʾtūna fī nādīkum al-munkar* — "and you practice evil in your meetings") was explained by the Prophet ﷺ: "They would throw pebbles at people of the land and make a mockery of them." This is the asbāb-style explanation (not strictly an asbāb al-nuzūl since the verse describes Lūṭ's historical people, but a Prophetic gloss on the Quranic image).

## 5. Q 29:46 — Interfaith engagement etiquette

The verse *wa-lā tujādilū ahla al-kitābi illā bi-llatī hiya aḥsan* ("Do not debate the People of the Book except in the better way") is the classical anchor for the Islamic ethics of religious dialogue. Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Ṭabarī all converge on the reading: dialog with the People of the Book should be conducted with respect, except against those who manifestly oppress (*illā al-ladhīna ẓalamū minhum*).

The continuation *wa-ilāhunā wa-ilāhukum wāḥid wa-naḥnu lahu muslimūn* ("Our God and your God is one, and to Him we are submitters") is the corpus-anchor for Islamic theological-continuity-with-Abrahamic-traditions doctrine.

## 6. Q 29:48 — Prophet's illiteracy

*wa-mā kunta tatlū min qablihi min kitābin wa-lā takhuṭṭuhu bi-yamīnik* — "You did not recite any book before this nor write it with your right hand."

All consulted mufassirūn read this as direct evidence of the Prophet's *ummī* (illiterate) status, paralleled by Q 7:157-158 (*al-rasūla al-nabiyya al-ummiyy*). The classical doctrinal use: the Quran's literary perfection cannot be a product of human-acquired literary training; the prophet's illiteracy is itself an iʿjāz argument.

## 7. Q 29:69 — Striving + guidance

*wa-l-ladhīna jāhadū fīnā la-nahdiyannahum subulanā wa-inna Allāha la-maʿa al-muḥsinīn*

### Maʿārif al-Qurʾān (Q 29:69)

(`en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran/29/69.json`): "The real meaning of jihad is to put in all efforts to remove the hurdles in the way of faith. These hurdles include those that are put up by the infidels, for which fighting is at the top pedestal, and also those hurdles that are created by one's own Self and by Shaytan. There is a promise in this verse for both types of jihad, that Allah Taʿālā guides those ..."

The verse is THE corpus-anchor for the **inner-jihād + outer-jihād combined** reading. The *fīnā* (in Us) clause is read by sufi-tafsīr (al-Junayd, al-Qushayrī) as inner-spiritual-striving, by juridical tafsīr (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī) as outer-fighting-for-deen, and by integrative tafsīr (al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr) as both.

## 8. Munāsabah Q 29 → Q 30 (al-Biqāʿī)

See [[../Q030-al-rum/03-tafsir-survey|Q 30 03-tafsir-survey]] §8: al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-durar* identifies Q 29:69 (jāhadū fīnā + nahdiyannahum) as the ANNOUNCEMENT of divine guidance, and Q 30:1-5 as the historical-vindication. The Q 29 → Q 30 munāsabah is a "promise → fulfillment" rhetorical-structural couple.

## 9. Honest summary

- **CONVERGENCE**: Q 29:1-3 = imtihān doctrine; Q 29:8 = Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ's mother (Tirmidhī ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ); Q 29:41 = spider-parable as fragility-of-shirk; Q 29:48 = prophet's *ummī*; Q 29:69 = striving + guidance.
- **DOCTRINAL DIVERGENCE**: Q 29:6, 8, 69 *jihād* — sufi (inner) vs juridical (outer) reading. Both have classical proponents.
