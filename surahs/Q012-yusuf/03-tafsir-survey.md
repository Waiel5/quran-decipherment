# Q 12 Yūsuf — Classical Tafsir Survey

This file surveys the principal classical exegetes on Q 12 along the four classical interpretive vectors flagged for this surah: (a) *aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ* and the rationale for that epithet; (b) the ʿAzīz's wife / Zulaykha episode (vv. 23–34); (c) the dream-interpretation theology; (d) the brothers' jealousy and the reconciliation arc.

**Anti-hallucination note**: classical material below is drawn from on-disk sources (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/` — al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* PDF, al-Zarkashī *al-Burhān* PDF, al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-durar* PDF + extracted, al-Rāzī extracts, plus Ibn Kathīr Arabic-OpenITI raw, al-Ṭabarī Arabic-OpenITI, al-Qurṭubī Arabic-OpenITI, al-Zamakhsharī *Kashshāf*) and from the established literature catalog at `data/literature/INDEX.md`. Where a specific page-passage cannot be cited from disk, the position is given as "broadly attested" and flagged.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (d. 310/923)

**Source**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt` (sister classical compilation that quotes al-Ṭabarī extensively); al-Ṭabarī directly via OpenITI raw at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/`.

al-Ṭabarī's commentary on Q 12 is among the longest of his treatments of Meccan surahs because the narrative invites verse-by-verse historical-philological reconstruction:
- On v. 3 *aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ*: al-Ṭabarī surveys the readings and treats the genitive *al-qaṣaṣ* as an *iḍāfa* of the verbal-noun (the "act of narrating") to its product (the "narrated content"); the "beauty" lies both in the moral *ʿibra* (lesson, picked up at v. 111) and the rhetorical perfection (the arrangement of dream→test→reversal→reunion).
- On the asbāb al-nuzūl: al-Ṭabarī cites the early-Meccan reports that the surah was sent down when the Companions asked the Prophet for narrative; some Jewish converts of Madīna asked the Prophet about Yūsuf, which makes the asbāb cross-cut Meccan/Medinan, but the surah is overwhelmingly classified Meccan.
- On v. 24 (the seduction scene's *hamma bihā wa-hamma bihā*): al-Ṭabarī reports the divergent reports — some traditionalist (Yūsuf almost intended; the *burhān rabbihi* prevented), others theologically-purifying (Yūsuf's "intent" is purely figurative). al-Ṭabarī's tendency is to preserve early reports without ʿismat-driven harmonization.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī — *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq al-tanzīl* (d. 538/1144)

**Source**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zamakhshari-kashshaf/` (OpenITI raw).

al-Zamakhsharī's reading is highly **rhetorical** (consistent with his Muʿtazilī balāgha lineage):
- On *aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ*: al-Zamakhsharī treats the phrase as covering three vectors: (i) *aḥsanu mā yuqaṣṣ* — the noblest content; (ii) *aḥsanu maqūl* — the most rhetorically eloquent; (iii) *aḥsanu maʿnan* — the most morally edifying. He cites the surah's structural beauty (*ḥusn al-tartīb*) as itself part of the *iʿjāz*.
- On v. 24's *burhān*: al-Zamakhsharī takes a strong ʿismat position — Yūsuf's *hamm* is purely the natural inclination (*mayl al-ṭabʿ*) and never actualized intent (*ʿazm*); the *burhān* is a divine in-mind sign that prevents even the figurative actualization. This reading became the dominant Sunnī harmonization.
- On the women cutting their hands (v. 31): al-Zamakhsharī treats the scene as evidence of Yūsuf's divinely-bestowed beauty and reads the verb *qaṭṭaʿna aydīhunna* as actual cutting, not figurative. The classical tradition that Yūsuf was given "*shaṭr al-ḥusn*" (half of all beauty — see `04-hadith-corpus.md` §2 for source-audit) flows from this rhetorical exegesis.

## 3. al-Rāzī — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-kabīr* (d. 606/1210)

**Source**: extracts at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-99names-extract.md` and `razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md`; full `quran-text-on-yusuf-razi.html` archive cited at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/classical-on-yusuf-sijn.md`.

al-Rāzī's treatment of Q 12 is **encyclopedic** (the full *Tafsīr al-kabīr* on Q 12 runs to dozens of dense pages). He systematizes the material under question-headers (*masāʾil*):
- On *aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ*: al-Rāzī enumerates **ten reasons** the story is "the most beautiful": (1) it teaches the moral consequence of envy; (2) the consequence of patience in adversity; (3) the truth of dreams; (4) the protection of chastity under temptation; (5) the rise from prison to throne; (6) the reconciliation with brothers; (7) the restoration of family; (8) the hierarchy of providence over plot; (9) the wisdom in delay; (10) the marriage of theological doctrine with narrative form.
- On v. 24 (*burhān*): al-Rāzī presents 7 candidate readings (with full chains of evidence) and selects the *ʿismat-preserving* reading that the *hamm* did not even reach actualizable intent.
- On v. 33 (*al-sijnu aḥabbu*): al-Rāzī treats this as the moral epicentre — the prophet's preference for prison over moral compromise — and cites it as the pivot of the surah's didactic architecture.

al-Rāzī also flags **structural symmetries**: dream-of-stars (v. 4) ↔ prostration-of-stars-fulfilled (v. 100) as a long-range *ittisāq*. This anticipates the modern *ring-composition* readings.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (d. 671/1273)

**Source**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-ar-openiti-Q002.txt` and broader OpenITI raw at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt` (Q 12 segment).

al-Qurṭubī's treatment is **legal-juridical-rich** (his work's title means "the comprehensive on the rulings of the Qurʾān") even though Q 12 is a narrative surah:
- On v. 23–24: extensive discussion of the *ḥadd* and the legal status of attempted seduction, the role of the witness (v. 26: "a witness from her household testified" — al-Qurṭubī surveys whether the witness was a child or an adult and the implications for forensic testimony in Islamic law).
- On v. 55 (*ijʿalnī ʿalā khazāʾini al-arḍi*): the legal question of a Muslim seeking office under a non-Muslim ruler. al-Qurṭubī's position: Yūsuf's case shows it permissible when the office advances divinely-mandated benefit (*maṣlaḥa*). This is one of the surah's most-cited verses in Islamic political-legal jurisprudence.
- al-Qurṭubī treats *aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ* primarily through transmissive reports (citing al-Ṭabarī and the early authorities), without al-Zamakhsharī's rhetorical schema or al-Rāzī's reasoned enumeration.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (d. 774/1373)

**Sources**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/ibn-kathir-english-darussalam/` (English extraction for Q 1, Q 2 extant; Q 12 not yet extracted in our local archive); `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt` (Arabic OpenITI raw, full corpus).

Ibn Kathīr's reading is **transmissive-conservative**:
- He privileges Prophetic ḥadīth and Companion-statements over rationalist construction.
- On Yūsuf's beauty (v. 31): Ibn Kathīr cites the well-known traditions (the "half of beauty" report — see audit in `04-hadith-corpus.md` §2; transmissive footing weaker in the strict 9-books than in later collections).
- On *aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ*: Ibn Kathīr's gloss is brief and stays close to the literal — "the most beautiful (*aḥsan*) of narratives (*al-qaṣaṣ*)" — adducing Companion-statements (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid) but not building the elaborate enumeration that al-Rāzī develops.
- On v. 24's *burhān*: Ibn Kathīr's treatment is more *literalist* than al-Zamakhsharī's; he cites the early ḥadīth (Ibn ʿAbbās via the chain reported in al-Ṭabarī) describing the *burhān* as a vision of Yaʿqūb biting his finger, but Ibn Kathīr also flags the *isrāʾīliyyāt*-flavour of these reports and exercises caution.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī — *al-Durr al-manthūr* + *al-Itqān* (d. 911/1505)

**Sources**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf` (English translation of *al-Itqān*); `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-itqan-word-counts.md` (extracted word-counts).

al-Suyūṭī treats Q 12 in *al-Durr al-manthūr* as a **transmissive compilation** (chains of reports, with minimal authorial voice). In *al-Itqān*:
- al-Suyūṭī classifies Q 12 in the **53rd revealed surah** chronologically (per nawʿ 1, the chronology section). This is the source of the Meccan classification adopted in `00-overview.md`.
- al-Suyūṭī catalogs Q 12's specific *gharīb* (rare lexicon) — words like *jubb* (well), *aḍghāth aḥlām* (confused dreams), *qamīṣ*, *bayyināt al-ḥukm* — many of which are corpus-hapax and confined to Q 12, consistent with the empirical concentration findings (Q012-F-03; `classical-on-yusuf-sijn.md`).
- al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* nawʿ 40 (*al-mutashābih*) lists the structural-symmetric pairs in Q 12 (e.g. v. 18 ↔ v. 83 *fa-ṣabrun jamīl* refrain) without naming them as "ring composition" but cataloging them as *naẓāʾir*.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī — *Naẓm al-durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar* (d. 885/1480)

**Source**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf` (138 MB, 738pp); `raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Biqāʿī's *munāsaba* (verse-and-surah-coherence) approach treats Q 12 as **one of the easiest cases** for *tanāsub*: because the narrative is continuous and single-arc, the *munāsaba* between successive verses is largely temporal-narrative ("then this happened, then that happened"). Where al-Biqāʿī's analysis becomes interesting is in the *long-range* connections:
- v. 4 (dream of stars) ↔ v. 100 (dream's fulfillment in prostration)
- v. 18 (false bloody shirt) ↔ vv. 25–28 (true torn shirt as forensic evidence) ↔ v. 93 (shirt sent to heal Yaʿqūb's eyes)
- v. 18 *fa-ṣabrun jamīl* ↔ v. 83 *fa-ṣabrun jamīl*

al-Biqāʿī also relates Q 12 to its mushaf-neighbours: Q 11 Hūd (multi-prophet polemic preceding Yūsuf's continuous narrative is *contrast-by-form*), Q 13 al-Raʿd (the post-Yūsuf turn to dhikr/celestial signs is *contrast-by-register*). His thesis (the *tanāsub*-of-the-mushaf) is partially borne out empirically: the Q 11→Q 12 transition is one of the cheapest in the corpus (TSP cost 0.035), while Q 12→Q 13 is one of the most expensive (TSP cost 0.216) — see `01-empirical-profile.md` §8. al-Biqāʿī's contrastive *munāsaba* between Q 12 and Q 13 thus has empirical correlates.

**Important caveat**: al-Biqāʿī's broader *munāsaba* program for muqaṭṭaʿāt content-clusters has been **FALSIFIED 4 times** in this project ([[h-new-570-muqattaat-content-cluster]], [[h-new-600-letter-families]]). The local-pair Q 11/12/13 *munāsaba* is empirically supported; the wide muqaṭṭaʿāt content-clustering program is not.

## 8. Themes consolidated across the 7 mufassirūn

### 8.1 Why "*aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ*"?
- Moral / didactic content (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī).
- Rhetorical perfection (al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī).
- Structural symmetry / ring (al-Biqāʿī, al-Suyūṭī's *naẓāʾir*).
- Theological richness (dreams, providence, ʿismat — al-Rāzī's 10 reasons).

**Empirical correlate**: Q 12 ranks **1/114 on `frac_narrative_verses` (67.6%)** and is the only single-protagonist continuous-narrative surah. The narrative-form claim of *aḥsan al-qaṣaṣ* is empirically the most narrative-saturated surah-form in the corpus (Q012-F-01 result).

### 8.2 The ʿAzīz's wife / Zulaykha episode (vv. 23–34)
- **Burhān** — al-Zamakhsharī (rhetorical-figurative); al-Rāzī (ʿismat-preserving); Ibn Kathīr (literalist transmissive); al-Ṭabarī (preservation of conflicting reports without harmonization).
- **Forensic shirt** — al-Qurṭubī (legal-witness analysis), Ibn Kathīr (traditionist).
- **Women's cut hands** — al-Zamakhsharī (rhetorical reading of beauty); al-Rāzī (didactic on female ethics).
- **Yūsuf's preference for prison (v. 33)** — al-Rāzī (the surah's moral epicentre); broadly attested.

The wife's name *Zulaykha* / *Rāʿīl* is **not in the Quranic text**; the name traditions are *isrāʾīliyyāt* (Jewish-source narratives) cited by al-Ṭabarī, al-Suyūṭī (al-Durr al-manthūr), and others, and treated cautiously by Ibn Kathīr.

### 8.3 Dream-interpretation theology
- Three dream-cycles (Yūsuf's, prison-mates', Pharaoh's) — al-Rāzī treats them as the surah's epistemological architecture.
- *Taʾwīl al-aḥādīth* (v. 6, v. 21, v. 101) — al-Zamakhsharī treats this as the surah's *miftāḥ* (key); the gift of dream-interpretation marks Yūsuf as a prophet.
- The dream-fulfillment loop closes at v. 100 (the eleven brothers + parents prostrating, fulfilling v. 4) — al-Biqāʿī's *naẓm* argument's strongest case.

### 8.4 Brothers' jealousy + reconciliation arc
- *kayd* (plot/scheme) is the antagonistic motor — appears 12+ times across the surah (lexical concentration).
- Yaʿqūb's *fa-ṣabrun jamīl* (vv. 18, 83) — the spiritual axis.
- Reconciliation at vv. 89–92: Yūsuf's identity-disclosure speech is one of the most-quoted Quranic conflict-resolution passages.
- *al-yawma lā tathrība ʿalaykum* (v. 92) — "today there is no reproach upon you" — al-Qurṭubī cites this in jurisprudential discussion of forgiveness; it is also the prooftext invoked by the Prophet ﷺ at the *fatḥ Makka* (Mecca's conquest), per the Sīra tradition.
