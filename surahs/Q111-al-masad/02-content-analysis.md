# Q 111 al-Masad — Content Analysis

## 1. Verse-by-verse summary

Source: `quran-no-tashkeel.json` Q111; cross-validated against min-tashkeel and full-tashkeel. QAC roots from `data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`.

### Verse 1 — *tabbat yadā abī lahabin wa-tabb*

- **Roots**: tbb × 2 (V perf 3FS — *tabbat*; V perf 3MS — *tabba*), ydy (N — *yadā* dual nominative), Abw (N — *abī* genitive), lhb (N indef — *lahabin* genitive).
- **Speech-act**: imprecation in perfect tense as prophetic-decree (the curse declared as already accomplished — al-Zamakhsharī's *māḍī al-yaqīn*).
- ***tabbat yadā* construction**: literally "perished the two hands of"; the dual *yadā* is a synecdoche for the whole person (the hands as the agent of action). The repetition *tabbat ... tabba* (frame-verb feminine for *yadā* + closing-verb masculine for the man himself) is a perfect chiastic doubling.
- ***abī lahab* (kunya/laqab)**: *abī lahab* = "father of flame" — the kunya of ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, the Prophet's paternal uncle. Classical reports unanimously identify this individual; the kunya is given as either descriptive (he was reddish-cheeked, fiery-tempered) or as proleptic-divine (the kunya itself anticipates his ultimate destiny — the flame of Hell, picked up in v.3). This kunya-construction is the corpus's only condemnation-by-cognomen of a contemporary individual.
- ***wa-tabb***: the closing imprecation closes the chiasm — *tabbat yadā ... wa-tabb* (perished his hands ... and he himself perished). The 5-word verse uses the tbb root twice, a 40% root-density on a single 5-word verse.
- **Theological function**: divine condemnation pronounced upon a specific contemporary opponent. Frames the surah.

### Verse 2 — *mā aghnā ʿanhu māluhu wa-mā kasab*

- **Roots**: gny (V perf 3MS — *aghnā*), Ean (P — *ʿan*), mwl (N — *māl* "wealth"), ksb (V perf 3MS — *kasab* "earned").
- **Speech-act**: declarative wealth-failure statement.
- ***mā aghnā ʿanhu*** = "did not avail him" — the perfect-tense negation, paralleling v.1's perfect-tense imprecation. The declaration is past-as-already-decided.
- ***māluhu wa-mā kasab*** = "his wealth and what he has earned" — *māl* (the existing inheritance/property) and *mā kasab* (what he has worked-for). The pair covers all forms of acquisition. Classical commentators (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr) note Abū Lahab was famously wealthy; the verse names this wealth as the very thing rendered useless.
- **Theological function**: refutes the wealth-as-shield Meccan paradigm. Abū Lahab's social-position (uncle of the Prophet, wealthy Quraysh notable) does not protect him from divine judgment.

### Verse 3 — *sa-yaṣlā nāran dhāta lahab*

- **Roots**: Sly (V impf 3MS — *yaṣlā* "will burn"), nwr (N — *nāran* "fire"), lhb (N indef — *lahab* "flame").
- **Speech-act**: future-tense damnation prophecy. The *sa-* prefix marks definite future.
- ***sa-yaṣlā nāran*** = "will burn (at) a Fire" — the verb *ṣalā* (here imperfect with *sa-*) is the Quranic verb-of-choice for damnation-burning (cf. Q 4:10, Q 14:29, Q 17:18 etc.).
- ***dhāta lahab*** = "of-flame possessor" / "endowed with flame" — the genitive construction *dhāt* + indefinite *lahabin* is rhetorically marked. The use of *lahab* here picks up *abī lahabin* from v.1: the *flame*-named opponent will burn in *flame*-fire. Classical commentators (al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī) note this as **muṭābaqa rhetorical figure**: the kunya proleptically names the destiny.
- **Theological function**: completes the temporal arc — past-tense imprecation (v.1) → past-tense wealth-failure (v.2) → future-tense damnation (v.3). The surah's first 3 verses span the full temporal scope: certain-past, completed-past, certain-future.

### Verse 4 — *wa-mraʾatuhu ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab*

- **Roots**: mrA (N — *imraʾat* "wife/woman"), Hml (N act ptc fs acc — *ḥammālata* "carrier-female"), HTb (N — *al-ḥaṭab* "the firewood").
- **Speech-act**: descriptive/predicative naming.
- ***wa-mraʾatuhu*** = "and his wife" — *imraʾatuhu* is a pronominally-bound construction; the woman is named via her relationship to Abū Lahab, NOT via her own kunya. The historical referent (per all classical sources: Bukhārī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī) is **Umm Jamīl bint Ḥarb ibn Umayya** — sister of Abū Sufyān, prominent Meccan opponent. She is NOT named directly; she is named via her marriage. (Compare Q 11:71 *imraʾatuhu* = Sarah; Q 28:9 *imraʾatu Firʿawn* = Pharaoh's wife = Āsiya.)
- ***ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab*** = "carrier of the firewood" — the construction is grammatically ambiguous: (a) accusative case (per ʿĀṣim of Kufa and the Hafs reading) attributing this status to her as a "perpetual quality"; (b) nominative case (per other readings, including Ibn Masʿūd's reading) treating *ḥammālatu l-ḥaṭab* as a descriptive noun-phrase. The classical glosses split:
  - Literal-physical: she gathered thorns and firewood and placed them in the Prophet's path to injure him (al-Ṭabarī primary; Ibn Kathīr extended).
  - Metaphorical-defamatory: *ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab* as Arabic idiom for "tale-bearer / slanderer" — *ḥaṭab* (firewood/dry-brush) being figurative for inflammatory speech (al-Zamakhsharī; al-Rāzī notes both).
  - Both: the literal physical-thorns + idiomatic-slander readings co-exist in classical tafsir.
- ***ḥammālata*** is from ROOT:Hml (carrying); active-participle-female ḥammāla (intensive form of carrier). The intensive form (*ḥammāla* not *ḥāmila*) suggests habitual-action, not single-incident.
- **Theological function**: condemns Umm Jamīl as Abū Lahab's complement — her hostile actions are bound into her husband's condemnation. Together they form the named-opponent complete-pair.

### Verse 5 — *fī jīdihā ḥablun min masad*

- **Roots**: jyd (N — *jīd* "neck"), Hbl (N — *ḥabl* "rope"), msd (N — *masad* "palm-fibre").
- **Speech-act**: descriptive terminal-tableau (final visual image).
- ***fī jīdihā*** = "around her neck" — *jīd* is a poetic word for *neck/throat*, distinct from *raqaba* (neck as anatomical) or *ʿunuq* (neck as locus-of-yoke). The QAC root jyd is a **corpus HAPAX** — attested ONLY at Q 111:5 in the entire 114-surah Quran. (Verified `data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`.)
- ***ḥablun min masad*** = "a rope of palm-fibre / twisted strands" — *masad* designates twisted palm-fibre or coarse rope-material; QAC root msd is a **corpus HAPAX** — attested ONLY at Q 111:5 in the entire 114-surah Quran. (Verified.)
- **The double-hapax terminal verse**: Q 111:5 contains TWO corpus-hapax roots (jyd + msd) in a single 4-word verse — a singular-density structural feature.
- **Classical glosses**: literal — a rope of palm-fibre around her neck in Hellfire; or — by which she is dragged to Hellfire; or — the rope she used to gather firewood, transformed in the afterlife into her own neck-burden (al-Suyūṭī *al-Durr al-manthūr*; al-Ṭabarī cites multiple chains). All glosses converge on a punishment-image; the disagreement is mechanism-of-punishment, not directionality.
- **Theological function**: closes the surah on a terminal-tableau — the wife in Hellfire bearing on her neck the very fibre she used to harm the Prophet. The surah ends on a single visual image, not a discursive exhortation.

## 2. Structural decomposition

The 5-verse surah is structurally:

```
v.1: imprecation-decree (PERSONAL CURSE — Abū Lahab named)
v.2: wealth-failure declaration (REFUTATION OF WEALTH-SHIELD)
v.3: future damnation (TEMPORAL DESTINY — flame-fire)
v.4: wife description (PARTNER-IN-CONDEMNATION)
v.5: terminal tableau (ETERNAL VISUAL IMAGE)
```

This is the **named-opponent-condemnation template**:

| Block | Verses | Function |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Personal | v.1-2 | the man + his failed wealth |
| Temporal | v.3 | future damnation |
| Familial | v.4-5 | his wife + her terminal tableau |

The 3-block structure traces a **complete genealogy of failure**: man → his property → his future → his wife → her destiny. No other corpus surah follows this template. Compare:
- Q 104 al-Humaza: categorical condemnation (no named individual)
- Q 109 al-Kāfirūn: declarative-rejection (no curse-formula)
- Q 105 al-Fīl: historical-condemnation of an army (Abraha implicit, NOT named)

## 3. The kunya-construction analysis

Q 111:1 names Abū Lahab via *abī* + *lahab* — a kunya construction using two common nouns (*abī* root Abw "father", *lahab* root lhb "flame"). This is structurally distinct from how all 107 other PN-tagged proper names function in the corpus:

- **Lemma-PN names**: muḥammad, mūsā, ʿīsā, ibrāhīm, etc. — single-lemma PN-tagged tokens.
- **Q 111:1 *abī lahabin***: kunya-pattern, two common-noun lemmas, kunya-bound.

The classical commentary tradition unanimously interprets *abī lahab* as referring to a specific individual (ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib), with the kunya having three classical glosses:
1. **Physical descriptor**: he was reddish-cheeked / had a fiery face (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr).
2. **Proleptic destiny**: the kunya itself names what awaits him (al-Zamakhsharī's *muṭābaqa* reading).
3. **Both** — the reddish-face descriptor was a Meccan kunya the Quran adopts and re-purposes proleptically (al-Rāzī's synthesis).

The kunya use serves a rhetorical function: by NOT using his birth-name *ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā* ("servant of al-ʿUzzā" — the pagan idol), the surah avoids reproducing the polytheistic theonym in scripture. The kunya is therefore both *theologically purifying* (no idol-name in scripture) and *rhetorically efficient* (it preserves identification while binding the curse to the kunya's own etymology — flame).

## 4. Major thematic blocks

Q 111 has a **single thematic block**: the named-opponent-condemnation. The 5 verses are functionally:
- 1 imprecation-decree (v.1)
- 1 wealth-refutation (v.2)
- 1 future-damnation (v.3)
- 1 wife-description (v.4)
- 1 terminal-tableau (v.5)

The single-block structure is consistent with the muʿawwidhāt-tail (Q 112 single-block tawḥīd; Q 113 single-block external-evil-refuge; Q 114 single-block internal-evil-refuge). All four Q 111-114 surahs have single-block structures — a folk-tradition structural commonality.

## 5. Content register

- **creedal-doctrinal**: ~10% (the implicit theology of v.2: wealth-shield refutation)
- **eschatological**: ~40% (v.3 flame-fire; v.5 terminal tableau)
- **biographical-historical**: ~50% (vv.1-4 describe specific historical individuals)
- **legal**: 0%
- **narrative**: 0%
- **wisdom**: 0%
- **ritual**: 0%

This is unusual: Q 111 is the corpus's **most biographical-historical surah by content register**. No other surah is so densely focused on specific contemporary individuals with no narrative-prophet-cycle frame.

## 6. Vocabulary distinctness

15 distinct roots in 15 root-tokens (every root appears exactly once except tbb which is the only doubled root in v.1). Roots:
- Common: ydy, Abw, gny, Ean, mwl, ksb, Sly, nwr, mrA, Hml
- Rare: tbb (4 attestations corpus-wide), Hbl (7 attestations across 5 surahs), HTb (2 attestations only — Q 72:15 and Q 111:4)
- Hapax-corpus: **jyd** (Q 111:5 only), **msd** (Q 111:5 only)
- 2 of 3 lhb-attestations corpus-wide (Q 77:31 has the third)

Of these 15 roots:
- **Corpus-hapax-roots**: 2 (jyd, msd) — both in v.5 alone
- **Corpus-rare-roots** (≤5 attestations): 4 (tbb, Hbl, HTb, lhb)
- **Corpus-frequent-roots**: 9

**Q 111's hapax-density and rare-root concentration in v.4-5 is striking**: 4 of 5 hapax/rare roots cluster in the wife-tableau verses (jyd, msd, HTb, Hbl in vv.4-5; only tbb is in v.1, and lhb spans v.1+v.3). The wife-tableau verses are the surah's lexical-singularity-density zone.

## 7. Repetition patterns

Within the 5-verse, 23-word, 81-letter surah:

- **tbb root** — 2 attestations (v.1: *tabbat* + *tabba*) — the perfect-tense imprecation doubling
- **lhb root** — 2 attestations (v.1: *abī lahabin* + v.3: *lahab*) — the kunya echo / muṭābaqa
- ***mā* particle** — 2 attestations (v.2: *mā aghnā* "did not avail" + *mā kasab* "what he earned") — different grammatical functions (negation vs relative)
- **ب-rhyme** — 4 attestations (vv.1, 2, 3, 4)
- **3rd-person-masc-singular pronominal suffix *-hu*** — 3 attestations (*ʿanhu*, *māluhu*, *imraʾatuhu*) — "his" — anchoring the named-opponent throughout

The **lhb root echo** between v.1 (kunya) and v.3 (his destination — the flame-fire) is the surah's signature *muṭābaqa* (rhetorical correspondence): the named is the burned. The cognomen anticipates the destiny.

## 8. The terminal-tableau v.5

Q 111:5 *fī jīdihā ḥablun min masad* deserves special analytical attention as the **single most lexically-singular verse in Q 111** and one of the corpus's most-distinctive 4-word verses. Its features:

- **Two corpus-hapax roots** in 4 words (jyd, msd) — the corpus's only double-hapax 4-word verse outside the muʿawwidhāt-cluster.
- **No verbs** — the verse is purely nominal/predicative. The image is static; no action is happening, just a state of being.
- **Tableau-form**: opens with locative *fī jīdihā* ("around her neck") — locating the image at a specific anatomical point, a tableau-camera-angle.
- **Indefinite *ḥabl*** ("a rope" — not "the rope") — generality of the punishment, not a specific identified rope.
- **Distinctive rope-material *masad***: in classical Arabic poetry, *masad* designates the twisted palm-fibre used for the coarsest rope (camel-tethers, well-buckets); it is socially-low-status material. The choice of *masad* (not *ḥabl* generic, not *qinab* refined) marks the punishment as ignominy-laden.

The verse is the Quran's most-economical example of *iʿjāz al-tableau*: maximum visual-impact density on minimum lexical material, with both lexemes corpus-singular.

## 9. Cross-surah content references

- **Q 26:214** *wa-andhir ʿashīrataka l-aqrabīn* — the trigger-verse for the Mt. Ṣafā public-warning incident that occasions Q 111 per the Bukhārī chains.
- **Q 11:71** — the only other corpus *imraʾatuhu* in genitive-construct construction (Sarah, wife of Abraham); Q 28:9 has *imraʾatu Firʿawn* (wife of Pharaoh = Āsiya). Both are positive-or-mixed-context. Q 111:4 is the only condemnation-context *imraʾatuhu*.
- **Q 72:15** — the only other corpus HTb (firewood) attestation: *wa-amma l-qāsiṭūna fa-kānū li-jahannama ḥaṭabā* ("As for the deviators, they are firewood for Hell"). Q 72:15 explicates the firewood-of-Hell-fuel theology that Q 111:4 relies on.
- **Q 77:31** — the only other corpus lhb attestation: *lā ẓalīlin wa-lā yughnī mina l-lahab* ("[a shadow that is] neither shading nor protecting from the flame"). Q 77 + Q 111 are the corpus's two surahs with the lhb root in a damnation-context.
- **Q 40:37** — the corpus's only tabāb (tbb-derived noun) attestation: *wa-mā kaydu Firʿawna illā fī tabāb* ("the scheming of Pharaoh is only in ruination"). Q 40:37 + Q 111:1 are the only two corpus-attestations of tbb in opponent-failure context.
- **Q 11:101** — the only corpus tatbīb (tbb-derived noun-of-causation) attestation: *fa-mā zādūhum ghayra tatbīb* ("they only added to them ruin"). With Q 40:37 + Q 111:1+1, this completes the 4 corpus tbb attestations.
- **Q 104** al-Humaza — the corpus's other curse-formula-extreme surah (categorical-class curse vs Q 111's named-individual curse). Q 104 is Q 111's #2 FR-nearest (0.2676); they form the corpus's curse-formula-twin pair.

## 10. The asbāb-al-nuzūl narrative thread

Per multiple Bukhārī chains (#4564, #4595, #4767, #1348; full text in `04-hadith-corpus.md`), the trigger event is:

1. The Prophet receives Q 26:214 *wa-andhir ʿashīrataka l-aqrabīn* (warn your nearest kin).
2. He ascends Mt. Ṣafā in Mecca and calls the Quraysh tribes by name (*yā banī Fihr! yā banī ʿAdī!*).
3. The tribes assemble; the Prophet warns of imminent severe punishment.
4. Abū Lahab, present in the assembly, retorts: *tabban laka, sāʾira l-yawmi! a-li-hādhā jamaʿtanā?* ("May you perish all the day! Is it for this you gathered us?")
5. Q 111 is revealed in direct response, reversing Abū Lahab's curse upon himself.

The asbāb is **chain-extreme** — not just attested but attested with verbatim *match* between the trigger-curse (*tabban laka*) and the Quranic response (*tabbat yadā ... wa-tabb*) using the same root tbb. This is one of the corpus's most-tight asbāb-text correspondences. See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` Claim 2 for chain-quality analysis.

## 11. Honest limits

1. **The 11-day-vs-2-year-vs-multi-decade timeline of Abū Lahab's death**: classical sources report Abū Lahab died ~7 days after Badr (year 2 AH), shortly after his exposure to plague. Q 111 was revealed years before Badr. The intervening period during which Abū Lahab COULD have publicly converted to Islam (and thereby falsified Q 111's prediction) is approximately a decade. He never converted — empirically vindicating the surah's prophetic-decree character. Yet the lengthy interval is sometimes cited (in modern apologetic / counter-apologetic discussion) as a relevant feature; we note it for transparency.
2. **The grammatical-case ambiguity of *ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab*** (v.4): accusative (Hafs Kufan) vs nominative (Ibn Masʿūd) reading. The Hafs reading binds *ḥammālata* as Abū Lahab-receiver-of-action; the nominative reading reads it as a noun-of-respect. We default to Hafs Kufan (rules-tuple).
3. **The literal-vs-idiomatic *ḥaṭab* gloss**: classical disagreement (literal firewood-thorns vs idiomatic tale-bearing). Both are doctrinally compatible; we report both.
4. **The *masad* hapax-status** is at QAC v0.4 root level. Pre-Quranic Arabic poetry attests *masad* extensively (al-Aʿshā, al-Mufaḍḍal Dīwān); the Quran's choice of this word once-only is what is corpus-novel, not the word itself.
