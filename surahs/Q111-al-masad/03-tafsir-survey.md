## 1. al-Ṭabarī (d. 310/923) — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabari-jami-bayan.openiti.raw.txt` (passage extraction pending; cited at level of canonical position).

**Major positions on Q 111**:

- **The opening *tabbat yadā abī lahabin***: al-Ṭabarī catalogues the perfect-tense imprecation as a form of *duʿāʾ* (curse-prayer-decree) which has been actualized — i.e., the perfect tense is "performative", declaring the failure already complete. The *yadā* (dual hands) is treated as synecdoche for the entire person.
- **Identity of *abī lahab***: al-Ṭabarī identifies him as ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, paternal uncle of the Prophet. The kunya *abū lahab* is given as an existing Meccan kunya (per Quraysh genealogical reports), grounded in his physical complexion, but al-Ṭabarī notes the proleptic-fitting reading.
- **The asbāb al-nuzūl**: al-Ṭabarī cites the Mt. Ṣafā chain via Ibn ʿAbbās → Saʿīd b. Jubayr → al-Aʿmash, which is the canonical Bukhārī-Muslim chain. The trigger curse *tabban laka* uttered by Abū Lahab is the precipitating event.
- **v.2 *mā aghnā ʿanhu māluhu wa-mā kasab***: al-Ṭabarī notes the dual *māluhu wa-mā kasab* covers (a) inherited wealth and (b) earned income; per a Mujāhid report, the *wa-mā kasab* phrase specifically denotes "and his sons" — kasab as offspring-acquisition. This Mujāhid gloss (sons = kasab) is widely cited in subsequent tafsir.
- **v.3 *sa-yaṣlā nāran dhāta lahab***: al-Ṭabarī treats this as future-certainty damnation. The phrase *dhāta lahab* explicitly echoes *abū lahab* — al-Ṭabarī cites this as the rhetorical-correspondence point ("the one of flame will burn in flame").
- **v.4 *ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab***: al-Ṭabarī gives PRIMACY to the literal-physical reading (Umm Jamīl gathered firewood / thorns to scatter in the Prophet's path) but catalogues the metaphorical *al-ḥaṭab* = tale-bearing reading as a secondary chain.
- **v.5 *fī jīdihā ḥablun min masad***: al-Ṭabarī catalogues two glosses: (a) the rope of palm-fibre is in Hellfire — a chain dragging her there; (b) it is the rope she habitually used (literal, in this world) which becomes her eschatological-burden. Both are within reported chains.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538/1144) — *al-Kashshāf*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` (passage extraction pending).

**Major positions**:

- **Rhetorical structure**: al-Zamakhsharī highlights the opening *tabbat yadā ... wa-tabb* as a **chiastic doubling** — the perfect-feminine *tabbat* (predicating his hands) and the perfect-masculine *tabba* (predicating him) bracket the verse. This is rhetorical-form *al-ḥazm bi-ʿaks al-uslūb* (emphatic-by-style-reversal).
- ***muṭābaqa*** (rhetorical-correspondence): al-Zamakhsharī explicitly notes the *abū lahab* / *dhāta lahab* correspondence as the surah's signature rhetorical figure. The kunya names the destiny.
- **v.1 perfect-tense as performative**: al-Zamakhsharī treats the perfect-tense imprecation as *māḍī al-yaqīn* — "the perfect of certainty" — i.e., the divine-decree is so settled that it is described as already accomplished. This is consistent with prophetic-curse pronouncements elsewhere (cf. Q 80:17 *qutila l-insānu mā akfarah*).
- **v.4 *ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab***: al-Zamakhsharī GIVES PRIMACY to the metaphorical reading (slander/tale-bearing) — *al-ḥaṭab* as figurative for incendiary speech that ignites discord. This contrasts with al-Ṭabarī's primary literal reading. al-Zamakhsharī notes the literal reading as also reported but secondary.
- **v.5 lexical analysis of *masad***: al-Zamakhsharī's *Kashshāf* is the most-detailed classical analysis of *masad* as twisted-palm-fibre, distinguishing it from *qinab* (refined hemp), *ḥabl* (generic rope), and *tann* (camel-rope). The choice of *masad* is socially-marked: the punishment-rope is the lowliest material.
- **The Muʿtazilite-rationalist frame**: al-Zamakhsharī (consistent with his Muʿtazilite leaning) reads the surah as a complete theological case-study: divine-justice is not partisan (Abū Lahab is the Prophet's uncle yet condemned); divine-judgment depends on action, not on familial-association.

## 3. al-Rāzī (d. 606/1209) — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:

- **The named-opponent uniqueness of Q 111**: al-Rāzī explicitly notes that Q 111 is "the only surah in which a specific opponent is named and condemned" — explicitly asks why this should be so. His answer: Abū Lahab's hostility was so distinct, so kin-internal (uncle of the Prophet), and so public that the surah serves as both (a) a particular-condemnation and (b) a *general principle* — that no kinship-tie protects the unbeliever.
- **The proleptic-naming**: al-Rāzī notes that calling him *abū lahab* (and not ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā) is theologically-justified by avoidance-of-idol-name: the Quran refuses to dignify *al-ʿUzzā* (the pagan idol whose name forms part of his birth-name) by reproducing it. The kunya substitutes.
- **v.2 *kasab* = sons**: al-Rāzī endorses the Mujāhid gloss (*kasab* = offspring) — a position al-Rāzī derives from the Arabic idiom *al-walad min kasb al-wālid* ("the child is from the parent's earning"). This reading explains why the Mujāhid gloss is widespread despite *kasab* normally meaning "earnings". al-Rāzī treats this gloss as theologically-pointed: Abū Lahab's children are also condemned-by-implication.
- **v.3 *dhāta lahab***: al-Rāzī devotes substantial space to the proleptic-rhetorical analysis. The kunya *abū lahab* is itself proleptic; the verse-3 echo *dhāta lahab* makes the prolepsis explicit. al-Rāzī treats this as a complete rhetorical-form: name → destiny matched in kind.
- **v.4 *ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab***: al-Rāzī, building on al-Zamakhsharī, treats the slander-reading as **rhetorically-primary** (since slander is a sin requiring divine condemnation), while accepting the literal-firewood reading as historically-attested. He notes both as *jamʿ al-maʿānī* — meaning-stacking, where the verse activates both readings simultaneously.
- **The 4-stage-decline structure**: al-Rāzī reads vv.1-5 as a 5-step description of Abū Lahab + wife's complete failure: (1) personal failure declared (v.1); (2) economic-failure declared (v.2); (3) future-religious-failure declared (v.3); (4) familial-failure declared (v.4 — his wife is a partner in destruction); (5) terminal-image declared (v.5 — the eternal visual). This is a *kalām* sequence: complete from action through destination.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī (d. 671/1273) — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:

- **Asbāb al-nuzūl**: al-Qurṭubī compiles the Mt. Ṣafā tradition with the same Bukhārī-Muslim chain. He adds the Umm Jamīl reaction-tradition: when she heard Q 111 had been revealed, she came to the Kaʿba bearing a stone to throw at the Prophet — but ʿĀʾisha and Abū Bakr were sitting beside the Prophet, and Allāh hid him from her sight. (Source: secondary chain, not in al-Bukhārī's *Saḥīḥ*, present in al-Wāḥidī *Asbāb al-nuzūl* and al-Ḥākim *al-Mustadrak*.) This is a non-Bukhārī chain and is treated as supplementary.
- **fiqh-implication: condemnation of slander**: al-Qurṭubī's *Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām* extracts a *fiqhī* lesson — that slander (*namīma*, *ghība*) is severely condemned, citing v.4's *ḥaṭab*-tale-bearing reading. This brings Q 111:4 into the corpus of slander-condemnation evidence (alongside Q 49:12, Q 104:1).
- ***mā kasab* = his children**: al-Qurṭubī catalogues the Mujāhid gloss (children) and the al-Ḥasan-al-Baṣrī gloss (his earnings) — both reported.
- **The kunya question**: al-Qurṭubī notes the Quran's avoidance of *ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā* (the polytheist theonym in his birth-name) and instead the use of the kunya. He treats this as a theological-purification rule.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr (d. 774/1373) — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:

- **The full Mt. Ṣafā tradition**: Ibn Kathīr cites the Bukhārī chain (#4564, #4595, #4767), Muslim chain (#7691), and Tirmidhī (#29227) — convergent on the Mt. Ṣafā public-warning incident as the asbāb. He treats this as a strongly-attested asbāb (multiple-chain).
- **The Umm Jamīl tradition**: Ibn Kathīr cites the supplementary Umm Jamīl-with-stone tradition via Ibn Isḥāq's *Sīra* and via al-Bayhaqī. He treats it as historically-supplementary; not chain-required for understanding Q 111.
- **v.1 *yadā***: Ibn Kathīr treats *yadā* (the two hands) as synecdoche-for-the-whole-person.
- **v.2 *mā kasab***: Ibn Kathīr cites both the literal "earnings" and the Mujāhid "children" reading; he notes that the Mujāhid reading is supported by the historical fact that Abū Lahab's named-children (e.g., his sons ʿUtba and ʿUtayba — both of whom had married daughters of the Prophet and divorced them at Abū Lahab's instigation) were prominently identified as opponents of the Prophet, lending the children-reading historical-resonance.
- **v.5 *masad***: Ibn Kathīr cites multiple chains for the *masad* gloss: (a) twisted-iron-rope-in-Hell, (b) the rope she habitually used in this world transformed in the afterlife, (c) the chain by which she will be dragged. He treats these as compatible-multi-chain reports.
- **Death of Abū Lahab**: Ibn Kathīr discusses Abū Lahab's death (~7 days after Badr, year 2 AH) — he died from plague (*ʿadasa*, a pustular disease) and was avoided in death by his own household; his sons buried him by leaning him against a wall and pelting him with stones from a distance. Ibn Kathīr treats this historical detail as fulfilling the surah's eschatological-condemnation in a this-worldly proleptic sense — the failure already attested before final-judgment.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (d. 911/1505) — *al-Durr al-manthūr* + *al-Itqān*

Source-masters: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-durr-manthur.openiti.raw.txt` and `suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:

- ***al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 111**: al-Suyūṭī compiles 50+ asbāb-traditions and gloss-traditions across the canonical 9-book corpus. The Mt. Ṣafā chain is foundational; the Umm Jamīl-stone-tradition is included as supplementary.
- ***al-Itqān* nawʿ on classical surah-names**: al-Suyūṭī catalogues the surah's three classical names — *al-Masad*, *al-Lahab*, *Tabbat* — noting that *al-Masad* is the dominant name in the Mecca/Medina manuscripts but *al-Lahab* and *Tabbat* are alternatively attested.
- ***al-Itqān* nawʿ on asbāb-attribution**: al-Suyūṭī cites Q 111 as the corpus's clearest example of a single-asbāb-attribution surah — the Mt. Ṣafā incident is THE direct-occasion, with no significant alternative chains.
- **The proleptic-naming**: al-Suyūṭī flags this in his *Itqān* discussion of *iʿjāz* — the surah's anticipation of Abū Lahab's continued non-conversion (as the Quran's content presupposes he will never repent) is itself an iʿjāz-marker. Abū Lahab in fact died ~10 years later without converting, despite ample opportunity to falsify the surah's content by professing Islam.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī (d. 885/1480) — *Naẓm al-durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:

- **Munāsaba between Q 110 al-Naṣr and Q 111 al-Masad**: al-Biqāʿī treats this transition as a contrastive-juxtaposition pair: Q 110 declares the *generalized* victory of the Prophet's mission ("when the help of Allah comes and the conquest, and you see people entering Allah's religion in throngs"); Q 111 declares the *particular* failure of one specific opponent (Abū Lahab + his wife). The pair is thematic-contrast: collective-victory (Q 110) → individual-failure (Q 111).
- **Munāsaba between Q 111 al-Masad and Q 112 al-Ikhlāṣ**: al-Biqāʿī treats this transition as the structural pivot from creature-failure to creator-success: Q 111 catalogues the temporal-failure of Abū Lahab; Q 112 declares the eternal-success of God (whose attributes negate any temporal-bounded failure). The transition is from *creature-failure* to *creator-success* — empirically corroborated by the cheap Q 111→Q 112 adjacency cost (rank 89/113).
- **Internal munāsaba within Q 111**: 5 verses, 5 stages of failure-cataloguing, in logical order — personal (v.1) → economic (v.2) → temporal-future (v.3) → familial (v.4) → eternal-image (v.5). al-Biqāʿī presents this as a complete *cataloguing* sequence.
- **Q 111's pair-with-Q 26:214**: al-Biqāʿī notes the asbāb-link between Q 26:214 (the trigger-verse) and Q 111 (the response-surah). This is Q 111's primary cross-reference link in the *Naẓm al-Durar*.

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī (d. 548/1153) — *Majmaʿ al-bayān*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabarsi-majma-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions** (Shīʿī-Imāmī perspective):

- al-Ṭabarsī treats Q 111 as the Quran's clearest case of *muqātalat al-mushrikīn bi-l-fiʿl* (combat against polytheism through name-condemnation). The surah's named-opponent-condemnation has no parallel in the Quran.
- **The Umm Jamīl tradition**: al-Ṭabarsī adds Imāmī-specific transmission chains — the same Umm Jamīl-stone tradition is included via al-Kāfī.
- **v.5 *masad* gloss**: al-Ṭabarsī follows the same gloss-cluster as Sunni tafsir, with no doctrine-specific divergence on Q 111 (the surah's content is not a Sunni-Shīʿī controversy point).

## 9. Convergence and disagreement

### Convergence (all 8 mufassirūn agree)

- **Identity of *abū lahab***: ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, paternal uncle of the Prophet.
- **Asbāb al-nuzūl**: the Mt. Ṣafā public-warning incident (al-Bukhārī chain via Ibn ʿAbbās).
- **Surah is meccan and early-meccan**: revelation order ~6-7 in the Egyptian / al-Suyūṭī standard.
- **The *muṭābaqa* between *abū lahab* (kunya) and *dhāta lahab* (destination)**: classical-rhetorical convergent reading.
- **The named-opponent-uniqueness of Q 111**: al-Rāzī, al-Suyūṭī, and others explicitly note no other surah names a contemporary opponent in this way.

### Disagreement (cross-tafsir disputes)

- **v.4 *ḥammālata l-ḥaṭab*** primary gloss:
  - al-Ṭabarī: literal-physical (firewood/thorns gathering)
  - al-Zamakhsharī: metaphorical (slander/tale-bearing)
  - al-Rāzī: both stacked
- **v.2 *mā kasab*** primary gloss:
  - Mujāhid (via al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr): "his children"
  - Most other reports: "his earnings"
- **v.5 *masad* mechanism**:
  - Eschatological rope dragging her to Hell
  - Rope she used in this world transformed
  - Iron-chain of Hell
  - These are reported parallel-glosses, not strict-disagreements
