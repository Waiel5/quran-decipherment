## 1. Aṣḥāb al-Qarya — "the city" (Q 36:13)

The single most-discussed asbāb-al-nuzūl identification in Q 36's commentary literature is the city-identity of *al-qarya* in Q 36:13. The classical positions:

### 1.1 al-Ṭabarī (Position 1: Antioch is the dominant view)

al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*, Q 36:13 commentary (raw offset ~12,999,483):

> القول في تأويل قوله تعالى: {واضرب لهم مثلا أصحاب القرية إذ جاءها المرسلون * إذ أرسلنا إليهم اثنين فكذبوهما فعززنا بثالث فقالوا: إنا إليكم مرسلون} [يس: 13_14]
> يقول تعالى ذكره: ومثل يا محمد لمشركي قومك مثلا أصحاب القرية ذكر أنها...

al-Ṭabarī then catalogs the views: the city is **Antioch** (the dominant view via Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda) where the apostles came; alternative reports identify two earlier prophet-pairs sent before a third reinforcement.

### 1.2 al-Qurṭubī (Position 2: enumerates 5 candidates)

al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, Q 36 ~ offset 6,622,488 (this is actually al-Qurṭubī's discussion of the *qarya-on-the-coast* tradition placed within the Q 18 al-Kahf section's parallel-discussion; al-Qurṭubī cross-references the Q 36 city in both contexts):

> أنطاكية وقيل: بجزيرة الأندلس، روي ذلك عن أبي هريرة وغيره، ويذكر أنها الجزيرة الخضراء. وقالت فرقة: هي باجروان وهي بناحية أذربيجان. وحكى السهيلي وقال: إنها برقة. الثعلبي: هي قرية من قرى الروم...

al-Qurṭubī enumerates **five candidates**: (1) Antioch (Anṭākiya); (2) the Iberian Peninsula al-Jazīra al-Khaḍrāʾ (per Abū Hurayra); (3) Bājarwān in Azerbaijan; (4) Burqa (per al-Suhaylī); (5) "a town of Byzantium" (per al-Thaʿlabī).

### 1.3 Ibn Kathīr (Position 3: Antioch + apostolic-tradition concordance)

Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*, Q 36:13 (raw offset ~6,787,513). Ibn Kathīr quotes Ibn ʿAbbās via Mujāhid + Qatāda: the city is Antioch, where the messengers were apostles of ʿĪsā. The two emissaries were Yūḥannā (John) and Bawlas (Paul); the third, sent for reinforcement, was Sham‘ūn al-Ṣafā (Simon Peter). Ibn Kathīr notes the dependence on isrāʾīlīyāt-style traditions and the difficulty of verification.

### 1.4 al-Suyūṭī al-Durr (Position 4: hadith-by-hadith catalog)

al-Suyūṭī, *al-Durr al-manthūr*, Q 36:13 (raw offset ~6,797,961):

> وأخرج عبد بن حميد وابن جرير وابن المنذر عن عكرمة رضي الله عنه في قوله {أصحاب القرية إذ جاءها المرسلون} قال: انطاكية

al-Suyūṭī catalogs traditions identifying the city via Ibn Abī Ḥātim, Burayda, ʿIkrima — all converging on Antioch.

### 1.5 al-Biqāʿī (Position 5: structural-narrative reading)

al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-durar*, Q 36:13 commentary (raw offset ~6,706,446):

> ثم قال {وآية لهم الأرض الميتة أحييناها} إلى قوله...

al-Biqāʿī treats the Aṣḥāb-al-Qarya pericope structurally: it is the *exemplum* of the prophet-rejection pattern that the entire surah develops. The Antioch identification is mentioned but secondary; al-Biqāʿī's interest is in the cohesion (*munāsaba*) between Block B (vv. 13-32) and the cosmic-signs Block C (vv. 33-44) as parallel evidentiary catalogs (historical-rejection + cosmological-sign-rejection).

### Empirical correlate

The *aṣḥāb al-qarya = Antioch* identification is the dominant classical view (8 of 9 surveyed mufassirūn report it as the primary or among the candidates). This is **not empirically testable** at the textual level — the text says "*al-qarya*" without naming. The verse-internal evidence (the messengers + the believing-man-from-the-far-side-of-the-city + the *ṣayḥa wāḥida* destruction) is consistent with the apostolic-tradition narrative but does not require it. The classical near-consensus is reported as **traditional-historical** with **NOT-EMPIRICALLY-TESTABLE** verdict — see `05-classical-claims-audit.md` Audit 4.

## 2. *Qalb al-Qurʾān* — the heart-of-the-Qurʾān tradition

Five of the eight surveyed mufassirūn (al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Suyūṭī, al-Ṭabarsī, al-Thaʿlabī = 8 of 9) cite the *qalb al-Qurʾān* tradition. Verbatim citations and grading remarks:

### 2.1 al-Rāzī

al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, Q 36 (raw offset ~12,877,654):

> النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم قال: «إن لكل شيء قلبا وقلب القرآن يس»
> وقال الغزالي فيه: إن ذلك لأن الإيمان صحته بالاعتراف بالحشر، والحشر مقرر في هذه السورة بأبلغ وجه، فجعله قلب القرآن لذلك، واستحسنه فخر الدين الرازي رحمه الله تعالى...

al-Rāzī cites the hadith and **al-Ghazālī's interpretive ground**: "the validity of faith is in confessing the *ḥashr* (resurrection-gathering), and the *ḥashr* is established in this surah with the most-expressive form, so it was made the heart of the Qurʾān for that reason." al-Rāzī endorses al-Ghazālī's reading.

### 2.2 al-Zamakhsharī

al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf*, Q 36 opening (raw offset ~3,289,850):

> قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «إن لكل شيء قلبا، وإن قلب القرآن يس، من قرأ يس يريد بها وجه الله، غفر الله تعالى له، وأعطى من الأجر كأنما قرأ القرآن اثنتين وعشرين مرة، وأيما مسلم قرئ عنده إذا نزل به ملك الموت سورة يس نزل بكل حرف منها عشرة أملاك يقو...»

al-Zamakhsharī cites the *qalb al-Qurʾān* hadith with the **expanded reward-formula** ("the reward of reciting the Qurʾān 22 times" — note: the al-Tirmidhī version says **10 times**; al-Zamakhsharī's source is therefore an *expanded* tradition not in the canonical Tirmidhī text). This *fadāʾil-inflation* pattern is characteristic of post-Tirmidhī popular collections; al-Zamakhsharī does not grade the chain.

### 2.3 al-Qurṭubī

al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ*, Q 36 (raw offset ~8,835,928):

> وذكر النحاس عن ms5163 عبد الرحمن بن أبي ليلى قال: لكل شي قلب وقلب القرآن يس من قرأها نهارا كفي همه ومن قرأها ليلا غفر ذنبه. وقال شهر ابن حوشب: يقرأ أهل الجنة" طه" و" يس" فقط. رفع هذه الأخبار الثلاثة الماوردي...

al-Qurṭubī catalogs **three traditions**: (a) the *qalb al-Qurʾān* with day-vs-night fortune, via ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Abī Laylā (a tābiʿī, *not* a Companion — so this is *mursal*-grade), (b) Shahr b. Ḥawshab's report that "the people of paradise will recite only Ṭāhā and Yāsīn", (c) al-Māwardī's marfūʿ chain via al-Ḍaḥḥāk → Ibn ʿAbbās. al-Qurṭubī does not personally grade these chains but presents them as the classical *fadāʾil*-tradition.

### 2.4 Ibn Kathīr

Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*, Q 2:255 commentary (raw offset 286,639) — Ibn Kathīr cites Yāsīn's *qalb al-Qurʾān* status while discussing āyat al-kursī:

> ويس: قلب القرآن، لا يقرؤها رجل يريد الله، والدار الآخرة إلا غفر له، واقرؤوها على موتاكم — انفرد به أحمد...
> وقد رواه أحمد - أيضا - عن عارم، عن عبد الله بن المبارك، عن سليمان التيمي عن أبي عثمان...

Ibn Kathīr explicitly notes the chain is **transmitted solely by Aḥmad** (انفرد به أحمد) — i.e., not in the *Ṣaḥīḥayn* — and cites the specific Ibn al-Mubārak ← Sulaymān al-Taymī ← Abū ʿUthmān ← his father ← Maʿqil b. Yasār chain. This is the canonical chain found in our hadith corpus at Abū Dāwūd #3122 (idInBook 3122, global id 23626) and Ibn Mājah #1448 (idInBook 1182, global id 31015).

### 2.5 al-Biqāʿī

al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-durar*, Q 36 opening (raw offset ~6,697,490):

> كما قالا النبي (صلى الله عليه وسلم) فيما رواه الترمذي عن أنس رضي الله عنه: " لكل شيء قلب وقلب القرآن يس " ورواه أبو يعلى الموصلي - وهذا لفظه والإمام أحمد في مسنديهما عن معقل بن يسار رضي الله عنه أن رسول الله (صلى الله عليه وسلم) قال " يس قلب القرآن لا يقرأها رجل يريد الله والدار الآخرة..."

al-Biqāʿī cites **two distinct chains**: (a) the al-Tirmidhī chain via Anas (this is Tirmidhī global #28750; idInBook 2970), (b) the Abū Yaʿlā al-Mawṣilī + Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal chain via Maʿqil b. Yasār. Note that the Abū Yaʿlā musnad is post-canonical (3rd c.); the cross-citation establishes the tradition's broad transmission across multiple late-2nd / early-3rd c. collections.

### 2.6 al-Ṭabarsī (Imāmī tafsir)

al-Ṭabarsī, *Majmaʿ al-bayān*, Q 36 (raw offset ~6,121,394):

> وعن أنس بن مالك عن النبي صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم قال: " إن لكل شئ قلبا، وقلب القرآن يس "...
> وروى أبو بصير، عن أبي عبد الله عليه السلام قال...

al-Ṭabarsī cites both the Sunnī Anas-chain (matching Tirmidhī #28750) AND **Imāmī Shīʿī chains** via Abū Baṣīr ← Imām Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq. The Imāmī tradition incorporates the *qalb al-Qurʾān* reading and adds graveyard-recitation merit. al-Ṭabarsī's catalog confirms the tradition's **cross-sectarian** reception in classical Islam.

### 2.7 al-Suyūṭī al-Durr

al-Suyūṭī, *al-Durr al-manthūr*, Q 36 opening (raw offset ~6,780,086):

> وأخرج البزار عن أبي هريرة قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: إن لكل شيء قلبا وقلب القرآن (يس)
> وأخرج الدارمي وأبو يعلى والطبراني في الأوسط وابن مردويه والبيهقي في شعب الإيمان عن أبي هريرة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: من قرأ (يس) في ليلة ابتغاء وجه الله غفر الله له تلك الليلة...

al-Suyūṭī's *Durr* is the most-comprehensive catalog: he traces the *qalb al-Qurʾān* via al-Bazzār ← Abū Hurayra (a different Companion-route from the Tirmidhī Anas-chain or the Aḥmad/Abū Dāwūd Maʿqil-chain), and the night-recitation merit via al-Dārimī + Abū Yaʿlā + al-Ṭabarānī al-Awṣat + Ibn Mardawayh + al-Bayhaqī's *Shuʿab al-īmān*. **Multiple-chain testimony** mitigates the weakness of any single chain.

### 2.8 al-Thaʿlabī

al-Thaʿlabī, *al-Kashf wa-l-bayān*, Q 36 (raw offset ~4,322,355):

> أبي محمد عن مقاتل بن حيان عن قتادة عن أنس: أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم قال: «لكل شيء قلب وإن قلب القرآن (يس) ومن قرأ (يس) كتب الله له بقراءتها قراءة القرآن عشر مرات».

al-Thaʿlabī cites the **same Tirmidhī chain** (Hārūn Abū Muḥammad ← Muqātil b. Ḥayyān ← Qatāda ← Anas) — identifying Hārūn Abū Muḥammad as the chain-key. **This Hārūn Abū Muḥammad is the named weakness in the chain**: classical critics (al-Dāraquṭnī, Ibn al-Jawzī) identify him as ḍaʿīf, and modern critic al-Albānī grades the chain ḍaʿīf jiddan or mawḍūʿ via Hārūn. See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` Audit 1.

### Empirical correlate

The *qalb al-Qurʾān* claim is **theological / liturgical**, not quantitative. The ḥadīth's chain is contested in classical ḥadīth criticism. The project's [[h-new-82-yasin-heart|H-NEW-82]] empirically tested 6 quantitative axes of "heart" and Q 36 ranked top-5 on **0/6** — multi-axis NULL.

What *does* survive is: (a) Q 36 is **moderately content-central** (lexical-centroid rank 18, theme-centroid rank 16); (b) Q 36 **is** the corpus's most-recited (post-Q 1) Meccan surah by classical practice (recitation-on-the-dying + Friday-evening + nightly traditions); (c) Q 36 ranks **top-tier (10/10)** on the project's hadith-fadāʾil rubric ([[h-new-860-hadith-architectural-alignment|H-NEW-860]]). The classical claim's **liturgical-theological content** is empirically intact; only the **multi-axis quantitative-centrality form** of the claim is falsified.

## 3. *Kun fa-yakūn* (Q 36:82) — the climax-verse

Major mufassirūn unanimously treat Q 36:82 as the surah's rhetorical climax.

### 3.1 al-Rāzī on Q 36:82

al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, Q 36:82 commentary: al-Rāzī treats the verse as the **rational sealing-argument** for resurrection: God's creative power is shown by the fact that He says "be" and a thing is. al-Rāzī references the Mu'tazilī-Ashʿarī debate over what "Be" addresses (a thing-not-yet-existent cannot be addressed; therefore the Quranic *kun* is metaphorical-of-power per the Ashʿarī reading).

### 3.2 al-Zamakhsharī on Q 36:82

al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf*, Q 36:82: al-Zamakhsharī (the Mu'tazilī) reads *kun* as the *verb-of-creation-through-pure-divine-fiat* without mediation. The verse's surrounding context (Q 36:78-83) is taken as a **single reasoning-unit**: man-from-a-drop (v.77) → bone-question (v.78) → answer (v.79-81) → climax via *kun* (v.82) → tasbīḥ (v.83).

### 3.3 Ibn Kathīr on Q 36:82

Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr*, Q 36:82: Ibn Kathīr cites Q 2:117 (the first *kun fa-yakūn* in mushaf order) and notes the structural parallelism with Q 36:82. The two verses bracket the *kun* tradition: Q 2:117 in creation-of-heavens-and-earth context; Q 36:82 in resurrection-of-bones context. Ibn Kathīr observes that the corpus uses *kun* exactly to address the **theodicy-of-creation-and-recreation**.

### Empirical correlate

The classical reading "Q 36:82 is the climax" is empirically vindicated at structural-position level: Q 36:82 is the **only one of the 8 corpus *kun fa-yakūn* verses positioned at >95% through its surah** (Q 36:82 = 98.8% of 83 verses; the next-closest is Q 40:68 at 80.0%). See `01-empirical-profile.md` §10 and the Q036-F-03 pre-registration. Verdict: **VINDICATED** at descriptive-position level.

## 4. *Wa-mā ʿallamnāhu al-shiʿra* (Q 36:69) — the anti-poetry verse

al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Biqāʿī all treat Q 36:69 (the "We did not teach him poetry" verse) as a **direct contradistinction** between the Qurʾān and pre-Islamic poetic style. Two complementary readings:

- **Defensive reading** (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr): Q 36:69 responds to the Meccan *shuʿarāʾ*-charge that Muḥammad is a poet (*shāʿir*); the verse asserts categorical non-poethood.
- **Stylistic-rhetorical reading** (al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī): Q 36:69 asserts that the Qurʾān is *not in the style of poetry*; the formal differences are theological, not just attributional.

### Empirical correlate

The classical "Quran is categorically distinct from poetry" claim has been **empirically vindicated at law-strength**:
- [[h-new-740-preislamic-poetry-control|H-NEW-740]] — Quran vs pre-Islamic poetry Fisher-z gap p < 10⁻¹⁰ at the content-rhyme anti-correlation level.
- [[h-new-730-content-rhyme-anticorrelation|H-NEW-730]] — Quran's window-level r(content × rhyme) = −0.86 vs poetry's positive correlation.
- [[cross-finding-007|cross-finding-007]] — Quran differs from 16 classical Arabic meters + 3 prose styles at p < 10⁻⁴ (Bonferroni-19).

**Verdict: VINDICATED at law-strength** (cf. `05-classical-claims-audit.md` Audit 7).

## 5. *Subḥāna alladhī khalaqa al-azwāja kullahā* (Q 36:36) — pairs-creation hymn

al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Zamakhsharī all treat Q 36:36 as the surah's *cosmic-sign tasbīḥ*. al-Rāzī expounds the three pair-classes (vegetable + human + the unknown-pairs) with reference to Q 51:49 (*min kulli shayʾin khalaqnā zawjayn*). al-Zamakhsharī notes the *azwāj* term implies *complementary opposition*, not just *pairs*: night-day, male-female, sun-moon, etc.

The Q 36:36 + Q 36:83 *subḥāna lladhī...kullihi/kulli shayʾ* construction (47 verses apart) is treated by al-Biqāʿī as a **near-ring opening-closing tasbīḥ pair** — the surah is hymn-bracketed.

## 6. *al-Ṣūr* (Q 36:51) — the trumpet

Classical tafsir on the Trumpet (*al-ṣūr*) is rich. al-Ṭabarī traces the term to Ibn Masʿūd's "*qarn*" (horn) reading; Ibn Kathīr cites the Israfīlite tradition that the Trumpet is blown by the angel Isrāfīl; al-Qurṭubī catalogs 12+ ḥadīth on the two-blast (annihilation + resurrection) tradition. The eschatological-trumpet vocabulary cluster (`Swr` root) appears 10× in the corpus per `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json` substring-search; Q 36:51 is one of these 10. See `02-content-analysis.md` §4.

## 7. The 2-letter muqaṭṭaʿāt (Q 36:1)

Classical tafsir on the *yā sīn* opening enumerates ~12 interpretive positions (al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 41 *al-muqaṭṭaʿāt*). Major positions:

- **al-Ṭabarī (Position A — semantic interpretations)**: yā sīn = "yā insān" ("O man") in some Ḥabashī readings; or = "yā sayyid al-mursalīn" ("O master of the messengers"); or = a divine name.
- **al-Rāzī (Position B — *al-asmāʾ al-mukhtaṣṣa*)**: muqaṭṭaʿāt are particular signs / abbreviations of attributes.
- **al-Suyūṭī catalog**: 14+ reported positions including *abjad-letter-numerology* (which the project has empirically falsified — see [[h-new-87-abjad-mashriqi|H-NEW-87]] REFUTED, [[cross-finding-015-classical-scholarship-validation-pattern|cross-finding-015]]).

### Empirical correlate

The project's empirically-supported reading is **muqaṭṭaʿāt as book-introduction markers** ([[cross-finding-008|cross-finding-008]] CONFIRMED; [[cross-finding-012-late-meccan-scripture-announcement|cross-finding-012]] PASS-DIRECTED; [[h-new-46-muqattaat-vs-surah-length|H-NEW-46]] correlation with surah length). Q 36's يس opening is consistent with this reading: a 2-letter book-introduction marker for a Meccan resurrection-and-prophet-rejection narrative.

The semantic-interpretation positions (Position A) are **not testable** empirically. The numerological-interpretation positions are **falsified** under the project's rules-tuple discipline.
