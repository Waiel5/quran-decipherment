## 2. Q 21:1 — *iqtaraba li-l-nāsi ḥisābuhum*

- **al-Ṭabarī**: gloss "the reckoning has approached"; reads *al-nās* generally (all humanity), with the implicit referent being the unbelievers of Mecca for whom the reckoning is the immediate eschatological warning. (al-Ṭabarī's habit: explain by parallel — Q 16:1, Q 54:1.)
- **al-Rāzī**: discusses why "approaching" applies to an event that has not yet occurred; the rhetorical figure of *taqrīb al-baʿīd* (bringing the distant near) — emphasis on certainty, not on temporal proximity. Cross-references Q 54:1 *iqtarabati al-sāʿatu* as the structural twin opening.
- **al-Qurṭubī**: reports the *asbāb al-nuzūl* attributed to Ibn ʿAbbās — surah revealed when the Quraysh disbelievers were dismissive of the Hour. Notes the *ḥisāb* here is the eschatological reckoning (judgment day), not the dunyawī *ḥisāb* of v. 47 (the just scales).
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: stylistic note — the topical prominence of *li-l-nās* (general humanity) before the specifying *ḥisābuhum* — gives the verse its universal-warning rhetorical force.
- **al-Biqāʿī** (*Naẓm al-durar*): **munāsabah** between Q 20 (closing on *fa-tarabbaṣū*: "wait, then") and Q 21 (opening on *iqtaraba*: "the reckoning has drawn near") — the wait is over, the reckoning approaches. Q 20 → Q 21 → Q 22 munāsabah triad: Q 20 ends "wait"; Q 21 opens "approaches"; Q 22 opens *yā ayyuhā al-nāsu it-taqū rabbakum* "fear your Lord". Eschatological tightening across three surahs.

## 3. Q 21:22 — burhān al-tamānuʿ

*law kāna fī-himā āliha illā Allāh la-fasadatā*

- **al-Ṭabarī**: classical reading — if the heavens and earth had multiple gods, they would be corrupted. Cites the exegetical tradition that links this verse to the kalām *burhān al-tamānuʿ* argument.
- **al-Rāzī**: the *locus classicus* for the rational-theological *burhān al-tamānuʿ*. al-Rāzī devotes an extended *masʾala* to this verse, deriving four sub-arguments: (1) two omnipotent wills cannot coexist over one object; (2) joint sovereignty is logically incoherent; (3) cosmic order presupposes singular causation; (4) the absence of corruption confirms unity. al-Rāzī treats Q 21:22 as the Quranic foundation of the whole rational-tawḥīd kalām edifice.
- **al-Qurṭubī**: aligns with al-Ṭabarī; adds that the verse is the *aṣl* (foundation) of the *burhān al-tamānuʿ* developed by the muʿtazila and adopted by ahl al-sunna.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: stylistic note on the conditional *law* — counterfactual, implying the consequent is impossible because the antecedent is impossible.
- **al-Biqāʿī**: connects Q 21:22 to Q 21:25 — the two together establish the surah's *tawḥīd*-axis: rational tawḥīd (v. 22) plus revelational tawḥīd (v. 25, "every prophet revealed: there is no god but Me").

## 4. Q 21:25 — universal-revelation tawḥīd

*wa-mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin illā nūḥī ilayhi annahu lā ilāha illā anā fa-ʿbudūn*

- **al-Ṭabarī**: every prophet without exception conveyed the same tawḥīd-message; the diversity is in *sharīʿa*, not in *ʿaqīda*.
- **al-Rāzī**: notes this verse is the doctrinal-architectural anchor of the entire prophet-cycle in vv. 48–91 — by stating the rule (every prophet → tawḥīd), the surah implicitly frames each named prophet as an instance of the rule.
- **al-Qurṭubī**: reports the disagreement on whether the reference is to all prophets (*anbiyāʾ*) or only messengers (*rusul*); the consensus reading takes *rasūl* in the broad sense covering both classes for the purpose of this universal claim.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: focuses on the strong preposition *illā* + the hortative *fa-ʿbudūn* — the two together produce a universal-modal claim ("every messenger, without exception, was revealed *to worship Me*").
- **al-Biqāʿī**: ⭐ the architectural function — Q 21:25 is the **thesis** that the prophet-catalog (Block E, vv. 48–91) is the **proof of**. The catalog is structured around this thesis: each prophet is presented as one instance of the universal-revelation rule.

## 5. Q 21:30 — heavens-earth-separation cosmological iʿjāz

*a-wa-lam yara alladhīna kafarū anna al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa kānatā ratqan fa-fataqnā-humā wa-jaʿalnā mina al-māʾi kulla shayʾin ḥayyin*

- **al-Ṭabarī**: cites multiple early reports — (a) the heavens-and-earth were one mass that God split (the *fatq* of cosmic origin); (b) the heavens were rainless and the earth plantless until God split-them-open (the *fatq* of meteorological/agricultural cycle). Both readings recorded; the cosmic-origin reading is dominant in the early period.
- **al-Rāzī**: extended treatment — the *ratq* / *fatq* pair as cosmological terminology. Discusses several *masāʾil*: (1) the meaning of "joined entity" — one mass vs concentric layers; (2) the connection to the modern philosophical / scientific cosmology of the time (the *Sabian* and *Greek* cosmologies); (3) the rhetorical force of *a-wa-lam yara* (a presupposition of common knowledge). al-Rāzī treats this verse as a key *iʿjāz al-mafāhīm* exemplar — the verse states a truth verifiable by reflection.
- **al-Qurṭubī**: the *ratq* / *fatq* lexicon discussion; reports of Ibn ʿAbbās favoring the cosmic-origin reading; aligns with al-Ṭabarī.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: stylistic — the *kānatā* dual (singular subject *al-samāwāt-wa-l-arḍ* is *grammatically* dual under *kāna*), the *fa-* of consequence, the *kulla shayʾin ḥayyin* generality.
- **al-Biqāʿī**: 4-verse cosmological cluster (vv. 30–33) is *one tartīb*: the *ratq-fatq* (v. 30), the *rawāsī* (v. 31), the *saqfan maḥfūẓan* (v. 32), the *kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn* (v. 33). Four cosmological signs in four consecutive verses. al-Biqāʿī notes the *naẓm* (composition) is itself an iʿjāz-marker. Q021-F-04 tests this empirically.

al-Bāqillānī (*Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*) is the locus-classicus for citing Q 21:30 as iʿjāz al-mafāhīm. This citation is widely attributed in the secondary literature though specific page-numbers are not VERIFIED in the on-disk al-Bāqillānī edition.

## 6. The prophet catalog (vv. 48–91) — exegetical positions

- **al-Ṭabarī**: each prophet's episode rendered in narrative prose with cross-references to earlier surahs (Q 6, Q 11, Q 12, Q 19, Q 26, Q 37). Q 21 is the *condensed* version; other surahs the *expanded*.
- **al-Rāzī**: focus on theological lessons each prophet exemplifies — Mūsā = the Furqān (criterion); Ibrāhīm = rational tawḥīd against idolatry; Lūṭ = moral judgment; Nūḥ = the great rescue; Dāwūd-Sulaymān = wisdom-and-kingship; Ayyūb = patience-and-restoration; Yūnus = repentance; Zakariyyā-Yaḥyā = continuity. al-Rāzī reads the catalog as an *abstract typology* — each prophet a *theological category*.
- **al-Qurṭubī**: detailed *asbāb al-nuzūl* for individual episodes (the Ibrāhīm-fire, the Sulaymān-jinn, the Ayyūb-affliction). Q 21:78 (Dāwūd-Sulaymān-flock-judgment) gets extensive *aḥkām* treatment.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: stylistic — the *wāw* of opening for each prophet (*wa-Nūḥan*, *wa-Dāwūda wa-Sulaymāna*, *wa-Ayyūba*, *wa-Ismāʿīla wa-Idrīsa*) creates a rhythmic catalog-form. The *wāw* is the catalog's structural device.
- **al-Biqāʿī**: ⭐ the catalog is the **proof** of Q 21:25. Each *wāw*-headed entry is one inductive instance of the universal claim. The catalog culminates in v. 91 (Maryam + son as a *sign*) and v. 92 (the *umma*-unity claim) — the catalog's punctuation as a single tawḥīd-and-mercy thesis.

## 7. Q 21:107 — *raḥmatan li-l-ʿālamīn*

- **al-Ṭabarī**: the *ʿālamīn* are all the worlds (humans + jinn at minimum, possibly all created realms). The *raḥma* (mercy) is the prophet himself — i.e., the Prophet's mission is itself mercy. al-Ṭabarī's gloss: not just *containing* mercy, but *being* mercy.
- **al-Rāzī**: detailed treatment of the universalism — *li-l-ʿālamīn* is universal (all peoples, all eras), establishing the Prophet's mission as the final, comprehensive *raḥma*. Cross-references Q 7:158 (universal Prophet); Q 34:28 (sent to all mankind).
- **al-Qurṭubī**: reports the *Ḥakam al-Ḥasanī* tradition that this verse is the most-cited single Quranic verse in arguments for the universality of the Prophetic mission.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: rhetorical — the verse is grammatically marked by the limiting *illā* + the universal *li-l-ʿālamīn*; the negation-+-universal-affirmation creates the strongest possible scope claim.
- **al-Biqāʿī**: closes the surah's universal-mercy frame — the surah opens on universal-reckoning (v. 1) and closes on universal-mercy (v. 107). Inclusio of universalism. al-Biqāʿī marks this as a *naẓm*-closure.

## 9. Disagreements

- *Ratq*/*fatq* in v. 30: cosmic-origin (Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Ṭabarī) vs meteorological-cycle (some early reports). Modern reception strongly favors cosmic-origin.
- The scope of *rasūl* in v. 25: messengers only vs all prophets. Consensus reads broadly.
- The status of Dhū-l-Kifl (v. 85): some classical sources identify him with Ezekiel; al-Qurṭubī reports the disagreement without resolving.
- The identity of the unnamed prophets — particularly v. 91 ("she who guarded her chastity" = Maryam, with her son ʿĪsā unnamed in the verse).
