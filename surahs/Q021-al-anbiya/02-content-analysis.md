# Q 21 al-Anbiyāʾ — Content Analysis

## 1. Verse-by-verse content blocks

### Block A — Eschatological warning + mocking (vv. 1–10)
- v. 1: *iqtaraba li-l-nāsi ḥisābuhum* — direct opening, eschatological imminence.
- vv. 2–5: revelation as fresh reminder (*muḥdath*); disbelievers' mocking (he is just a poet, a man like us).
- vv. 6–10: prior destroyed nations; messengers always sent men who ate food; reminder that the Quran is a *dhikr*.

### Block B — Cosmic sovereignty + tawḥīd architecture (vv. 11–25)
- vv. 11–18: more destroyed peoples; God did not create the heavens and earth in jest.
- vv. 19–20: angels in unwearying tasbīḥ — the angelology baseline.
- v. 22: *law kāna fī-himā āliha illā Allāh la-fasadatā* — "if there were gods other than Allah in them [heavens and earth], they would have been corrupted." This is the *burhān al-tamānuʿ* (argument-from-mutual-impedance) classical mutakallimūn cite as the rational tawḥīd-proof.
- v. 25: ⭐ ARCHITECTURAL VERSE — *wa-mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin illā nūḥī ilayhi annahu lā ilāha illā anā fa-ʿbudūn* — "We never sent before you any messenger except that We revealed to him: there is no god but Me, so worship Me." This verse establishes the surah's core thesis: **all prophets, across all eras, carry the same tawḥīd-message**. The prophet-catalog that follows is the empirical proof of this thesis.

### Block C — Cosmological iʿjāz cluster (vv. 30–33) ⭐
- v. 30: *a-wa-lam yara alladhīna kafarū anna al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍa kānatā ratqan fa-fataqnā-humā* — "Have those who disbelieved not seen that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, and We separated them, and made every living thing from water." Classical iʿjāz reference (al-Bāqillānī *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*; al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*).
- v. 31: mountains as *rawāsī* (anchors) so the earth does not sway.
- v. 32: heaven as *saqfan maḥfūẓan* (protected canopy).
- v. 33: the day, night, sun, moon — *kullun fī falakin yasbaḥūn* (each in an orbit, swimming).

This 4-verse cluster is the mid-Quran's densest cosmological-iʿjāz passage. Q021-F-04 tests whether vv. 30–33 form a distinct internal sub-cluster by lexical cohesion vs the rest of Q 21.

### Block D — Mortality + judgment (vv. 34–47)
- v. 35: every soul tastes death.
- v. 47: *al-mawāzīna al-qisṭa li-yawmi al-qiyāma* — the just scales of judgment day.

### Block F — Eschatological signs + mercy + closing (vv. 92–112)
- vv. 92–93: one *umma*, but they split.
- vv. 96–97: Yaʾjūj wa-Maʾjūj let loose; the true promise approaches.
- v. 105: *kataba-nā fī al-zabūri min baʿdi al-dhikri anna al-arḍa yarithu-hā ʿibādiya al-ṣāliḥūn* — "We have written in the Zabūr after the Dhikr that the earth shall be inherited by My righteous servants." A direct intra-Quranic reference to the Psalms (Zabūr).
- v. 107: ⭐ *wa-mā arsalnāka illā raḥmatan li-l-ʿālamīn* — "We sent you only as a mercy to all worlds." Most-cited universal-mercy verse in the Quran.
- v. 112: closing prayer: *qāla rabbi-ḥkum bi-l-ḥaqqi wa-rabbu-nā al-Raḥmān al-mustaʿānu ʿalā mā taṣifūn* — the same lexical key Q 21 opens with (*rabb*-judgment) and closes with.

## 2. Major thematic blocks

| Block | Verses | n | Theme | Lexical key |
|:--|:--|:--:|:--|:--|
| A | 1–10 | 10 | eschatology + mocking | *ḥisāb*, *dhikr*, *ghafla* |
| B | 11–25 | 15 | cosmic sovereignty + tawḥīd | *samāwāt*, *lā ilāha illā Allāh* |
| C | 26–47 | 22 | angels + cosmology + judgment | *malāʾika*, *raqq*-and-*fatq*, *mawāzīn* |
| **E (peak)** | 48–91 | 44 | **prophet catalog** | prophet PN names |
| F | 92–112 | 21 | eschat-signs + mercy | *Yaʾjūj*, *raḥmatan li-l-ʿālamīn* |

44 of 112 verses (39 %) are in the prophet-catalog block — a structurally exceptional concentration. By comparison, Q 26 al-Shuʿarāʾ has multiple shorter prophet-blocks; Q 11 Hūd has a single sustained block; Q 12 Yūsuf is an entirely-Yūsuf surah.

## 3. Content register

Q 21 is **mixed-but-prophet-narrative-dominated**:
- 39 % prophet-catalog (block E)
- 19 % cosmology (block C)
- 19 % eschatology (block F)
- 13 % cosmic-sovereignty (block B)
- 9 % opening-warning (block A)

This makes Q 21 unique among the major prophet-cycle surahs: it embeds an unusually broad prophet catalog inside a cosmological-eschatological frame, both opening (v. 1 reckoning) and closing (v. 107 mercy) on universal-scope claims.

## 4. Vocabulary distinctness

Q 21 has 284 distinct STEM roots in 1,236 words = 0.230 root-density. Compare:
- Q 12 Yūsuf: ~0.220 (continuous narrative compresses vocabulary).
- Q 6 al-Anʿām: ~0.250 (more dispositional / theological prose).
- Q 26 al-Shuʿarāʾ: ~0.218.

Q 21 is mid-density — not lexically rich (per the prophet-cycle compression) and not lexically thin.

## 5. Repetition patterns

The catalog block (vv. 48–91) repeats a structural refrain:
- *wa-X idh nādā rabbahu* / *wa-ātaynā X*: each prophet introduced by either a calling-out-to-the-Lord formula or a granting-by-God formula.
- Each block ends with a 1-2-verse summary (*kullun min al-ṣāliḥīn*, *fa-najjaynāhu*).
- vv. 73, 90: corpus-level matched: *kānū lanā khāshiʿīn* / *kānū lanā khāshiʿīn* — identical close.

## 6. Cross-surah content references

- Q 21:30 (cosmology) ↔ Q 41:11 (heavens-and-earth as smoke); Q 51:47 (heavens built with might).
- Q 21:25 (universal tawḥīd-revelation) ↔ Q 16:36 (every nation had a messenger calling to Allah).
- Q 21:107 (mercy-to-the-worlds) ↔ Q 7:158 (universal Prophet for all peoples); Q 34:28 (sent to all mankind).
- Q 21:48 (Mūsā + Hārūn → al-Furqān) ↔ Q 25:1 (al-Furqān as title); Q 2:53 (the Furqān to Mūsā).
- Q 21:51 (Ibrāhīm + idol-fire trial) ↔ Q 6:74–84 (Ibrāhīm's argument with father); Q 37:83–98 (the fire-trial again).
- Q 21:78–82 (Sulaymān + jinn divers) ↔ Q 27:17, 38–40 (Sulaymān + jinn carrying the throne); Q 34:12–14 (the wind, jinn under Sulaymān).
- Q 21:87 (Dhū-l-Nūn) ↔ Q 37:139–148 (Yūnus narrative explicit); Q 68:48–50 (companion-of-the-fish).

## 7. The prophet-cycle template question

Q 21's prophet-order is *not* the Q 6:83-87 order. Q021-F-02 tests whether Q 21 is closer to other prophet-cycles (Q 11 Hūd, Q 26 al-Shuʿarāʾ, Q 37 al-Ṣāffāt) than to Q 6.

Empirical observation locked into Q021-F-02: Q 6 places the patriarchal triad first (Ibrāhīm → Isḥāq → Yaʿqūb → Nūḥ → David-Solomon); Q 21 places Mūsā/Hārūn first (vv. 48–50), then Ibrāhīm (vv. 51–73), then a temporally-mixed catalog. The two orderings are radically different.
