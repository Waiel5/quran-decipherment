# Q 10 Yūnus — Tafsir survey

Pre-flight: tafsir extracts produced by `surahs/Q010-yunus/scripts/extract_q010_tafsir.py` from the OpenITI raw files. Outputs at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/{tafsir}-openiti-Q010.txt`.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabari-openiti-Q010.txt` (lines 178484-182933 of the unified raw, 4,449 lines).

### Key positions

- **On v. 1 الر**: al-Ṭabarī compiles multiple Companion / Tābiʿ readings: Ibn ʿAbbās (variant: "I am God who sees"); Mujāhid (the Torah and the Gospel); Qatāda (the previous Books); plus al-Ṭabarī's own metalinguistic position (these are letters whose meaning is known only to God; see also al-Suyūṭī's *Itqān* nawʿ 40 for the convergent classical conclusion).

- **On v. 62 *awliyāʾ Allāh***: al-Ṭabarī gives extensive isnād chains (`tabari-openiti-Q010.txt` line 2244 onward). Key transmitted views:
  - Ibn ʿAbbās: "those who, when seen, remind one of God" — citing the canonical hadith.
  - Saʿīd b. Jubayr: connects to silent dhikr and visual recognition.
  - The verse establishes the *waliāya* concept: walāya = mutual-protective-relationship between God and the believer-pious.

- **On v. 98 *qawm Yūnus***: al-Ṭabarī cites Qatāda + Saʿīd b. Jubayr + Mujāhid: the people are *ahl Naynawā* (Nineveh, in modern Mosul region of Iraq). Their repentance was unique in salvation history. al-Ṭabarī explicitly cross-references Q 37:139-148 for the full Yūnus narrative.

- **On v. 94 *fa-in kunta fī shakkin***: al-Ṭabarī gives both readings — addressed-to-Muḥammad reading (which all classical mufassirūn deny was literal doubt) and the *iltifāt*-reading (the addressee shifts to the listening community). al-Ṭabarī favours the latter.

### Methodological notes

al-Ṭabarī is the foundational riwāya-tafsir layer; his Q 10 commentary is among the longest in Q 10 literature (~4,400 lines for ~109 verses ≈ 40 lines/verse). He treats Q 10 as a doctrinal-polemical surah with two narrative anchors (Mūsā vv. 75-93; Yūnus's people v. 98).

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-openiti-Q010.txt` (1,328 lines).

### Key positions

- **Verse count and revelation**: "Q 10 is Meccan, except verses 40, 94, 95, and 96 which are Medinan; 109 verses; revealed after Q 17 al-Isrāʾ" (`zamakhshari-openiti-Q010.txt` lines 2-3). This is one of the major attested Meccan/Medinan-mixing claims for Q 10.

- **On v. 1 الر**: "the listing of the letters [is] by way of *taḥaddī* (challenge)" — al-Zamakhsharī's signature interpretation: the muqaṭṭaʿāt are themselves the inimitability proof, demonstrating that the Quran is composed of the very letters that the Arabs use, yet they cannot reproduce it.

- **On v. 62 *awliyāʾ Allāh***: "the awliyāʾ of God are those who are devoted to Him by obedience and to whom He is devoted by honour. This has been explained in the verse *those who believed and were pious* — that is their devotion to Him; *they have good news in this world and the next* — that is His devotion to them" (`zamakhshari-openiti-Q010.txt` lines 785-787). al-Zamakhsharī's mutual-walāya formula is the canonical Muʿtazilī gloss.

- **On v. 38 *fa'tū bi-sūratin mithlihī***: al-Zamakhsharī treats this as the *taḥaddī* per excellence; the one-surah threshold (vs Q 2:23's *bi-sūratin min mithlihī*) is the clearest demonstration of inimitability.

### Distinctive emphasis

al-Zamakhsharī's Q 10 commentary is grammatical-rhetorical (*balāgha*-rich). He emphasises the Quran's *iʿjāz al-bayān* throughout — appropriate given Q 10 is named after the prophet whose people responded to a divine warning, paralleling how the Arabs are being challenged to respond to the Quran's challenge.

## 3. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-openiti-Q010.txt` (3,175 lines).

### Key positions

- **On the surah's overall structure**: al-Rāzī sees Q 10 as a **theological argument-text**. The opening establishes God's *ḥikma* (the Book is "wise"); the body argues for tawḥīd via cosmic signs, against shirk via critique of intercessor-doctrine, against denial-of-resurrection via parable, against *iftirāʾ*-charge via the tahaddī, and finally validates the Prophetic mission via the prophet-narrative anchors.

- **On v. 5 *huwa alladhī jaʿala al-shamsa ḍiyāʾan***: al-Rāzī distinguishes *ḍiyāʾ* (intrinsic light) from *nūr* (reflected/dispersive light), noting this matches the ancient astronomical view that the moon reflects sunlight. Classical *iʿjāz ʿilmī* anchor.

- **On v. 38 the tahaddī**: al-Rāzī enumerates the Quran's inimitability through (a) eloquence, (b) prophecy fulfilment, (c) coherence-without-revision, (d) doctrinal correctness. He treats Q 10:38's challenge as the canonical iʿjāz-anchor, and as evidence that the surah's claim *al-Kitāb al-Ḥakīm* is self-warranting.

- **On v. 62 *awliyāʾ Allāh***: al-Rāzī devotes substantial space to the question "who are the *awliyāʾ Allāh*?" — surveying Sufi, Muʿtazilī, and Ashʿarī positions. His own preferred reading: the *awliyāʾ* are those whose hearts achieve *yaqīn* (certainty) such that fear and grief cannot reach them. He links to Q 41:30 (*alladhīna qālū rabbunā Allāh thumma istaqāmū*) for the *istiqāma* parallel.

### Methodological notes

al-Rāzī is the structural-rhetorical-theological layer. His Q 10 commentary is approximately 2× the verse count in word density — heavy interpretive load on the polemical and theological verses.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-openiti-Q010.txt` (~2,085 lines).

### Key positions

- **Legal extractions (typical for al-Qurṭubī)**: from Q 10, primarily on *istiqāma* (v. 105 *aqim wajhaka li-l-dīn*), *intiẓār-of-judgment* (v. 102), *isnād*-of-prophet-status (v. 94 — *fa-s'al alladhīna yaqraʾūna al-kitāba min qablika*).

- **On v. 62**: al-Qurṭubī supplies a full chain of hadith on the *awliyāʾ* including the *taḥābbū fī Allāh* (mutual-love-in-God) traditions and the "fortyfour-or-seventy parts of prophecy" hadith on the *ruʾyā ṣāliḥa* (good vision). Maps to Bukhārī, Muslim, Abū Dāwūd attested chains.

- **On v. 98**: al-Qurṭubī notes the geography (Naynawā = Mosul region), the timeline (the people repented over forty days of supplication; *labisu al-musūḥ wa-farraqū bayna kulli bahīma wa-waladihā*), and Yūnus's own subsequent return (referencing Q 37, Q 21, Q 68).

### Distinctive emphasis

al-Qurṭubī's Mālikī-jurisprudential framework leads him to extract Q 10:62-64 as a foundational *waliāya* doctrine source. The juristic implications include: who counts as a walī, evidentiary weight of pious dreams, and the legitimacy of the *ruʾyā ṣāliḥa* as *al-mubashshirāt*.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-openiti-Q010.txt` (1,520 lines).

### Key positions

- **Opening**: ibn Kathīr immediately identifies Q 10 as Meccan and gives the muqaṭṭaʿāt cross-reference back to al-Baqara. He cites Mujāhid's "Torah and Gospel" and Ibn ʿAbbās's "I am God who sees" readings as transmitted variants.

- **On v. 5 *huwa alladhī jaʿala al-shamsa ḍiyāʾan***: ibn Kathīr's natural-theological emphasis: the regularities of the celestial bodies (sun's ḍiyāʾ, moon's nūr, ḥisāb, manāzil) are signs for those who reason; cross-references Q 36:39-40, Q 6:96, Q 38:27, Q 23:115-116.

- **On v. 62 *awliyāʾ Allāh*** (`ibn-kathir-openiti-Q010.txt` lines 885-979): ibn Kathīr supplies the most extensive hadith-chain catalogue of any Q 10 mufassir on this verse. Key chains:
  - The Prophet's reply to "who are the awliyāʾ Allāh?" — "those who, when seen, remind one of God" (al-Bazzār chain via Saʿīd b. Jubayr ← Ibn ʿAbbās).
  - Ibn Jarīr's chain via Abū Hurayra: the Prophet said "among God's servants are servants whom even the Prophets and martyrs envy... they will be on platforms of light, not afraid when others fear, not grieving when others grieve" — and the Prophet then recited Q 10:62.
  - The *ruʾyā ṣāliḥa* tradition for the *bushrā fī al-ḥayāti al-dunyā* of v. 64: ʿUbāda b. al-Ṣāmit (in Aḥmad), Abū al-Dardāʾ (in Ibn Jarīr), Abū Dharr (in Muslim) — the good vision is "one of forty-four / seventy parts of prophecy" and constitutes the worldly portion of the awliyāʾ's good news.

- **On v. 98 *qawm Yūnus*** (`ibn-kathir-openiti-Q010.txt` lines 1408-1446): ibn Kathīr presents the standard classical narrative — the people of Nineveh sensed punishment imminent, donned haircloth, separated young from mothers (animals included), prayed for forty nights; God in His mercy averted the punishment. Cites Qatāda, Ibn Masʿūd, Mujāhid, Saʿīd b. Jubayr. Notes Ibn Masʿūd's non-canonical reading *fa-hallā kānat qaryatun āmanat*.

### Methodological notes

ibn Kathīr's Q 10 is hadith-citation-rich, exegetically conservative, with cross-Quranic anchoring. He is the central reference for the Q 10:62 → hadith mapping and Q 10:98 → Q 37:139-148 cross-link.

## 6. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-openiti-Q010.txt` (2,772 lines).

### Key positions

- **The surah's *maqṣūd*** (`biqai-openiti-Q010.txt` lines 1-13): "the *maqṣūd* of Q 10 is to describe the Book as being from God, on the basis of the wisdom it contains; this is impossible without a singular [divine] author, since no other being can produce anything of its like. The decisive proof of this is the story of Yūnus's people: when they believed [in response to the warning], the warning was lifted from them — proof that the One who brought the warning is the same God in whom they believed; for had it been other than Him, their belief would have caused affliction."

This is the **clearest classical answer to "why is Q 10 named after Yūnus"**: the surah's argumentative climax is the *qawm Yūnus* episode. The narrative proportion (1 verse) is irrelevant; the THESIS-naming model holds.

- **Munāsabāt with Q 9**: al-Biqāʿī (`biqai-openiti-Q010.txt` lines 50-75 ff., quoting Abū Jaʿfar Ibn al-Zubayr) treats Q 10 as a thematic continuation of Q 9 al-Tawba: where Q 9 announced the divine *barāʾa* against the polytheists with comprehensive rights to fight them, Q 10 establishes the doctrinal foundation by re-asserting the divine-unity claim that justifies the dispossession.

- **Munāsabāt with Q 11 Hūd**: Q 10 ends with the prophet-narratives compressed; Q 11 begins with them expanded. Q 10's role is the polemical-frame; Q 11's role is the elaborated narrative-evidence.

### Distinctive emphasis

al-Biqāʿī's Q 10 commentary is the locus classicus for the *thesis-naming* interpretation of Q 10's eponymity. Empirical correspondence: Q010-F-01 (yūnus token concentration: only 1× in Q 10) is consistent with this reading. The surah is named for its *maqṣūd*-completing argument, not for its dominant subject matter.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr*

Sources:
- `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-itqan-openiti-Q010.txt` (filtered for Yūnus-mentions, 34 lines).
- `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-durr-openiti-Q010.txt` (1,408 lines).

### Key positions from *al-Itqān*

- **Verse count**: 110 (or "all but one" = 109). The Hafs-Kufan reckoning is 109 (`suyuti-itqan-openiti-Q010.txt` line 17).
- **Meccan classification**: Two narrations from Ibn ʿAbbās; the well-known view is Meccan; *istithnāʾ* (exception verses) include vv. 40, 94, 95, 96 (per al-Zamakhsharī's report), or just vv. 94-96 per other reports.
- **Position in *al-sabʿ al-ṭiwāl***: seventh of the seven long surahs (when Anfāl + Barāʾa are joined as one).
- **Letter saturation**: "Q 10 has more than two hundred occurrences of the letter rāʾ" (line 23). This is consonantal saturation and is an empirically distinctive Q 10 signature (linked to its ALR muqaṭṭaʿāt).

### Key positions from *al-Durr al-manthūr*

- **Atharic supplement**: extensive transmission chains for Q 10:62-64 (the *awliyāʾ* / *bushrā* verses), Q 10:98 (the *qawm Yūnus* episode), and Q 10:101 (the cosmic-call *unẓurū*). al-Suyūṭī's chain-aggregation is the largest classical riwāya-corpus on these key verses.

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī (al-Imāmī tradition), *Majmaʿ al-bayān*

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabarsi-openiti-Q010.txt` (~2,333 lines).

### Key positions

- **Reciting Q 10 (faḍāʾil)**: al-Ṭabarsī cites a transmission from Imām al-Ṣādiq: "Whoever recites Q 10 every two or three months will not be among the heedless on the Day of Judgment, and will be among the *muqarrabīn* of God" (`tabarsi-openiti-Q010.txt` line 1, attributing to Abū ʿAbd Allāh = al-Ṣādiq). This is a Twelver-Shīʿī faḍāʾil tradition not found in the Sunni 9-book corpus.

- **On v. 62 *awliyāʾ Allāh***: al-Ṭabarsī's Imāmī interpretation includes a layer absent from the Sunni mufassirūn — the *awliyāʾ Allāh* are paradigmatically the Imāms (in their teaching role), as well as more generally the believers. This is consistent with the Imāmī walāya doctrine.

- **Verse count**: 109 — agreement with Hafs-Kufan reckoning. al-Ṭabarsī's *qirāʾāt* notes confirm minor variants (e.g., Kūfan reading of *sāḥir* vs *siḥr* at v. 2).
