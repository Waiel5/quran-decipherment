## 1. Why Q 2 is *sanām al-Qurʾān* (the hump/peak)

The single most distinctive classical position on Q 2 is its identification as the **hump (sanām) of the Quran**. Multiple chains:

### al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, in the chapter on *fawātiḥ al-suwar* / surah-naming

Direct citation from `suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt` (the lines anchoring the *sanām* tradition; PageV01P138-191):

> «البقرة سنام القرآن وذروته نزل مع كل آية منها ثمانون ملكا واستخرجت {الله لا إله إلا هو الحي القيوم} من تحت العرش فوصلت بها» ‒ Aḥmad in his *Musnad* via Maʿqil b. Yasār

> «إن لكل شيء سناما وسنام القرآن سورة البقرة، من قرأها في بيته نهارا لم يدخله الشيطان ثلاثة أيام، ومن قرأها في بيته ليلا لم يدخله الشيطان ثلاث ليال» ‒ Ibn Ḥibbān (Ṣaḥīḥ) via Sahl b. Saʿd

> «إن لكل شيء سناما وإن سنام القرآن البقرة، وفيها آية هي سيدة آي القرآن آية الكرسي» ‒ al-Tirmidhī, *Sunan*, and al-Ḥākim, *Mustadrak*, via Abū Hurayra (the chain that explicitly **pairs** *sanām al-Qurʾān* claim with the *sayyidat āy al-Qurʾān* claim about Q 2:255)

al-Suyūṭī also notes (PageV01P191): "And Khālid b. Maʿdān used to call it the *fusṭāṭ al-Qurʾān* (tent of the Quran), and there is a narration in the *Musnad al-Firdaws* (marfūʿ) using this name, on account of its size and what is gathered in it of legal rulings (*aḥkām*) not mentioned elsewhere; and in a ḥadīth in the *Mustadrak* it is named *sanām al-Qurʾān*, and *sanām* of anything is its highest (*aʿlāhu*)."

**Three classical names** for Q 2 in al-Suyūṭī:
1. *al-Baqara* — canonical, from the cow-narrative.
2. *Sanām al-Qurʾān* — "Hump/Peak of the Quran." al-Tirmidhī, Ibn Ḥibbān, Aḥmad chains.
3. *Fusṭāṭ al-Qurʾān* — "Tent of the Quran." Khālid b. Maʿdān, *Musnad al-Firdaws*.
4. *al-Zahrāʾ* — "The Brilliant" (paired with Q 3 as *al-Zahrāwān*; Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim).

al-Suyūṭī's interpretive note: *sanām* means "the highest" — not the literal hump of a camel but its semantic correlate of *aʿlā*. So al-Baqara is the Quran's "highest" — by comprehensiveness (*aʿẓam* of legal rulings, narrative integration, creedal scope), not by chronology or numerology.

### al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar*, opening to surah Q 2

Direct citation from `biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` (PageV01P055-057):

> "(*sūrat al-baqara*) maqṣūdu-hā iqāmat al-dalīl ʿalā anna l-Kitāb hudan li-yuttabaʿu fī kulli mā qāla. Wa-aʿẓam mā yahdī ilayhi al-īmān bi-l-ghayb, wa-mujmaʿuhu al-īmān bi-l-ākhira, fa-madāruhu al-īmān bi-l-baʿth alladhī aʿrabat ʿanhu qiṣṣat al-baqara."

(Translation: "Sūrat al-Baqara — its maqṣūd [intended purpose] is to establish proof that the Book is guidance to be followed in everything it says. The greatest thing it guides to is faith in the Unseen (*al-īmān bi-l-ghayb*), the gathering-point of which is faith in the Hereafter (*al-īmān bi-l-ākhira*), the axis of which is faith in resurrection (*al-īmān bi-l-baʿth*) — which the cow-narrative (*qiṣṣat al-baqara*) most expressly demonstrates.")

al-Biqāʿī's argument for why the cow-narrative is the *axis*: "The revival in the cow-narrative happened through a weak apparent cause (*sabab ḍaʿīf fī al-ẓāhir*, namely a struck dead-body with part of a slaughtered cow) by the hand of an ordinary person — hence it is more indicative of God's power (*adall ʿalā al-qudra*) than other resurrection accounts in the surah, like the dead Children of Israel revived after the *ṣaʿqa* or other parallel cases." This is why the surah is named for the cow: because the cow-narrative is the **clinching demonstration** of the resurrection-doctrine that is the surah's purpose.

al-Biqāʿī then states (Q 2's *fasṭāṭ al-Qurʾān* / *zahrāʾ* nicknames): "She is named *al-Zahrāʾ* because she illuminates the path of guidance and sufficiency in this world and the next, and because she causes the faces to be radiant (*isfār al-wujūh*) on the Day of Recompense for those who believed in the Unseen and were not in dubious doubt … and *al-sanām* because no [object of] faith above tawḥīd, which is the foundation upon which every good is built, is more comprehensive than the faith in the Hereafter."

This al-Biqāʿī passage is foundational for the project's [[cross-finding-024-five-factor-cohesion-model]]: al-Biqāʿī sees Q 2's coherence (*tanāsub*) as flowing from a single thematic axis (resurrection as gathering-point), with subsidiary structures (Adam, Banū Isrāʾīl, Ibrahim, qibla, law) all serving this axis.

### al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, on Q 2

al-Rāzī's volumes on al-Baqara (raw text in `razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`) elaborate the surah's status differently — as **the foundational treatment of the four "great certainties" (*kubrayāt al-yaqīn*)**: (i) the existence and oneness of God, (ii) prophecy and the authority of Muḥammad, (iii) the Hereafter and resurrection, (iv) the moral framework of *taklīf* (legal-ethical responsibility).

al-Rāzī aligns with al-Biqāʿī on resurrection as central but adds an emphasis on **al-Baqara as the establishment of the *millat Ibrāhīm* (the Abrahamic religion)** — verses 124-141 are not parenthetical but the surah's polemical-theological pivot: Islam's claim to be the original Abrahamic religion against Judaic and Christian counter-claims. al-Rāzī devotes substantial space (extracted in the project's `razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md` notes) to Q 2's munāsabāt — especially the connection to Q 1 (the prayer for guidance is *answered* by "*dhālika l-Kitābu lā rayba fīhi hudan li-l-muttaqīn*") and Q 3 (al-Baqara establishes the Abrahamic premise; āl-ʿImrān continues with prophetic-history elaborations and the Maryam/Jesus polemic).

This al-Rāzī "answer-to-Q-1's-question" interpretation has **strong empirical correlate**: H-NEW-720 finds Q 1-Q 2 the most expensive canonical adjacency by FR distance (because Q 1's prayer-creed and Q 2's massive Medinan compendium occupy very different content-distribution regions). The classical answer-to-question framing is precisely what would *justify* the structural choice of an expensive transition.

### al-Zarkashī, *al-Burhān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

al-Zarkashī's *Burhān* (PDF on disk, key positions integrated into project KNOWLEDGE-GRAPH.md) classifies Q 2 within his **mufaṣṣal/ṭiwāl tripartite scheme**: Q 2 is in the *ṭiwāl* (long-surah) class, NOT in the *mufaṣṣal*. The mufaṣṣal is post-Q 50 (or post-Q 49); Q 2-49 are *ṭiwāl/mathānī*. The project's H-NEW-790 has empirically vindicated al-Zarkashī's tripartite scheme at t = +23.2 (significant): Q 2 is correctly classified as part of the structural-significance group that is OPPOSED to the iʿjāz-al-fawāṣil mufaṣṣal-qiṣār group.

al-Zarkashī also has a chapter on *munāsabat al-suwar* (correspondence between adjacent surahs) where he analyzes the Q 1-Q 2 sequencing: al-Fātiḥa is the *introduction* of the Quran ("the Mother of the Book") and Q 2 is the *substantive expansion* — relationship of *ijmāl* (synopsis) → *tafṣīl* (elaboration). Specific verse correspondences (per al-Zarkashī, also vindicated by Farrin 2010): Q 1:6 *"ihdinā al-ṣirāṭa al-mustaqīm"* (guide us to the straight path) is answered immediately by Q 2:1-2 *"alif lām mīm. dhālika l-kitābu lā rayba fīhi hudan li-l-muttaqīn"* (Alif Lām Mīm. That Book, no doubt, guidance for the muttaqīn).

## 2. The ALM muqaṭṭaʿāt — classical interpretations

### al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ on *fawātiḥ al-suwar* (chapter 60) and on *al-mutashābih*

Direct citation from `suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt`:

al-Suyūṭī provides the **canonical agnostic position** (the *al-mukhtar* / preferred position) on the muqaṭṭaʿāt:

> "wa-min al-mutashābihi awāʾilu al-suwar, wa-l-mukhtāru fīhā **anna-hā min al-asrār allatī lā yaʿlamuhā illā Allāh taʿālā**." (PageV03P024)

(Translation: "Among the *mutashābih* are the openings of the surahs, and the preferred position regarding them is that **they are among the secrets known only to Allah, exalted is He.**")

He cites al-Shaʿbī's foundational opinion: "Every book has a secret, and the secret of this Quran is the *fawātiḥ al-suwar*." (Ibn al-Mundhir's chain.)

al-Suyūṭī then enumerates the **non-agnostic glossing positions** (without endorsing them):

1. **From Ibn ʿAbbās** (Ibn Abī Ḥātim's chain via Abū al-Ḍuḥā):
   - الم (ALM) = *Anā Allāh aʿlam* ("I, Allāh, know best")
   - المص (ALMS) = *Anā Allāh ufaṣṣil* ("I, Allāh, distinguish/elaborate")
   - الر (ALR) = *Anā Allāh arā* ("I, Allāh, see")

2. **From Ibn ʿAbbās** (Saʿīd b. Jubayr's chain): الم, حم, ن are *ism muqaṭṭaʿ* ("a sliced/disjoined name") — i.e., they are letters of one of God's names that has been cut into pieces, with each surah's muqaṭṭaʿāt being part of the same divine name reconstructable when assembled.

3. **From Ibn ʿAbbās** (ʿIkrima's chain): الر, حم, ن = "letters of *al-Raḥmān* spread out" (*ḥurūf al-Raḥmān mufarraqa*). Compatible reading: the muqaṭṭaʿāt across all 29 surahs collectively reconstitute one or more divine names, particularly *al-Raḥmān*.

4. **From Muḥammad b. Kaʿb al-Quraẓī** (Abū al-Shaykh's chain):
   - الر (ALR) = "alif lām rāʾ from *al-Raḥmān*"
   - المص (ALMS) = "alif from *Allāh*, mīm from *al-Raḥmān*, ṣād from *al-Ṣamad*"

5. **From al-Ḍaḥḥāk**: المص (ALMS) = *anā Allāh al-ṣādiq* ("I am Allāh, the Truthful"). Or: ALMS is an abbreviation of *al-Muṣawwir* ("the Fashioner") — a divine name.

6. **From Ibn ʿAbbās** (al-Suddī chain) on كهيعص (KHYʿṢ, opening Q 19): "It is a sliced spelling: kāf from *al-Mulk* (or *al-Karīm*), hāʾ from *Allāh* (or *Hādin*), yāʾ from al-ʿAzīz (or *Ḥakīm*), ʿayn from al-ʿAzīz (or *ʿAlīm*), ṣād from *al-Muṣawwir* (or *al-Ṣādiq*)." Multiple non-uniform glossings exist.

7. **From al-Mubarrid, al-Farrāʾ, Quṭrub, al-Zamakhsharī** (cited by Ibn Kathīr in his Q 2 introduction, en-Q002.txt line 68-69): "These letters testify to the miracle of the Quran. Indeed, the servants are unable to produce something like the Quran, although it is comprised of the same letters with which they speak to each other. … 14 distinct letters appear in the muqaṭṭaʿāt — exactly half of the 28-letter Arabic alphabet — and Allāh chose specifically these 14 to demonstrate the challenge."

### Ibn Kathīr's enumeration of opinions

From `ibn-kathir-en-Q002.txt`, lines 59-100:

> "The individual letters in the beginning of some Surahs are among those things whose knowledge Allah has kept only for Himself. This was reported from Abu Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthman, ʿAli and Ibn Masʿud. It was said that these letters are the names of some of the Surahs. It was also said that they are the beginnings that Allah chose to start the Surahs of the Qur'an with. Khasif stated that Mujahid said, 'The beginnings of the Surahs, such as Qaf, Sad, Ta Sin Mim and Alif Lam Ra, are just some letters of the alphabet.'"

Ibn Kathīr enumerates the position-counts:
- 1-letter muqaṭṭaʿāt: ص (Q 38), ن (Q 68), ق (Q 50)
- 2-letter: حم (Q 40-46, the ḥawāmīm seven), طه (Q 20), يس (Q 36), طس (Q 27)
- 3-letter: الم (Q 2, 3, 29, 30, 31, 32) — the ALM-6
- 3-letter: الر (Q 10, 11, 12, 14, 15) — the ALR-5
- 3-letter: طسم (Q 26, 28)
- 4-letter: المر (Q 13)
- 4-letter: المص (Q 7)
- 5-letter: كهيعص (Q 19), حم عسق (Q 42)

Total: 29 surahs open with muqaṭṭaʿāt. **14 distinct letters** appear; the alphabet has 28 letters, so exactly half are used.

### al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-nuzūl*, on Q 2:1

The Wāḥidī Q002 file (`asbab-nuzul-wahidi-en-Q002.txt`) preserves a Sufi/poetical interpretation of ALM, attributing to Anṣārī:

This is a tradition cited as *qīla* (it has been said) — preserving a poetic-Sufi gloss rather than a foundational opinion. The full poetic excursus runs many pages in the Wāḥidī text.

## 3. Q 2:255 *āyat al-kursī* — the Throne Verse

### al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, *Faṣl: mā warada fī āyat al-kursī*

Direct citation from `suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt`:

> "Sahih Muslim from Ubayy b. Kaʿb: 'aʿẓam āyatin fī kitābillāh āyat al-kursī' ('the greatest verse in the Book of Allah is āyat al-kursī')."

> "al-Tirmidhī and al-Ḥākim from Abū Hurayra: 'inna li-kulli shayʾin sanāman wa-inna sanāma al-Qurʾān al-Baqara wa-fīhā āyatun hiya sayyidatu āyi al-Qurʾān āyatu al-kursī' ('Everything has a hump, and the hump of the Quran is al-Baqara, and in it is a verse that is the **mistress** of the verses of the Quran — āyat al-kursī')."

> "al-Ḥārith b. Abī Usāma from al-Ḥasan (mursal): 'afḍal al-Qurʾān sūratu l-Baqara wa-aʿẓam āyatin fīhā āyatu l-kursī' ('the most excellent of the Quran is sūrat al-Baqara, and the greatest verse in it is āyat al-kursī')."

> "Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Nasāʾī from Abū Umāma: 'man qaraʾa āyat al-kursī dubura kulli ṣalātin maktūbatin lam yamnaʿhu min dukhūl al-jannati illā an yamūta' ('whoever recites āyat al-kursī after every prescribed prayer, nothing prevents him from entering Paradise except death')."

The convergence of attestations places āyat al-kursī as the **single most-recited individual verse outside of al-Fātiḥa** in classical Islamic devotional life. The Q 2 ↔ Q 2:255 ↔ *sayyidat al-āyāt* ↔ *sanām* chain is a TIGHT THEOLOGICAL HIERARCHY: al-Baqara is the hump of the Quran; āyat al-kursī is the hump of al-Baqara.

### Ibn Kathīr's expansion

From `ibn-kathir-en-Q002.txt`, lines 8346-8500+:

> "**Imam Ahmad** recorded that ʿUbayy bin Kaʿb said that the Prophet asked him about the greatest Ayah in the Book of Allah, and ʿUbayy answered, 'Allah and His Messenger know better.' When the Prophet repeated his question several times, ʿUbayy said, 'Ayat Al-Kursi.' The Prophet commented:
>
> «Congratulations for having knowledge, O Abu Al-Mundhir! By He in Whose Hand is my soul! This Ayah has a tongue and two lips with which she praises the King (Allah) next to the leg of the Throne.»"

Ibn Kathīr also records the **Abū Hurayra Ramadan-charity-thief incident** (the Shayṭān trying to steal Sadaqa-grain from Abū Hurayra's storehouse three nights, on the third night teaching Abū Hurayra to recite āyat al-kursī before sleeping for protection — at which the Prophet identifies that the supposed thief was Shayṭān himself, who told the truth (about the verse's protective power) although he is a liar). This is the foundational *fadāʾil*-text for the **night-recitation-of-āyat-al-kursī as protection-against-Shayṭān** practice.

### al-Rāzī's structural interpretation of Q 2:255

al-Rāzī gives Q 2:255 a tightly-organized internal structure (in `razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`, the section on Q 2:255):

The verse names **God by 9 attributes**, organized:
1. *Allāh* (proper name)
2. *lā ilāha illā huwa* (negation of associates)
3. *al-Ḥayy* (Living)
4. *al-Qayyūm* (Self-Subsistent)
5. *lā taʾkhudhuhu sina wa-lā nawm* (no slumber, no sleep)
6. *lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍ* (cosmic possession)
7. *man dhā alladhī yashfaʿu ʿindahu illā bi-idhnih* (intercession only with permission)
8. *yaʿlamu mā bayna aydīhim wa-mā khalfahum* (omniscience: past and future)
9. *wasiʿa kursiyyuhu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* (the Kursī encompasses the cosmos)
10. *wa-huwa l-ʿaliyyu l-ʿaẓīm* (closing dyad: the Most High, the Most Great)

al-Rāzī treats this as a **complete theology in one verse** — every category of attribute is touched: the divine essence (*dhāt*), negative attributes (*ṣifāt al-salb*), affirmative attributes (*ṣifāt al-thubūt*), and active attributes (*ṣifāt al-fiʿl*). This is, on al-Rāzī's account, *why* the Prophet ﷺ called it *aʿẓam āya* — it is the verse with the highest theological **information density per word**.

### Modern structural analyses (Farrin 2010 + 114chambers)

**Farrin 2010** treats Q 2:255 within the larger ring composition of section B' (verses 254-284), where the *kursī* verse is the central element of B' (verses 255-260). It is internally a "verse about God's all-powerful and all-knowing nature; God is an ally of those who believe; God gives life and death, resurrects" — paralleling section B (verses 21-39) where the verses about God's creation of Adam are central. This makes Q 2:255 a **B-arm structural pivot**, not the absolute centre of the surah (which is verse 143).

**114chambers blog** (`/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/misc/114chambers-ayat-al-kursi-ring-composition.md`) proposes a **9-section internal ring** for Q 2:255 itself, with **humanity at the centre**. The proposed structure:
- Outer (1, 9): Allāh's supreme power (al-Ḥayy/al-Qayyūm; al-ʿAlī/al-ʿAẓīm)
- Inner (2, 8): continuous awareness; inexhaustible energy
- Middle (3, 7): dominion over heavens/earth; Kursī encompassing
- Adjacent-centre (4, 6): intercession only with permission; nothing escapes God's knowledge
- Centre (5): "He knows what is between their hands and behind them" — humanity's free-will-window

## 4. Q 2:284-286 *khawātim al-Baqara*

### al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 61 (chapter on *khawātim al-suwar*) and on āyat al-kursī

Direct citation from `suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt` (PageV03P365-366):

al-Suyūṭī begins his chapter on the surah-closings:

> "They (the surah-closings) are like the openings (*fawātiḥ*) in beauty, because they are the last to fall on the ear, so they came containing wonderful meanings (*maʿānī badīʿa*) along with informing the listener of the discourse's end. … Examples include the supplication that closes sūrat al-Baqara, and the recommendations that close āl-ʿImrān, and the inheritance laws that close al-Nisāʾ, and the magnification that closes al-Māʾida, and the promise/threat that closes al-Anʿām…"

al-Suyūṭī thus puts Q 2:284-286 in the genre of **"the supplication-close"** — surahs whose terminal verses are a duʿāʾ. This is the foundational generic typology of *khawātim al-suwar*.

> al-Suyūṭī, on the muqātaʿāt chapter (PageV01P138): "And as for *āyat al-kursī*, there has come about it and about all the verses of al-Baqara a ḥadīth from Aḥmad in his *Musnad* via Maʿqil b. Yasār: 'al-Baqara is the hump of the Quran and its peak; with each verse from it descended eighty angels, and {Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa al-Ḥayy al-Qayyūm} was extracted from beneath the Throne and joined to it.'"

al-Suyūṭī also reports al-Ḍaḥḥāk b. Muzāḥim (Saʿīd b. Manṣūr's chain): "khawātim sūrat al-Baqara — Jibrīl came with them, accompanied by what Allāh willed of angels."

### al-Bukhārī ḥadīth on the khawātim

From `ibn-kathir-en-Q002.txt`, lines 9928-10037 (Q 2:285-286 commentary):

> "**al-Bukhārī** recorded that Abu Masʿūd said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: «Whoever recites the last two Ayat in Surat Al-Baqarah at night, **kafatāhu** (they will suffice for him).»"

This is **al-Bukhārī ḥadīth #4802** in the modern numbering (in Q002-citations.md, line 93-99 of the file at `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/hadith/Q002-citations.md`). The Arabic: «من قرأ بالآيتين من آخر سورة البقرة في ليلة كفتاه». **The "kafatāhu" / "they suffice him" claim** is the foundational fadāʾil-text for nightly recitation of Q 2:285-286.

The Sahih Muslim parallel (#1769) adds the **angel-from-newly-opened-heaven-gate** narrative: "Rejoice in two lights given to you which have not been given to any prophet before you: Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and the concluding verses of Sūrat al-Baqara. You will never recite a letter from them for which you will not be given (a reward)."

These two verses are thus the **only Quranic-passage** (besides al-Fātiḥa) classed in classical hadith as *nūr* (a light) given uniquely to Muḥammad's ﷺ ummah — a direct structural parallel between the **opening of the second surah and the closing of the second surah**, both elevated to *nūr* status. This is one of the clearest cases of classical recognition of the **ring-architectural anchoring**: Fātiḥa = opening *nūr*; khawātim al-Baqara = closing *nūr* of the foundational surah-pair.

### Ibn Kathīr on the abrogation of Q 2:284 by Q 2:286

From `ibn-kathir-en-Q002.txt`, lines 9879-9926:

The classical exegetical drama around Q 2:284 ("And whether you disclose what is in yourselves or conceal it, Allāh will call you to account for it") is recorded in detail by Ibn Kathīr citing Imām Aḥmad's Musnad via Abū Hurayra:

> "When this Ayah was revealed to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, it was very hard for the Companions. The Companions came and fell to their knees saying, 'O Messenger of Allah! We were asked to perform what we can bear of deeds: the prayer, the fast, jihād and charity. However, this Ayah was revealed to you, and we cannot bear it.' The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: «Do you want to repeat what the People of the Two Scriptures before you said, that is, "We hear and we disobey"? Rather, say, "We hear and we obey, and we seek Your forgiveness, O our Lord, and the Return is to You."»
>
> When the people accepted this statement and their tongues recited it, Allah sent down afterwards: {āmana l-rasūlu bi-mā unzila ilayhi min rabbihi wa-l-muʾminūn…} … and Allah abrogated the Ayah 2:284 and sent down: {lā yukallifu Llāhu nafsan illā wusʿahā…}"

This is the **Q 2:284 → Q 2:285-286 abrogation tradition**. The interpretation: Q 2:284 promises divine accountability for even-thoughts; Q 2:285-286 abrogates this by introducing "Allāh does not burden a soul beyond its capacity" + the famous closing duʿāʾ. The *naskh* here is internal to a single surah's khawātim — the surah closes with its own self-correction. This is a rare structural feature.

### al-Biqāʿī on the *khawātim* structurally completing the surah

al-Biqāʿī (in his treatment of Q 2:284-286 in `biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`) returns to the surah's *maqṣūd* (faith in the Unseen → faith in the Hereafter → faith in resurrection):

The closing duʿāʾ "*rabbanā lā tuʾākhidhnā in nasīnā aw akhṭaʾnā…*" through "*fa-anṣurnā ʿalā al-qawmi al-kāfirīn*" is, on al-Biqāʿī's account, the **practical-ethical correlate** of the surah's theological centre. The surah opens with "guidance for the muttaqīn" (Q 2:2-5); it closes with the muttaqīn's prayer. The structural arc is **command (open) → demonstration (cow-narrative + Adam + Ibrahim + qibla + law) → response (close as prayer of acceptance)**.

## 5. The cow-narrative (Q 2:67-71) and the surah's name

### Why is Q 2 named "the cow"?

The narrative (Q 2:67-71): Mūsā commands Banū Isrāʾīl to slaughter a cow as part of resolving a murder mystery in the community. The Banū Isrāʾīl repeatedly question Mūsā for details (what kind of cow? what color? what age?), demonstrating their hesitancy and legalistic diversion. Eventually they slaughter a yellow heifer. The slaughtered cow's piece is then struck against the dead body, which revives momentarily and identifies its murderer.

### Why is *this* the surah's name, given the surah's vast scope?

al-Biqāʿī's answer (above): because the cow-narrative is the **clinching demonstration of resurrection** — a revival via a weak apparent cause + an ordinary human agent. This makes the cow-narrative *adall ʿalā al-qudra* (more indicative of God's power) than other resurrection-stories in the surah (the dead Children of Israel revived after the *ṣaʿqa*; Q 2:243, the people who fled their land in fear of death; Q 2:259, Uzayr and the village; Q 2:260, Ibrāhīm's question about reviving the dead birds).

al-Rāzī (in `razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`) gives a different and complementary answer: the cow-narrative is named after because it is the **most extended example of "ḍalāl al-yahūd" (the misguidance of the Jews)** — they were given a simple command, they evaded it through legalistic hair-splitting, and the simple obedient slaughter would have sufficed. The narrative is paradigmatic of Q 2's broader engagement with *Banū Isrāʾīl* (verses 40-103 + 122-141), establishing the polemic that Islam corrects the Mosaic legacy. **Naming the surah for this narrative emphasizes** Islam's claim to be the *taḥrīr* (liberation) of the Abrahamic mission from Banū Isrāʾīl's legalism.

Farrin 2010 (PDF, page 5-6, full extraction in this file) places the cow-narrative at the **structural centre of section C** (verses 40-103, the long Banū Isrāʾīl section). The mention of the calf (*ʿijl*) in verses 51, 54 and 92, 93 forms a chiastic frame; the cow (*baqara*) sits inside this frame at verses 67-74, with the calf-mentions before and after creating a ring. So the cow-narrative is structurally the **innermost inner ring of the Banū Isrāʾīl section**.

### Empirical correlate: Banū Isrāʾīl vocabulary in Q 2

Computed (no-tashkeel):
- *إسرائيل* (Israel) appears 6 times in Q 2 / 43 times in corpus = 14.0% in 8% of corpus by word = 1.7× over-representation.
- *اليهود* (al-Yahūd) appears 3 times in Q 2 / 8 times in corpus = 37.5% — **4.7× over-representation**.

This empirically vindicates the classical observation that Q 2 is the **single foundational surah for Quranic engagement with Banū Isrāʾīl as a community in dialogue with the new religion**. Notably, however, *Mūsā* in Q 2 (13 tokens) is NOT over-represented (9.6% of his 136 corpus tokens vs 8% expected) — Q 2 talks ABOUT Banū Isrāʾīl more than it talks about Mūsā specifically. (Surahs that talk about Mūsā specifically tend to be Meccan: Q 7, 10, 11, 18, 20, 26, 27, 28.)

## 6. Q 2 as the FIRST sustained Medinan revelation — chronology vs mushaf-position

### The classical chronological position (al-Suyūṭī, al-Wāḥidī)

al-Suyūṭī (*Itqān*, nawʿ 9 on Meccan/Medinan) and al-Wāḥidī (*Asbāb al-nuzūl*) place Q 2's chronological revelation at **position 87** in the Nöldeke-style ordering — i.e., al-Baqara was revealed gradually over approximately ten years (per the Wikipedia summary verifying the classical position) starting in the months leading up to March 624 CE / Ramaḍān 2 AH (Farrin 2010, p. 3, citing the Standard Egyptian text tradition).

Multiple Q 2 verses are widely-attested to be among the **last verses revealed**:
- Q 2:281 ("And fear a Day when you will be returned to Allāh...") — Ibn Kathīr (line 47-49) reports this as **the very last verse of the Quran revealed**.
- Q 2:275-281 — the riba (usury) prohibition, "among the last verses revealed."

### The mushaf-position (#2)

The mushaf places this *late-revealed surah second*. This is the cleanest single instance of the **chronology vs mushaf-order divergence** in the Quran: Q 2 is chronologically position 87 of 114, but mushaf-position 2 of 114.

### What does this mean for *tartīb tawqīfī* (divine ordination of the order)?

The classical answer (al-Bukhārī ḥadīth #4786, in Q002-citations.md) preserves **ʿĀʾisha's testimony** to Yusuf b. Māhak (an Iraqi who came asking about copying the Quran):

> "What does it matter which part of it you read first? (Be informed) that the first thing that was revealed thereof was a Sūra from al-Mufaṣṣal, and in it was mentioned Paradise and the Fire. … While I was a young girl of playing age, the following Verse was revealed in Mecca to Muḥammad: 'Nay! But the Hour is their appointed time…' (54:46). And **Sūra al-Baqara (the Cow) and Sūrat al-Nisāʾ (the Women) were revealed while I was with him**." Then ʿĀʾisha took out the copy of the Qur'ān for the man and dictated to him the Verses of the Sūras (in their proper order).

The point: ʿĀʾisha attests that **the mushaf order is NOT the revelation order** — but it IS the order in which the Prophet ﷺ taught the verses to be arranged in the *muṣḥaf*. This is the **classical *tartīb tawqīfī* doctrine** (the order is divinely instructed).

### Empirical interaction

The project's [[h-new-660-compression-tail-gradient]] finds the mushaf has a content-cohesion-distance gradient that kinks at s = 50 (window-midpoint at Q 56-57 — the Hijra hinge). This kink is at exactly the chronological-Meccan/Medinan boundary in al-Suyūṭī's chronology. So the mushaf-order RESPECTS chronology globally as a 1-D phenomenon — but specific surahs are placed against chronology to enforce structural commitments. **Q 2 (chrono #87, mushaf #2) is the most extreme case of intentional structural placement.**

al-Zarkashī, *al-Burhān*, in his chapter on *munāsabat al-suwar*, presents the classical defense: Q 2 is placed second because (a) it is the *fusṭāṭ al-Qurʾān* (the tent containing the corpus's greatest scope of legal rulings, narrative, and creed); (b) it follows naturally from Q 1 as *tafṣīl* of Q 1's *ijmāl* (Q 1's prayer for guidance is answered by Q 2's announcement of the guiding Book); (c) it provides the **foundational material that the rest of the corpus elaborates** — Adam, Ibrahim, Banū Isrāʾīl, qibla, law, riba, contract, jihad, marriage, divorce, charity, fasting, ḥajj, etc. The post-Q 2 corpus is, in this reading, an unfolding of premises Q 2 establishes.

Empirical correlate: Q 1-Q 2 is the **most expensive canonical adjacency** (7.50% of TSP-residual). The mushaf "pays" to enforce Q 2 second; Q 2-Q 3 is then nearly free (the natural pair *al-Zahrāwān*).

## 8. Honest limits and rules-tuple sensitivity

1. The classical "Q 2 = sanām al-Qurʾān" tradition is NOT in the canonical six (al-Bukhārī, Muslim, Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah) under that exact label — it is in al-Tirmidhī's *Sunan* and Ibn Ḥibbān's *Ṣaḥīḥ* and Aḥmad's *Musnad*. It is **mawqūf** (companion-attributed) in some chains and **marfūʿ** (Prophet-attributed) in others. Rules-tuple-sensitive: under the strict *Bukhārī-Muslim-only* gate, *sanām al-Qurʾān* is NOT supported; under the *six-canonical* gate, it is.
2. The ALM "sliced divine name" interpretations (Ibn ʿAbbās attributions) are attested via several chains in al-Ṭabarī, al-Ḥākim, Ibn Abī Ḥātim — but the chains are themselves classed as variable in strength. al-Suyūṭī's *al-mukhtār* / preferred position (epistemic humility, *Allāh aʿlam*) explicitly de-priorities the glossing positions.
3. Q 2:255 word/letter counts: **rules-tuple-sensitive**. No-tashkeel + whitespace tokenization gives 50/189; some classical and popular sources give 57/182 (likely min-tashkeel + clitic-counting). The 114chambers source claims 57=3×19, which is a Code-19 echo and falsified by [[h-new-127]] series across the corpus.
4. The Q 2:284 → Q 2:286 abrogation tradition is *intra-surah naskh* — a rare phenomenon. It is well-attested in Aḥmad, Muslim, Ibn Kathīr but the legal-doctrine status of this *naskh* is contested in classical *uṣūl al-fiqh*: some scholars (e.g. al-Ṭabarī) argue Q 2:284 was not abrogated but qualified by Q 2:286.
5. The Farrin 2010 ring-structure is a **literary-critical proposal**, not a classical position. While it has substantial empirical support (the qibla-verse 143 is the verse-positional midpoint; sections do show thematic mirroring), the empirical test of "is this ring-structure statistically anomalous against verse-shuffle null?" is the [[Q002-F-04]] novel-finding pre-reg.
