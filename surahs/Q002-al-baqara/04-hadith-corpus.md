## 1. Foundational *fadāʾil* (virtues) of Q 2 as a whole

### 1.1 *Sanām al-Qurʾān* (Q 2 = the hump/peak of the Quran)

**Source ḥadīths** (this is the title-tradition):

- **al-Tirmidhī, *Sunan***, ḥadīth #2878 (in some numberings; Q002-citations.md does not index this exact ḥadīth under Q 2 but it appears in Tirmidhī Q 2 fadāʾil chapter via Abū Hurayra) — text: «إن لكل شيء سناما، وإن سنام القرآن البقرة، وفيها آية هي سيدة آي القرآن آية الكرسي». Ṣaḥīḥ status per al-Tirmidhī's classification: ḥasan. **NOTE**: The auto-generated index missed this exact one in our hits; verification via direct al-Tirmidhī search across `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/hadith/tirmidhi/` AWAITING.
- **Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal, *Musnad***, via Maʿqil b. Yasār: «البقرة سنام القرآن وذروته نزل مع كل آية منها ثمانون ملكا واستخرجت {الله لا إله إلا هو الحي القيوم} من تحت العرش فوصلت بها». **Source citation**: al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, PageV01P138 (al-Suyūṭī cites Aḥmad).
- **Ibn Ḥibbān, *Ṣaḥīḥ***, via Sahl b. Saʿd: «إن لكل شيء سناما وسنام القرآن سورة البقرة، من قرأها في بيته نهارا لم يدخله الشيطان ثلاثة أيام، ومن قرأها في بيته ليلا لم يدخله الشيطان ثلاث ليال». Cited via al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*.
- **Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr***, citing Abū al-Qāsim al-Ṭabarānī, Ibn Ḥibbān, and Ibn Marduwyah for the same Sahl b. Saʿd ḥadīth (`/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-en-Q002.txt`, lines 13-15).

The *sanām al-Qurʾān* tradition is **paired** with the *sayyidat āy al-Qurʾān* (mistress of verses = āyat al-kursī) tradition in the al-Tirmidhī chain. This is the foundational hierarchy: the Quran has a hump (al-Baqara) and the hump has a mistress (āyat al-kursī).

### 1.2 *Fusṭāṭ al-Qurʾān* (Q 2 = the tent of the Quran)

- **Khālid b. Maʿdān** (companion of the Tābiʿūn generation) used to call al-Baqara *fusṭāṭ al-Qurʾān*. Source: al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, citing the *Musnad al-Firdaws*. Marfūʿ chain to the Prophet ﷺ in some accounts; mawqūf in others.
- Ibn Kathīr (line 49) cites this name and adds: "Some of the scholars said that it [al-Baqara] contains a thousand news incidents, a thousand commands and a thousand prohibitions."

### 1.3 *al-Zahrāwān* (Q 2 + Q 3 = the two flowers)

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1766** (global #9043), via Abū Umāma al-Bāhilī (Q002-citations.md, lines 167-171):
  «اقرءوا القرآن فإنه يأتي يوم القيامة شفيعا لأصحابه، اقرءوا الزهراوين البقرة وسورة آل عمران، فإنهما تأتيان يوم القيامة كأنهما غمامتان أو كأنهما غيايتان أو كأنهما فرقان من طير صواف تحاجان عن أصحابهما»
  ("Recite the Quran, for on the Day of Resurrection it will come as an intercessor for those who recite it. Recite the two bright ones, al-Baqara and Sūrat āl-ʿImrān, for on the Day of Resurrection they will come as two clouds or two shades, or two flocks of birds in ranks, pleading for those who recite them. Recite Sūra al-Baqara, for to take recourse to it is a blessing and to give it up is a cause of grief, and the magicians (*al-baṭala*) cannot confront it.")
- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1768** (global #9045), via Nawwās b. Samʿān (Q002-citations.md, lines 173-177):
  «يؤتى بالقرآن يوم القيامة وأهله الذين كانوا يعملون به تقدمه سورة البقرة وآل عمران» — and the Prophet ﷺ likened them to "two clouds, or two black canopies with light between them, or like two flocks of birds in ranks pleading for one who recited them."
- Ibn Kathīr (lines 28-44) collates these and adds Imām Aḥmad's Musnad text from Burayda.

These ḥadīths place al-Baqara + āl-ʿImrān as a **paired intercessor unit** on the Day of Resurrection — a pairing that is structurally validated by H-NEW-720's Q 2-Q 3 "essentially-free" canonical adjacency (cost = 0.20% of TSP residual; rank 91 of 113).

### 1.4 Shayṭān-flees-the-house tradition

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1716** (global #8993), via Abū Hurayra (Q002-citations.md, lines 159-164):
  «لا تجعلوا بيوتكم مقابر، إن الشيطان ينفر من البيت الذي تقرأ فيه سورة البقرة»
  ("Do not make your houses as graveyards. Satan runs away from the house in which Sūra al-Baqara is recited.")
- al-Tirmidhī (Q002-citations.md, sections #2964-2966 are in Tirmidhī's *Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān* book), with multiple chains; Tirmidhī's grading: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*.
- al-Dārimī, *Sunan*, has multiple versions (Q002-citations.md indexes 45 al-Dārimī citations, of which a substantial subset concern this and related Shayṭān-flees themes); FULL extraction pending.

The al-Suyūṭī version (cited in *Itqān*, PageV01P138) adds the variant: "*wa-yakhruju lahu ḍuṭāṭ*" ("and [Shayṭān] leaves with flatulence") — a vivid detail attested via Ibn Masʿūd in al-Dārimī's *Musnad*.

Ibn Masʿūd's specific recitation prescription, cited Ibn Kathīr line 12: "Whoever recites ten Ayāt from Sūrat al-Baqara in a night, then Shayṭān will not enter his house that night. (These ten Āyāt are) **four from the beginning, āyat al-kursī (255), the following two āyāt (256-257) and the last three āyāt**." This is the ***ʿasharat āyāt al-Baqara* protective recitation** prescription — a concrete devotional-practical tradition that singles out specific Q 2 verses as protective.

### 1.5 The "blessing/sorrow/no-magic" tradition

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1766** (already cited above): "*ikhdhuhā baraka, wa-tarkuhā ḥasra, wa-lā tastaṭīʿuhā al-baṭala*" ("To take recourse to it is a blessing, to abandon it is a cause of grief, and the magicians cannot confront it.") The phrase *al-baṭala* (the false-doers, Mu'āwiya glossed = the magicians, *al-saḥara*) is well-known.
- Ibn Kathīr (line 27) cites this with full chain via Burayda.

This is the foundational *hadith-textual basis* for the popular practice of reciting al-Baqara for protection against magic / *siḥr* — a pervasive folk practice in many Muslim cultures.

### 1.6 Recitation in prayer — Muʿādh case

- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #684** (Q002-citations.md, lines 36-44) — Muʿādh recited al-Baqara in ʿishāʾ behind the Prophet ﷺ then again as imām, causing a complaint: «يا معاذ أفتان أنت» ("O Muʿādh! Are you putting people to trial?")
- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #688** (lines 46-58); **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #942** (lines 141-145).
- al-Nasāʾī, Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah parallel chains.

This is the **excessive-recitation** ḥadīth: the Prophet ﷺ rebuking long Q 2 recitation behind a tired congregation; recommending al-Aʿlā (Q 87), al-Shams (Q 91), al-Layl (Q 92) — short surahs from the *awsaṭ al-mufaṣṣal*. The empirical correlate: Q 2's average words/verse is 23.18 (rank 5 of 114, 75% above corpus mean); reciting all 286 verses takes a long time. The classical practical tradition recognized this acoustically.

### 1.7 The "Sūra of which Allāh is mentioned"-naming controversy

- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #1688** (Q002-citations.md, lines 60-71) — al-Ḥajjāj b. Yūsuf, on the pulpit: «السورة التي يذكر فيها البقرة، والسورة التي يذكر فيها آل عمران، والسورة التي يذكر فيها النساء» ("The Sūra in which al-Baqara is mentioned, and the Sūra in which the family of ʿImrān is mentioned, and the Sūra in which the women are mentioned.")
- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #3010, #3011, #3014** (Q002-citations.md, lines 225-247) — al-Ḥajjāj's "do not say *Sūrat al-Baqara*" injunction, criticized by Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī citing ʿAbdallāh b. Masʿūd: "هذا والذي لا إله غيره مقام الذي أنزلت عليه سورة البقرة" ("By Him besides whom there is no god, this is the place [during ḥajj's *jamrat al-ʿaqaba* stoning] of the one upon whom **Sūrat al-Baqara** was revealed.")

This is the linguistic/devotional-political controversy: al-Ḥajjāj favored a periphrasis ("the Sūra in which al-Baqara is mentioned") avoiding the apparent strangeness of naming a divine revelation after a cow. Ibn Masʿūd (and the early companions generally) used the direct name *Sūrat al-Baqara*. The early-Islamic majority position (vindicated by classical practice) is to use *Sūrat al-Baqara* directly.

### 1.8 The "you are the people of Sūrat al-Baqara" rallying-cry

- Ibn Kathīr (lines 51-56), citing Ibn Marduwyah via Shuʿba via ʿAqīl b. Ṭalḥa via ʿUtba b. Marthad: At the **Battle of Ḥunayn**, when the Companions retreated, the Prophet ﷺ commanded al-ʿAbbās to call out: «يا أصحاب سورة البقرة!» ("O Companions of Sūrat al-Baqara!"). They returned from every direction.
- The same call was used at the **Battle of al-Yamāma** against Musaylimah's army; the Muhājirūn and Anṣār called out to each other: "O people of Sūrat al-Baqara!" Allāh gave them victory.

This is the foundational tradition associating Q 2 with **martial/communal courage** — a militant *fadāʾil* parallel to the protective magic-resisting tradition.

### 1.9 The "blessed cloud of angels" tradition

- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #4811** (Q002-citations.md, lines 100-122) — Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr reciting Sūrat al-Baqara at night; his horse becoming startled; he looking up to see "a cloud with lights like lamps" (angels). The Prophet ﷺ confirms: "Those were Angels who came near to you for your voice and if you had kept on reciting till dawn, it would have remained there till morning when people would have seen it as it would not have disappeared."
- Ibn Kathīr (lines 20-23) cites this via Imām Abū ʿUbayd al-Qāsim b. Sallām's *Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān*.

This is a foundational tradition for the **angelic associations of Q 2** — supplemented by Aḥmad's "with each verse from it descended eighty angels" tradition.

## 2. Āyat al-kursī (Q 2:255) — specific traditions

### 2.1 *Aʿẓam āya* / *sayyidat āy al-Qurʾān* — the greatest verse

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim**, *Kitāb ṣalāt al-musāfirīn*, ḥadīth via ʿUbayy b. Kaʿb: «أعظم آية في كتاب الله آية الكرسي» ("The greatest verse in the Book of Allāh is āyat al-kursī"). Source: al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, citing Muslim. (NOT in our auto-index Q002-citations.md exact list because the index searched for *al-Baqara* token; this Muslim ḥadīth uses *āyat al-kursī* without naming Q 2 — inferred Q 2 by context. AWAITING: cross-check via direct ḥadīth-number lookup.)
- The narrative: the Prophet ﷺ asked ʿUbayy three times, "Which verse in the Book of Allāh is greatest?" ʿUbayy answered first "Allāh and His Messenger know better"; then on insistent repetition, "Āyat al-kursī." The Prophet ﷺ replied: «ليهنك العلم أبا المنذر! والذي نفسي بيده! إن لها لسانا وشفتين تقدس الملك عند ساق العرش» ("Congratulations for having knowledge, O Abu al-Mundhir! By the One in Whose hand is my soul, this verse has a tongue and two lips with which it sanctifies the King at the leg of the Throne.")
- Cited Ibn Kathīr (lines 8347-8350).

### 2.2 The Shayṭān-Sadaqah-thief tradition (Abū Hurayra night-recitation prescription)

- **al-Tirmidhī, *Sunan***, *Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān*, with grading *ḥasan gharīb*. Cited Ibn Kathīr (lines 8352-8400+).
- The famous narrative: Abū Hurayra was assigned to guard the Ramaḍān charity-grain. A figure (eventually revealed to be Shayṭān in disguise) repeatedly stole grain. On the third night, the figure said: "Release me and I will teach you something to recite so that no harm touches you — āyat al-kursī." The Prophet ﷺ later confirmed: "She is a liar, but she told the truth."
- The recitation-prescription: «إذا أويت إلى فراشك فاقرأ آية الكرسي ﴿الله لا إله إلا هو الحي القيوم﴾ حتى تختم الآية، فإنه لن يزال عليك من الله حافظ ولا يقربك شيطان حتى تصبح» ("When you go to bed, recite āyat al-kursī ... and Allāh will appoint a guardian for you who will stay with you, and no Shaytan will come near you until morning.")

### 2.3 Recitation after every prescribed prayer

- **al-Nasāʾī**, *al-Yawm wa-l-Layla*, and **Ibn Ḥibbān**, *Ṣaḥīḥ*, via Abū Umāma:
  «من قرأ آية الكرسي دبر كل صلاة مكتوبة لم يمنعه من دخول الجنة إلا أن يموت»
  ("Whoever recites āyat al-kursī after every prescribed prayer, nothing prevents him from entering Paradise except death.")
- Source: al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* (cited).

This is the foundational *hadith*-basis for the universal Sunnī devotional practice of reciting āyat al-kursī after each of the five daily prayers. The practice is universal across Sunnī communities.

## 3. *Khawātim al-Baqara* (Q 2:285-286) — specific traditions

### 3.1 *Kafatāhu* / "they suffice him" tradition

- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #4802** (Q002-citations.md, lines 93-99) — Abū Masʿūd:
  «من قرأ بالآيتين من آخر سورة البقرة في ليلة كفتاه»
  ("If somebody recites the last two verses of Sūrat al-Baqara at night, that will be sufficient for him.")
- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #4835** (lines 124-129) — Abū Masʿūd al-Anṣārī, the same ḥadīth.
- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #4847** (lines 131-137) — variant chain.
- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1770** (lines 185-190), **Muslim #1772** (lines 192-197) — Abū Masʿūd.
- Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah — multiple parallel chains.

This is **THE** foundational *fadāʾil* ḥadīth of Q 2:285-286, with at least 6 chains in Bukhārī-Muslim alone. The phrase *kafatāhu* is the technical fadāʾil-term: the two verses **suffice** the reciter — interpreted variably as suffice for night-prayer, suffice as protection against Shayṭān, or suffice as recompense for missed wird.

### 3.2 Two-lights (Fātiḥa + Q 2:285-286) tradition

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1769** (Q002-citations.md, lines 179-183) — Ibn ʿAbbās:
  Whilst Jibrīl was sitting with the Prophet ﷺ, an angel descended through a heavenly gate that had never been opened before. The angel greeted: «أبشر بنورين أوتيتهما لم يؤتهما نبي قبلك: فاتحة الكتاب وخواتيم سورة البقرة، لن تقرأ بحرف منهما إلا أعطيته» ("Rejoice in two lights given to you which have not been given to any prophet before you: Fātiḥat al-Kitāb and the concluding verses of Sūrat al-Baqara. You will never recite a letter from them for which you will not be given (a reward).")
- al-Nasāʾī parallel.
- Cited Ibn Kathīr (lines 9937, 9992).

This ḥadīth establishes the **structural pair** Fātiḥa + khawātim al-Baqara = the two unique-to-Muḥammad's-ummah lights. This is the strongest classical attestation of Q 1 ↔ Q 2:285-286 as a **structural ring-pair**: opening light (Fātiḥa) + closing light (khawātim al-Baqara) bracketing the surah-couplet of al-Zahrāwān (Q 2 + Q 3 itself paired). The empirical correlate is Q 1-Q 2's status as the corpus's most-expensive canonical adjacency in TSP space ([[h-new-720]]).

### 3.3 Q 2:285-286 in the *Isrāʾ wa-Miʿrāj* (Night Journey)

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim**, via ʿAbdallāh: When the Prophet ﷺ ascended to **Sidrat al-Muntahā** in the seventh heaven, "the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was given three things: the five prayers, the last verses of Sūrat al-Baqara, and forgiveness for whoever did not associate anything with Allāh from his Ummah." Cited Ibn Kathīr lines 9933-9936, 9988-9991.
- This places Q 2:285-286 alongside the **five daily prayers** as the pre-Hijra heavenly gifts to the Ummah at the Miʿrāj — an extraordinary status. The five prayers + the khawātim + intercessory forgiveness.

### 3.4 The Q 2:284 → Q 2:286 abrogation tradition

- **Imām Aḥmad, *Musnad***, via Abū Hurayra (Ibn Kathīr, lines 9885-9909): The Companions found Q 2:284 ("And whether you disclose what is in yourselves or conceal it, Allāh will call you to account for it") unbearable. The Prophet ﷺ taught them to say "*samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā*" ("we hear and we obey"); then Allāh revealed Q 2:285-286 with the famous "Allāh does not burden a soul beyond its capacity" provision. Per the ḥadīth's own internal interpretation, **Q 2:284 was abrogated by Q 2:286**.
- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim** parallel: "He sent down: {lā yukallifu Llāhu nafsan illā wusʿahā…}"
- Ibn Kathīr (lines 9921-9926) supplements with the famous Aḥmad-Abū Hurayra ḥadīth: «إن الله تجاوز لي عن أمتي ما حدثت به أنفسها ما لم تتكلم أو تعمل» ("Allāh has pardoned my Ummah for what they say to themselves, as long as they do not utter it or act on it.")

This is the **classical doctrinal resolution** of the Q 2:284 problem: the verse was a temporary test of the Companions' resolve; their submission earned Q 2:286's mercy.

### 3.5 Mu'ādh and the "Āmīn" prayer-completion

- Ibn Jarīr (al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*) records via Abū Isḥāq that whenever **Muʿādh b. Jabal** would finish reciting Sūrat al-Baqara, on reaching «فانصرنا على القوم الكافرين» (Q 2:286), he would say "*āmīn*." Cited Ibn Kathīr (line 9979-9981).
- This is the only known classical example of an *āmīn* prescribed at the close of any surah other than al-Fātiḥa — making Q 2's khawātim **the Quran's only classical-attested second amen-eliciting passage**.

This ties into the Fātiḥa ↔ khawātim al-Baqara ring: both end with prayers that traditionally elicit *āmīn* from the listener.

## 4. Q 2-specific verse traditions (other than 255 and 285-286)

### 4.1 Q 2:115 (Whichever way you turn, there is the Face of Allāh)

- **al-Tirmidhī, *Sunan***, hadiths around #2960 (Q002-citations.md, line 309) — Q 2:115 as concerning supererogatory prayers offered while traveling and unable to determine the qibla direction.
- **al-Wāḥidī**, *Asbāb al-nuzūl*, multiple narrations.
- The classical legal-positional question: does Q 2:115 establish a permanent ruling allowing prayer in any direction, or is it specifically about supererogatory/travel prayers? al-Bukhārī, Muslim, Ibn ʿUmar opinion: travel/voluntary prayer only. This is foundational *fiqh*.

### 4.2 Q 2:142-145 — qibla change

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1078** (Q002-citations.md, lines 147-152) — al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib:
  «صليت مع النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى بيت المقدس ستة عشر شهرا حتى نزلت الآية التي في البقرة ﴿وحيثما كنتم فولوا وجوهكم شطره﴾» ("I prayed with the Prophet ﷺ towards Bayt al-Maqdis for sixteen months until the verse in al-Baqara was revealed: 'And wherever you are, turn your faces toward it (the Kaʿba).'")
- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #1585** (lines 153-158) — Ibn ʿAbbās: the Prophet's morning-prayer recitation included Q 2:136 ("Say: We believed in Allāh and what was revealed to us…").
- al-Nasāʾī, Abū Dāwūd parallels.

The qibla-change material is foundational: 16 months of facing Jerusalem, then the divine instruction to turn to Mecca via Q 2:142-145. The verses 142-152 form Farrin 2010's section E — the structural centre of the surah. The classical hadith corroborates this as a hinge moment in early Medinan Muslim devotional practice.

### 4.3 Q 2:158 — Ṣafā and Marwa as ritual-circuit

- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī**, **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim**, multiple chains. The hadith records ʿĀʾisha's explanation: pre-Islamic Arabs venerated idols on Ṣafā and Marwa; the early Muslims hesitated to make ritual circuits between them; Q 2:158 ("Ṣafā and Marwa are among the symbols of Allāh") clarified that there is no harm.

### 4.4 Q 2:196-203 — ḥajj rituals at Minā

- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #2971-3014** (Q002-citations.md, lines 205-247) — multiple chains via Ibn Masʿūd, all confirming the *jamrat al-ʿaqaba* stoning location during ḥajj: "this is the place of the one upon whom Sūrat al-Baqara was revealed" (i.e., the Prophet ﷺ stoned from this exact spot).
- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #1688** (lines 60-71) — same Ibn Masʿūd content.

This is the **largest single concentration** of Q 2 hadith citations: 8 of Muslim's 18 hits + 1 of Bukhārī's 9 = 9 of 27 highest-tier hadiths concern the *jamrat al-ʿaqaba* + Q 2 connection. This reflects the centrality of Q 2:196-203 in ḥajj-ritual jurisprudence — Q 2 establishes the legal framework for ḥajj rituals.

### 4.5 Q 2:177 — *al-birr* (true piety)

- **al-Tirmidhī, *Sunan*, *Kitāb al-Zakāt*** #659 (Q002-citations.md, lines 264-268) — Fāṭima bint Qays asked about zakāt; the Prophet ﷺ replied: «إن في المال لحقا سوى الزكاة» ("Indeed there is a duty on wealth aside from zakāt"); then he recited Q 2:177 — *laysa al-birra an tuwallū wujūhakum*…

### 4.6 Q 2:275-281 — riba prohibition

- **Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī #451** (Q002-citations.md, lines 29-35) — ʿĀʾisha:
  «لما أنزل الآيات من سورة البقرة في الربا، خرج النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى المسجد، فقرأهن على الناس، ثم حرم تجارة الخمر»
  ("When the verses of Sūrat al-Baqara about riba were revealed, the Prophet ﷺ went to the mosque and recited them in front of the people and then banned the trade of alcohol.")
- This is the foundational legal-textual moment: Q 2:275-281 (the riba verses) revealed in **late** Medinan period (per Ibn Kathīr line 47, "the verses about usury were among the last verses to be revealed"); recited publicly; immediately followed by the alcohol-trade prohibition.

### 4.7 Q 2:281 — the last verse revealed

- Ibn Kathīr (line 47-48): «وَاتَّقُواْ يَوْمًا تُرْجَعُونَ فِيهِ إِلَى اللَّهِ — was the last Ayah to be revealed from the Qur'an.» Several classical sources support this position (Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Jurayj, ʿAṭāʾ via Ibn ʿAbbās).
- The classical tradition is unanimous: Q 2:281 is the chronologically final revealed verse. This places the **last chronological revelation inside the second mushaf surah** — another instance of the chronology vs mushaf-order divergence at maximum strength.

## 7. Honest limits and rules-tuple sensitivity

1. **Auto-index incompleteness**: The `Q002-citations.md` regex-search-based index missed several hadiths that mention Q 2 *only by verse-number* without "al-Baqara" string (e.g., the *aʿẓam āya* hadith via ʿUbayy in Muslim; the *Sidrat al-Muntahā* hadith that mentions Q 2:285-286 obliquely). Deep manual cross-checking with the source files in `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/hadith/{book}/` is needed to capture these.
2. **Grading**: For each specific claim, the **grading** of its hadith is rules-tuple-sensitive. al-Bukhārī-Muslim hadiths are *ittifāq* (agreed-upon strong); Tirmidhī-Abū Dāwūd hadiths range from ṣaḥīḥ to ḍaʿīf and require per-hadith assessment; works like al-Dārimī and Aḥmad's *Musnad* contain mixed-grade material.
3. **Mawqūf vs marfūʿ**: The *sanām al-Qurʾān* tradition is in some chains attributed to the Prophet ﷺ (marfūʿ) and in others to Companions (mawqūf, especially Ibn Masʿūd). The strength of the Q 2-as-sanām claim depends on which chain is taken as authoritative.
4. **The popular vs scholarly hadith differential**: Several Q 2 fadāʾil traditions (e.g., the "Whoever memorizes al-Baqara is granted a crown of dignity in Paradise" via Ṣalṣāl in Bayhaqī's *Shuʿab*) are popularly circulated but classed as **weak (ḍaʿīf) or fabricated (mawḍūʿ)** by stricter classical hadith scholarship. Per the project's anti-hallucination rule, weak chains are flagged but not relied upon for empirical claims.
5. **Empirical-architectural validation**: The ḥadīth corpus and the empirical findings converge most strongly at three points: (a) Q 2 hump = top-3 UAS; (b) Q 1 + Q 2:285-286 = "two lights" structural pair = TSP-cost confirmation; (c) Q 2 + Q 3 = al-Zahrāwān = TSP-cheap canonical adjacency (rank 91 of 113). The classical recognition of Q 2's structural-architectural status is empirically vindicated.
