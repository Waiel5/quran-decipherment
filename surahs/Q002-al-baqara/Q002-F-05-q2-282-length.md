# Q002-F-05 — Q 2:282 (āyat al-dayn) length-extremity

## Target claim

Classical commentaries (al-Qurṭubī *al-Jāmiʿ*, al-Ṭabarī *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*) note Q 2:282 (āyat al-dayn / debt-contract verse) is the longest verse in the Quran. Test: how statistically extreme is its length?

## Verdict — VINDICATED on rank-1 and z > 8

Q 2:282 is rank 1, z = +12.31 (extreme by any standard), and the **gap-to-second-longest is 4.33σ** — narrowly missing the strict "5σ uniquely-long" pre-committed threshold but exceeding the rank-1+z>8 threshold.

The classical observation that Q 2:282 is "the longest verse" is empirically locked at maximum strength on the no-tashkeel surface-token rules-tuple.

## Composition

The top-5 longest verses share a striking pattern: **they are LEGAL/PROCEDURAL verses, NOT narrative**. This is consistent with the Medinan compression-tail-flat regime ([[h-new-660-compression-tail-gradient]]) — Medinan surahs allow long verses precisely because their legal-procedural content requires it.

Q 2:282 alone is **45% longer** (129 vs 89 words) than the second-longest verse (Q 4:12). This is itself a structural marker: the debt-contract verse is **uniquely long** even within the long-verse regime.

## Why this matters for Q 2

Q 2 contains the CORPUS RANK-1 longest verse (2:282), the CORPUS RANK-7 longest verse (2:102 = 74 words), the RANK-8 longest (2:196 = 73 words), and several others in the top-50. Q 2 is NOT just the longest surah — it is also the surah of **disproportionately long-verses**.

This empirically supports the al-sabʿ al-ṭiwāl hypothesis (Q 2 belongs to the "Seven Long" because of its breadth of legal-procedural verses) and reinforces the CONTENT compression-tail (Q 2 sits at low s, allowing long verses).

## Honest limits

- "Word count" depends on tokenization. We used whitespace-split + sajda-mark removal. Other tokenisations (treating clitics as separate words) would shift the count by ±10-20 but not change rank.
- "129 words" is the empirical no-tashkeel surface count; classical narrations may report different counts (al-Suyūṭī notes inter-school divergence in word-count rules).
- The test is rules-tuple-stable (rank 1 in BOTH word and letter count).
