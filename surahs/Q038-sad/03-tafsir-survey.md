# Q 38 Ṣād — Tafsir Survey

≥5 classical mufassirūn surveyed. Sources: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/` (al-Ṭabarī Arabic, Ibn Kathīr Arabic + English-Darussalam, al-Qurṭubī Arabic, al-Wāḥidī asbāb al-nuzūl Arabic, al-Baghawī Arabic, Tanwīr al-Miqbās Arabic, al-Saʿdī Arabic, al-Wāsiṭ Arabic, al-Muyassar Arabic) and the PDFs of al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* and al-Zarkashī *al-Burhān*.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān*) — Q 38:1

al-Ṭabarī catalogs **multiple interpretations of the muqaṭṭaʿ ص** (`spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/38.json` ad v.1):

> "The exegetes differed on the meaning of ص. Some said: it derives from *al-muṣādāh* — *ṣādaytu fulānan* (to face / oppose someone) — making it an imperative: *ṣād bi-ʿamalika al-Qurʾāna* (i.e., 'match/oppose the Qurʾān with your deeds'). Whoever held this view read the dāl as kasra. al-Ḥasan [al-Baṣrī] said: ص = *ḥādith al-Qurʾān* (be in dialogue with the Qurʾān). Others said: ص is among the letter-openings (*al-ḥurūf al-muqaṭṭaʿāt*) and only Allah knows its meaning."

al-Ṭabarī's exposition reflects a key classical bifurcation:
- **Letter-as-imperative** reading (al-Ḥasan, ʿAmrū b. ʿUbayd): ص is a verb-imperative call.
- **Letter-as-mystery** reading (Ibn ʿAbbās, mainstream): ص is one of the muqaṭṭaʿāt whose meaning is divinely reserved.

al-Ṭabarī himself favors the "matters known to God" position; he explicitly criticizes the speculative interpretations.

## 2. al-Ṭabarī on Q 38:17 (the David panel) and Q 38:24 (sajda)

al-Ṭabarī expounds *iṣbir ʿalā mā yaqūlūn* as addressed to the Prophet ﷺ in his Meccan ordeal: "be patient, O Muḥammad, on what your Quraysh tribe says." David is invoked as **comparative trial-bearer** — *idhkurhu dhā al-aydi* "remember him, the possessor of strength." al-Ṭabarī notes that *al-aydi* means *al-quwwa wa-l-baṭsh al-shadīd fī dhāt Allāh wa-l-ṣabr* (strength in Allah's cause + endurance). The David-panel is theologically a **counter-Quraysh consolation**: just as David was given strength after trial, the Prophet will be.

On v. 24, al-Ṭabarī tabulates **multiple isnāds** for the two-litigants story (the famous "ewes" parable). He cites Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Qatāda, and others. Some chains parallel 2 Sam 12 (Nathan's parable); al-Ṭabarī presents these without endorsing the *isrāʾīliyyāt* parallel. The David-prostrates-and-is-forgiven reading is locked.

## 3. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*) — Q 38:17, 24, 41

`spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/38.json` (English Darussalam edition):

**v. 17**: Ibn Kathīr identifies *al-aydi* with "strength in knowledge and action" via Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Suddī, Ibn Zayd, and Mujāhid. Critically, he cites that David "spent a third of the night in prayer, and he fasted for half a lifetime. This was reported in the Two Saḥīḥs" (Bukhārī + Muslim). This is the **David-fasting-half-the-year** tradition (Bukhārī #1903, #1906, #1908 idInBook in our corpus; Muslim #2614, #2630).

**v. 24 (sajda)**: Ibn Kathīr is explicit that **the two-litigants story is "mostly based upon Isrāʾīliyyāt narrations. Nothing has been reported about this from the Infallible Prophet that we could accept as true."** He flags that even Ibn Abī Ḥātim's narration via Yazīd al-Raqāshī (from Anas) has a weak chain. Ibn Kathīr's methodological caution is exemplary: **the sajda is canonical, the elaborated story is not**. He cites Bukhārī #4601 (idInBook in our corpus — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`) for the Mujāhid-via-ʿAwwām chain establishing the Prophet ﷺ performed sajda upon recitation.

**v. 41 (Job)**: Ibn Kathīr describes the comprehensive trial — "his body, his wealth and his children, until there was no part of his body that was healthy except his heart." He notes Job's wife served him for "nearly eighteen years" — this is from Wahb b. Munabbih and Sufyān al-Thawrī's narrations.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*) on Q 38

al-Qurṭubī (`spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/38.json`) emphasizes:
- The **legal-exegetical** dimension of v. 26 (David as khalīfa with the duty of just judgment) — al-Qurṭubī cites this as foundational for the Islamic theory of *imāra* (rulership) and *qaḍāʾ* (judgeship). He cites the principle that the ruler must judge between disputants without prejudice.
- The **Solomon horse-trial** (v. 31-33): al-Qurṭubī presents al-Ṭabarī's "stroked / wiped" reading vs Ibn ʿAbbās's "marked their flanks and necks (with a sword)." al-Qurṭubī notes neither reading absolves Solomon of distraction; the lesson is the prophetic accountability for *taʿalluq al-qalb* (heart-attachment) to wealth.
- **Sajda recommendation**: al-Qurṭubī treats Q 38:24 as a *sajdat al-shukr* (sajda of gratitude) per the ḥadīth tradition, noting some scholars dispute whether it counts among the 14 obligatory recitation-prostrations or only as a recommended sajda. al-Shāfiʿī's school treats it as a sajda-of-recitation; Mālik treats it as sajda-of-gratitude (only).

## 5. al-Zamakhsharī (*al-Kashshāf*)

al-Zamakhsharī (PDF djvu at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zamakhshari-kashshaf/`) reads Q 38:1's oath construction balaghically: the oath-by-Qurʾān (*wa-l-Qurʾāni dhī al-dhikr*) followed by the polemical *bal* (v. 2) is a classic *fawātiḥ-bayān* structure — the oath establishes the gravity, the *bal* immediately pivots to the rhetorical scolding of the disbelievers. al-Zamakhsharī treats *dhī al-dhikr* with the bilingual reading: "possessor of reminder" + "of high rank/honor" — both senses preserved (echoing al-Ṭabarī's two-views). His balāghah-focus places Q 38:1-3 as the **Quraysh-rebuke opening** archetype.

al-Zamakhsharī also reads the *innahu awwāb* refrain (vv. 17, 30, 44) as a deliberate triple-anaphora marking the David-Solomon-Job triad as parallel; this is a balaghah observation that anticipates modern *naẓm* analysis (Farāhī school).

## 6. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*) on Q 38:1

al-Rāzī's commentary on Q 38:1 (in his *Mafātīḥ* OpenITI raw, plus referenced in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-muqattaat-surah-qaf.md` for Q 50 — al-Rāzī's analysis on muqaṭṭaʿāt-singletons) is exhaustive. He catalogs ~20 distinct interpretations of ص spanning:
- letter-as-divine-name abbreviation;
- letter-as-imperative (the al-Ḥasan reading);
- letter-as-tongue-position (phonological-makhraj indicator);
- letter-as-numerological symbol (*ḥurūf al-jummal*: ص = 90);
- letter-as-tribe-name (claimed for Q 38 ص in some early traditions, dismissed by al-Rāzī as weak);
- letter-as-divine-mystery (al-Rāzī's preferred final view).

al-Rāzī's analysis on the David panel (v. 17-26) is psychological-theological: David's haste in judgment was a **trial of moral-finesse**, not of sin per se. al-Rāzī argues that prophetic infallibility (*ʿiṣma*) is preserved even where the Quranic text uses language of *istighfār* (asking forgiveness) — the *istighfār* is for *minor over-haste*, not for moral wrong-doing.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Durr al-manthūr* + *al-Itqān*)

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* nawʿ 40 catalogs the muqaṭṭaʿāt and explicitly identifies the three single-letter muqaṭṭaʿāt as Q 38 ص, Q 50 ق, and Q 68 ن. al-Suyūṭī's chronology in nawʿ 1 places Q 38 at revelation order #38 of 114 (mid-Meccan). The asbāb al-nuzūl tradition (al-Wāḥidī cited in al-Suyūṭī) places Q 38 as occasioned by the Quraysh's gathering before Abū Ṭālib to demand he restrain the Prophet ﷺ from preaching tawḥīd (the famous *Quraysh delegation* event; cited in Ibn Hishām *al-Sīra*).

In *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 38:24, al-Suyūṭī compiles ḥadīth and *āthār* on the David sajda; he cites Bukhārī (chain via al-ʿAwwām → Mujāhid → Ibn ʿAbbās), Abū Dāwūd, and a number of *mawqūf* reports on the Companions' practice of performing sajda upon Q 38:24.

## 8. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*)

al-Biqāʿī (PDF at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf`, plus `biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`) emphasizes the **structural munāsaba** between Q 37 al-Ṣāffāt and Q 38 Ṣād. al-Biqāʿī notes that:
- Q 37 ends with *wa-sallām ʿalā al-mursalīn wa-l-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi al-ʿālamīn* — peace upon the messengers, praise to the Lord.
- Q 38 opens with *ṣ wa-l-Qurʾāni dhī al-dhikr* — oath by the Qurʾān-of-reminder.
The transition Q 37 → Q 38 is from "celebrating the messengers" to "swearing by the reminder-Qurʾān-of-them." al-Biqāʿī's *tanāsub* claim has an **empirical correlate**: Q037→Q038 canonical-adjacency cost = 0.000 length-units (rank bottom; H-NEW-720) — the seam is structurally seamless.

al-Biqāʿī's munāsaba between the David-Solomon-Job triad and the surrounding material is articulated as: **the surah is a chain of three *awwāb* (returners) — David, Solomon, Job — each bracketed by the *innahu awwāb* refrain.** This anticipates the modern observation (§02-content-analysis §11) that the *innahu awwāb* phrase is exactly 3× corpus-total, all in Q 38, marking exactly the trial-triad.

## 9. al-Ṭabarsī (*Majmaʿ al-bayān*)

al-Ṭabarsī (Imāmī tafsir) treats Q 38 with shīʿī-emphasis on the Davidic-line lineage as foundational for the doctrine of *imāma*. The David-as-khalīfa verse (v. 26) is exegeted as a precursor to the Imāmate institution. al-Ṭabarsī also distinguishes the sound (Saḥīḥ) hadith chains for the David-fasting tradition from the weak chains; his methodology aligns with Ibn Kathīr's (despite their sectarian distinctions).

## 10. Modern *naẓm* commentary (Farāhī / Iṣlāḥī)

Modern *naẓm* analysis (Farāhī's school, expanded by Amīn Aḥsan Iṣlāḥī in *Tadabbur-i Qurʾān*) reads Q 38 as the **opening of pair-cluster Q 37-38** (al-Ṣāffāti + Ṣād — both oath-introduced, both prophet-cycle), positioned within the larger Q 37-50 mid-Meccan oath-and-prophet-cycle band. Iṣlāḥī's pair-thesis maps closely to the H-NEW-720 finding of Q 37→Q 38 being structurally seamless (delta=0.000 length-units).

## 11. Convergences and disagreements among the mufassirūn

| Topic | al-Ṭabarī | Ibn Kathīr | al-Qurṭubī | al-Zamakhsharī | al-Rāzī |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| ص meaning | mystery (favors silence) | mystery + hadith chain | mystery + jurisprudential | mystery + balaghah | mystery (after listing 20 views) |
| Two-litigants story | tabulates isnāds | flags isrāʾīliyyāt | legal-derivation | balaghah | psycho-moral |
| David's istighfār | minor over-haste | forgiveness-by-grace | accountability-of-rulers | balaghah | ʿiṣma preserved |
| Solomon horse-trial | *ṭafiqa masḥan* = stroking/marking, both views | both views | both views, legal | balaghah of *masḥ* | psycho-moral |
| Job affliction | Wahb-tradition | Wahb-tradition w/ caveat | medical-juristic | balaghah | psychological-theological |
| Sajda v. 24 | recommended | canonical (Bukhārī chain) | Shāfiʿī obligatory / Mālikī shukr | n/a | n/a |

**Convergence**: All five accept the muqaṭṭaʿ ص as letter-of-mystery (with the al-Ḥasan imperative-reading as a minority view), the David panel as trial-of-judgment, the trial-triad parallel via *innahu awwāb*, and the v. 24 sajda. The main classical disagreement is the **status of the elaborated two-litigants narrative** — Ibn Kathīr's caution (*isrāʾīliyyāt*) vs al-Ṭabarī's tabulation-without-endorsement.

## 12. Empirical correlates for classical claims

- al-Biqāʿī's Q 37 → Q 38 *munāsaba*: H-NEW-720 confirms delta=0.000 (seamless seam). **VINDICATED**.
- al-Zamakhsharī's *innahu awwāb* triple-anaphora: §02 §11 confirms phrase is exactly 3× corpus-total, all in Q 38, exactly at the trial-triad boundaries (vv. 17, 30, 44). **VINDICATED**.
- al-Suyūṭī's classification of Q 38, Q 50, Q 68 as singleton-muqaṭṭaʿāt: structurally confirmed in classical-mutq-cluster taxonomy.
- al-Suyūṭī's late-Meccan #38 chronology placement: matches modern Nöldeke chronology and asbāb al-nuzūl traditions.
- al-Rāzī's "20 views on ص": the existence of multiple readings reflects the classical recognition that the muqaṭṭaʿāt are not uniquely decodable (consistent with H-NEW-145 WEAK-SIGNAL on muq letter-set decoding).
- The structural-twin Q 38 ↔ Q 50 is empirically locked at the verse level (Q038-F-01) and at the FR-row level (Q 50 is Q 38's 2nd-nearest neighbor).

The classical commentary tradition on Q 38 is thus **largely consonant with the empirical findings**, with the major disagreement (isrāʾīliyyāt status of the two-litigants elaboration) being a textual-historical question rather than an architectural one.

## 13. Honest limits

- al-Rāzī's full *Mafātīḥ* on Q 38 is large; this survey extracts only the major exegetical positions on the opening and the David-Solomon-Job triad.
- al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar* is a 738-page dense Arabic text; the munāsaba claim cited is from the section on Q 37/Q 38 transition, page ranges roughly mid-volume.
- al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* nawʿ 40 catalogs the muqaṭṭaʿāt; the chronology in nawʿ 1 places Q 38 at order 38, but al-Suyūṭī himself acknowledges multiple chronologies.
- The Mu'tazilī al-Zamakhsharī's reading of David's istighfār as moral-finesse-not-sin is theologically distinctive; later commentators (al-Rāzī, Sunni mainstream) generally agree on *ʿiṣma*-preservation but with different mechanisms.
