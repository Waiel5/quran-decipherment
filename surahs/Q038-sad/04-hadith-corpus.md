## 1. The David sajda — Bukhārī #4601 (idInBook)

**Bukhārī, kitāb al-tafsīr, sūrat ص**, idInBook = **4601**:

Arabic text (verbatim from `bukhari.json`):

> حَدَّثَنِي مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ، حَدَّثَنَا مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عُبَيْدٍ الطَّنَافِسِيُّ، عَنِ الْعَوَّامِ، قَالَ سَأَلْتُ مُجَاهِدًا عَنْ سَجْدَةِ، ص فَقَالَ سَأَلْتُ ابْنَ عَبَّاسٍ مِنْ أَيْنَ سَجَدْتَ فَقَالَ أَوَمَا تَقْرَأُ ‏{‏وَمِنْ ذُرِّيَّتِهِ دَاوُدَ وَسُلَيْمَانَ‏}‏ ‏{‏أُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ هَدَى اللَّهُ فَبِهُدَاهُمُ اقْتَدِهْ‏}‏ فَكَانَ دَاوُدُ مِمَّنْ أُمِرَ نَبِيُّكُمْ صلى الله عليه وسلم أَنْ يَقْتَدِيَ بِهِ، فَسَجَدَهَا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم‏.‏

English (Bukhārī Darussalam translation, AhmedBaset extract):

> Narrated Al-Awwām: I asked Mujāhid regarding the prostration in Sūrat Ṣād. He said, "I asked Ibn ʿAbbās, 'What evidence makes you prostrate?' He said, 'Don't you recite [Q 6:84]: *And among his progeny, David and Solomon...* [and Q 6:90]: *Those are they whom Allāh had guided. So follow their guidance.* So David was one of those prophets whom the Prophet (Muḥammad) was ordered to follow. David prostrated, so Allāh's Messenger (ﷺ) (Muḥammad) performed this prostration too.'"

**Significance**: This is the canonical hadith establishing **Q 38:24 as a sajdat al-tilāwa** in the recitation tradition. The chain Mujāhid → Ibn ʿAbbās → al-ʿAwwām → Bukhārī is sound (ṣaḥīḥ). The hadith ALSO contains a series of vocabulary-glosses on Q 38 (*ʿujāb* = ʿajīb, *al-qiṭṭ* = ṣaḥīfa, *fawāq* = rujūʿ, *qiṭṭanā* = ʿadhābanā, etc.) that Mujāhid relays from Ibn ʿAbbās; these are the foundational *gharīb al-Qurʾān* glosses on the surah.

## 2. The David fasting / prayer ḥadīth — Bukhārī #1903, #1906, #1908, #3279 (idInBook)

The **fasting-of-David** tradition (alternate-day fasting, half-the-year) is preserved in multiple Bukhārī chains via ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ. From the AhmedBaset JSON:

**Bukhārī idInBook 1903** — *kitāb al-ṣawm*:

> Narrated Abū Salama: I asked ʿAbd Allāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ … I then asked about the fasting of David. The Prophet (ﷺ) replied, "Half of the year," (i.e., he used to fast on every alternate day).

**Bukhārī idInBook 1906**: same theme, *kāna yaṣūmu yawman wa-yufṭiru yawman* ("he used to fast a day, break a day").

**Bukhārī idInBook 1908**: same chain, with Abū al-ʿAbbās al-Makkī.

**Bukhārī idInBook 3279** — *kitāb aḥādīth al-anbiyāʾ*: *aḥabbu al-ṣiyāmi ilā Allāhi ṣiyāmu Dāwūda* ("the most beloved fasting to Allah is the fasting of David"); same alternate-day pattern. This appears in the **prophets chapter**, locking the practice as a Davidic-prophetic legacy.

**Bukhārī idInBook 1098** — early occurrence of *yaṣūmu yawman wa-yufṭiru yawman*.

The fasting-of-David tradition is therefore **multi-chain ṣaḥīḥ** in our local archive. The classical *fadāʾil*-of-David block in *kitāb aḥādīth al-anbiyāʾ* (idInBook range ~3279-3290) cross-references with Q 38:17's description of David as *dhā al-aydi … awwāb*.

## 4. Sajdat Ṣād in Abū Dāwūd — idInBook #1402

Abū Dāwūd, *kitāb al-ṣalāh*, *bāb sajda al-Qurʾān*, idInBook **1402**:

> Narrated ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ: "The Prophet (ﷺ) taught me **fifteen prostrations** while reciting the Qurʾān, including three in al-mufaṣṣal and two in Sūrat al-Ḥajj."
> Abū Dāwūd said: Abū al-Dardāʾ has reported eleven prostrations from the Prophet (ﷺ), but the chain of this tradition is weak.

This anchors Q 38:24 as **part of the 15-sajda count** (vs the alternate 14-count which excludes Q 38:24). The chain is graded ṣaḥīḥ by some, ḍaʿīf by others; modern editions typically accept the 15-count if Q 38:24 is included as sajdat al-shukr.

## 6. Solomon traditions — Bukhārī ~550 mentions

The name **Sulaymān** appears in 550 Bukhārī ḥadīth-entries (computed via grep on `bukhari.json`); however, the vast majority are narrators named "Sulaymān b. X" (e.g., Sulaymān b. Bilāl, Sulaymān b. Ḥarb), NOT references to the prophet Solomon. The substantive Solomon-prophet ḥadīth are concentrated in:

- **Bukhārī, kitāb aḥādīth al-anbiyāʾ** (book of the prophets, idInBook range ~3260-3370): including the Sulaymān-100-wives narrative (cross-referenced with Q 38:30-40 horse-trial).
- **Bukhārī, kitāb al-jihād**: the ʿifrīt episode (cross-referenced with Q 27:39-40 — Q 38 doesn't contain this episode).

For Q 38-specific Solomon material, the primary anchor is Bukhārī kitāb al-anbiyāʾ on Solomon's wisdom and his judgment-prowess; these are general Solomon traditions, NOT specific Q 38:30-40 *asbāb al-nuzūl*.

## 7. Job traditions

**Job (Ayyūb) hadith** in Bukhārī are sparse (idInBook 279 is one citation in tahara context; not Q 38-specific). The Job-affliction-and-restoration narrative is primarily a **Quranic narrative** rather than a hadith-narrated one. The Wahb b. Munabbih *isrāʾīliyyāt* tradition (cited by al-Ṭabarī ad Q 38:41) is exegetical, not Saḥīḥ-grade ḥadīth.

The classical claim "Job's wife served him for 18 years" derives from these *isrāʾīliyyāt*, not from canonical hadith.

## 8. Quraysh delegation asbāb al-nuzūl for Q 38

Per al-Wāḥidī (*Asbāb al-nuzūl*, ad Q 38), the surah was occasioned by the **Quraysh delegation event**: when the Prophet ﷺ began openly preaching tawḥīd, leaders of Quraysh (Walīd b. al-Mughīra, Abū Jahl, Abū Lahab, ʿUtba and Shayba b. Rabīʿa, Umayya and Ubayy b. Khalaf, ʿAbd Allāh b. Abī Umayya, al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil) gathered with Abū Ṭālib and demanded he restrain his nephew. Abū Ṭālib counseled the Prophet, who refused to recant. Q 38:1-7 is read as the divine response, with the Quraysh-rebuke (vv. 4-7: *wa-ʿajibū an jāʾahum mundhirun minhum… in hādhā illā ikhtilāq*).

This *asbāb* tradition is preserved in the Sunan al-Tirmidhī (*kitāb al-tafsīr*; not located by exact idInBook in current AhmedBaset extraction), Sunan al-Nasāʾī, and Aḥmad's *Musnad* (Aḥmad #1731 in some numberings). It is also in al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr; and in Ibn Hishām's *Sīra*. The chain Ibn ʿAbbās → ʿIkrima → … is mostly accepted as historically reliable.

## 9. Q 38 in liturgical practice

- The David-sajda (v. 24) is a recitation-prostration in the 15-sajda count tradition.
- Q 38 is one of the **mid-Meccan recitation surahs** in the Ramaḍān tarāwīḥ cycle.
- Recitation virtues (*fadāʾil*) for Q 38 specifically are not richly preserved in the Saḥīḥ corpora; the surah is grouped with the broader prophet-cycle surahs.
