## 1. The Ibn ʿAbbās "Sūrat Badr" identification chain

This is the **prime hadith** for Q 8's identity-as-Badr-narrative (cited by Ibn Kathīr at the head of his Q 8 tafsir). It exists in TWO closely-parallel forms — a short and a long version — both via Hushaym → Abū Bishr → Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās.

### 1a. Bukhārī, idInBook=4439 (kitāb al-tafsīr, chapter 65)
- **Sanad**: Muḥammad b. ʿAbd al-Raḥīm → Saʿīd b. Sulaymān → Hushaym → Abū Bishr → Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās.
- **Matn (verified Arabic)**: قُلْتُ لاِبْنِ عَبَّاسٍ سُورَةُ الأَنْفَالِ قَالَ نَزَلَتْ فِي بَدْرٍ — "I asked Ibn ʿAbbās about Sūrat al-Anfāl. He said: 'It was revealed in connection with Badr.'"
- **Continuation**: the Bukhārī form continues with **lexical glosses** (al-shawkah = al-ḥadd; mardifīn = wave-after-wave; dhūqū = experience-and-try; yarkumahu = he gathers; sharrid = scatter; jaḥaḥū = sought; yuthkhin = subdues; mukāʾan / taṣdiya as Mujāhid's explanation: putting fingers in mouths / whistling). This is a **gloss-extension** appended to the prime identification — Bukhārī preserves the chain plus the philological-tafsir of Q 8's distinctive lexicon.
- **Significance**: Q 8 is the only surah named in a major hadith chain by a SECOND name (Sūrat Badr). The naming-by-content (Badr) supersedes the naming-by-opening-word (Anfāl) as a classical-tradition fingerprint.

### 1b. Bukhārī, idInBook=4674 (kitāb al-tafsīr)
- **Sanad**: same as 1a (Muḥammad b. ʿAbd al-Raḥīm → Saʿīd b. Sulaymān → Hushaym → Abū Bishr → Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās).
- **Matn (verified Arabic)**: قُلْتُ لاِبْنِ عَبَّاسٍ سُورَةُ التَّوْبَةِ قَالَ التَّوْبَةُ هِيَ الْفَاضِحَةُ ... قَالَ قُلْتُ سُورَةُ الأَنْفَالِ قَالَ نَزَلَتْ فِي بَدْرٍ ... قَالَ قُلْتُ سُورَةُ الْحَشْرِ قَالَ نَزَلَتْ فِي بَنِي النَّضِيرِ — Saʿīd b. Jubayr's three-question chain: (1) "What about Sūrat al-Tawba?" — "Sūrat al-Tawba is *al-fāḍiḥa* (the Exposer); it kept revealing 'and of them ... and of them ...' until [the hypocrites] thought no one would be left unmentioned." (2) "What about Sūrat al-Anfāl?" — "It was revealed in connection with Badr." (3) "What about Sūrat al-Ḥashr?" — "It was revealed in connection with Banū al-Naḍīr."
- **Significance**: a **3-surah identification chain** that ANCHORS Q 8 (Badr), Q 9 (al-Tawba = al-Fāḍiḥa), and Q 59 (al-Ḥashr = Banū al-Naḍīr) to specific historical events. The Q 9 → al-Fāḍiḥa identification is the SISTER-anchor for the Q 8 → Sūrat-Badr identification — both via the same Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās chain.

### 1c. Muslim, idInBook=7363 (kitāb al-tafsīr, chapter 56)
- **Sanad**: ʿAbdallāh b. Muṭīʿ → Hushaym → Abū Bishr → Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās.
- **Matn (verified Arabic)**: same 3-surah question-chain as Bukhārī 4674; Muslim's wording for Anfāl: قَالَ تِلْكَ سُورَةُ بَدْرٍ — "He said: 'That [is] Sūrat Badr.'"
- **Significance**: Muslim's chain is independent of Bukhārī's at the Companion-of-the-third-tier level (ʿAbdallāh b. Muṭīʿ vs Saʿīd b. Sulaymān) but identical from Hushaym onward. The two-collection convergence on the same Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās matn is **ṣaḥīḥ-by-double-attestation**.
- **Lexical note**: Muslim's wording *tilka Sūrat Badr* ("THAT [is] Sūrat Badr") is a stronger predicative-identification than Bukhārī's *nazalat fī Badr* ("[it] was revealed in [connection-with] Badr"). Muslim's wording justifies the strong title-substitution (Sūrat Badr ≡ Sūrat al-Anfāl); Bukhārī's wording justifies the asbāb-claim (Sūrat al-Anfāl was revealed about Badr). Both readings co-exist in the classical tradition.

## 2. The Q 8:1 *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-anfāl* asbāb chain — the Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ sword

### Tirmidhī, idInBook=3163 (kitāb tafsīr al-Qurʾān, sūrat al-Anfāl)
- **Sanad**: Abū Kurayb → Abū Bakr b. ʿAyyāsh → ʿĀṣim b. Bahdala → Muṣʿab b. Saʿd → his father (Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ).
- **Matn (verified Arabic)**: لَمَّا كَانَ يَوْمُ بَدْرٍ جِئْتُ بِسَيْفٍ فَقُلْتُ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ إِنَّ اللَّهَ قَدْ شَفَى صَدْرِي مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ ... هَبْ لِي هَذَا السَّيْفَ. فَقَالَ هَذَا لَيْسَ لِي وَلاَ لَكَ ... فَنَزَلَتْ يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الأَنْفَالِ — Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ's account: at Badr, he brought a sword, asked the Prophet ﷺ for it; the Prophet ﷺ said "this is not mine to give, nor yours to take"; Saʿd raised the concern that the sword would go to one less-deserving; the Prophet ﷺ then returned and said "you asked when it was not for me; now it has come to me, so it is yours"; THEN was revealed *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-anfāl*.
- **Significance**: this is the **canonical *asbāb al-nuzūl*** for Q 8:1 — the direct textual context for the legal-question-opener of the surah. The Saʿd-sword episode shows that the *anfāl* dispute at Badr was over individual-warrior-claims (*salab* / *zawāʾid*), and the Quranic verdict establishes the divine-and-Messenger ownership-in-principle.
- **Verified**: this hadith DIRECTLY contains the Q 8:1 phrase *yasʾalūnaka ʿan al-anfāl* (verified by direct Arabic-text grep).

## 3. Q 8:67 prisoner-ransom episode — the ʿUmar / Abū Bakr debate

### Muslim, idInBook=4456 (kitāb al-jihād wa-l-siyar)
- **Sanad**: Hannād b. al-Sariyy → Ibn al-Mubārak → ʿIkrima b. ʿAmmār → Simāk al-Ḥanafī → Ibn ʿAbbās → ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb // ALT-CHAIN: Zuhayr b. Ḥarb → ʿUmar b. Yūnus al-Ḥanafī → ʿIkrima b. ʿAmmār → Abū Zumayl (= Simāk al-Ḥanafī) → ʿAbdallāh b. ʿAbbās → ʿUmar.
- **Matn (verified Arabic)**: when it was the day of Badr, the Messenger ﷺ looked at the disbelievers (1,000) and at his Companions (319). The Prophet ﷺ turned toward the qibla and supplicated: *Allāhumma anjiz lī mā waʿadtanī ... Allāhumma in tahlik hādhihi al-ʿiṣāba al-muslima fa-lā tuʿbad fī al-arḍ*. After the battle, when the prisoners were taken, the Prophet ﷺ asked Abū Bakr and ʿUmar what to do with them. **Abū Bakr argued for ransom and kinship** (since they were "your brothers and clan, and ransom may benefit us against the disbelievers and may be a path to their guidance"); **ʿUmar argued for execution** (since they were the leaders of disbelief). The Prophet ﷺ initially inclined to Abū Bakr's view; the next day ʿUmar found the Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr weeping. **Q 8:67-68 (*mā kāna li-nabīyin an yakūna lahu asrā ḥattā yuthkhina fī al-arḍ*) was revealed as the divine reproach for the ransom decision.**
- **Significance**: this is the **canonical *asbāb*** for Q 8:67-68 and is one of the rare hadiths to record a **Quranic correction of a prophetic-judgment** episode. Muslim's chain is the most-detailed; the parallel Tirmidhī chain (#3168 below) preserves a complementary version.

### Tirmidhī, idInBook=3168 (kitāb tafsīr al-Qurʾān)
- **Sanad**: Hannād → Abū Muʿāwiya → al-Aʿmash → ʿAmr b. Murra → Abū ʿUbayda b. ʿAbdallāh → ʿAbdallāh b. Masʿūd.
- **Matn (verified Arabic)**: when the prisoners were brought at Badr, the Messenger ﷺ asked the Companions about them; the Prophet ﷺ said: *lā yanfalitanna minhum aḥad illā bi-fidāʾin aw ḍarbi ʿunuqin* — "let none of them escape except by ransom or beheading." Ibn Masʿūd interjected naming Suhayl b. al-Bayḍāʾ (whom he had heard mention Islam, suggesting a possible exception). The Prophet ﷺ was silent, indicating tacit acknowledgment of the exception. The narrative continues with the v.67 reproach.
- **Verified**: contains the EXACT Q 8:67 phrase *mā kāna li-nabīyin an yakūna lahu asrā* (verified by direct Arabic-text grep).
- **Significance**: parallel Ibn Masʿūd chain to the Muslim ʿUmar chain. Together they triangulate the Badr-prisoner-ransom historical episode and the Q 8:67 reproach.

## 4. The 313-warriors-at-Badr count

### Bukhārī, idInBook=3793 (kitāb al-Maghāzī, bāb-Badr)
- **Sanad**: ʿAmr b. Khālid → Zuhayr → Abū Isḥāq → al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib (with the report from "the Companions of Muḥammad ﷺ who witnessed Badr").
- **Matn (verified Arabic)**: حَدَّثَنِي أَصْحَابُ مُحَمَّدٍ صلى الله عليه وسلم مِمَّنْ شَهِدَ بَدْرًا أَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا عِدَّةَ أَصْحَابِ طَالُوتَ الَّذِينَ جَازُوا مَعَهُ النَّهَرَ بِضْعَةَ عَشَرَ وَثَلاَثَمِائَةٍ — "the Companions who witnessed Badr told me that they numbered the same as the Companions of Ṭālūt (Saul) who crossed the river with him: 313 men [literally: 'over three-hundred-and-ten']." Al-Barāʾ closes: "By God, none crossed the river [in Q 2:249] but a believer."
- **Significance**: this is the **canonical 313-Badr-warriors** count, anchored by typological-parallel to Ṭālūt's army at Q 2:249 (the *fa-shariba minhu illā qalīlan minhum* test). The Bukhārī chain establishes the Q 8 (Badr) ↔ Q 2:249 (Ṭālūt) typological pair.
- **Note**: the Muslim 4456 chain (above §3) gives the count as 319 ("his own Companions were three hundred and nineteen"). The two numbers (313 / 319) are both classical; the difference (3 men) is variously explained in the tradition (e.g., 3 men joined later or were counted differently). **MW-6 verification status: PENDING** — a single canonical edition of Bukhārī should be consulted before quoting either as definitive.

## 5. The Q 8:65-66 naskh-trigger pair

The classical *naskh* (abrogation) tradition cites Q 8:65 → Q 8:66 as one of the corpus's clearest internal naskh-pairs (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 47). The hadith-corpus on this pair is preserved primarily in:

### Bukhārī, kitāb al-tafsīr, sūrat al-Anfāl (chapter id 65)
- The al-Bukhārī chain (via Ibn ʿAbbās) reports that the original 1:10 endurance command was felt as a hardship; "now God has eased" introduces the 1:2 ratio. The hadith-text DIRECTLY echoes the Quranic *al-āna khaffafa allāhu ʿankum*. This chain is preserved in al-Bukhārī's Anfāl-tafsīr cluster (idInBook range surrounding 4439); the exact idInBook for the naskh-specific chain requires verification against print-edition (MW-6 PENDING).
- **Cross-tradition**: al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 47 cites this pair as canonical-naskh; al-Ṭabarī *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* gives it extensive treatment.

## 6. Bukhārī Maghāzī Badr cluster (overview)

Q 8 is the **densest Quranic source** for the Battle-of-Badr asbāb-narrative. The Bukhārī **Kitāb al-Maghāzī** (chapter id 64 in the AhmedBaset JSON) contains the canonical Badr-cluster of hadith.

### Cluster anchors (verified)
- **Bukhārī Kitāb al-Maghāzī** total hadith: 488 (idInBook range **3785–4272**).
- **Hadith mentioning *Badr*** in Maghāzī: **73**.
- **First-Maghāzī Badr-mention**: idInBook 3786.
- **Last-Maghāzī Badr-mention**: idInBook 4224.
- **Density**: ~15% of Maghāzī hadith mention Badr (73/488).

The cluster includes:
- The 313-warriors count (idInBook 3793-3795 area).
- The list of named Badr-Companions (al-Bukhārī preserves several rosters with sand-and-stand-in-sequence narratives).
- The angel-host (1,000 angels in succession, Q 8:9 *mardifīn*).
- The Quraysh-side details (the 70-killed, 70-prisoners count).
- The Prophet's ﷺ duʿāʾ at the *ʿarīsh* (the wooden shelter where the Prophet ﷺ supplicated during the battle).

The "Sūrat-al-Anfāl = Sūrat-Badr" identification (§1) anchors the **textual link** from this hadith-cluster to the surah.

## 7. Muslim Kitāb al-Jihād — Badr cluster

Muslim's **kitāb al-jihād wa-l-siyar** (chapter id includes the prisoner-ransom hadith #4456 above) preserves the parallel Badr-cluster. Notable hadiths:
- **Muslim 4456** (the ʿUmar / Abū Bakr ransom-debate, §3 above) — the prime asbāb for Q 8:67-68.
- Multiple chains on the *Badr-day-supplication* (the *Allāhumma anjiz lī mā waʿadtanī* duʿāʾ).
- The al-Khattab → ʿUmar narrative of the *aʿyun al-jaysh* (the eyes of the army — i.e., the 1,000-vs-313 numerical mismatch).

The Muslim cluster has 63 *Badr*-mentions across all chapters (verified by direct text-search).

## 9. The Q 8:17 *wa-mā ramayta idh ramayta* hadith-context

Surprisingly, the EXACT Q 8:17 phrase *wa-mā ramayta idh ramayta* is **NOT directly quoted as a hadith-matn** in any of the 9 canonical books (verified by direct Arabic-text grep — 0 hits across Bukhārī / Muslim / Tirmidhī / Abū Dāwūd / Nasāʾī / Ibn Mājah / Mālik / Aḥmad / Dārimī).

The verse's **content** is preserved in hadith-narrative form (the pebble-throw episode is recounted in al-Ṭabarī's tafsir and al-Wāqidī's *Maghāzī*), but the **exact Quranic phrase** does not appear as a hadith-matn — meaning the verse is invoked-as-citation (in tafsir) rather than echoed-as-prayer-formula (as e.g. Q 1, Q 36 are).

This is consistent with the verse's **uniquely-Quranic** status: the *wa-mā ramayta idh ramayta wa-lākin* construction is found exactly **once in 6,236 verses** (corpus-unique, verified — see `06-novel-findings.md` Q008-F-02), and that uniqueness is preserved in the hadith-tradition's restraint from echoing it. The verse is iʿjāz-specific in a way that hadith-tradition treats as singular-Quranic-property.
