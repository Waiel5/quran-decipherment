# Q 8 al-Anfāl — Classical Tafsīr Survey

Survey of the major classical mufassirūn on Q 8's principal exegetical loci: (a) the legal verdict on *al-anfāl* (vv. 1, 41); (b) the Q 8:17 yaqīn-formula and its iʿjāz-status; (c) the Badr-narrative integration; (d) the muhājirūn/anṣār walāʾ-formula (vv. 72-75); (e) the *naskh*-trigger pair vv. 65-66; (f) the Q 8 ↔ Q 9 munāsabah and the "one-surah" classical claim.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*)

Source: standard print-edition (Hijr / Dār al-Maʿārif), Q 8 begins at vol. 13 p. 369 onward (Hijr 2001 ed.).

### Q 8:1 — "the *anfāl* belong to God and the Messenger"
al-Ṭabarī catalogs the major Companion+Successor positions on what *anfāl* refers to:
- **The spoils-of-war taken at Badr** (Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Ibn Zayd) — the dominant view; the *asbāb al-nuzūl* (cause-of-revelation) is the dispute among the Badr-warriors over how to divide the spoils, with the youth (those who did the fighting) and the elders (who stayed with the Prophet ﷺ) and the chase-pursuers each claiming entitlement.
- **The supererogatory share given to a warrior beyond his standard portion** (a more legal-technical reading; e.g., the *salab* — the slain enemy's personal effects — given to the killer).
- **The whole of the spoils, before the *khums*-extraction was legislated** (some early views read v.1 as reading the verdict as "the *anfāl* belong wholly to God and the Messenger" UNTIL the v.41 *khums*-distribution superseded this; this is a *naskh*-claim catalogued by al-Ṭabarī).

al-Ṭabarī's editorial position favors the Badr-narrative *asbāb*; he records al-ʿUbāda b. al-Ṣāmit's account that the prophet ﷺ resolved the Badr-dispute by re-distributing the spoils equally after the v.1 + v.41 verses came down.

### Q 8:17 — "you did not throw"
al-Ṭabarī catalogs the dominant view: the verse refers to the Prophet's ﷺ throwing of a handful of pebbles at the Badr-encounter, which (by divine intervention) reached every disbeliever's eyes and disabled them. al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās via several chains (al-Ḍaḥḥāk, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid) that the *ramy* refers specifically to this pebble-throw.

The *takhrīj al-fāʿil* (transferring agency) reading is given as the key theological point: while the human-side of the action is real, the True Cause is divine. al-Ṭabarī notes the parallel with the preceding clause *fa-lam taqtulūhum wa-lākinna allāha qatalahum* — both clauses establish the same agency-transfer principle.

### Q 8:41 — "*yawm al-furqān*"
al-Ṭabarī identifies this unanimously as the Day of Badr (Friday, 17 Ramaḍān 2 AH). The 6-fold *khums*-distribution (God / Messenger / kin / orphans / indigent / wayfarer) is catalogued with companion-disagreement on the post-prophetic disposition of the *khums* portion (after the Prophet's ﷺ death, the categories that include "the Messenger" and "his kin" became a major early-Islamic legal dispute — Sunni and Shīʿī jurisprudences diverge here).

### Q 8:65-66 — naskh-trigger pair
al-Ṭabarī catalogs this as one of the **clearest classical *naskh* (abrogation) cases**: v.65 commands believers to stand firm against ten times their number; v.66 immediately follows with *al-āna khaffafa allāhu ʿankum* — "now God has eased it" — replacing the 1:10 ratio with 1:2. al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās via several chains as the prime authority for this naskh-pair; al-Suyūṭī later canonizes it in *al-Itqān* nawʿ 47 (al-nāsikh wa-l-mansūkh) as one of the surah's two attested naskh-cases (the other being v.61 about peace-inclination, contested as abrogated by Q 9:5).

## 2. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` (full text); Q 8 section opens around vol. 15 p. 116 onward (standard Beirut Dār Iḥyāʾ ed.).

### Q 8:17 — al-Rāzī's iʿjāz al-takhrīj exposition
al-Rāzī gives the verse what is arguably the corpus's most thorough treatment of the *takhrīj al-fāʿil* (agency-transfer) doctrine. He distinguishes 3 levels:
- **Level 1 (apparent)**: the Prophet ﷺ visibly threw the pebbles.
- **Level 2 (proximate)**: the human throw is real, but its cause-effect chain is finite-natural (a handful of pebbles cannot disable a whole army).
- **Level 3 (real-cause)**: God is the *al-fāʿil al-ḥaqīqī* — the True Agent — Whose creating-of-the-effect is what the throw "really" was. The verse's iʿjāz is the simultaneous-affirmation of human-action AND the assertion that, at the level of efficient causation, *Allah* is the true subject of the verb.

al-Rāzī also notes the rhetorical-symmetry of the double-clause (*fa-lam taqtulūhum wa-lākinna allāha qatalahum* + *wa-mā ramayta idh ramayta wa-lākinna allāha ramā*) and links it to the *muḥkamāt* (firm-meaning verses) on divine-omnipotence (Q 81:29 *wa-mā tashāʾūna illā an yashāʾa allāhu rabbu al-ʿālamīn* — "you do not will except as God wills"). The verse anchors a CHAIN of *al-fāʿil al-ḥaqīqī* verses through the corpus.

### Q 8:1 + 8:41 — the *anfāl* / *ghanīma* distinction
al-Rāzī works through the linguistic distinction: *al-anfāl* is from the root *n-f-l* (supererogatory addition); *al-ghanīma* (used in v.41) is from *gh-n-m* (acquisition). His position: vv. 1-4 establish the divine-Messenger ownership of the *anfāl* in PRINCIPLE (i.e., the meta-rule that the spoils belong to God-and-the-Messenger to distribute as they will); v.41 establishes the SPECIFIC distribution rule (1/5 in 6 categories, 4/5 to the warriors). The two are NOT in *naskh*-relation but in PRINCIPLE-vs-EXECUTION relation.

### Q 8:72-75 — the muhājirūn/anṣār *walāʾ*-formula
al-Rāzī identifies this as the **constitutional founding of the Madīnan Islamic polity**. He notes the 4-fold qualification structure for muhājirūn (believed + emigrated + strove with wealth + strove with souls) and the 2-fold for anṣār (sheltered + helped). Non-emigrant believers do NOT have *walāʾ* with the polity until they emigrate, with the EXCEPTION of religious-help in matters of dīn (this is the v.72 *illā ʿalā qawmin baynakum wa-baynahum mīthāq* clause — the exception clause for tribes with whom the Madīnan polity has a treaty).

al-Rāzī makes a notable observation: the *al-muʾminūn ḥaqqā* phrase appears at v.4 AND at v.74 — the surah opens with the faith-prayer-spending definition and closes with the emigration-help-jihād definition. He reads this as an INCLUSIO marking the surah's full circle: the surah's first-block defines the *muʾmin ḥaqqā* in the religious-spiritual sense, and the closing-block defines the same term in the polity-formation sense. The surah is the **constitutional charter of the Madīnan dīn-polity**.

## 3. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt`; Q 8 section opens around vol. 7 p. 350 onward (standard Cairo Dār al-Kutub ed.).

### Q 8:1 — *al-anfāl* legal-technical exposition
al-Qurṭubī (Mālikī) provides the most extensive **legal-jurisprudential** treatment among classical mufassirūn. He catalogs the 4 mājor positions on *anfāl*:
- (1) the spoils-as-a-whole (the v.1 wording is general; the *khums* of v.41 is the specific-distribution rule);
- (2) the *salab* (the slain-enemy's effects given to the killer; reflects the Prophet's ﷺ practice of awarding *salab* to specific warriors);
- (3) the *zawāʾid* (the additions-beyond-standard-share given to leaders/exemplary-warriors);
- (4) the *fayʾ* (the spoils-without-fighting; al-Qurṭubī notes the al-Ḥanbalī school distinguishes *anfāl/ghanīma/fayʾ* differently).

al-Qurṭubī's Mālikī position favors (3): the *anfāl* are the *zawāʾid* given to specific warriors, with the *khums* / 4/5 distribution ruling the bulk. He cites the Prophet's ﷺ resolution at Badr as the precedent.

### Q 8:67-68 — the prisoners-of-war episode
al-Qurṭubī (with extensive ḥadīth-citation) catalogs the ʿUmar/Abū Bakr Badr-prisoner-debate: ʿUmar argued for execution (citing the disbelief-vengeance principle); Abū Bakr argued for ransom (citing kinship + future-conversion-hope); the Prophet ﷺ accepted Abū Bakr's view; the verse v.67 came down as the divine reproach. al-Qurṭubī notes the *ḥadīth* preserved in Muslim (#1763 in the al-ʿAlamī numbering) that the Prophet ﷺ wept upon receiving v.67 and said: "The torment had nearly come upon you for what you took; if a torment had come down, none would have escaped except [Saʿd b. Muʿādh]" (or a similar small-named-list). The Q 8:67-68 pair is therefore a **prophetic-judgment-correction** episode preserved BOTH in Quranic-text AND in ḥadīth.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`; Q 8 section opens around vol. 4 p. 4 onward (Dār al-Ṭayba ed.).

### Q 8 introduction: "Sūrat al-Anfāl is the *Sūrat al-Badr*"
Ibn Kathīr opens Q 8 with the al-Bukhārī *ḥadīth* (Bukhārī 4882, classical Fatḥ al-Bārī numbering for *al-Maghāzī, bāb shuhūd al-malāʾika Badran*) via Saʿīd b. Jubayr, who asked Ibn ʿAbbās about al-Anfāl, and Ibn ʿAbbās replied: ***Sūrat Badr*** ("the surah of Badr") — emphasizing the surah's identity as the asbāb-narrative of the battle, not just the legal-apparatus of spoils-distribution.

Ibn Kathīr structures his commentary around the Badr-narrative chain. He cites:
- **Bukhārī Maghāzī Badr cluster** (idInBook range covering al-Bukhārī kitāb al-Maghāzī bāb-Badr — in the AhmedBaset JSON corpus this maps to idInBook 3950-4030; classical Fatḥ al-Bārī numbering 3950-3996 approximately, with the prime ḥadīth at #3953 (Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās "Sūrat Badr" identification) and #3982-3985 (the 313-warriors-at-Badr count chains).
- **Muslim Jihād cluster** (Muslim kitāb al-jihād bāb ghazwat Badr; classical Muslim numbering #1763 for the prisoner-ransom episode + several adjacent ḥadīth).

### Q 8:17 — Ibn Kathīr's pebble-throw narrative
Ibn Kathīr cites the al-Ṭabarānī (al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr) chain (via Hishām b. ʿUrwa → ʿUrwa b. al-Zubayr) that the Prophet ﷺ took a handful of pebbles, threw them at the Quraysh army with the words *shāhat al-wujūh* ("disgraced be the faces!"), and that no Qurashi disbeliever escaped without dust in his eyes from that throw. The verse Q 8:17 came down as the divine-affirmation that this throw was, in efficient-causation terms, God's throw.

Ibn Kathīr also notes the ḥadīth via Anas b. Mālik (in al-Bukhārī) that on the morning of Badr the Prophet ﷺ saw Jibrīl on a horse with his teeth bared in laughter, signaling divine help. This grounds the verse's frame in the broader Badr-day theophany cluster.

### Q 8:75 + Q 9:1 transition
Ibn Kathīr's final paragraphs of Q 8 explicitly cite the classical view (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān) that *Sūrat al-Anfāl* and *Sūrat al-Tawba* are connected as a single thematic-and-legal unit. He cites the Ibn ʿAbbās ḥadīth via Saʿīd b. Jubayr that the basmala-omission between Q 8 and Q 9 was deliberately preserved on this view — though Ibn Kathīr himself does NOT endorse the strict "one surah" reading; he distinguishes the *thematic continuity* claim from the *one-surah identity* claim. (See `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for full audit.)

## 5. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-durr-manthur.openiti.raw.txt` and the print Itqān (Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyya / Dār Ibn Kathīr eds.).

### *al-Itqān* nawʿ on the number-of-surahs
al-Suyūṭī catalogs the Ibn ʿAbbās tradition that Q 8 + Q 9 are reportedly recited as one surah (per Ubayy b. Kaʿb's muṣḥaf reading; preserved primarily in al-Tirmidhī tafsīr al-Qurʾān bāb on Q 9 — specifically al-Tirmidhī kitāb tafsīr al-Qurʾān, the chain via Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ibn ʿAbbās → ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān, where ʿUthmān is reported to say that Q 8 + Q 9 were of the *as-sabʿ al-ṭiwāl* and that he placed them together because the basmala had not come down to separate them).

al-Suyūṭī's editorial position is **agnostic-cataloging**: he records both views (one surah / two surahs) and notes the structural-argument that the basmala-omission is the key textual marker — but does NOT endorse the strict identity-claim. This pattern (catalog-without-endorsement) is the al-Suyūṭī signature in *al-Itqān*.

### *al-Itqān* nawʿ 47 — al-nāsikh wa-l-mansūkh
al-Suyūṭī catalogs the Q 8:65-66 pair as one of the *clearest* corpus-internal naskh-cases (alongside Q 2:184 → Q 2:185 fasting; Q 4:43 → Q 5:90 wine-prohibition stages). He cites the v.65 → v.66 ratio-relaxation as canonical-undisputed.

### *al-Durr al-manthūr* — Q 8 ḥadīth-compilation
al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* gives the surah extensive *riwāya*-treatment, with chains drawn from:
- al-Bukhārī (the al-Anfāl chain via Ibn ʿAbbās, classical #3953 in Fatḥ al-Bārī).
- Muslim (the prisoner-ransom episode, #1763).
- al-Tirmidhī tafsīr-al-Qurʾān (the v.65-66 *naskh* chain).
- Ahmad b. Ḥanbal *al-Musnad* (multiple chains on the pebble-throw episode of v.17).
- al-Ṭabarānī, Saʿīd b. Manṣūr, Ibn al-Mundhir.

## 6. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*) — the Q 8 ↔ Q 9 munāsabah

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` (where available) — al-Biqāʿī's 22-volume munāsabah-tafsīr.

al-Biqāʿī's Q 8 ↔ Q 9 munāsabah-analysis is a key locus for the basmala-omission tradition. His position:
- Q 8 establishes the **walāʾ-foundation** (*al-ladhīna āmanū wa-hājarū wa-jāhadū fī sabīl allāh* etc.); Q 9 is the **walāʾ-disownment** (*barāʾatun min allāhi wa-rasūlihi* — divine-and-prophetic disavowal of the disbeliever-treaties).
- The basmala-omission is the structural-marker of this walāʾ-foundation → walāʾ-disownment continuity.
- al-Biqāʿī does NOT endorse the "one-surah" strict reading; he reads the omission as a *deliberate textual signal of the thematic-legal continuity*, with the two surahs preserved as distinct units.

This is the **classical-balāgha refinement** of the Ibn ʿAbbās claim: the unity is at the *thematic-legal-continuation* level (which the canonical mushaf preserves via basmala-omission), NOT at the *surah-identity* level. The Q 8 + Q 9 are TWO distinct surahs whose continuation is structurally marked.

**Empirical convergence**: this al-Biqāʿī reading is empirically VINDICATED by H-NEW-890 T1 (Q 8 + Q 9 are FR-distinct: rank 81/113 in adjacent-pair distance; see `06-novel-findings.md` Q008-F-01). The mushaf-tradition's basmala-asymmetry is preserved as a thematic-continuity marker, not a unity-claim.
