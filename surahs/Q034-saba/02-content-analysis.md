# Q 34 Sabaʾ — Content Analysis

## 1. Macro-structure

Q 34 is a **late-Meccan creedal-historical-eschatological compendium** organized in 4 thematic macro-blocks. The Sabaʾ-flood narrative (Block C, vv. 15-19) is the structural HINGE between two prophet-precedent vignettes (David/Solomon, Block B) and the long polemic-eschatology section (Block D).

| Block | vv. | Length | Theme | Function |
|:--|:-:|:-:|:--|:--|
| A | 1-9 | 9 | al-ḥamdu li-llāh + creator-omniscience + scripture-frame | doctrinal preamble |
| B | 10-14 | 5 | David + Solomon vignette (mountains-praising; jinn-workers; termite-death) | blessed-prophet precedent |
| C | 15-19 | 5 | Sabaʾ-flood narrative (the two-garden kingdom; sayl al-ʿarim; mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq) | punished-kingdom precedent |
| D | 20-54 | 35 | prophet-rejection polemic + eschatology + tawḥīd (kāffatan li-l-nās; angels-disavow-shirk; final-judgment imagery) | contemporary application + meta-doctrine |

The B/C juxtaposition is rhetorically pivotal: David and Solomon are blessed for their gratitude (*al-shukūr*, v.13), while the Sabaeans are punished for their ingratitude (*ẓalamū anfusahum*, v.19). The sequence sets up the Block D polemic by showing two historical precedents that the contemporary Meccan audience must choose between.

## 2. The qul-imperative density

Q 34 contains **15 *qul* imperatives across 14 verses** — a 5.22× enrichment over corpus mean (corpus-total 332 *qul* across 6,236 verses per H-NEW-74; mean rate 0.053 per verse vs Q 34's 0.278 per verse). The *qul* imperatives concentrate in:
- v.3 (1): *qul balā wa-rabbī la-taʾtiyannakum* — the resurrection-affirmation oath.
- vv. 22-30 (8 *qul* in 9 verses): the *qul* polemic-cluster — directing the Prophet ﷺ to issue 8 successive challenges to the polytheists.
- vv. 36-50 (6 *qul* in 15 verses): the second polemic-cluster — sustenance, recompense, the one-thing-only homiletic, return-of-truth.

This is one of the corpus's most *qul*-dense surahs, consistent with its late-Meccan position (H-NEW-74 KW p=10⁻⁷ Late-Meccan *qul*-peak). The *qul*-density signature places Q 34 in the *al-Suyūṭī Late-Meccan scripture-announcement* class (cross-finding-012 Pattern-B markers).

## 3. Verse-by-verse content summary

### Block A (1-9): al-ḥamdu li-llāh + omniscience + scripture-frame

#### v.1 — the dual-ḥamd opening (corpus-unique)
*al-ḥamdu li-llāhi alladhī lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi wa-lahu al-ḥamdu fī al-ākhirati wa-huwa al-ḥakīmu al-khabīr*

The corpus's **only** verse with two occurrences of *al-ḥamd* — a dual-praise structure (this-world + Hereafter). The opening explicitly extends praise into the eschatological frame, establishing the surah's eschatology theme from the first verse. Closing divine names *al-ḥakīm al-khabīr* (the All-Wise, the All-Aware) prefigure the omniscience theme of v.2-3.

#### v.2 — the four-direction omniscience axis
*yaʿlamu mā yaliju fī al-arḍi wa-mā yakhruju minhā wa-mā yanzilu min al-samāʾi wa-mā yaʿruju fīhā*

The 4-direction omniscience formula: (down into earth / up out of earth / down from heaven / up to heaven). A complete cosmological-omniscience grid. The verse closes with *al-raḥīm al-ghafūr* (the Merciful, the Forgiving), pivoting from omniscience to soteriology.

#### v.3 — the *mithqāl dharra* doctrine
*qul balā wa-rabbī la-taʾtiyannakum ʿālimi al-ghaybi lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍi wa-lā aṣghara min dhālika wa-lā akbara illā fī kitābin mubīn*

The Quran's two attestations of the *mithqāl dharra* (atom's-weight) doctrine are Q 34:3 here and Q 34:22 (parallel polemic verse below). The "atom's weight" is the smallest conceivable mass; the doctrine is that NOT EVEN that mass escapes God's knowledge. The verse closes with *kitābin mubīn* (a clear book) — the divine record. This is one of the corpus's strongest **omniscience + recording** doctrines.

#### vv. 4-5 — the binary recompense
- v.4: those who believed and did good have *maghfira* + *rizq karīm*.
- v.5: those who *saʿaw fī āyātinā muʿājizīn* (strove against Our signs in vain) have *ʿadhāb min rijz alīm*.

Standard creedal binary; the pair recurs at vv. 37-38 ("the wealth-and-progeny do not bring you near unless you believe + work" / "those striving against Our signs are present in the Punishment").

#### v.6 — the scripture-frame
*wa-yarā alladhīna ūtū al-ʿilma alladhī unzila ilayka min rabbika huwa al-ḥaqqa wa-yahdī ilā ṣirāṭi al-ʿazīzi al-ḥamīd*

The People-of-Knowledge confirm that the revelation is the truth. This is one of the surah's scripture-frame markers — alongside v.31 (*lan nuʾmina bi-hādhā al-qurʾān*) and v.43 (*idhā tutlā ʿalayhim āyātunā bayyināt*). The *al-ʿazīz al-ḥamīd* closing-pair connects back to v.1's *al-ḥakīm al-khabīr* and forward to v.27's *al-ʿazīz al-ḥakīm*.

#### vv. 7-8 — the deniers' mockery + the response
- v.7: *wa-qāla alladhīna kafarū hal nadullukum ʿalā rajulin yunabbiʾukum idhā muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq innakum la-fī khalqin jadīd* — "Shall we point you to a man who informs you that when you have been broken into a thousand pieces, you will be in a new creation?" The mocking frame is crucial: *muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq* will recur in v.19 as the FATE of the Sabaeans (*mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq*) — the mockers' phrase BECOMES the description of the historical-precedent kingdom they should be heeding.
- v.8: *aftarā ʿalā allāhi kadhiban am bihi jinnatun* — "Has he forged a lie about Allah, or is he possessed?" Standard polemic-against-the-Prophet formula (cf. Q 23:25).

The *muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq* / *mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq* pairing across v.7 and v.19 is a striking **rhetorical inclusio** within the surah, with the mocking accusers (v.7) condemning the very fate that befell the Sabaeans (v.19) — a classical *iltifāt* (rhetorical pivot) marker.

#### v.9 — the cosmic-warning pendant
*a-fa-lam yarū ilā mā bayna aydīhim wa-mā khalfahum min al-samāʾi wa-l-arḍ in nashaʾ nakhsif bihim al-arḍa aw nusqiṭ ʿalayhim kisafan min al-samāʾ inna fī dhālika la-āyatan li-kulli ʿabdin munīb*

The cosmic-threat verse: God could collapse the earth under them or drop fragments of the sky upon them. This pendant verse sets up the historical-warning narrative of Block B-C: the cosmic-threat is the abstract version of the Sabaʾ-flood-of-the-dam concrete instance.

### Block B (10-14): David + Solomon

#### vv. 10-11 — David: mountains-praising + iron-softening
- v.10: *wa-laqad ātaynā dāwūda minnā faḍlan yā jibālu awwibī maʿahu wa-l-ṭayra wa-alannā lahu al-ḥadīd* — "We gave David a favor from Us; O mountains, repeat with him, and the birds; and We softened the iron for him."
- v.11: *an iʿmal sābighātin wa-qaddir fī al-sard wa-ʿmalū ṣāliḥan innī bi-mā taʿmalūna baṣīr* — "Make full coats of mail and measure links accurately; do good — I see what you do."

David's faḍl (favor) is detailed in three concrete miracles: mountains-and-birds repeating his praise, iron-softening (the divine accommodation of metalwork), and chain-mail-craft (per al-Suyūṭī tafsir of *sābighāt*). This is the corpus's most concentrated David-craftsmanship passage.

#### vv. 12-13 — Solomon: wind, molten-copper, jinn-workers, palace-craft
- v.12: *wa-li-sulaymāna al-rīḥa ghuduwwuhā shahrun wa-rawāḥuhā shahrun wa-asalnā lahu ʿayna al-qiṭr wa-mina al-jinni man yaʿmalu bayna yadayhi bi-idhni rabbihi* — "And to Solomon the wind: its morning a month and its evening a month; and We caused a spring of molten copper to flow for him; and from the jinn, those who worked before him by his Lord's permission."
- v.13: *yaʿmalūna lahu mā yashāʾu min maḥārība wa-tamāthīla wa-jifānin ka-l-jawābi wa-qudūrin rāsiyāt iʿmalū āla dāwūda shukran wa-qalīlun min ʿibādiya al-shakūr* — "They worked for him whatever he wished — sanctuaries, statues, basins like reservoirs, and immovable cooking-pots. Work, family of David, in gratitude — and few of My servants are grateful."

Solomon's faḍl: wind-control (a 1-month-each-way travel range), molten-copper spring, jinn-workers, and palatial-craft (4 craft-categories: *maḥārīb*, *tamāthīl*, *jifān ka-l-jawāb*, *qudūr rāsiyāt*). The closing *qalīlun min ʿibādī al-shakūr* (few of My servants are grateful) is the **structural inversion-pivot**: Solomon's family IS grateful (*shukran*), but most are not — and the Sabaeans (Block C) are about to demonstrate the not-grateful failure mode.

#### v.14 — Solomon's death by termite
*fa-lammā qaḍaynā ʿalayhi al-mawta mā dallahum ʿalā mawtihi illā dābbatu al-arḍi taʾkulu minsaʾatahu fa-lammā kharra tabayyanat al-jinnu an law kānū yaʿlamūna al-ghayba mā labithū fī al-ʿadhābi al-muhīn*

The famous termite-death scene: Solomon's death is concealed from the jinn-workers (who continued laboring under coercion) until a termite (*dābbatu al-arḍ*) ate his staff and his body fell. The verse's punchline: *law kānū yaʿlamūna al-ghayba mā labithū fī al-ʿadhābi al-muhīn* — "had they known the unseen, they would not have remained in the humiliating punishment" — refutes any jinn-claims to knowledge-of-the-unseen. This connects Block B to Block A's omniscience theme: only God knows the unseen, even Solomon's death was hidden from the jinn.

### Block C (15-19): The Sabaʾ-flood narrative

#### v.15 — the two-garden kingdom + the call to gratitude
*laqad kāna li-sabaʾin fī maskanihim āyatun jannatāni ʿan yamīnin wa-shimāl kulū min rizqi rabbikum wa-shkurū lahu baldatun ṭayyibatun wa-rabbun ghafūr*

The opening of the Sabaʾ-block establishes the kingdom's blessing: TWO gardens flanking the dwelling on right and left, with the divine command *kulū... wa-shkurū lahu* — "eat... and be grateful." The phrase *baldatun ṭayyibatun wa-rabbun ghafūr* — "a good land, and a Forgiving Lord" — is the structural-mirror of Q 27:91-92 (*innamā umirtu an aʿbuda rabba hādhihi al-baldatī alladhī ḥarramahā* — Mecca's framing). The Sabaeans had a "good land" with a "Forgiving Lord" — the gift was unambiguous.

The proper noun *sabaʾ* (LEM:saba<) appears here as the corpus's 2nd attestation (the 1st being Q 27:22). See Q034-F-02 for the eponymous-pair test.

#### v.16 — the great-flood
*fa-aʿraḍū fa-arsalnā ʿalayhim sayla al-ʿarim wa-baddalnāhum bi-jannatayhim jannatayni dhawātay ukulin khamṭin wa-athlin wa-shayʾin min sidrin qalīl*

The Sabaeans turned away (*fa-aʿraḍū*), so God sent the **flood-of-the-dam** (*sayl al-ʿarim*) and exchanged their two gardens for two gardens of (a) bitter inedible fruit (*ukul khamṭ*), (b) tamarisk (*athl*), (c) a few lote-trees (*shayʾ min sidr qalīl*). The classical commentators identify this with the historical breach of the Maʾrib dam (early 6th century CE per pre-modern Yemeni chronicles cited in al-Hamdānī *Iklīl* and reflected in al-Ṭabarī's *Tārīkh*; see 03-tafsir-survey for details).

The 3-fruit list (khamṭ, athl, sidr) is botanically specific: khamṭ is bitter-arāk (the Salvadora persica tree-fruit, distasteful), athl is the tamarisk (Tamarix aphylla, a brackish-saline drought-tolerant tree), and sidr is the lote-tree (Ziziphus spina-christi, with thorny growth and small dry fruit). All three are characteristic of arid post-irrigation-collapse landscapes — the lexical-botanical specificity matches the agro-environmental claim of irrigation-system collapse.

#### v.17 — the recompense doctrine
*dhālika jazaynāhum bi-mā kafarū wa-hal nujāzī illā al-kafūr*

"That We recompensed them for what they denied. Do We recompense any except the ungrateful denier?" The *al-kafūr* here is the intensified-form of *kāfir* — denoting the maximally-ingrateful one. Connects to v.13's *qalīlun min ʿibādī al-shakūr*: the surah operates a *shakūr/kafūr* binary (the rare grateful vs the punishable ingrate), with the Sabaeans demonstrating the *kafūr* pole.

#### v.18 — the connected-cities + the safe-travel
*wa-jaʿalnā baynahum wa-bayna al-qurā allatī bāraknā fīhā quran ẓāhiratan wa-qaddarnā fīhā al-sayra sīrū fīhā layāliya wa-ayyāman āminīn*

Pre-judgment, the Sabaeans had been given a chain of "connected, visible villages" between their kingdom and the *qurā mubāraka* (the blessed cities — classically identified with Syria/Sham per al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr). Travel was paced and safe — *layāliya wa-ayyāman āminīn* (nights and days in security). The historical motif: the South-Arabian incense-route from Yemen to Sham passing through a chain of caravan-stations.

#### v.19 — the request for distance + the mazzaqnāhum punchline
*fa-qālū rabbanā bāʿid bayna asfārinā wa-ẓalamū anfusahum fa-jaʿalnāhum aḥādītha wa-mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq inna fī dhālika la-āyātin li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr*

The Sabaeans asked: *rabbanā bāʿid bayna asfārinā* — "Our Lord, lengthen the distance between our journeys" (i.e., they asked for greater distance / desired separation from their neighbors). They wronged themselves; God made them *aḥādīth* (legends, stories) — scattered into oral-tradition memory — and *mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq* — "We tore them into a thousand tearings."

The verb *mazzaqa* echoes v.7 *muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq* (the Meccan deniers' mocking phrase about resurrection). The Sabaean ending IS what the Meccans were mockingly invoking — a historical proof-of-concept of the very dispersion-and-reassembly the deniers were rejecting.

The final phrase *li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr* — "for every patient grateful one" — closes the kingdom-narrative with the *ṣabr/shukr* twin-virtue, the ethical core of the surah. The block C ending makes the lesson explicit: this is a "sign" (*āyātun*) only for those who are simultaneously patient and grateful — the qualified audience of historical-revelation.

### Block D (20-54): prophet-rejection polemic + eschatology + tawḥīd

This is the surah's longest block (35 verses, 65% of total length). It deploys the Block-C lesson against the contemporary Meccan polytheists. Five sub-units:

#### Sub-unit D1 (vv. 20-21): Iblīs's vindication
- v.20: *wa-laqad ṣaddaqa ʿalayhim iblīsu ẓannahu fa-ttabaʿūhu illā farīqan min al-muʾminīn* — "Iblīs verified his suspicion about them; they followed him except a portion of the believers." The Sabaeans' fall is here re-described as the post-Adamic Satan-following pattern.
- v.21: *wa-mā kāna lahu ʿalayhim min sulṭānin illā li-naʿlama man yuʾminu bi-l-ākhirati mimman huwa minhā fī shakk* — Iblīs has no real authority over humanity; the test is for God to KNOW (i.e., make manifest) the believers in the Hereafter from the doubters.

#### Sub-unit D2 (vv. 22-30): the *qul* polemic-cluster (8 *qul* in 9 verses)
- v.22: *qul idʿū alladhīna zaʿamtum* — "Call those you allege..." The 4-fold negation of polytheist-deity-power: cannot own *mithqāl dharra* / no *shirk* (partnership) / no *ẓahīr* (helper) / [v.23] no intercession-without-permission. This is the surah's sharpest tawḥīd-polemic.
- v.23: shafāʿa cannot help; the trembling-of-hearts at the announcement.
- v.24: *qul man yarzuqukum... qul allāh* — "Say: who provides for you... Say: Allah." The minimal-creedal exchange.
- v.25-27: the "we are not asked about your sins, you are not asked about ours" (v.25) / "God will gather and judge between us" (v.26) / "show me your alleged partners" (v.27).
- v.28: ⭐ ***wa-mā arsalnāka illā kāffatan li-l-nāsi bashīran wa-nadhīran wa-lākinna akthara al-nāsi lā yaʿlamūn*** — "And We have not sent you except to all of humanity as a bringer-of-good-tidings and a warner; but most people do not know." The corpus's strongest universalist statement of prophetic mission. (See 03-tafsir-survey §1 for al-Ṭabarī on the comparison with Q 21:107 *wa-mā arsalnāka illā raḥmatan li-l-ʿālamīn*.)
- v.29-30: the "when is this promise?" challenge / the response — appointed-day, no-delay, no-hastening.

#### Sub-unit D3 (vv. 31-33): the strong-vs-weak debate on Judgment Day
- v.31: the strong-and-weak deniers will face each other; the weak say "but for you we would have been believers."
- v.32: the strong reply "did we turn you from guidance after it had come? you were criminals."
- v.33: the weak rebut "no, your night-and-day-scheming, when you commanded us to deny Allah." Both groups are silenced and yokes are placed on the deniers' necks.

#### Sub-unit D4 (vv. 34-39): the wealth-and-progeny doctrine
- v.34: every prophet's *mutrafū qaryatihim* (luxury-elites of his city) say *innā bi-mā ursiltum bihi kāfirūn* — "We deny what you were sent with." The pattern is universal across prophetic history.
- v.35-37: the wealthy say "we are richer in wealth and children, we will not be punished" (v.35). Reply: God expands provision for whomever He wills (v.36); your wealth and children do NOT bring you near except believers + good-deeds, who get *jazāʾ al-ḍiʿf* (recompense doubled) and dwell *fī al-ghurufāt* (in the upper-chambers) safe (v.37). v.38: those striving against signs are *fī al-ʿadhābi muḥḍarūn*.
- v.39: *wa-mā anfaqtum min shayʾin fa-huwa yukhlifuhu wa-huwa khayru al-rāziqīn* — "Whatever you spend, He replaces; He is the best of providers." Connects to the Sabaean ingratitude theme: provision is not the audience's possession but a divine-gift-to-be-shared.

#### Sub-unit D5 (vv. 40-54): the eschatological-final-judgment scenes
- vv. 40-42: the angels' disavowal — "they were worshipping the jinn." The angels deny having been the polytheists' targets; the polytheists were redirected to the jinn (per the classical demonological reading of pre-Islamic-Arab cult).
- v.43: when our signs are recited, they say "this is just a man wanting to turn you from what your fathers worshipped" / "this is forged hearsay" / "this is clear magic."
- v.44: *wa-mā ātaynāhum min kutubin yadrusūnahā wa-mā arsalnā ilayhim qablaka min nadhīrin* — they had no books, no prior warner. (The Meccan Arabs are the unprecedented audience.)
- v.45: prior nations rejected, achieving not even *miʿshār* (one-tenth) of what the Meccans were given; their lying was met with My *nukur* (rejection).
- v.46: ⭐ *qul innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥidatin an taqūmū li-llāhi mathnā wa-furādā thumma tatafakkarū* — "Say: I exhort you with one thing only — that you stand for Allah in pairs and individually, and then reflect." The classical homiletic-anchor verse, cited extensively in tafsir for its meta-doctrinal "one thing only" (*bi-wāḥida*).
- vv. 47-50: the "I have no fee from you" series of *qul* verses: my-reward-is-with-Allah / God-throws-the-truth / truth-came-falsehood-can't-create / if-I-am-misguided-I-am-misguided-against-myself.
- vv. 51-54: the eschatological-arrest scene: "if you could see when they panic" (v.51) / "they say 'we believe' but how? from a far place" (v.52) / they had denied before; now they conjecture about the unseen from a far place (v.53) / "barriers were placed between them and what they desire as had been done to their predecessors; they were in suspicious doubt" (v.54).

The closing v.54 phrase *fī shakkin murīb* (in suspicious doubt) is the surah's final word, structurally inverting the opening v.1 *al-ḥakīm al-khabīr* — the surah opens with God's certain knowledge and closes with humanity's suspicious doubt.

## 4. Recurring rhetorical motifs

### 4.1 The shakūr / kafūr binary
- v.13: *wa-qalīlun min ʿibādī al-shakūr* (David-Solomon family commanded to gratitude; few are grateful)
- v.17: *wa-hal nujāzī illā al-kafūr* (Sabaean kingdom punished as kafūr)
- v.19: *li-kulli ṣabbārin shakūr* (the historical-sign requires patience+gratitude)
- v.46: *qul innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥida* (the meta-doctrinal one-thing exhortation)

The shakūr/kafūr opposition is the surah's ethical core. David-Solomon = shakūr; Sabaeans = kafūr; the sign IS for *kulli ṣabbārin shakūr*.

### 4.2 The mithqāl dharra doctrine
- v.3: *lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍ* (omniscience-axis)
- v.22: *lā yamlikūna mithqāla dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍ* (polemic-axis)

The same *mithqāl dharra* phrase appears twice in the surah, in mirror frames: God's *knowledge* extends to atomic scale (v.3), the polytheist-deities' *power* does NOT extend to atomic scale (v.22). This is one of the corpus's tightest internal-parallelism structures.

### 4.3 The mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq inclusio
- v.7: *muzziqtum kulla mumazzaq* (Meccan deniers' mocking phrase about resurrection)
- v.19: *mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq* (Sabaean kingdom's actual fate)

The mocking-phrase becomes the kingdom-fate, demonstrating that what the Meccan deniers were rejecting in resurrection-context HAD ALREADY HAPPENED in historical-context.

### 4.4 The samāwāt wa-l-arḍ omniscience axis
- v.1: *lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍ* (ownership)
- v.2: *yaʿlamu mā yaliju fī al-arḍ wa-mā yakhruju minhā wa-mā yanzilu min al-samāʾi wa-mā yaʿruju fīhā* (omniscience-direction grid)
- v.3: *lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍ* (omniscience-mass-scale)
- v.9: *mā bayna aydīhim wa-mā khalfahum min al-samāʾi wa-l-arḍ* (cosmic-power)
- v.22: *lā yamlikūna mithqāla dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍ* (polemic-power)
- v.24: *qul man yarzuqukum min al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ qul allāh* (rizq-source)

Six occurrences of the heaven-and-earth phrase distributed strategically across Block A (5×) and Sub-unit D2 (2×), establishing the cosmological frame for the doctrinal argument.

### 4.5 al-ākhira theme
- v.1: *wa-lahu al-ḥamdu fī al-ākhira* (dual-ḥamd opening)
- v.8: *bal alladhīna lā yuʾminūna bi-l-ākhirati fī al-ʿadhābi wa-l-ḍalāli al-baʿīd*
- v.21: *li-naʿlama man yuʾminu bi-l-ākhirati mimman huwa minhā fī shakk*

3 explicit *al-ākhira* references in Block A + start of Block D, establishing the eschatological-axis early.

## 5. Genre and rhetorical class

Q 34 sits in the Late-Meccan *creedal-historical-eschatological compendium* genre — the Block-A doctrinal preamble + Block-B/C historical-precedents + Block-D contemporary-application + final-judgment imagery is a recurrent structural template across:
- Q 6 al-Anʿām (long Late-Meccan al-ḥamd-opener)
- Q 17 al-Isrāʾ
- Q 25 al-Furqān
- Q 32 al-Sajda
- Q 41 Fuṣṣilat (Q 34's rank-1 FR neighbor)
- Q 46 al-Aḥqāf
- Q 67 al-Mulk

The shared template is: cosmic-creedal preamble → historical-precedent narrative(s) → polemical-application → eschatological-final-imagery. Q 34's Block-C Sabaʾ-narrative is the SHORTEST historical-precedent block in this template-cluster (5 verses), but the most kingdom-specific (other surahs' precedent-narratives focus on prophet-figures; Q 34's focuses on a kingdom-as-collective-actor).

## 6. Structural fingerprint summary

Q 34's structural fingerprints:
1. **Dual-ḥamd v.1**: corpus-unique double-occurrence of *al-ḥamd* in a single verse.
2. **Sabaʾ proper-noun**: 1 of 2 corpus attestations (other Q 27:22).
