# Q 34 Sabaʾ — Classical Tafsīr Survey

Survey of 6 classical mufassirūn on the major exegetical loci of Q 34: (a) the dual-ḥamd opening Q 34:1; (b) the David-Solomon vignettes Q 34:10-14; (c) the Sabaʾ-flood narrative Q 34:15-19; (d) the universalist v.28 (*kāffatan li-l-nās*); (e) the *mithqāl dharra* doctrine (vv. 3, 22); (f) the one-thing-only homiletic v.46.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*)

### Q 34:1 — al-ḥamdu li-llāh + dual-ḥamd
al-Ṭabarī comments on the structure of v.1: "The praise of God is twofold — once initial (*al-ḥamdu li-llāhi alladhī lahu...*) and once eschatological (*wa-lahu al-ḥamdu fī al-ākhira*); these are not redundant but are *taʾkīd* (emphatic-doubling) signaling that praise belongs to God in BOTH realms — the immediate cosmological-ownership realm and the post-mortem realm. The doubling is the verse's structural fingerprint."

al-Ṭabarī's interpretation links the dual-ḥamd to the surah's theological program: tawḥīd at the cosmological level (v.1 first ḥamd) and tawḥīd at the eschatological level (v.1 second ḥamd). The eschatological note prefigures Block D (vv. 20-54).

### Q 34:14 — Solomon's death by termite
al-Ṭabarī catalogs the mainstream identification of the *dābbat al-arḍ* with the *araḍah* (termite, Coleoptera *Reticulitermes*). He cites multiple Companion-Successor reports:
- Ibn ʿAbbās: the termite ate Solomon's staff for an extended period, allowing the jinn to continue their forced labor.
- Mujāhid: the duration was "a year".
- al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī: the duration was the time it took to complete Solomon's last commissioned project.

The exegetical purpose: the verse refutes pre-Islamic Arab and Jewish jinn-divination claims by showing that even Solomon's intimate jinn-workers did not detect his own death. The doctrinal payload is *al-ʿilm al-ghayb li-llāh waḥdahu* — knowledge-of-the-unseen belongs to God alone.

### Q 34:15-19 — the Sabaʾ-flood narrative
al-Ṭabarī's treatment is among his longest historical-narrative treatments in the late-Meccan section. He synthesizes Yemeni-tribal genealogies (citing al-Suddī, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid) with the Quranic text:
- **Sabaʾ** = Sabaʾ b. Yashjub b. Yaʿrub b. Qaḥṭān, eponymous patriarch of South-Arabian tribes.
- **The two gardens** = gardens flanking the Maʾrib dam (the *sadd Maʾrib*), irrigation-engineered through the dam's sluice-gates.
- **al-ʿarim** = the dam itself OR its powerful flood. al-Ṭabarī cites BOTH lexical readings: (i) ʿarim as the dam-name (so *sayl al-ʿarim* = "the flood from [the breach of] the ʿarim-dam"); (ii) ʿarim as adjective meaning "destructive, severe" (so *sayl al-ʿarim* = "the destructive flood"). al-Ṭabarī gives marginal preference to (i) based on Yemeni-tribal recall.
- **The fruits-of-punishment**: *khamṭ* (bitter fruit), *athl* (tamarisk), *sidr* (lote-tree). al-Ṭabarī explains each:
  - *khamṭ* — the "arāk" tree's fruit, bitter and inedible (cf. al-Khalīl in *Kitāb al-ʿAyn*).
  - *athl* — the tamarisk, a brackish/saline-tolerant tree of arid post-irrigation landscapes.
  - *sidr* — the *Ziziphus spina-christi*, characterized by thorny growth and sparse fruit.
- **The dispersion** (*mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq*): al-Ṭabarī cites Yemeni-tribal traditions of the post-flood diaspora — Ghassānids to Syria, Lakhmids to Iraq, ʿAk and Azd to Tihāmah, etc. The pre-Islamic Arabian tribal map is interpreted as the post-flood Sabaean dispersion.

al-Ṭabarī is notable for resisting any allegorical reading: the Sabaʾ-flood is for him a HISTORICAL event with concrete geographic referents, not a parable. This sets the tone for the Sunni mainstream tradition.

### Q 34:28 — *kāffatan li-l-nās* (universal prophecy)
al-Ṭabarī's discussion is theologically substantial:
"*Kāffah* here is in the *ḥāl* (circumstantial) accusative; the meaning is 'a comprehensive [mission] to humanity' — i.e., the Prophet was sent INCLUSIVELY of all human beings, not exclusively to one tribe or region. This contrasts with the prior prophets: Hūd to ʿĀd, Ṣāliḥ to Thamūd, Mūsā to Banī Isrāʾīl, etc."

al-Ṭabarī cites the Bukhārī chain (Bukhārī 431 / Muslim 1069) on the 5-things granted to the Prophet, item 4 of which is *kāna al-nabiyyu yubʿathu ilā qawmihi khāṣṣatan wa-buʿithtu ilā al-nāsi kāffatan*. The verse is the qurʾānic anchor; the hadith is its homiletic unfolding.

He also notes a subtle exegetical point: the syntactic ambiguity of *kāffah* (could modify *al-nās* OR be a participle from *kaffa* "to hold/restrain", meaning "as a withholding [from disbelief]"). al-Ṭabarī gives mainstream weight to the comprehensive-universal reading.

## 2. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr*)

### Q 34:1 — al-Rāzī on the dual-ḥamd
al-Rāzī provides his characteristic exhaustive multi-vajh enumeration:

**Why does v.1 contain *al-ḥamd* twice?** al-Rāzī catalogs four scenarios:
- **Vajh 1** — Tawḥīd of cosmology + tawḥīd of soteriology: the first *al-ḥamd* declares God's praise as Owner-of-heavens-and-earth (creation-praise); the second *al-ḥamd fī al-ākhira* declares God's praise as Sovereign-of-the-Hereafter (judgment-praise). The two together constitute the DUAL ALPHA-OMEGA of cognition: God is praised at both endpoints of being.
- **Vajh 2** — Two genres of praise: the first is *al-ḥamd al-ḥālī* (the immediate, cosmological praise rendered by all creatures); the second is *al-ḥamd al-aṣīlī* (the eternal praise of paradise-dwellers in the Hereafter; cf. Q 7:43, Q 35:34).
- **Vajh 3** — Initial-marker + closing-marker: the first establishes the surah's opening; the second prefigures the surah's eschatological closing. The dual-ḥamd is a TAṢDĪR ('opening-shaped' rhetorical figure) operating on the macro-surah level.
- **Vajh 4** — Reverence + comprehensiveness: the first is *al-ḥamd al-istighrāqī* (comprehensive praise covering all attributes); the second is *al-ḥamd al-takhṣīṣī* (specific praise covering the post-mortem-realm-attribute). The doubling assures both COMPREHENSIVENESS and SPECIFICITY.

al-Rāzī notes the Quranic frequency-pattern: the phrase *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* opens 5 surahs (Q 1, 6, 18, 34, 35), but only Q 34:1 contains the doubled construction. This rhetorical-figure uniqueness (corpus-empirical-confirmed) is, for al-Rāzī, evidence of *iʿjāz al-bayānī* — the rhetorical-design singularity.

### Q 34:3, 22 — the *mithqāl dharra* parallelism
al-Rāzī notes the surah's tightest internal-parallelism structure: the same phrase *mithqāla dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍ* appears in v.3 (omniscience-axis) and v.22 (polemic-axis):
- v.3: *lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu* — nothing escapes Him at the atom's-weight scale (knowledge-coverage).
- v.22: *lā yamlikūna* — they (polytheist deities) do not OWN at the atom's-weight scale (power-deficit).

al-Rāzī's reading: this is a deliberate KNOWLEDGE/POWER mirror. God's knowledge extends to *mithqāl dharra*; the deities' power does not. The single verse-pair establishes the surah's complete tawḥīd-program — God has both omniscience and omnipotence; the deities have neither, even at the smallest scale.

al-Rāzī also catalogs the *dharra* lexical debate:
- **al-Khalīl b. Aḥmad**: *dharra* is the smallest red-ant (perceptual-minimum visible mass).
- **Ibn ʿAbbās**: a particle of dust visible in a sunbeam through a window-pane (the smallest-perceptible particulate).
- **al-Suyūṭī later**: extended the lexical range to include any maximally-small mass.

### Q 34:10-14 — David-Solomon vignettes
al-Rāzī's reading emphasizes the *faḍl* (favor) doctrine: David's faḍl is the gift of mountains-and-birds-praising + iron-softening + chain-mail-craft; Solomon's faḍl is wind-control + molten-copper + jinn-workers + palace-craft. The 4 elements of David's faḍl mirror the 4 elements of Solomon's.

al-Rāzī notes that the verse-sequence is **chiastic** in structure:
```
  v.10: David — mountain praise + birds + iron-softening
    v.11: David — work-command (sābighāt + sard)
       v.12: Solomon — wind + molten-copper + jinn
    v.13: Solomon — work-command (maḥārīb + tamāthīl + jifān + qudūr) + family-of-David exhortation
  v.14: Solomon — death-by-termite + jinn-knowledge-deficit
```

The work-commands at the chiastic core (vv. 11, 13) ground the David-Solomon faḍl in *active-craftsmanship-dependence-on-divine-command*. The closing v.14 (Solomon's death) is the chiastic-bookend to v.10 (David's gifts).

### Q 34:15-19 — Sabaʾ-flood
al-Rāzī treats the narrative as a **didactic miniature** rather than a free-standing historical account. His exegetical points:
- The two-garden description (*jannatāni ʿan yamīnin wa-shimāl*) is structurally parallel to Q 18:32-33 (the two-vineyard parable in al-Kahf). al-Rāzī suggests Q 34:15 is a HISTORICAL instance of the same warning-against-prosperity pattern.
- The *kulū min rizqi rabbikum wa-shkurū lahu* (eat from your Lord's provision and be grateful) is the explicit divine-command. Their *aʿraḍū* (turning-away) is therefore disobedience-of-explicit-command, not mere passive ingratitude.
- The *bāʿid bayna asfārinā* request (v.19) is exegetically obscure. al-Rāzī catalogs three readings:
  - (i) They asked to make their journeys LONGER (so they would not have to travel often) — a preference for separation over connectedness.
  - (ii) They asked God to MOVE OUT the gardens (so different stages of the journey would have different fruits, defeating the original blessing-of-uniform-abundance).
  - (iii) Some early commentators read *bāʿid* as a perfect tense ("our Lord HAS made distant our journeys"), reading the verse as a complaint after the breach. al-Rāzī gives mainstream weight to (i).
- The *mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq* (v.19) recalls v.7 (the deniers' mocking phrase). al-Rāzī explicitly notes this internal echo: "The same root *mzq* appears at v.7 in the deniers' mouths (about resurrection) and at v.19 in God's voice (about the Sabaeans). The deniers' mockery becomes the historical-precedent's reality."

### Q 34:46 — the one-thing-only homiletic
al-Rāzī's analysis of *qul innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥidatin an taqūmū li-llāhi mathnā wa-furādā thumma tatafakkarū* is one of his most-cited tafsir passages on practical ethics:
- *bi-wāḥidah* — "with one [exhortation]". The use of feminine *wāḥidah* (with no antecedent) is exegetically debated. al-Rāzī's preferred reading: an unstated *khaṣla* or *naṣīḥa* — "one quality / one piece of advice". The whole-of-the-Quran's doctrinal teaching is reducible to ONE practical step: *qiyām li-llāh + tafakkur*.
- *mathnā wa-furādā* — "in pairs and individually". The dual modes of reflection: collective (paired) and individual (alone). al-Rāzī comments that authentic *tafakkur* requires both: the collective verifies through dialogue, the individual verifies through silence.
- *thumma tatafakkarū* — "then reflect". The sequence is critical: STAND first (a posture of moral resolve), THEN reflect. Reflection without prior moral commitment is empty cognition.

al-Rāzī uses this verse to ground Sufi *maqāma*-doctrine: the verse encodes the foundational Sufi practice of *qiyām + dhikr + fikr*. The rhetorical-balāgha of the verse: a single conditional construction (*innamā ... bi-wāḥida*) carries the whole-of-the-Quran's homiletic program.

## 3. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*)

### Q 34:14 — termite legal-precedent
al-Qurṭubī's interest is fiqh-applied: the verse is one of the texts that established (in classical Sunni jurisprudence) that *jinn-and-demonic claims to knowledge-of-the-unseen are categorically false*. The legal precedent: any divinatory practice claiming jinn-mediated knowledge of the unseen is *muḥarram* (forbidden) on the basis of Q 34:14.

al-Qurṭubī cites a chain on the timing of Solomon's body's discovery: Companions of Solomon (his wives and household) noticed the staff buckling and his body falling, after which "the jinn knew" — meaning, the jinn learned of the death AT THE SAME TIME as the rest of Solomon's household, not before. This refutes any pre-Solomonic-death-jinn-omen tradition.

### Q 34:15-19 — Sabaʾ tribal-genealogy
al-Qurṭubī devotes substantial space to the post-Sabaʾ-flood diaspora. He cites the al-Suddī tribal-genealogy:
- **Ghassān** = the Banī al-Aws (those who went to Syria after the dam's breach)
- **Khazāʿah** = those who went to Mecca and surrounding regions
- **Lakhm and Judhām** = those who went to Iraq
- **al-Azd al-ʿUmān** = those who went to Oman
- **al-Anṣār (al-Aws and al-Khazraj)** = the Median branch — the people of Yathrib/Medina who became the Anṣār (helpers) of the Prophet ﷺ

The latter point is religiously significant: Medina's Anṣār are tracing their ancestral line BACK to the Sabaʾ-flood. Their hosting of the Prophet is, in this exegetical-genealogical reading, a "redemption" of the ancestral pattern.

### Q 34:46 — qiyām + tafakkur
al-Qurṭubī cites this verse in his *kitāb al-ʿibāda* applications. The legal-applied reading: the verse establishes that **moral reflection requires two postures** — collective discussion AND individual silence — neither alone is sufficient.

He also notes the verse's homiletic-role in Sufi practice: the chain of *qiyām → tafakkur → tabayyun* (standing → reflecting → clarification) is the structural template for *muḥāsabat al-nafs* (self-accounting), one of the foundational practices of classical Sunni-Sufi spiritual discipline.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*)

Ibn Kathīr's tafsir of Q 34 is notable for its hadith-density. He opens his Q 34 commentary with extensive citations from:
- The 5/6-things-given hadith (Bukhārī 431 / Muslim 1069 / al-Tirmidhī 1590 / al-Nasāʾī 433 / Aḥmad 907 / al-Dārimī 709) on Q 34:28 *kāffatan li-l-nās*.
- The Sabaʾ-tribal-question hadith (al-Tirmidhī 3306, Abū Dāwūd 3029) on Q 34:15.
- Yemeni-tribal-genealogy traditions on Q 34:19.

### Q 34:14 — the termite-jinn lesson
Ibn Kathīr cites the doctrinal payload directly: "*ḥādhā al-āyatu dalīlun ʿalā anna al-jinna lā yaʿlamūna al-ghaybu illā mā ʿallamahum allāhu* — this verse is proof that the jinn do not know the unseen except what God teaches them." He extends the doctrine: every classical Islamic refutation of *kahānah* (soothsaying) and *taḥnīṭ* (divinatory rituals) cites Q 34:14 as the foundational textual anchor.

### Q 34:15-19 — Sabaʾ historical detail
Ibn Kathīr is one of the most extensive on the historical-detail layer. He cites Yemeni-Islamic chroniclers (al-Hamdānī, *Iklīl* via secondary chains) on the Maʾrib dam: it was constructed in the 8th century BCE under the Sabaean monarchs; its breach occurred in the early 6th century CE per pre-Islamic Yemeni chronology; the breach scattered the Sabaean tribes northward.

Ibn Kathīr notes that the al-ʿarim is described in pre-Islamic poetry (cf. Imruʾ al-Qays) as the dam's name, supporting al-Ṭabarī's reading (i).

### Q 34:28 — the 5-things hadith
Ibn Kathīr provides one of the fullest hadith-concentrations on this verse. He lists seven distinct chains for the *baʿathtu ilā al-nās kāffah* hadith and harmonizes the variants (5-things vs 6-things vs 7-things) by noting that different transmitters preserved different sub-sets of the Prophet's 5/6/7-element discourse.

## 5. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-āy wa-l-Suwar*)

al-Biqāʿī's specialty is **inter-surah munāsabah** (rhetorical-thematic linkage). His Q 34 commentary establishes the position of the surah in the canonical sequence:

### Q 33 → Q 34 munāsabah
al-Biqāʿī notes that despite the rough mushaf-seam (per H-NEW-720 rank 111/113, an empirical confirmation of his observation), there IS a *munāsabah* link: Q 33 al-Aḥzāb closes with the *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* doxological frame at v.71-72 (*man yuṭiʿi allāha wa-rasūlahu fa-qad fāza fawzan ʿaẓīma*); Q 34 opens with *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*. The two surahs are linked via doxological-bookending: Q 33 closes the Medinan legal-section with a doxological-tail, Q 34 opens the Late-Meccan creedal-section with a doxological-head.

(Note: this is a relatively WEAK munāsabah link by al-Biqāʿī's standards; the empirical-roughness of the Q 33→Q 34 seam at FR rank 111/113 is consistent with al-Biqāʿī's relatively brief treatment. See 05-classical-claims-audit §1.)

### Q 34 → Q 35 munāsabah
al-Biqāʿī's Q 34 → Q 35 link is much stronger: both surahs open with *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*, both establish creator-of-heavens-and-earth doctrine, both polemicize against polytheist-deity-claims. al-Biqāʿī treats the pair as a thematic-unit. (Empirical check: Q 34→Q 35 fraction_residual = 0.0090, rank 65/113 — moderate but not seamless. The shared opener form does not guarantee FR seamlessness; see 05-classical-claims-audit §3.)

### Internal Q 34 structure
al-Biqāʿī's reading of Q 34's macro-blocks:
- vv. 1-9 establish the **creedal program**: ownership-of-heavens-and-earth + omniscience + recompense.
- vv. 10-14 establish the **blessed-prophet precedent**: David and Solomon as the *grateful* prophets.
- vv. 15-19 establish the **punished-kingdom precedent**: Sabaeans as the *ungrateful* people.
- vv. 20-54 establish the **contemporary application**: Meccan polytheists in their late-Meccan polemic context, with eschatological-imagery as the closing.

al-Biqāʿī's COHESION CLAIM (the load-bearing classical-audit claim in §05): the surah is HIGHLY COHESIVE around the *shakūr/kafūr* binary (vv. 13, 17, 19, 46) which structures the entire prophetic-precedent → kingdom-precedent → polemic-application sequence. The Sabaʾ-block (vv. 15-19) is not a digression but the HINGE that connects the David-Solomon faḍl to the Meccan-polemic in Block D.

(Empirical check: Q 34's local cohesion at H-NEW-750 rank 33/114 is upper-tertile — empirically consistent with al-Biqāʿī's cohesion claim. See 05-classical-claims-audit §2.)

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-Manthūr*)

### Q 34 in al-Itqān structural classifications
al-Suyūṭī classifies Q 34 in his *Itqān* navāʿ:
- **Nawʿ 1** (chronology): Q 34 is Late Meccan, revelation-order rank 58 (per the al-Suyūṭī chronology, the Quranic Standard Egyptian Edition "Tanzil" anchor).
- **Nawʿ 2** (Meccan/Medinan): clearly Meccan; al-Suyūṭī cites Ibn ʿAbbās's chronology and notes no significant Medinan-revelation claim for any Q 34 verse.
- **Nawʿ 3** (subject-types): Q 34 is in the *mukhtalit* class — surahs combining *creedal* (vv. 1-9) + *qaṣaṣ* (vv. 10-19) + *jadalī* (vv. 20-54). This three-element structure is shared with Q 6 al-Anʿām and Q 17 al-Isrāʾ.
- **Nawʿ 67** (al-aqsām): Q 34 is NOT in the oath-opener class (no oath-particle in v.1). Q 34's opener is a doxological-particle (*al-ḥamd*).

al-Suyūṭī notes that Q 34 belongs to the **al-ḥamdu li-llāh opening class** (5 surahs: Q 1, 6, 18, 34, 35), discussing this as a *fasl* (sub-class) within the broader *iftitāḥāt* (opening-types) classification.

### Q 34:28 in al-Suyūṭī
al-Suyūṭī treats the verse in *al-Durr al-Manthūr* with extensive hadith-citation. He classifies it under the *nubuwwah* + *risālah* (prophecy and messengerhood) doctrinal heading. The verse is one of the corpus's strongest universalist statements; al-Suyūṭī notes it pairs with Q 21:107 (*wa-mā arsalnāka illā raḥmatan li-l-ʿālamīn*) and Q 25:1 (*tabāraka alladhī nazzala al-furqān ʿalā ʿabdihi li-yakūna li-l-ʿālamīna nadhīrā*).

### Q 34 lexical singularities (al-Itqān nawʿ 50 — *al-gharīb*)
al-Suyūṭī identifies several lexical rarities in Q 34:
- *al-ʿarim* (v.16) — disputed lexical reading; al-Suyūṭī catalogs both major readings (dam-name vs adjective).
- *khamṭ* (v.16) — rare in classical Arabic; al-Suyūṭī cites al-Khalīl on the bitter-arāk identification.
- *minsaʾah* (v.14) — the term for Solomon's staff. Cited in Tirmidhī 2047 (a *gharīb* hadith) as a generic term for *staff*; classical lexicographers preserve it under root *n-s-ʾ* with the nuance "that which postpones / extends" — an ironic etymological note since the staff CONCEALED Solomon's death and thus "extended" the jinn's labor period.

The mufassirūn agree on:
1. The Sabaʾ-flood is a HISTORICAL event, not a parable.
2. The David-Solomon vignettes establish the *shukr*-precedent that the Sabaeans fail.
3. The *mithqāl dharra* doctrine pairs (v.3 and v.22) are deliberate knowledge/power mirrors.
4. The dual-ḥamd v.1 is the corpus's most rhetorically-distinctive opener (al-Rāzī's strongest claim).
5. Q 34:28 is the corpus's strongest universal-prophecy statement, paired with Q 21:107.

The mufassirūn diverge on:
- The *bāʿid bayna asfārinā* (v.19) reading (3 distinct interpretations).
- The *al-ʿarim* (v.16) lexical analysis (dam-name vs adjective).
- The relative weighting of the tribal-genealogy material vs the doctrinal-eschatological frame.
