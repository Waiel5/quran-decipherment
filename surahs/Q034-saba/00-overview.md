## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **Sabaʾ** after v.15 *laqad kāna li-sabaʾin fī maskanihim āyatun* ("Indeed there was for [the people of] Sabaʾ in their dwelling-place a sign"). The Arabic *sabaʾ* is a proper noun designating the South-Arabian kingdom of Sabaʾ (biblical Sheba), located in present-day Yemen, whose capital was Maʾrib and whose centerpiece was the Maʾrib dam (the *sayl al-ʿarim* of v.16). Classical lexicographers (al-Jawharī *al-Ṣiḥāḥ*, Ibn Manẓūr *Lisān al-ʿArab* under س-ب-أ) note that Sabaʾ was simultaneously (a) the eponymous patriarch of the South-Arabian tribes (Sabaʾ b. Yashjub b. Yaʿrub b. Qaḥṭān per al-Ṭabarī's tribal genealogies) and (b) the kingdom-name. The Quranic usage is the kingdom-and-people; v.20 explicitly says *wa-laqad ṣaddaqa ʿalayhim iblīsu ẓannahu* — referring to the Sabaeans collectively.

**Empirical corpus-distribution fact (CONFIRMED at the QAC v0.4 morphology level):** The proper noun *sabaʾ* (LEM:saba<, POS:PN) appears **exactly 2 times** in the Quran:
- Q 27:22 — *wa-jiʾtuka min sabaʾin bi-nabaʾin yaqīn* ("And I have come to you from Sabaʾ with sure tidings"; the hoopoe-bird's report to Solomon).
- Q 34:15 — *laqad kāna li-sabaʾin fī maskanihim āyatun* ("Indeed there was for Sabaʾ in their dwelling-place a sign").

These are the corpus's ONLY two attestations of the locale-name. The same kingdom is referenced under different angles in the two surahs: Q 27 narrates Solomon's encounter with the Queen of Sheba (Bilqīs); Q 34 narrates the historical-eschatological end of the Sabaean kingdom (the great-flood-of-the-dam). See Q034-F-01 for the eponymous-pair structural test.

## 3. Opening formula — al-ḥamdu li-llāh + heaven-and-earth template

Q 34:1 (no-tashkeel):

> **الحمد لله الذي له ما في السماوات وما في الأرض وله الحمد في الآخرة وهو الحكيم الخبير**
>
> *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi alladhī lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍi wa-lahu al-ḥamdu fī al-ākhirati wa-huwa al-ḥakīmu al-khabīr*
>
> "Praise be to Allah, to whom belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth, and to whom belongs the Praise in the Hereafter; He is the All-Wise, the All-Aware."

Q 34 is one of **exactly 5 surahs** in the Quran whose first verse begins with the exact phrase *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*: **{Q 1, Q 6, Q 18, Q 34, Q 35}**. Of these:

| Surah | Opening al-ḥamd verse | Continuation theme |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| Q 1 | *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi rabbi al-ʿālamīn* | Lord of all worlds |
| Q 6 | *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi alladhī khalaqa al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ wa-jaʿala al-ẓulumāti wa-l-nūr* | Creator of heavens and earth, light and darkness |
| Q 18 | *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi alladhī anzala ʿalā ʿabdihi al-kitāb wa-lam yajʿal lahu ʿiwajā* | Revealer of the (uncrooked) Book |
| Q 34 | *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi alladhī lahu mā fī al-samāwāti wa-mā fī al-arḍ wa-lahu al-ḥamdu fī al-ākhira* | Owner of heavens and earth, recipient of dual ḥamd (this-world + ākhira) |
| Q 35 | *al-ḥamdu li-llāhi fāṭiri al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ jāʿili al-malāʾikati rusulā* | Originator of heavens and earth, who makes angels messengers |

**Three structural sub-patterns** within the 5-surah cluster:
- **Heaven-and-earth opening (4 of 5)**: Q 6, Q 18 partial (*ʿiwaj* not heavens/earth), Q 34, Q 35. Of these, Q 6, Q 34, Q 35 explicitly name *al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ*; Q 1 and Q 18 do not.
- **al-ḥamdu li-llāh + alladhī (relative clause) (3 of 5)**: Q 6, Q 18, Q 34. Q 1 and Q 35 use direct apposition (*rabb*, *fāṭir*).
- **Dual-ḥamd construction (1 of 5, UNIQUE to Q 34)**: only Q 34:1 contains *al-ḥamdu li-llāh* twice in the same verse (initial + medial *wa-lahu al-ḥamdu fī al-ākhira*). This is the corpus's **only** dual-ḥamd verse — a structural fingerprint unique to Q 34.

**Empirical test of cluster cohesion:** see Q034-F-01 — the al-ḥamdu li-llāh opener cluster {Q 1, 6, 18, 34, 35} is **NOT FR-cohesive** at the group level. Mean within-cluster Fisher-Rao distance = 0.9902 vs corpus pairwise mean 0.9226 (p=0.75 in the COHESIVE direction; p=0.25 in the ANTI-cohesive direction). Even dropping Q 1 (sui-generis), the 4-cluster mean (0.9466) sits ABOVE corpus mean. The classical-rhetorical "al-ḥamd opener" classification is a **formal-opening-template parallel, not a content-fingerprint cohesion**. **OQ-3-CANDIDATE-ANSWER:** the al-ḥamdu li-llāh opener cluster is **NOT a second book-introduction-marker class** in the way the muqaṭṭāʿat are — it is a doxological-formula parallel without underlying content cohesion. The empirically-strongest sub-pattern is the heaven-and-earth template (4 of 5) plus the alladhī-relative (3 of 5), neither of which forms a cohesive content fingerprint detectable by FR-on-roots.

## 4. Q 34 + Q 27 — the Sabaʾ-narrative-pair

Q 34 (the eponymous Sabaʾ surah) and Q 27 al-Naml (the only other surah containing the proper noun *sabaʾ*) form a structural pair narrating two complementary aspects of the South-Arabian kingdom:

| Surah | Sabaʾ verse | Sabaʾ narrative | Length of Sabaʾ block |
|:-:|:-:|:--|:-:|
| Q 27 | v.22 (*min sabaʾin bi-nabaʾin yaqīn*) | Solomon's encounter with Queen Bilqīs (the throne-letter, the conversion, the glass-floor scene) | vv. 20-44 (25 verses) |
| Q 34 | v.15 (*li-sabaʾin fī maskanihim āyatun*) | The great-flood-of-the-dam (*sayl al-ʿarim*); destruction of the two-garden kingdom; dispersion (*mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq*) | vv. 15-19 (5 verses) |

**Empirical test of Q 27 ↔ Q 34 pair cohesion (Q034-F-02):**
- FR distance Q 27 ↔ Q 34 = 0.8661 (vs corpus pairwise median 0.9567 — Q 27-Q 34 sit at the 31.3rd percentile of all-pair distances, MORE-similar-than-median).
- **Q 34 is rank 8 in Q 27's top-10 nearest neighbors** (after Q 7, Q 10, Q 28, Q 6, Q 29, Q 21, Q 41).
- **Q 27 is rank 10 in Q 34's top-10 nearest neighbors** (after Q 41, Q 46, Q 32, Q 36, Q 10, Q 25, Q 67, Q 45, Q 17).
- **MUTUAL TOP-10**: each surah is in the other's top-10 nearest FR neighbors. This is structurally significant — only ~9% of surah-pairs achieve mutual top-10 status under random matching.

The Sabaʾ-narrative-pair is **moderately FR-cohesive** at the bilateral level. They are not each other's *closest* neighbor (each has closer ones in the same Late-Meccan creedal-narrative band), but mutual top-10 is a non-trivial structural relation. See Q034-F-02 for the full diagnostic.

## 5. Eponymous-naming + corpus-monopoly fact

Q 34 is the only surah named after a *kingdom-people-locale* in the Quran (other place-name surahs are Q 17 al-Isrāʾ and Q 105 al-Fīl, both event-named, not locale-named; Q 14 Ibrāhīm and Q 12 Yūsuf are prophet-named). The corpus-monopoly is sharper at the proper-noun level: the lemma *sabaʾ* (LEM:saba<) appears only in Q 27:22 and Q 34:15, and the *eponymous-naming-with-narrative-content* relation is exclusive to Q 34 (Q 27 mentions but is not eponymously named after Sabaʾ).

This makes Q 34 the corpus's **only example of "kingdom-name + kingdom-narrative-as-major-block"** — a structural fingerprint distinct from prophet-named surahs (Q 10 Yūnus, Q 11 Hūd, Q 12 Yūsuf, Q 14 Ibrāhīm, Q 19 Maryam, Q 71 Nūḥ) and from concept-named surahs (Q 16 al-Naḥl, Q 27 al-Naml, Q 36 Yāsīn).

## 6. Macro-structure (4 thematic blocks)

Q 34 organizes into 4 macro-blocks per the brief's specification:

| Block | vv. | Length | Theme | Notable verses |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:--|:-:|
| A | 1-9 | 9 | al-ḥamdu li-llāh opening + scripture-frame + creator-omniscience | v.1 (dual-ḥamd), v.6 (those-given-knowledge), v.9 (heavens-as-sign) |
| B | 10-14 | 5 | David + Solomon (mountains-praising; jinn-workers; Solomon's death by termite) | v.10 (Dāwūd + birds), v.12-13 (Sulaymān + jinn-workers), v.14 (termite reveals Solomon's death) |
| C | 15-19 | 5 | The Sabaʾ-flood narrative (the two-garden kingdom; the great-flood; the dispersion) | v.15 (li-sabaʾin), v.16 (sayl al-ʿarim), v.19 (mazzaqnāhum kulla mumazzaq) |
| D | 20-54 | 35 | Prophet-rejection polemic + eschatology + tawḥīd (universal-prophecy v.28; tawḥīd-debate; resurrection-affirmation) | v.28 (kāffatan li-l-nās), v.40-41 (angels-disavow-shirk), v.46 (qul innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥida) |

The 4-block structure follows a **historical-eschatological progression**: praise → prophet-precedents (David/Solomon: blessed obedience) → kingdom-precedent (Sabaʾ: punished disobedience) → contemporary-application (Meccan polemic + final-judgment imagery). The Sabaʾ block (C, 5 verses) is the structural HINGE between the two blessed-prophet vignettes (B, 5 verses) and the long polemic-eschatology section (D, 35 verses).

## 7. Eschatology + tawḥīd themes

Q 34 is one of the corpus's most concentrated **omniscience-eschatology-tawḥīd** surahs. Key thematic markers:

### Omniscience (v.1-3)
- v.1: *al-ḥakīm al-khabīr* (the All-Aware).
- v.2: *yaʿlamu mā yaliju fī al-arḍi wa-mā yakhruju minhā wa-mā yanzilu min al-samāʾi wa-mā yaʿruju fīhā* (He knows what enters the earth, what comes out, what descends from heaven, what ascends to it).
- v.3: *lā taʾtīnā al-sāʿah qul balā wa-rabbī la-taʾtiyannakum ʿālimi al-ghaybi lā yaʿzubu ʿanhu mithqālu dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍ* (the famous *mithqāl dharra* "atom's-weight" doctrine of complete divine knowledge).

### Universal prophecy (v.28)
- v.28: *wa-mā arsalnāka illā kāffatan li-l-nāsi bashīran wa-nadhīran* — "And We have not sent you except to all of humanity as a bringer-of-good-tidings and a warner." This is one of the corpus's most-cited verses for the **universalist** theology of prophecy. Classical hadith collections cite this verse to establish that Muḥammad's mission is not restricted to Arab tribes (Bukhārī Ṣaḥīḥ, hadith of Jābir b. ʿAbdallāh on the 5 things granted to the Prophet ﷺ that no prophet before him was granted; one item being: *kāna al-nabiyyu yubʿathu ilā qawmihi khāṣṣatan wa-buʿithtu ilā al-nāsi ʿāmmatan*).

### Tawḥīd-debate (vv. 22-27, 40-42)
- v.22: *qul idʿū alladhīna zaʿamtum min dūni allāhi lā yamlikūna mithqāla dharratin fī al-samāwāti wa-lā fī al-arḍi wa-mā lahum fīhimā min shirkin wa-mā lahu minhum min ẓahīr* (the polytheist-deities cannot own / cannot share / cannot help). 4-fold negation (mithqāla dharra / shirk / ẓahīr / + the implied 4th in v.23).
- v.40-41: angels disavow having been worshipped — "they were worshipping the jinn".
- v.46: *qul innamā aʿiẓukum bi-wāḥidah an taqūmū li-llāhi mathnā wa-furādā* — "Say: I exhort you with one thing only: stand for Allah in pairs and individually." A meta-doctrinal one-thing-only formulation, classical homiletic anchor.

### Resurrection (vv. 3, 7-8, 31-33)
- v.7-8: the deniers' mockery (*hal nadullukum ʿalā rajulin yunabbiʾukum...*) and the response (*aftarā ʿalā allāhi kadhiban am bihi jinnatun*).
- v.31-33: the dialogue of the misled with their misleaders on the Day.

## 10. The munāsabah / context — Q 33 → Q 34 → Q 35 transition

Q 34 sits between Q 33 al-Aḥzāb (Medinan, 73 verses, legal/social) and Q 35 Fāṭir (Late Meccan, 45 verses, al-ḥamdu li-llāh opener). The Q 33 → Q 34 transition is one of the corpus's **roughest** mushaf-adjacencies (rank 111/113 in H-NEW-720; period_Medinan_to_Meccan + phase_Medinan_to_Late_Meccan boundary in H-NEW-130). The Q 34 → Q 35 transition is moderate (rank 65/113), driven by:
- **Shared opener**: both begin with *al-ḥamdu li-llāh*.
- **Shared themes**: heavens-and-earth ownership, divine omniscience, prophetic-warning function.
- **Length proximity**: 54 verses (Q 34) vs 45 verses (Q 35), both Late-Meccan medium.
- **No structural-boundary markers**: H-NEW-130 places no boundary between Q 34 and Q 35.

Despite the shared opener, Q 34→Q 35 is NOT clamped-zero seamless — the surahs share opener-form but differ in content focus (Q 34: kingdom-narrative + universal-prophecy; Q 35: angels-as-messengers + post-mortem-sorting). See Q034-F-04 for the seam diagnostic.
