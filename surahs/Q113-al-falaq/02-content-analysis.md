## 1. Verse-by-verse summary

Source: `quran-no-tashkeel.json` Q113; cross-validated against min-tashkeel and full-tashkeel. QAC roots from `data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt`.

### Verse 1 — *qul aʿūdhu bi-rabbi l-falaq*

- **Roots**: qwl (V impv 2MS — *qul*), Ew (V impf 1MS — *aʿūdhu* "I take refuge"), rbb (N — *rabb*), flq (N def — *al-falaq*).
- **Speech-act**: imperative refuge-declaration. The Prophet is instructed to *say* "I take refuge".
- ***falaq* (الفلق)**: classical glosses diverge: (i) *al-ṣubḥ* "the dawn" (Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Ṭabarī); (ii) *al-khalq* "the creation in general"; (iii) *jubb fī jahannam* "a well in hell" (a minority Companion-tradition); (iv) "any cleaving / splitting" — including birth, sprouting seed, daybreak. The dominant gloss is **the dawn**, with secondary glosses extending to "the cleavage by which God brings forth being from non-being". The root flq appears in 3 places in the Quran: Q 6:95 (cleaver of grain and seed), Q 6:96 (cleaver of dawn), Q 113:1.
- **Theological function**: invokes God by His attribute as the *cleaver* who brings forth creation — a creative-power frame for the refuge.

### Verse 2 — *min sharri mā khalaq*

- **Roots**: ... (*min sharri* — particle + N "evil"; the root شر = *sh-r-r*), xlq (V perf 3MS — *khalaq*).
- **Refuge-target #1**: **the evil of all created things in general**.
- **Theological structure**: a *universal-quantifier* refuge — covers any evil emerging from any created entity. This is the broadest-possible refuge-target.

### Verse 3 — *wa-min sharri ghāsiqin idhā waqab*

- **Roots**: gsq (N indef — *ghāsiq*), wqb (V perf 3MS — *waqab*).
- **Refuge-target #2**: **the evil of darkness/twilight when it descends**.
- **Lexical**: *ghāsiq* = the dense darkness; classical glosses: (i) night when its darkness deepens (al-Ṭabarī); (ii) the moon when it sets; (iii) any obscuring/setting body (broader sense). The hadith (al-Tirmidhī #3366; chain *ḥasan*) cites the Prophet pointing at the moon and saying *taʿawwadhī bi-llāhi min hādhā fa-innahu l-ghāsiq idhā waqab* ("seek refuge in Allāh from this, for it is the *ghāsiq* when it descends"). Both root gsq and root wqb are corpus-rare (gsq: 2 attestations; wqb: 1 — a strict hapax).
- **Theological function**: refuge from *temporal-cosmic* danger — the threat of nightfall and concealing darkness.

### Verse 4 — *wa-min sharri l-naffāthāti fī l-ʿuqad*

- **Roots**: nfv (N pl fem — *al-naffāthāt* "those who blow"), Eqd (N — *al-ʿuqad* "knots").
- **Refuge-target #3**: **the evil of the women who blow on knots** (= sorceresses practicing knot-magic).
- **Lexical**: *al-naffāthāt* is feminine plural active participle of *nafatha* (to blow with spittle). *al-ʿuqad* is plural of *ʿuqda* (knot). The expression is widely understood as a specific reference to a sorcery practice — tying knots in cord/hair while blowing magical incantations onto them.
- **Asbāb-al-nuzūl link**: this verse is most directly tied to the Labīd ibn al-Aʿṣam sorcery-asbāb (al-Bukhārī #5763, #3268; see `04-hadith-corpus.md` and `05-classical-claims-audit.md`). The 11 knots tradition reads vv.2-5 as a 4-verse refuge-formula whose recitation untied the 11 knots.
- **Both roots nfv and Eqd are corpus-rare**: nfv attested only here (hapax); Eqd attested in 2 places only (here + Q 5:89 *ʿaqadtum al-aymān* "you have bound oaths").
- **Theological function**: refuge from *human-magical* danger — the threat of intentional malevolent action.

### Verse 5 — *wa-min sharri ḥāsidin idhā ḥasad*

- **Roots**: Hsd × 2 (one as participle *ḥāsid*, one as verb *ḥasad*).
- **Refuge-target #4**: **the evil of an envier when he envies**.
- **Lexical**: the root Hsd appears 5 times in the Quran (Q 2:109, Q 4:54, Q 48:15, Q 113:5 ×2). The verse couples active-participle + perfect-verb of the same root — a morphological pairing similar to Q 112's wld-active+passive.
- **Theological function**: refuge from *human-affective* danger — the threat of malicious envy.

## 2. The 4-evil typology

The 5-verse surah is structurally:

```
v.1: refuge-formula (LOCATION: with the Lord of daybreak)
v.2: evil-typology #1 — UNIVERSAL (any created evil)
v.3: evil-typology #2 — COSMIC (descending darkness)
v.4: evil-typology #3 — MAGICAL (knot-blowing sorceresses)
v.5: evil-typology #4 — AFFECTIVE (the envier when he envies)
```

The 4 evils form a **typology of EXTERNAL evil**:

| Typology | Source-locus | Verse |
|:--|:--|:-:|
| Universal | from any created thing | v.2 |
| Cosmic | from cosmic darkness | v.3 |
| Magical | from sorcery acts | v.4 |
| Affective | from human envy | v.5 |

This is the **EXTERNAL-evil refuge-formula**. Compare with Q 114, which is the **INTERNAL-evil refuge-formula** (refuge from al-waswās al-khannās — internal whispering). Q 113 + Q 114 form a thematic pair: Q 113 covers external-evil (cosmos, magic, envy); Q 114 covers internal-evil (whispering in the breast).

## 3. The *min sharri* refrain

The verse-opening refrain *min sharri* (or *wa-min sharri*) appears in vv.2, 3, 4, 5 — **4 attestations in 4 consecutive verses**. This is one of the corpus's tightest refrains by density (4-fold refrain in a 5-verse surah).

The refrain alternates between two grammatical types:
- *min sharri mā khalaq* (v.2) — relative-clause object (the evil of *what He created*)
- *min sharri X* + qualifying phrase (vv.3, 4, 5) — direct object + modifier

This refrain-density is structurally a high-density *takrīr* signature (see [[cross-finding-027-ijaz-al-takrir|cross-finding-027]] which falsified the takrīr-as-axis hypothesis but confirmed Q 55 as sui-generis takrīr-extreme; Q 113's takrīr-density is *moderate* — high refrain-fraction but on a small base).

## 4. Major thematic blocks

Q 113 has a **single thematic block**: refuge-from-external-evil. The 5 verses are functionally:
- 1 refuge-formula (v.1)
- 4 evil-typology entries (vv.2-5)

The single-block structure is consistent with Q 114 (also single-block: refuge-from-internal-evil) and contrasts with Q 112's tawḥīd single-block. The 3 muʿawwidhāt-3 surahs (Q 112-113-114) all have single-block structures — a folk-tradition structural commonality.

## 8. Cross-surah content references

- **Q 6:95-96** — the only other *flq*-root attestations: God as *fāliq al-ḥabb wa-l-nawā* and *fāliq al-iṣbāḥ*. Q 113:1 invokes the same divine attribute.
- **Q 5:89** — the only other *Eqd*-root attestation: *ʿaqadtum al-aymān* (you have bound oaths) — connects Q 113's *al-ʿuqad* (knots) to oath-binding semantics.
- **Q 2:109, Q 4:54, Q 48:15** — the other *Hsd*-root attestations: envy of unbelievers (Q 2:109), of believers (Q 4:54), of those who follow the Prophet (Q 48:15). Q 113:5 is the corpus's only *self-protective* envy-mention.

## 9. Honest limits

1. **The Labīd ibn al-Aʿṣam asbāb-tradition is doctrinally sensitive**: traditional commentators (Ibn Kathīr) accept the report; some moderns question it on theological grounds (the Prophet being effectively bewitched). The asbāb does NOT determine the surah's text or chain-quality; it bears on interpretive context only.
2. **The 11-knots / 11-verses count** (Q 113 + Q 114 = 5 + 6 = 11) is widely cited but is a coincidental count, not pre-registered for a formal test.
3. **The *al-naffāthāt* gendering** (feminine plural) is grammatical; whether the asbāb specifies female sorceresses or whether the form is generic-feminine for *the practice itself* is contested.
