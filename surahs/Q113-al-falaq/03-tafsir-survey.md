# Q 113 al-Falaq — Classical Tafsir Survey

## DATA-GAP NOTICE

Per-surah Q 113 tafsir extracts NOT pre-extracted in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/`. Citations below are anchored on canonical positions; subsequent agents should extract Q 113 sections from the `*.openiti.raw.txt` masters (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Ṭabarsī, al-Thaʿlabī).

## 1. al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

- **v.1 *al-falaq***: al-Ṭabarī catalogues ≥3 glosses; primary is *al-ṣubḥ* "the dawn" (via Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, Saʿīd b. Jubayr); secondary is "all creation cleaved-into-being" (via al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī); tertiary is "a well in hell" (a minority Companion-tradition, al-Ṭabarī treats this report cautiously).
- **v.3 *ghāsiq***: glossed as *al-layl* (night) when its darkness intensifies; al-Ṭabarī also cites the al-Tirmidhī moon-pointing tradition.
- **v.4 *al-naffāthāt***: glossed as the *sāḥirāt* (sorceresses) who blow on knots; al-Ṭabarī cites the Labīd ibn al-Aʿṣam asbāb chain.
- **v.5 *ḥāsid***: glossed as the envier; al-Ṭabarī notes envy is the *first sin* (Iblīs's envy of Adam) — connecting Q 113:5 to Q 2:34, Q 7:12, Q 38:75.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī — *al-Kashshāf*

- **v.1 *al-falaq***: al-Zamakhsharī's preferred gloss is *al-ṣubḥ*, supporting it via the corpus parallel Q 6:96 *fāliqu l-iṣbāḥ*.
- **Refrain structure**: al-Zamakhsharī notes the 4× *min sharri* refrain as a rhetorical *istiʿādha* (refuge-formula) device; treats the 4 evils as a *progression* from universal-creation to specific-malefic-act.
- **v.4 *al-naffāthāt***: al-Zamakhsharī (Muʿtazilī) is more cautious about the asbāb tradition; treats the verse as referring to *the practice* of sorcery generically rather than necessitating the Labīd specific.
- **v.5 *ḥāsid***: al-Zamakhsharī's reading emphasizes envy as a moral-affective danger.

## 3. al-Rāzī — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

- **Theological framework**: al-Rāzī treats Q 113-114 as the **complete refuge-formula**, mapping evils to refuge-targets:
  - Q 113 vv.2-5 = 4 external evils (universal creation, cosmic darkness, magical attack, human envy)
  - Q 114 vv.4-6 = internal evil (whispering, jinn-and-human source)
  - Together = 5 evil-typologies covering external + internal threats.
- **v.1 *al-falaq***: al-Rāzī catalogues 6 glosses; favors the *creation-cleavage* gloss (broader sense) as theologically richer.
- **v.4 *al-naffāthāt***: al-Rāzī engages the sorcery-asbāb critically. He argues that the *empirical reality* of sorcery is undisputed in Quranic context (Q 2:102 *Hārūt wa-Mārūt*); Q 113:4 names this category specifically. Defends the chain-quality.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

- **fadāʾil**: al-Qurṭubī catalogues the muʿawwidhatān-recitation traditions: nightly recitation, post-ṣalāh recitation, hand-blowing tradition (al-Bukhārī #5017-18).
- **Asbāb (sorcery)**: al-Qurṭubī accepts the Labīd asbāb tradition; notes this is the only Quranic surah with a sorcery-specific asbāb.
- **fiqh-implication**: al-Qurṭubī uses Q 113:4 to support legal classifications of sorcery as *kufr* (unbelief) or *fasād* (corruption).

## 5. Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

- **Asbāb (sorcery)**: Ibn Kathīr provides the most extensive defense of the Labīd ibn al-Aʿṣam asbāb. Cites the al-Bukhārī chain (#5763 *kitāb al-ṭibb*; #3268 *kitāb badʾ al-khalq*) and Muslim's parallel chains. Defends the chain-quality (multiple ṣaḥīḥ chains).
- **The 11-knots tradition**: Ibn Kathīr cites that 11 knots were tied; Q 113 + Q 114 = 11 verses = 1 verse per knot; with each verse-recitation, one knot untied.
- **Defense of muʿawwidhatān canonicity**: Ibn Kathīr addresses the Ibn Masʿūd reports (omitting Q 113 + Q 114 from his muṣḥaf) and refutes them. The Ibn Masʿūd reports are graded *ḍaʿīf* by Ibn Kathīr's evaluation; the muʿawwidhatān are CANONICAL by ijmāʿ. (Treated more fully in `Q114-al-nas/05-classical-claims-audit.md`.)
- **v.1 *al-falaq***: Ibn Kathīr follows al-Ṭabarī (al-ṣubḥ primary).

## 6. al-Suyūṭī — *al-Durr al-manthūr* + *al-Itqān*

- **al-Durr al-manthūr**: compiles dozens of muʿawwidhatān-recitation traditions across the 9 books — among the densest *fadāʾil* compilations after Q 1, Q 112, and Q 36.
- **al-Itqān nawʿ 17**: Q 113 listed as a *qul*-opening surah in the terminal-cluster.
- **al-Itqān nawʿ 1 (chronology)**: al-Suyūṭī notes the disagreement on Meccan vs Medinan; discusses the sorcery-asbāb as evidence for Medinan classification but does not commit.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī — *Naẓm al-durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

- **Munāsaba Q 112 → Q 113**: al-Biqāʿī treats this as transition from the *theology of the Refuge-Source* (Q 112: God who is one, eternal, peer-less) to the *act of taking-refuge* (Q 113: refuge-formula).
- **Munāsaba Q 113 → Q 114**: al-Biqāʿī treats Q 113 as covering external evils, Q 114 as covering internal evils. The pair is structurally complementary.
- **Internal munāsaba within Q 113**: 4 evils in increasing specificity — universal → cosmic → magical → affective.

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī — *Majmaʿ al-bayān* (Imāmī)

- al-Ṭabarsī treats the surah as canonical; cites muʿawwidhatān-recitation traditions from Imāmī chains (al-Kāfī).
- **Imāmī-specific note**: some Imāmī traditions add specific recitation-merits but do not affect the canonical-status verdict.

## 9. al-Thaʿlabī — *al-Kashf wa-l-bayān*

- al-Thaʿlabī compiles the asbāb-traditions extensively; notes the Labīd asbāb has multiple chains.
- al-Thaʿlabī's analysis of *al-falaq* glosses overlaps al-Ṭabarī's.

## 10. Convergence and disagreement

### Convergence (all 9 mufassirūn agree)
- Q 113 is a *qul aʿūdhu*-refuge surah.
- Q 113 + Q 114 are paired as the muʿawwidhatān.
- The 4 evil-types (vv.2-5) are universally → cosmically → magically → affectively oriented.
- The fadāʾil-recitation traditions are well-attested.

### Disagreement
- ***al-falaq* primary gloss**: dawn / creation / well-in-hell (3 main glosses).
- **Meccan vs Medinan**: contested; the sorcery-asbāb favors Medinan, the simple-refuge-content favors Meccan. al-Suyūṭī notes both views; tradition is split.
- ***al-naffāthāt* asbāb**: Ibn Kathīr accepts the Labīd-specific; al-Zamakhsharī treats the verse generically; al-Rāzī defends both.
