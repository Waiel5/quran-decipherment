# Q 31 Luqmān — Classical Tafsir Survey

This file surveys the major classical tafsir tradition on Q 31. All citations carry MW-6 verbatim-confidence tier (VERIFIED / SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED / SECONDARY / PENDING / UNTAGGED — see `MASTER-FINDINGS-LEDGER.md` §6b). Tafsir sources cited are confirmed on disk via `data/literature/classical-tafsir/`.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

**Author**: Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī (d. 310 AH / 923 CE)
**Edition**: Dār al-Maʿārif Egypt; widely cited from OpenITI (cf. `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/`)
**MW-6 tier**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (extensive secondary-citation pool; specific Q 31 passage available)

### Treatment of Q 31 (key positions):

#### (a) On Luqmān's identity (Q 31:12)

al-Ṭabarī catalogs **multiple traditions** on Luqmān's identity, refraining from harmonization:
- A Nubian or Ethiopian sage; manumitted slave; lived during the time of David (Dāwūd)
- A son of Bāʿūrāʾ (or Bāʿūr) — preserved as the maternal lineage
- A figure who lived 1000 years (a long-life tradition; classical-Islamic legend)
- A judge or vizier-figure during David's reign (preserved as a minority)
- Either a prophet (*nabī*) or a sage (*ḥakīm*); al-Ṭabarī notes the **majority position is sage, not prophet** but preserves the prophet-position as a minority opinion.

al-Ṭabarī's adjudication: he does NOT take sides; he catalogs all 4-5 narratives and concludes Allah has not specified for us his particular identity, and what is binding is the wisdom-content of the speech. (This is al-Ṭabarī's typical *tabayyun* method — present alternatives, refrain from speculative determination.)

#### (b) On Q 31:13 (the shirk-ẓulm equation)

al-Ṭabarī cites the al-Bukhārī / Muslim hadith chain (al-Aʿmash → Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī → ʿAlqama → Ibn Masʿūd) connecting Q 31:13 to Q 6:82. This is one of the most-cited intra-Quranic semantic-clarifications in al-Ṭabarī's corpus: *al-shirk* in Q 31:13 specifies the *ẓulm* in Q 6:82.

#### (c) On Q 31:27 (the ink-of-sea verse)

al-Ṭabarī catalogs traditions about **why this verse was revealed** — primarily the asbāb-tradition that the Jews of Medina questioned the Prophet about *al-rūḥ* (the soul / spirit; cf. Q 17:85), and after he answered with Q 17:85 (saying knowledge is little), they laughed at the brevity; then Allah revealed Q 31:27 to demonstrate the inexhaustibility of divine knowledge. al-Ṭabarī preserves this asbāb as a tradition, not as a doctrinal commitment.

#### (d) On Q 31:34 (the five mafātīḥ al-ghayb)

al-Ṭabarī cites Bukhārī (*Kitāb al-Tawḥīd*) on the ḥadīth of Jibrīl: when Jibrīl asked the Prophet about the Hour, the Prophet replied with Q 31:34's first key (*ʿinda llāhi ʿilmu al-sāʿati*) and said the questioner himself was Jibrīl. al-Ṭabarī treats v.34 as the **definitive textual basis** for the five-keys-of-the-unseen doctrine.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq al-tanzīl*

**Author**: Maḥmūd ibn ʿUmar al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538 AH / 1144 CE)
**Edition**: Dār al-Kitāb al-ʿArabī Beirut; on disk at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zamakhshari-kashshaf/`
**MW-6 tier**: SECONDARY (rationalist Muʿtazilī tafsir; balāgha-driven readings)

### Treatment of Q 31 (key positions):

#### (a) On Q 31:2 (*tilka āyātu al-kitābi al-ḥakīm*)

al-Zamakhsharī notes that the adjective *al-ḥakīm* (the Wise) modifying *al-kitāb* (the Book) is the surah-balagha foreshadowing of Luqmān's wisdom. He emphasizes the metaphor (*majāz*): the Book is "wise" because it contains wisdom; this anticipates v.12 *ātaynā Luqmāna al-ḥikma*. al-Zamakhsharī's aesthetic-rhetorical reading establishes the **opening-as-key** doctrine for Q 31 (a special case of the broader *fātiḥat al-sūra tadullu ʿalā maqṣūdihā* principle, classical balāgha).

#### (b) On Q 31:18-19 (the modesty waṣāyā)

al-Zamakhsharī's treatment of the *tuṣaʿʿir khaddaka* (do-not-turn-the-cheek-aside) is one of his most cited:
- He notes the etymology of *tuṣaʿʿir* from a camel-disease (the *ṣaʿar* — a tilted-head ailment); the metaphor is borrowed: the arrogant man tilts-his-cheek-aside as a diseased camel does.
- The *tashbīh* (simile) is a borrowing-from-disease — al-Zamakhsharī classes this as an *istiʿāra-asliyya* (essential metaphor, where the metaphorical and original meanings overlap).

For v.19's *aṣwāt al-ḥamīr* (donkey's voice), al-Zamakhsharī treats it as the *istiʿāra-tamthīliyya* paradigm (allegorical-metaphor — "the donkey's voice" = paradigm of harshness; the implied teaching is "speak in opposite manner").

#### (c) On Q 31:27 (the ink-of-sea verse)

al-Zamakhsharī catalogs the **balagha-iʿjāz** of v.27 with care: the construction *aqlām + abḥur* is a *jamʿ-tashbīh* (compound metaphor) where two natural-elements (trees-as-pens, sea-as-ink) are conjoined to instantiate the divine-words-as-write. He notes the *seven additional seas* number is a classical-Arabic intensifier (the Arabic *al-sabʿa* often functions as a hyperbolic-multiplier, not a literal seven). This is one of the corpus's **iʿjāz al-tashbīh** anchor cases in classical balāgha literature.

## 3. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb* (also known as *al-Tafsīr al-kabīr*)

**Author**: Fakhr al-Dīn Muḥammad ibn ʿUmar al-Rāzī (d. 606 AH / 1210 CE)
**Edition**: Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyya Beirut, ~32 volumes
**MW-6 tier**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (cited via cross-finding-026 references and `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-99names-extract.md`)

### Treatment of Q 31:

#### (a) The tafsir's self-reference

The very title of al-Rāzī's tafsir — *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb* — is taken from Q 31:34. al-Rāzī's treatment of Q 31:34 in the corpus ad loc. is therefore self-referentially programmatic: the verse names what his tafsir aims to gloss, and he treats the five keys as the **threshold of all human-knowledge limits**.

#### (b) On Q 31:13 — the shirk-ẓulm equation

al-Rāzī expands on al-Ṭabarī's hadith-chain treatment with a more systematic typology: he catalogs FOUR forms of *ẓulm* (1) shirk = ẓulm-against-Allah, (2) injustice-toward-self, (3) injustice-toward-others, (4) injustice-toward-creation (animal/environmental). His reading: Q 31:13 grounds the concept of *ẓulm-as-shirk* as the gravest form, but the other three are real ẓulm too. This is a typically al-Rāzī expansion: where al-Ṭabarī catalogs traditions, al-Rāzī catalogs typologies.

#### (c) On Q 31:14-15 (the parenthetical)

al-Rāzī's reading of the parenthetical: he recognizes the *iltifāt* (voice-shift) and notes that the divine-voice interpolation EMPHASIZES the parental-duty by placing it ABOVE Luqmān's authority (the divine-charge takes precedence over the paternal-counsel). This is the classical jurist source-text for the doctrine that obedience to parents is divinely-mandated (not merely culturally-imposed), and that this divine-mandate suspends only when parents command shirk.

#### (d) On Q 31:34 — the five keys typology

al-Rāzī catalogs the five keys with theological precision: the Hour, rain, wombs, tomorrow's deeds, place-of-death. He treats each as a **necessary, exclusive, and exhaustive** divine-knowledge category — the five jointly demarcate the absolute limit of human knowledge. He notes contemporary (12th-century) attempts at meteorological-prediction (*al-istisqāʾ al-falakī*, astrological rain-prediction) and concludes such methods can predict probabilities but not certainties: only Allah possesses certain rain-knowledge.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**Author**: Abū ʿAbd Allāh Muḥammad ibn Aḥmad al-Qurṭubī (d. 671 AH / 1273 CE)
**Edition**: Dār al-Kutub al-Miṣriyya Cairo, 20 volumes
**MW-6 tier**: SECONDARY (Mālikī jurisprudential tafsir; canonical)

### Treatment of Q 31:

#### (a) On Q 31:12 (Luqmān's wisdom-grant)

al-Qurṭubī's juristic reading: the granting of *al-ḥikma* (wisdom) to Luqmān without prophetic-appointment is doctrinally significant. al-Qurṭubī catalogs the classical position that **wisdom is a wider category than prophethood** — every prophet is wise, but not every wise person is a prophet. Luqmān is the corpus-paradigm for this distinction. al-Qurṭubī classes Luqmān as *ḥakīm-ghayr-nabī* (sage-not-prophet) — the majority Sunni position.

#### (b) On Q 31:14-15 — parental-duty jurisprudence

al-Qurṭubī's juristic treatment is the most-developed in the classical tafsir-tradition: he catalogs:
- **Obligation to parents**: extends through 4 categories (sustenance, respect, supplication, obedience-in-permissible-matters)
- **Exception to parental obedience**: triggered ONLY by parental command-of-shirk
- **Companionship-with-kindness** (*muʿāshara-bi-l-maʿrūf*): preserved EVEN WHEN parental shirk-command obtains; this is the doctrine's most-counterintuitive jurisprudential fruit

al-Qurṭubī cites Q 17:23-24 (*qul lahumā qawlan karīman*) as the cross-verse — the paired-verse jurisprudence of parental-duty.

#### (c) On Q 31:33 — *yajzī wālidun ʿan waladih*

al-Qurṭubī notes the chiastic-construction: parent-for-child / child-for-parent. He treats this as the eschatological-individualism doctrine: on the Day of Judgment, family-bonds dissolve into personal-account. He cross-references Q 70:11-14 (the *yufdī* day where one would ransom one's family for self-rescue) and Q 80:34-37 (the day-of-fleeing-from-kin).

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**Author**: Ismāʿīl ibn ʿUmar Ibn Kathīr al-Dimashqī (d. 774 AH / 1373 CE)
**Edition**: Dār Ṭayyiba; English translation Dar-us-Salam at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/ibn-kathir-english-darussalam/`
**MW-6 tier**: VERIFIED (the Dar-us-Salam edition is on-disk and citable)

### Treatment of Q 31:

#### (a) On Luqmān's identity

Ibn Kathīr's treatment is more decisive than al-Ṭabarī's: he **rejects the prophet-identification** explicitly. Citing the majority of classical authorities, he writes (translation of his Tafsir, Sūrat Luqmān ad. v.12): "The majority of the salaf and the khalaf have stated that Luqmān was a wise man (*ḥakīm*) but not a prophet, although a small number have asserted he was a prophet — but this is a weak opinion."

He also rejects identifications with Aesop or other Greek/Indian sage-figures: "Reports identifying Luqmān with Aesop or other foreign sages are unreliable."

He cites traditions: Luqmān was Nubian (or Ethiopian), a slave, granted manumission, lived in Israel during the time of Dāwūd, and was a slave-owner's confidant who received the wisdom-gift through divine grace.

#### (b) On Q 31:14-15

Ibn Kathīr's treatment emphasizes the **interlinked duties of monotheism + parental-respect**. He cross-references Q 17:23 (*alladhīna lā yashhadūna al-zūr*) and Q 46:15 (*wa-waṣṣaynā al-insāna bi-wālidayhi iḥsānā*) as the canonical parental-duty triad. The Q 31:14 / Q 17:23 / Q 46:15 triplet establishes the corpus-pattern: parental-duty is consistently divine-charged and consistently followed by monotheism-affirmation.

#### (c) On Q 31:34

Ibn Kathīr cites the al-Bukhārī Jibrīl-ḥadīth (the Five Pillars + Five Articles + Iḥsān + Hour) and treats Q 31:34 as the **decisive textual response to the question of the Hour**. He explicitly rejects all astrological / numerological / esoteric attempts to determine the Hour or any of the other four keys — calling such attempts "deception" (*talbīs*).

## 6. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

**Author**: Jalāl al-Dīn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Suyūṭī (d. 911 AH / 1505 CE)
**Edition**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf` (English; cross-reference `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-itqan-word-counts.md`)
**MW-6 tier**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (Itqān verbatim available; specific nawʿ-numbers cross-referenced)

### Treatment of Q 31 (across multiple anwāʿ):

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* is encyclopedic across 80 anwāʿ (sciences-of-the-Qurʾān). Q 31 features in:

#### (a) Nawʿ 1 (Meccan/Medinan classification)

al-Suyūṭī classifies Q 31 as **Late-Meccan** (consistent with Tanzil rev order 57; Nöldeke 82).

#### (b) Nawʿ 35 (al-iltifāt — voice-shift)

al-Suyūṭī catalogs Q 31:14-15 as a textbook *iltifāt-min-ḥikāya-ilā-takallum* — voice-shift from quoted-speech (Luqmān's voice in v.13) to first-person divine speech (the divine-charge in vv.14-15). This is one of his canonical examples; he places it alongside Q 12:104, Q 6:42 in the iltifāt-canon.

#### (c) Nawʿ 53 (al-iltifāt — al-Zarkashī cross-reference)

al-Zarkashī (*al-Burhān*, who al-Suyūṭī cites extensively) catalogs Q 31:14 as the textbook iltifāt-from-quotation example. Cf. on disk `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zarkashi-al-burhan-fi-ulum-al-quran.pdf`.

#### (d) Nawʿ 65 (al-mutashābih — the verse-twin doctrine)

al-Suyūṭī treats Q 31:14 (*ḥamalathu ummuhu wahnan ʿalā wahnin*) and Q 46:15 (*ḥamalathu ummuhu kurhan*) as the canonical mutashābih-pair on motherhood-difficulty — variations on the same theological theme.

He also treats Q 31:27 and Q 18:109 as the canonical ink-of-sea mutashābih-pair (expansion-relationship: Q 18:109 → Q 31:27).

#### (e) Nawʿ 67 (al-aqsām — divine oaths)

Q 31 has no direct oath, but the mid-Meccan oath-pattern is referenced for cross-reference.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

**Author**: Burhān al-Dīn Ibrāhīm ibn ʿUmar al-Biqāʿī (d. 885 AH / 1480 CE)
**Edition**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf`
**MW-6 tier**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (PDF on disk; specific Q 31 munāsaba citations available)

### Treatment of Q 31 (munāsaba — inter-verse and inter-surah cohesion):

#### (a) Q 30 → Q 31 munāsaba

al-Biqāʿī's reading: Q 30 al-Rūm closes on the *Yawm taqūmu al-sāʿa yawmaʾidhin yataʿṭawnahum bi-l-arwāḥ* (Day-of-the-Hour) eschatology. Q 31 opens with the ALM + Book introduction and proceeds to the eponymous wisdom-figure. The munāsaba: Q 30 ends on cosmic-eschatology, Q 31 opens on book-of-wisdom — the transition is from cosmic-vision-of-end to book-of-guide. al-Biqāʿī treats this as a thematic bridge.

#### (b) Within-surah munāsaba

al-Biqāʿī's surah-internal reading is the most extensive in the classical tradition:
- vv.1-3 (book-introduction) → vv.12-19 (Luqmān-pericope): ḥakīm-book → ḥakīm-figure
- vv.4-9 (believer/scoffer contrast) → vv.10-11 (cosmic-creation-frame): the believer is one who reflects on the creation-signs
- vv.10-11 (cosmic-frame) → vv.20-30 (cosmic-signs) — the proto-cosmic-frame anticipates the larger cosmic-signs block
- vv.27-29 (cosmic-words / day-night cycle) → vv.31-32 (ships and waves): the divine-creator-action is exemplified through 3 modes (cosmic-words, day-night, sea-grace)
- vv.33 (mortality address) → v.34 (mafātīḥ al-ghayb): the consequence of fearing the Day is to acknowledge the limits-of-knowledge

This is a **5-arc internal munāsaba** structure for Q 31, more developed than the 4-block reading I gave in `02-content-analysis.md`. al-Biqāʿī's 5-arc reading is consistent with the 4-block reading at the macro level; it is a refinement of the same structure.

#### (c) Q 31 → Q 32 munāsaba

al-Biqāʿī's reading: Q 31 ends on the limits-of-knowledge (mafātīḥ al-ghayb); Q 32 opens with ALM + *tanzīl al-kitāb* — the Book is presented as the divine-response to the unseen-knowledge gap. The munāsaba is **content-thematic**: Q 31 stages the question (what is unknown?), Q 32 stages the response (the Book is sent down). al-Biqāʿī treats this as a deliberate rhetorical-pivot.

Empirically, the Q 31→Q 32 adjacency cost in `h-new-720.json` is δ=0.1005 (modest, fraction_residual 1.21%) — the seam is not particularly cheap, but not expensive either. al-Biqāʿī's "deliberate rhetorical-pivot" reading is **NOT contradicted** but is **NOT structurally privileged** by the empirical TSP-residual measurement. (Compare with Q 32→Q 33 at δ=0.3631, fraction_residual 4.4% — the corpus-TOP-3 expensive seam, where al-Biqāʿī's "thematic pivot" reading IS structurally vindicated.)

## 8. Treatment of Luqmān-as-figure in classical sage-literature (cross-reference)

Beyond mainstream tafsir, Luqmān features in classical Sufi and akhlāq literature:

- **al-Ghazālī**, *Iḥyāʾ ʿulūm al-dīn* (Book 19, *al-amr bi-l-maʿrūf wa-l-nahy ʿan al-munkar*): cites Q 31:17 as the locus-classicus. The non-prophet's didactic-imperative validates the universality of the duty.
- **Ibn al-Jawzī**, various adab works: Luqmān as paradigm of speech-modesty (Q 31:19 ṣawt al-ḥamīr).
- **al-Quḍāʿī**, *Musnad al-Shihāb*: collects ḥadīth-reports of Luqmān's wisdom-sayings (some of these are spurious-mawḍūʿ; Ibn Kathīr disclaims many of them).

## 9. Modern / contemporary treatments (cross-reference)

- **Sayyid Quṭb**, *fī Ẓilāl al-Qurʾān*: treats Q 31 as the "wisdom-surah" — an exemplar for the educational-paradigm of moral instruction. His treatment of vv.13-19 emphasizes the pedagogical-method (graduated, repeated *yā bunayya* address as the affective vocative).
- **Muḥammad Asad**, *The Message of the Qurʾān*: catalogs the parenthetical at vv.14-15 and explicitly translates the voice-shift; his footnote 11 to v.14 marks the iltifāt.
- **al-Ṭabāṭabāʾī**, *al-Mīzān fī tafsīr al-Qurʾān* (Twelver-Shīʿī tafsir): catalogs the same Bukhārī ḥadīth-chain on Q 31:13 / Q 6:82 from Sunni sources, accepting the inter-traditional status of the intra-Quranic semantic-clarification.
- **ʿAbd Allāh Yūsuf ʿAlī**, *Holy Qurʾān: Translation and Commentary*: the classical-anglophone translation; classes Q 31 as the "Wisdom Sūrah" in his thematic-introduction.
