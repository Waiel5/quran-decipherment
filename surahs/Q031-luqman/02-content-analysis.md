# Q 31 Luqmān — Content Analysis

## 1. Macro-structure

Q 31 is a **didactic-wisdom compendium** opened by an ALM book-introduction couplet, organized around a single eponymous moral-pericope (vv.12-19), expanded with cosmic-signs in vv.20-30, and closed on the limits of human knowledge (vv.31-34). The 4-block arc is:

| Block | vv. | Length | Theme | Voice |
|:--|:-:|:-:|:--|:--|
| **A** | **1-11** | **11** | **Frame: ALM + Book + believer/scoffer contrast** | Divine narrator + warning |
| **B** | **12-19** | **8** | **THE LUQMĀN PERICOPE — 8 *waṣāyā* of a non-prophet sage** | Quoted-Luqmān (with parenthetical at vv.14-15) |
| **C** | **20-30** | **11** | **Cosmic-signs + rebuke of obstinate-rejection** | Divine + rhetorical-question |
| **D** | **31-34** | **4** | **Mortality + the five mafātīḥ al-ghayb** | Eschatological-warning + theology of unseen |

## 2. Block A (vv. 1-11) — frame and contrast

### v. 1: ALM
The muqaṭṭaʿāt opener. Q 31 is one of 6 ALM surahs corpus-wide (cross-finding-008 cohort).

### v. 2-3: Book-self-reference
*tilka āyātu al-kitābi al-ḥakīm — hudan wa-raḥmatan li-l-muḥsinīn*
"These are the verses of the Wise Book — a guidance and a mercy for the doers-of-good."

The adjective *al-ḥakīm* (the Wise) — modifier of *al-kitāb* — anticipates the surah's eponymous *ḥikma* at v.12 (*ātaynā Luqmāna al-ḥikma*). al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar*) reads this as the surah's foundational *taṣdīr-nazm* connection: the surah's name (Luqmān) and its key concept (*ḥikma* = wisdom) are foreshadowed in v.2.

### v. 4-5: Believer characterization
The believers are described by 4 markers: establish ṣalāh, give zakāh, certain of the hereafter (*yūqinūn*) — they are upon guidance from their Lord and are the felicitous (*muflihūn*).

### v. 6-7: Scoffer characterization
*wa-min al-nāsi man yashtarī lahwa al-ḥadīthi…* — "Among people there is one who buys idle talk to mislead from the way of Allah without knowledge and takes it in mockery."

Classical asbāb al-nuzūl (al-Wāḥidī, al-Suyūṭī *Lubāb al-nuqūl*) cite traditions linking v.6 to al-Naḍr b. al-Ḥārith, a Quraysh figure who reportedly purchased Persian story-books (the romance-cycle of Rostam and Isfandiyār) and recited them as alternatives to the Qurʾān. The exegetical specificity is contested but the type-figure (the *muskaltrik* who buys distraction-narratives) is what classical exegesis primarily emphasizes.

### v. 8-9: Reaffirmation of the believer-promise
For those who believe and do righteous deeds — the gardens of bliss; eternal there. Allah's promise is true.

### v. 10-11: Cosmological signs (proto-Block-C)
Heavens raised without visible pillars; mountains placed in the earth as anchors; varieties of creatures dispersed. Water sent down — vegetation grows. *hādhā khalqu llāhi fa-arūnī mādhā khalaqa al-ladhīna min dūnih* — "This is the creation of Allah; show me what those-other-than-Him have created." Pivot-question: nothing comes from anything else. The wrong-doers are in manifest error.

This v.10-11 block is a **micro-anticipation** of the larger Block-C cosmic-signs section (vv.20-30); al-Biqāʿī notes the structural parallel.

## 3. Block B (vv. 12-19) — THE LUQMĀN PERICOPE

This is the surah's structural core and the locus of its eponymous identity.

### v. 12: Frame — Allah granted Luqmān al-ḥikma
*wa-laqad ātaynā Luqmāna al-ḥikmata an aškur li-llāhi — wa-man yaškur fa-innamā yaškuru li-nafsih — wa-man kafara fa-inna llāha ghaniyyun ḥamīd*

"And We granted Luqmān al-ḥikma — that he be thankful to Allah; and whoever is thankful is thankful for himself; and whoever is ungrateful — Allah is rich, praiseworthy."

The verse establishes 4 things:
1. **Allah is the granter of ḥikma** (passive *ātaynā* — divine bestowal).
2. **Luqmān is named** without prophetic-title (NOT *nabiyyan* nor *rasūlan*).
3. **The content of the ḥikma** is shukr (thankfulness) toward Allah.
4. **Theological reflexivity** — gratitude benefits the grateful, not the recipient of gratitude.

### v. 13: Luqmān to son (1st waṣiyya) — DO NOT shirk
*wa-iḏ qāla Luqmānu li-ibnihi wa-huwa yaʿiẓuhu yā bunayya lā tushrik bi-llāh — inna al-shirka la-ẓulmun ʿaẓīm*

"And [recall] when Luqmān said to his son, while admonishing him: 'O my little son, do not associate any partner with Allah — verily, shirk is a tremendous wrong (*ẓulm ʿaẓīm*).'"

This verse is the source of one of the Quran's most-cited classical-tafsir hadith chains. al-Bukhārī records 5 hadith (ids 3222, 3287, 4570, 6662, 6680) all narrating the same incident: when Q 6:82 (*al-ladhīna āmanū wa-lam yalbisū īmānahum bi-ẓulm*) was revealed, the Companions feared "who among us has not wronged himself?", and the Prophet replied that *ẓulm* in Q 6:82 means **shirk specifically**, citing Q 31:13 as the proof-text. This is one of the corpus's clearest **intra-Quranic** semantic-clarifications: Q 31:13 disambiguates the sense of *ẓulm* in Q 6:82.

The hadith chain (Bukhārī 3222 / Muslim 234 / Tirmidhī 3151 — all 3 Sound chains via al-Aʿmash → Ibrāhīm al-Nakhaʿī → ʿAlqama → ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd):

> "Lammā nazalat *al-ladhīna āmanū wa-lam yalbisū īmānahum bi-ẓulm*, the Companions said: 'O Messenger of Allah, who among us has not wronged himself?' He said: 'It is not as you suppose. Have you not heard the saying of Luqmān to his son: *yā bunayya lā tushrik bi-llāhi inna al-shirka la-ẓulmun ʿaẓīm*?'"

This is THE definitive **prophetic exegesis of one Quranic verse by another** — and the bridge is delivered through a non-prophet's quoted speech. See `04-hadith-corpus.md` and `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for verification details.

### v. 14: Parenthetical-1 — parental duty
*wa-waṣṣaynā al-insāna bi-wālidayhi — ḥamalathu ummuhu wahnan ʿalā wahnin wa-fiṣāluhu fī ʿāmayni an aškur lī wa-li-wālidayka — ilayya al-maṣīr*

"We have charged the human-being with [duty toward] his two parents — his mother bore him in weakness upon weakness, and his weaning is in two years; that you be thankful to Me and to your parents — to Me is the destination."

This verse is a **divine-voice interpolation** between Luqmān's quoted words. The voice shifts from Luqmān's *yā bunayya* (v.13) to divine *waṣṣaynā* (v.14, "We have charged"). al-Suyūṭī classifies this as *iltifāt* (voice-shift), specifically the classical *iltifāt-min-ḥikāya-ilā-takallum* (shift from cited-speech to first-person divine speech). al-Zarkashī catalogs this exact verse as a textbook example in *al-Burhān*, nawʿ 53.

The parenthetical performs a critical theological role: it **places parental-duty under divine charge, NOT under Luqmān's authority**. Luqmān's own authority is paternal-didactic; the duty *to* parents is divine-revelational. The parenthetical thus establishes a hierarchical structure: divine-charge → Luqmān's-counsel → son's-action.

The "two years" weaning specification is doctrinal source-material classical jurists use; cf. Q 2:233 *wa-l-wālidātu yurḍiʿna awlādahunna ḥawlayni kāmilayn* (mothers nurse their children for two complete years). Q 31:14 and Q 2:233 are an intra-Quranic mutashābih pair (verse-twin network H-NEW-66; see `07-cross-references.md`).

### v. 15: Parenthetical-2 — exception clause
*wa-in jāhadāka ʿalā an tushrika bī mā laysa laka bihi ʿilmun fa-lā tuṭiʿhumā — wa-ṣāḥibhumā fī al-dunyā maʿrūfan — wa-ttabiʿ sabīla man anāba ilayya — thumma ilayya marjiʿukum fa-unabbiʾukum bi-mā kuntum taʿmalūn*

"And if they strive against you to make you associate-partners-with Me what you have no knowledge of, then DO NOT obey them — but accompany them in this world with kindness; and follow the path of him who turns to Me; then to Me is your return — and I shall inform you of what you used-to-do."

The exception clause: parental duty is suspended only when parents command shirk, but even then *companionship-with-kindness* (*muʿāshara bi-l-maʿrūf*) is preserved. This is the classical jurist source-text for the doctrine that obedience is owed to parents in all permissible matters but disobedience is owed when parents command sin.

### v. 16: 2nd waṣiyya (yā bunayya, mustard-seed)
*yā bunayya innahā in taku mithqāla ḥabbatin min khardalin fa-takun fī ṣakhratin aw fī al-samāwāti aw fī al-arḍi yaʾti bi-hā llāh — inna llāha laṭīfun khabīr*

"O my little son, [verily] if it should be the weight of a mustard-seed and lie in a rock or in the heavens or in the earth — Allah will bring it forth. Verily, Allah is subtle (laṭīf) and aware (khabīr)."

The waṣiyya: the moral consequence-of-action principle — no act is too small for divine reckoning. The verse's *laṭīf-khabīr* divine-name pair is one of the corpus's classic divine-pair attestations (al-Rāzī catalogs Q 31:16 as canonical for this pairing).

### v. 17: 3rd waṣiyya (yā bunayya, ṣalāh + maʿrūf + munkar + ṣabr)
*yā bunayya aqim al-ṣalāta wa-ʾmur bi-l-maʿrūfi wa-nha ʿan al-munkari wa-ṣbir ʿalā mā aṣābaka — inna ḏālika min ʿazm al-umūr*

"O my little son, establish ṣalāh, command good and forbid evil, and endure with patience whatever befalls you — verily that is from the resolution of [important] matters."

Three pillars-of-action condensed: *iqāmat al-ṣalāh + amr bi-l-maʿrūf wa-nahy ʿan al-munkar + ṣabr*. This v.17 is the locus classicus for the classical *amr bi-l-maʿrūf* doctrine (al-Ghazālī's *Iḥyāʾ*, Book 19; Ibn Taymiyya's *al-Ḥisba*); the imperative is delivered in the **non-prophet's voice**, which classical jurists treat as a striking rhetorical-validation: even Luqmān, a non-prophet, communicates the universal duty.

### v. 18: 4th waṣiyya (negative-form: arrogance prohibitions)
*wa-lā tuṣaʿʿir khaddaka li-l-nāsi wa-lā tamši fī al-arḍi maraḥan — inna llāha lā yuḥibbu kulla mukhtālin fakhūr*

"Do not turn the cheek away in [contempt of] people, and do not walk in the earth with arrogance (*maraḥan*) — verily Allah does not love every arrogant boaster."

The verb *tuṣaʿʿir* (rare, from root ṣ-ʿ-r — to turn the head sideways in scorn, an Arabian-camel-disease borrowed metaphorically) is one of Q 31's distinctive lexemes. The pair *mukhtāl-fakhūr* (proud-boaster) is also rare: Q 4:36 has the same dual at the social-relations command (Q 4:36 *wa-lā yujibbu llāha man kāna mukhtālan fakhūrā* — "and Allah does not love whoever is a proud boaster"). The Q 31:18 ↔ Q 4:36 pair is an intra-Quranic phrase-twin (H-NEW-66 cohort).

### v. 19: 5th-waṣiyya (positive-form: modesty in walking + speaking)
*wa-qṣid fī mašyika wa-ghḍuḍ min ṣawtika — inna ankara al-aṣwāti la-ṣawtu al-ḥamīr*

"And walk modestly (*waqṣid*), and lower [some] of your voice (*waghḍuḍ min ṣawtika*) — verily the most-disagreeable of voices is the donkey's voice."

The closing image (*ṣawt al-ḥamīr* — the donkey's voice) is one of the Quran's most distinctive zoological metaphors. al-Zamakhsharī (*Kashshāf*) and al-Rāzī both note this as a paradigmatic *istiʿāra-tashbīh* (metaphorical simile) closure: the donkey's bray represents the maximum of harsh-voice; Luqmān's didactic conclusion is that human speech should be its opposite. Classical *adab* literature (al-Ghazālī, Ibn al-Jawzī) cites Q 31:19 as the source-text for *ḥusn al-ṣawt* (good voice / vocal-modesty) ethics.

The 8 waṣāyā of Luqmān at vv.13-19 thus map (classical reading):

1. v.13: theological foundation — DO NOT shirk
2. v.14 (parenthetical): parental-duty under divine charge
3. v.15 (parenthetical): exception to parental obedience
4. v.16: theology of meticulous accountability
5. v.17: ṣalāh + amr/nahy + ṣabr (action-ethic)
6. v.18: prohibition of arrogance (negative)
7. v.19: modesty in walk and speech (positive)

The pericope is dispositional-ethical condensation: monotheism → ritual-ethic → social-ethic → speech-ethic.

## 4. Block C (vv. 20-30) — Cosmic-signs + rebuke of obstinacy

### v. 20: Cosmic-grant rhetorical opening
"Have you not seen that Allah has subjected to you what is in the heavens and what is in the earth, and made complete to you His blessings, both apparent and hidden? Yet among people are some who dispute about Allah without knowledge or guidance or an illuminating Book."

The triplet *ʿilmin / hudan / kitābin munīr* is parallel-structured (3-fold negation of basis-for-disputation).

### v. 21-22: The fathers-followed argument
"And when it is said to them: 'Follow what Allah has revealed,' they say: 'Rather, we follow what we found our fathers upon.' What — even though Satan was calling them to the punishment of the Blaze?"

This is the *vājidhīn* parental-imitation argument — paralleled at Q 2:170, Q 5:104, Q 7:28, Q 26:74, Q 43:22. Q 31:21 is one of 6 corpus instances; classical ḥadīth Q 5:104-related.

### v. 22: Submission as the firmest handhold
"And whoever submits his face to Allah — being a doer-of-good — has grasped the firmest handhold (*al-ʿurwa al-wuthqā*) — and to Allah is the conclusion of [all] matters."

The phrase *al-ʿurwa al-wuthqā* (the firmest handhold) is a corpus-rare token: Q 2:256 has the EXACT same phrase (*man yakfur bi-l-ṭāghūti wa-yuʾmin bi-llāhi fa-qad istamsaka bi-l-ʿurwati al-wuthqā*). Q 31:22 + Q 2:256 are the only 2 corpus-instances; this is a **2-instance verse-twin** (H-NEW-66 cohort).

### v. 23-24: Disbelief response and short-term enjoyment
"And whoever disbelieves — let not his disbelief grieve you. To Us is their return; then We shall inform them what they did. Verily Allah is aware of what is in the breasts. We give them enjoyment for a little; then We shall force them to harsh punishment."

### v. 25-26: Theological declaration
"If you ask them: 'Who created the heavens and the earth?' — they will say: 'Allah.' Say: 'Praise be to Allah' — but most of them do not know. To Allah belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth — verily Allah is the Self-Sufficient, the Praiseworthy."

The rhetorical question + assumed answer is a recurring corpus-pattern (cf. Q 29:61, Q 39:38, Q 43:9). It establishes the dialectical position: even the polytheists concede divine creatorship — what they reject is divine *mawlā* (sovereignty).

### v. 27: The trees-as-pens / ink-of-the-sea verse
*wa-law anna mā fī al-arḍi min šajaratin aqlāmun wa-l-baḥru yamudduhu min baʿdihi sabʿatu abḥurin mā nafidat kalimātu llāhi — inna llāha ʿazīzun ḥakīm*

"If all the trees on earth were pens, and the sea, with seven seas after it (yet to be added) [were ink], the words of Allah would not be exhausted — verily Allah is mighty, wise."

This verse is one of the corpus's foundational *iʿjāz al-tashbīh* verses. Its twin (using the same metaphor) is Q 18:109:
*qul law kāna al-baḥru midādan li-kalimāti rabbī la-nafida al-baḥru qabla an tanfada kalimātu rabbī wa-law jiʾnā bi-mithlihi madadā*

Q 18:109 is the 2-element version (sea-as-ink); Q 31:27 is the 4-element expansion (trees-as-pens + sea + 7 additional seas). The 2 verses form an **expansion-pair** in the verse-twin network. al-Bāqillānī (*Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*) cites the Q 31:27 / Q 18:109 pair as evidence of the Quran's stylistic-iʿjāz: the same metaphor delivered in different rhetorical-amplifications.

The closing divine-name pair *ʿazīz-ḥakīm* — recurring across the corpus (~ 47 instances; Q 31 itself has it 3× at vv.9, 27, 30) — is one of the canonical *asmāʾ mutazāwijah* (paired-names) cataloged at H-NEW-140 (CONFIRMED at 13.87× above Poisson-independence; classical al-Rāzī / al-Zamakhsharī pair-taxonomy).

### v. 28: Easy creation-and-resurrection
"Your creation and resurrection are not but as the [creation-and-resurrection of] one self — verily Allah is hearing, seeing."

### v. 29: Day/night cosmological cycle
"Have you not seen that Allah merges night into day and merges day into night, and has subjected the sun and moon, each running for a stated term, and that Allah is aware of what you do?"

The *yulijju al-layla fī al-nahār wa-yulijju al-nahāra fī al-layl* construction is a corpus-recurrent palindrome-template (cf. H-NEW finding on cosmic-inversion 5-word palindromes; 13 instances at z=+6.84 — see MASTER-LEDGER §3 #14). Q 31:29's *yulijju al-X* construction is one of the 13 cosmic-inversion instances.

### v. 30: Theological summary
"That [Allah] is the Truth (*al-Ḥaqq*); and what they invoke other than Him is the falsehood; and that Allah is the most-high, the most-great."

## 5. Block D (vv. 31-34) — Mortality + the five mafātīḥ al-ghayb

### v. 31: Ships as signs
"Have you not seen that ships sail in the sea by the grace of Allah — that He may show you of His signs? In that are signs for every patient and grateful one."

### v. 32: The wave/affliction figure
"And when waves cover them like canopies, they call upon Allah, sincere to Him in religion. But when He delivers them to the land, some are moderate; and none denies Our signs except every faithless ingrate."

The classical *fitra* doctrine source: under extreme distress (waves-like-canopies), even the disbelievers turn to Allah. The verse parallels Q 10:22, Q 17:67, Q 29:65 — classical mutashābih cluster.

### v. 33: Final mortality address
*yā ayyuhā al-nāsu ittaqū rabbakum wa-khshaw yawman lā yajzī wālidun ʿan waladih wa-lā mawlūdun huwa jāzin ʿan wālidihi šayʾā — inna waʿda llāhi ḥaqq — fa-lā taghurranakum al-ḥayātu al-dunyā wa-lā yaghurranakum bi-llāhi al-gharūr*

"O mankind, fear your Lord and dread a Day on which no parent will avail his child, nor any child his parent — verily the promise of Allah is true; so let not the worldly life deceive you, and let not the deceiver deceive you concerning Allah."

The address *yā ayyuhā al-nās* (O mankind) marks one of the corpus's general human addresses (~ 18 instances). The structural detail: the verse uses *jazā ʿan* (avail FOR) twice, in chiasm: parent-for-child / child-for-parent — eschatological-ethic of individual responsibility. The ending *al-gharūr* (the deceiver, classically interpreted as Iblīs) is an Arabic indeterminate-form-of-emphasis (literally "the great deceiver").

### v. 34: THE FIVE MAFĀTĪḤ AL-GHAYB (the five keys of the unseen)
*inna llāha ʿindahu ʿilmu al-sāʿati wa-yunazzilu al-ghaytha wa-yaʿlamu mā fī al-arḥām wa-mā tadrī nafsun māḏā taksibu ghadan wa-mā tadrī nafsun bi-ayyi arḍin tamūt — inna llāha ʿalīmun khabīr*

"Verily Allah — with Him is the knowledge of the Hour; and He sends down the rain; and He knows what is in the wombs; and no soul knows what it shall earn tomorrow; and no soul knows in what land it shall die. Verily Allah is knowing, aware."

The verse is the corpus's locus classicus for the **five keys of the unseen** doctrine (mafātīḥ al-ghayb):

1. **ʿilm al-sāʿa** — knowledge of the Hour (eschatological time)
2. **inzāl al-ghayth** — sending down of rain (meteorological knowledge)
3. **mā fī al-arḥām** — what is in the wombs (gestational knowledge)
4. **mā taksibu ghadan** — what one will earn tomorrow (ethical-personal future)
5. **bi-ayyi arḍin tamūt** — in what land one will die (geographic-personal end)

The fivefold structure has classical-theological weight: al-Bukhārī (*Tawḥīd* 4778) records the ḥadīth of Jibrīl asking the Prophet about the Hour — the Prophet's response cites Q 31:34 directly. al-Rāzī's massive 32-volume tafsir is titled *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb* in honor of this verse. al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr all treat v.34 as the foundational verse for the limits-of-knowledge doctrine.

The closing divine-name pair *ʿalīm-khabīr* (knowing-aware) is a canonical paired-name (al-Rāzī's *asmāʾ mutazāwijah* tradition; H-NEW-140 cohort).

## 7. Voice-register analysis

Q 31's voice-register stratification (per al-Suyūṭī classical iltifāt taxonomy):

| Block | Primary voice | Secondary voice |
|:--|:--|:--|
| A (1-11) | Divine narrator | Implied audience-warning |
| B-frame (12) | Divine narrator | Theological-reflexive ("whoever is thankful…") |
| **B-quoted (13)** | **Quoted Luqmān** | (paternal direct address) |
| **B-parenthetical (14-15)** | **Divine narrator (interpolation)** | Reverts to general human address |
| **B-quoted (16-19)** | **Quoted Luqmān** | (paternal direct address) |
| C (20-26) | Divine + rhetorical-question | Implied polemicist |
| C (27-30) | Divine declarative + tashbīh | — |
| D (31-32) | Divine-narrator + simile | Implicit moral subject |
| D (33-34) | Divine address (yā ayyuhā al-nās) | Eschatological-warning |

The voice-register is **5-strata**, with the most-distinctive feature being the embedded-quotation of Luqmān at vv.13, 16-19 (interrupted by divine-voice parenthetical at 14-15). This nested-voice structure is studied in `06-novel-findings.md` Q031-F-02 as a candidate for empirical iltifāt density measurement.

## 8. The Luqmān-pericope as didactic-genre singleton

Q 31 is the ONLY corpus surah where a **non-prophet human** delivers a sustained moral-discourse pericope (8 verses). The only structural parallels:

| Surah | Speaker | Verses | Genre |
|:--|:--|:-:|:--|
| Q 12 | Yaʿqūb to Yūsuf | 5, 67, 87 | scattered patriarchal advice |
| Q 12 | Yūsuf in dialogue | various | prophet-quoted speech |
| Q 18 | al-Khaḍir to Mūsā | vv. 71-82 | wisdom-exposition (Khaḍir is "abdan min ʿibādinā", non-named-prophet) |
| Q 26 | Various prophets | scattered | prophet-quoted speech |
| Q 31 | **Luqmān to son** | **13, 16-19** | **non-prophet moral discourse, sustained 8-verse block** |
| Q 37 | Ibrāhīm to Ismāʿīl | v.102 | prophet-quoted (sacrifice-vision) |

The al-Khaḍir pericope (Q 18:71-82) is the closest parallel — also a sustained discourse from a non-prophet-named figure. But the al-Khaḍir pericope is **embedded in a prophet's (Mūsā's) narrative** as a wisdom-instruction. Q 31's Luqmān pericope is **freestanding** — Luqmān speaks to his son without any prophet-intermediary in the surah. This is the corpus-singleton property of Q 31.
