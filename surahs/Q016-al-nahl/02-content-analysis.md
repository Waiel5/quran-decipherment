# Q 16 al-Naḥl — Content Analysis

128 verses. Late-Meccan declarative-monorhyme rhetoric (-ūn/-īn). Three structurally-distinctive features:

1. **Niʿmah-catalog saturation** (Q016-F-01).
2. **Bee-passage with corpus-hapax lemma `n~aHol`** (Q016-F-02).
3. **Abraham-as-imam-of-nations 4-verse coda** (Q016-F-04).

## 2. Niʿmah-catalog detailed inventory (Q016-F-01 target)

### Catalog I (vv. 3–18)

- v. 3: created heavens/earth in truth
- v. 5: **anʿām** (cattle) created — warmth, food, beauty
- v. 6: cattle as adornment when bringing in/out
- v. 7: cattle bear loads to distant lands
- v. 8: horses, mules, donkeys for riding + adornment
- v. 9: (digression: divine guidance metaphor)
- v. 10: **anzala min al-samāʾi māʾan** (rain) — drink + plants for grazing
- v. 11: **yunbitu lakum** (causes-grow) crops, olives, palms, grapes, all fruits
- v. 12: subjugated (sakhkhara) day, night, sun, moon, stars
- v. 13: subjugated colors of all that is created on earth
- v. 14: subjugated **al-baḥr** (the sea) — fresh meat + ornaments + ships
- v. 15: **al-jibāl** (mountains) as anchors so earth doesn't shake
- v. 16: landmarks; stars for guidance
- v. 17: rhetorical: is the Creator like the non-creator?
- v. 18: niʿmah-of-Allah uncountable

### Catalog II (vv. 65–69)

- v. 65: rain → revives dead earth
- v. 66: **al-anʿām** lesson — milk between dung and blood, pure for drinkers
- v. 67: **al-thamarāt** of palms and grapes, intoxicants and good provision
- v. 68: **al-naḥl** revelation — make habitations in mountains, trees, trellises
- v. 69: eat from all fruits, follow ways of Lord; honey of varying colors with **shifāʾ** for people

### Catalog III (vv. 77–83)

- v. 80: homes from animal-skins; wool/fur/hair as furnishings
- v. 81: shade from what He created; mountain-shelters; clothing protecting from heat + war
- v. 82–83: response of deniers; recognition + denial of niʿmah

## 3. Bee passage Q 16:68–69 — exegetical anatomy

### Arabic + literal gloss

```
وَأَوْحَىٰ رَبُّكَ إِلَى ٱلنَّحْلِ
"And your Lord revealed to the bee:"

أَنِ ٱتَّخِذِي مِنَ ٱلْجِبَالِ بُيُوتًا وَمِنَ ٱلشَّجَرِ وَمِمَّا يَعْرِشُونَ
"Take to yourself, from the mountains, dwellings — and from the trees, and from what they trellis."

ثُمَّ كُلِي مِن كُلِّ ٱلثَّمَرَاتِ فَٱسْلُكِي سُبُلَ رَبِّكِ ذُلُلًا ۚ
"Then eat from every fruit; tread the ways of your Lord, made-easy."

يَخْرُجُ مِن بُطُونِهَا شَرَابٌ مُّخْتَلِفٌ أَلْوَانُهُ فِيهِ شِفَاءٌ لِلنَّاسِ ۗ
"There comes forth from their bellies a drink of varying colors; in it is healing for people."

إِنَّ فِي ذَٰلِكَ لَآيَةً لِّقَوْمٍ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ
"Truly in that is a sign for people who reflect."
```

### Lexical features

- **`n~aHol`** (the bee) — corpus-hapax LEMMA; only here (Q 4:4 *niḥla* = "free gift" is a separate QAC lemma).
- **`*ulul`** "submissive/easy-paths" — corpus-hapax LEMMA at Q 16:69.
- **`ʿarsh` as verb-form** (yaʿrishūn) "trellis" — rare meaning shift; the root is otherwise dominantly used for the divine ʿarsh (throne).
- **`shrb` shifāʾ** "drink-as-healing" — semantically tied to Bukhari ḥadīth #5466 *al-shifāʾ fī thalātha*.

### Classical exegesis

- **al-Ṭabarī**: *waḥy* here = *ilhām* (inspiration), not prophetic wahy. God "alhama" the bee with the building-instinct.
- **al-Qurṭubī**: 3 masāʾil — (1) wahy as ilhām is divinely-imparted instinct; (2) the bee is FEMININE (use of *ittakhidhī*), reflecting that the worker bees are female; (3) the meaning of *yaʿrishūn* = "human-built trellises (kurūm)".
- **Ibn Kathīr**: emphasizes the geometric perfection of the hexagonal cell (*tasdīsuhā wa-raṣṣuhā*) — a pre-modern recognition of bee-geometry.
- **al-Rāzī**: extensive treatment as cosmological *iʿjāz* — the bee's revelation is one of the strongest natural-theology arguments in the Quran.
- **al-Baghawī**: literal *ilhām* + ABU IBN ZAYD attribution that *yaʿrishūn* refers to grape-trellises (*kurūm*).

## 4. Q 16:90 — the *jumʿah* verse (ʿadl wa-iḥsān)

```
إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَأْمُرُ بِٱلْعَدْلِ وَٱلْإِحْسَٰنِ وَإِيتَآئِ ذِى ٱلْقُرْبَىٰ
وَيَنْهَىٰ عَنِ ٱلْفَحْشَآءِ وَٱلْمُنكَرِ وَٱلْبَغْىِ ۚ يَعِظُكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَذَكَّرُونَ
```

Triple-positive (justice, beneficence, kin-giving) + triple-negative (lewdness, evil, oppression). Universally regarded in classical jurisprudence as the most-comprehensive ethical injunction in the Quran. ʿUthmān ibn Maẓʿūn famously identifies it as the verse that "settled Islam in his heart" in al-Bayhaqī's *Sunan al-kubrā* (not in 9-books — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`). al-Ṭabarī: this verse comprises *jamīʿ al-khayr wa-tark jamīʿ al-sharr*.

The classical claim that it is the canonical Friday-khuṭba closing verse (per al-Shāfiʿī's recommendation, *al-Umm*) is asbāb-tradition-attested but NOT a sahih ḥadīth in the canonical 9-books on Q 16:90 specifically (Bukhārī 4436 is on Q 7:199, NOT Q 16:90 — see `04-hadith-corpus.md` audit).

## 5. Abraham coda Q 16:120–123

```
v. 120: إن إبراهيم كان أمة قانتا لله حنيفا ولم يك من المشركين
        "Truly Abraham was a UMMA — devoutly-obedient to Allah, ḥanīf, never of the polytheists."
v. 121: شاكرا لأنعمه ۚ اجتباه وهداه إلى صراط مستقيم
        "Grateful for His niʿam — He chose him and guided him to a straight path."
v. 122: وآتيناه في الدنيا حسنة ۖ وإنه في الآخرة لمن الصالحين
        "We gave him in this world a good [reward], and in the next he is among the righteous."
v. 123: ثم أوحينا إليك أن اتبع ملة إبراهيم حنيفا ۖ وما كان من المشركين
        "Then We revealed to you: follow the religion of Abraham, ḥanīf, never of the polytheists."
```

### Notable structural features

- **`umma`** here = "an-individual-equivalent-to-a-nation" (per al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī) — not the standard collective sense. This is a corpus-rare semantic.
- **`ḥanīf`** appears twice (vv. 120, 123), framing the coda.
- **`shākiran li-anʿumihi`** (v. 121) — the only attestation of the plural *anʿumihi* (his blessings) in the entire Quran. Direct lexical link to the *Sūrat al-Niʿam* alt-name.
- **`ittabiʿ millat ibrāhīm ḥanīfan`** (v. 123) — same imperative formula as Q 3:95 and Q 4:125 (cross-references).

## 6. Q 16:106–111 — apostasy under coercion (the ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir asbāb)

The verse Q 16:106 — *man kafara billāhi min baʿdi īmānihi illā man ukriha wa-qalbuhu muṭmaʾinnun bi-l-īmān* — is the locus classicus for *taqiyya* permission under coercion. Asbāb al-nuzūl tradition (al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-nuzūl*; al-Ṭabarī's tafsīr) attributes the verse to ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir's renunciation under torture in Mecca. The verse permits coerced verbal denial as long as the heart remains in faith.

This verse is one of the strongest empirically-late-Meccan markers: it presupposes both an organized Mecca-persecution context AND a developed *īmān-qalb* doctrine. Its presence in Q 16 supports the late-Meccan (rev-rank 70) classification.

## 7. Final 5 verses (124–128) — Sabbath + daʿwa method + patience-closing

- v. 124: Sabbath was made for those who differed about it.
- v. 125: **udʿu ilā sabīli rabbika bi-l-ḥikmati wa-l-mawʿiẓati al-ḥasanati wa-jādilhum bi-llatī hiya aḥsan** — "Call to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good exhortation, and dispute with them in the best way." This is the **canonical daʿwa-method verse**, cited universally in classical *uṣūl al-daʿwa* literature.
- v. 126: retaliation principle (matching offense with equal punishment, but patience is better).
- v. 127: imperative *iṣbir wa-mā ṣabruka illā billāh*.
- v. 128: closing — *inna llāha maʿa lladhīna ttaqaw wa-lladhīna hum muḥsinūn*. Note: the *muḥsinūn* closing rhymes back to v. 90's *iḥsān* — possible ring-composition signal.
