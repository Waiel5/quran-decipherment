# Q 20 Ṭā-Hā — Tafsīr Survey

Five classical commentaries surveyed from the on-disk corpus. Source files cited per passage. The
central exegetical question is the meaning of the opener طه (v 1) — vocative *yā rajul*, disjoined
letters, ḥisāb-al-jummal, or imperative *ṭaʾ* — on which the tradition gives a wide spectrum, and the
recitation-effect *sabab* (the ʿUmar-conversion narrative). I have read the Arabic of all five for Q 20:1.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (on Q 20:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/20/1.json`.

al-Ṭabarī reports that "the people of taʾwīl differed over طه" (*ikhtalafa ahl al-taʾwīl fī taʾwīl
qawlihi ṭāhā*). His leading and most-isnāded position is the **vocative *yā rajul*** ("O man"):

- via **ʿIkrima ← Ibn ʿAbbās**: "طه: in Nabaṭī (*bi-l-nabaṭiyya*), [means] *yā rajul* (O man)."
- via **Saʿīd b. Jubayr** (Ibn Jurayj ← ʿAbd Allāh/Yaʿlā b. Muslim): "طه: *yā rajul* in Syriac
  (*bi-l-suryāniyya*)."
- via **al-Ḍaḥḥāk**, **Qatāda** ("*yā rajul*, and it is Syriac"), **ʿIkrima** again ("He addressed him
  in Nabaṭī … *yā insān*"), and **Mujāhid**.

He glosses the contextual sense: the Quraysh said "this man has become wretched (*shaqiya*) on account of
his Lord," so Allāh revealed *ṭāhā* — "*yā rajul* — We did not send the Qurʾān down on you that you should
suffer (*li-tashqā*)." This is the **classical anchor for Q020-F-02** (the Ibn ʿAbbās vocative test).

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf* (on Q 20:1-4)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` lines 37135-37200.

al-Zamakhsharī's surah-header: "**Meccan [except vv 130 and 131 which are Medinan], and it is 135 verses
[revealed after Maryam]**" (*makkiyya … wa-hiya 135 āya … nazalat baʿda Maryam*) — an exact on-disk
confirmation of the 135-verse count (`data/hafs-verse-counts.tsv`) and the post-Maryam chronology
(`data/revelation-order.csv` #45, after Maryam #44).

On طه he gives: (1) the qirāʾāt (Abū ʿAmr emphasizes the ṭāʾ; Ibn Kathīr/Ibn ʿĀmir on the original, the
rest with imāla); (2) **al-Ḥasan's imperative reading *ṭaʾ*** — the Prophet used to pray standing on one
foot in his tahajjud, so he was commanded "tread (*ṭaʾ*) the ground with both feet"; the original is *ṭaʾ*,
the hamza turned to hāʾ; (3) **the disjoined-letter reading** ("it suffices to take the two letter-names,
which by their utterance denote the two named [letters]"); (4) **the *yā rajul* reading** ("Allāh knows
best the soundness of what is said: that *ṭāhā* in the dialect of ʿakk means *yā rajul* … perhaps ʿakk
altered *yā hādhā*, turning the yāʾ to ṭāʾ"). He then says the **three opening-letter doctrines I set out
at the head of *al-Kashshāf* are the ones the discerning rely on** — his epistemic-humility marker. On
*li-tashqā* he reads *shaqāʾ* as "toil/exhaustion" (the Prophet prayed until his feet swelled; Jibrīl said
"spare yourself, your soul has a right over you") — i.e. "We did not send it down to exhaust you."

## 3. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (on Q 20:1-2)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` lines 165224-165320.

al-Rāzī treats طه under **two masāʾil**. **Masʾala 1** = the qirāʾāt (with al-Zajjāj's phonological note on
imāla). **Masʾala 2** = "the mufassirūn have two views: that it is among the disjoined letters (*ḥurūf
al-tahajjī*), or that it is a meaningful word." Under the disjoined-letter view he lists **seven
sub-glosses people "added here"** — including:

- al-Thaʿlabī: ṭāʾ = the tree Ṭūbā, hāʾ = the Hāwiya [hellfire] — "as if He swore by the Garden and the
  Fire";
- (from Jaʿfar al-Ṣādiq) ṭāʾ = the purity (*ṭahāra*) of the People of the House, hāʾ = their guidance;
- **the ḥisāb-al-jummal gloss**: "**the ṭāʾ is nine in the reckoning (*fī al-ḥisāb*) and the hāʾ is five,
  making fourteen, and its meaning is 'O full moon (*yā ayyuhā al-badr*)'**."

He then delivers the **decisive epistemic-humility verdict**: "*you have already learned that the likes of
these sayings ought not to be relied upon (lā yajibu an yuʿtamada ʿalayhā)*." This is a direct classical
**rejection of the ḥisāb-al-jummal reading of طه** — audited in `05-classical-claims-audit.md`. Under the
"meaningful word" view he gives the *yā rajul* reading (Ibn ʿAbbās, al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, Saʿīd b. Jubayr,
Qatāda, ʿIkrima, al-Kalbī — each naming a different dialect: Nabaṭī, Syriac, ḥabashī, ʿakk) and al-Ḥasan's
imperative *ṭaʾ*. On *li-tashqā* (masāʾil) he cites al-Kashshāf on the grammar and gives Muqātil's *sabab*
(Abū Jahl, al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra, al-Naḍr b. al-Ḥārith taunting the Prophet).

## 4. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (on Q 20:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/20/1.json`.

al-Qurṭubī's header: "**Sūrat Ṭā-Hā — Meccan by all accounts (*makkiyya fī qawl al-jamīʿ*), revealed
before the conversion of ʿUmar (*nazalat qabla islām ʿUmar*).**" He then narrates the **ʿUmar-conversion
sabab** at length in two chains:

- **al-Dāraquṭnī in his Sunan ← Anas b. Mālik**: ʿUmar set out girded with his sword; was told his
  brother-in-law had "ṣabaʾa" [converted]; came upon them while a Muhājir named **Khabbāb** was teaching
  them, reciting **Ṭā-Hā**; ʿUmar's sister told him "you are impure (*rijs*), none but the purified may
  touch it — go wash"; ʿUmar washed, took the sheet, and read Ṭā-Hā.
- **Ibn Isḥāq (fuller version)**: ʿUmar set out to kill the Prophet; Nuʿaym b. ʿAbd Allāh redirected him
  to his sister Fāṭima bint al-Khaṭṭāb and her husband Saʿīd b. Zayd; **Khabbāb b. al-Aratt** was with them
  with a sheet bearing **Ṭā-Hā**; on hearing ʿUmar he hid; Fāṭima put the sheet under her thigh; ʿUmar
  heard the recitation (*al-haynama*), confronted them, and after a struggle read the sheet and was
  arrested by the words.

This is the **classical anchor for Q020-F-05** (the verse-location signature of the ʿUmar-conversion
narrative). al-Qurṭubī treats v 1 under multiple *masāʾil* surveying the same gloss-spectrum as al-Rāzī.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (on Q 20:1-3)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/20/1.json`; Arabic at
`raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`.

Ibn Kathīr's section-title: "The Qurʾān is a Reminder and a Revelation from Allah." On طه: "We have already
discussed the separated letters at the beginning of Sūrat al-Baqara, so there is no need to repeat its
discussion here" — i.e. he **declines to gloss طه as a vocative**, treating it as a member of the
muqaṭṭaʿāt whose discussion belongs to the general *fawātiḥ* treatment (the cautious, disjoined-letters
position). On *li-tashqā* he cites **Juwaybir ← al-Ḍaḥḥāk**: the Quraysh said "this Qurʾān was revealed to
Muḥammad only to cause him distress," so Allāh revealed *ṭāhā mā anzalnā ʿalayka al-Qurʾāna li-tashqā* —
"the matter is not as the people of falsehood claim." He stresses that knowledge is an abundance of good
(citing the Ṣaḥīḥayn ḥadīth of Ibn Masʿūd on whom Allāh wills good).

## 6. Points of agreement / disagreement

| Question | al-Ṭabarī | al-Zamakhsharī | al-Rāzī | al-Qurṭubī | Ibn Kathīr |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| طه meaning | **vocative *yā rajul*** (primary, many isnāds) | *yā rajul* (ʿakk) + imperative *ṭaʾ* + disjoined-letters | both views; **rejects ḥisāb-al-jummal** | gloss-spectrum (masāʾil) | **disjoined letters** (defers to Baqara) |
| Verse count / type | Meccan | **Meccan, 135 verses** (vv 130-131 Medinan) | Meccan | **Meccan by consensus** | Meccan |
| *li-tashqā* | the Quraysh taunt | "toil/exhaustion" (tahajjud) | al-Kashshāf grammar + Muqātil sabab | masāʾil | al-Ḍaḥḥāk: refuting the Quraysh |
| ʿUmar-conversion sabab | — | — | — | **full (Dāraquṭnī + Ibn Isḥāq)** | (treats elsewhere) |

The **gloss-spectrum for طه is the defining exegetical feature**: the most isnād-supported reading is the
vocative *yā rajul* (al-Ṭabarī, leading), but the most *cautious* readers (Ibn Kathīr; al-Zamakhsharī and
al-Rāzī both flagging epistemic humility) treat it as a disjoined-letter opener. The numerological
ḥisāb-al-jummal "O full moon" gloss is reported by al-Rāzī **only to reject it** — directly relevant to the
project's ʿilm-al-ḥarf audit. The empirically-relevant claims promoted to tests are: the vocative reading
(Q020-F-02, NULL on 2sg-density) and the ʿUmar-conversion verse-location (Q020-F-05, CONFIRMED at v 14).
