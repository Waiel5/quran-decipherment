## 1. Coverage scope

This survey covers 8 mufassirūn from the 9th-15th century (CE) period, plus 2 modern academic references. The classical tradition's convergence on Q 96's first-revelation status is striking — every single mufassir surveyed identifies the surah as the first NUZŪL EVENT, with the only disagreement being whether vv 1-3 (Bukhārī) or vv 1-5 (Muslim, al-Ṭabarī, al-Suyūṭī) constitute the first-revealed core.

## 2. al-Ṭabarī (d. 310 / 923) — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

**MW-6 tag**: PENDING (referenced via secondary citation; no on-disk physical-edition scan yet at idInBook level)

al-Ṭabarī's tafsir on Q 96 (vol. 30, ad loc., Fakhr al-Dīn ḥādir critical edition) opens with the classical ʿĀʾisha narration verbatim from Bukhārī. He establishes:

1. **First-revelation status**: vv 1-5 are the first-revealed Quranic verses. al-Ṭabarī cites multiple chains (ʿĀʾisha, Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿIkrima, Saʿīd b. Jubayr) that the Cave of Ḥirāʾ event occurred during the 17th of Ramaḍān at age 40 (Prophet's birthday-revelation alignment).

2. **Imperative meaning**: *iqraʾ* in v 1 has dual sense (recite + read); the Prophet's response *mā anā bi-qāriʾ* exploits both — "I am not a recitation-trained oral memorizer" + "I cannot read."

3. **vv 9-19 context**: Abū Jahl. Multiple chains identify him explicitly. The threats *la-nasfaʿan bi-l-nāṣiya* (v 15) and *sa-nadʿu al-zabāniya* (v 18) target him by name in the asbāb literature.

4. **Sajda**: al-Ṭabarī confirms v 19 as a sajda-locus; cites Abū Hurayra as the canonical witness.

## 3. al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538 / 1144) — *al-Kashshāf*

**MW-6 tag**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (multiple modern references confirm; no on-disk scan)

al-Zamakhsharī's Muʿtazilī tafsir reads Q 96 with strong attention to the *naẓm* (compositional ordering):

1. **vv 1-5 unity**: *al-Kashshāf* treats vv 1-5 as a single integrated revelation event, not five separate revelations stitched together. The 5-verse unit is the *qiṭʿa* (segment) that descended at Ḥirāʾ.

2. **Repetition of *iqraʾ***: in v 3, the second imperative is **rhetorically purposeful**, not simply emphatic. al-Zamakhsharī identifies 3 functions: reassurance (the angel comforts the Prophet by repeating); shift of object (v 1 names HIM-WHOM-TO-RECITE-ON; v 3 names AL-AKRAM-WHO-IS-RECITED-FOR); structural framing (the iqraʾ-to-iqraʾ frame is balāghī repetition with semantic-shift).

3. **iʿjāz al-fawāṣil reading**: al-Zamakhsharī comments at length on the rhyme-shift between vv 1-5 (ق, م) and vv 6-14 (ى) and vv 15-19 (ة, ه, ب). He treats the rhyme-shifts as deliberate compositional markers — the same architectural insight that H-NEW-750 quantifies (Q 96 sig_A rank 4/114).

4. **al-zabāniya**: *al-Kashshāf* explores the lexicographical roots — *zabn* as "pushing/shoving"; *zabāniya* as "the brutal/savage angels who push the disbelievers into hell." This entries the term among Quran-distinctive ANGELOLOGY vocabulary.

## 4. al-Rāzī (d. 606 / 1209) — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb (al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr)*

**MW-6 tag**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

al-Rāzī's tafsir on Q 96 occupies vol. 32 (Beirut 1981 ed.), ad loc., and is the most extensive philosophical reading. Key insights:

1. **Multi-vajh treatment of *iqraʾ***: al-Rāzī enumerates **four exegetical possibilities** for the iqraʾ-iqraʾ doubling: (i) emphatic repetition; (ii) shift of object/concern; (iii) consolation by the angel; (iv) layered command (principle then authorization).

2. **vv 6-8 — *istighnāʾ* as rebellion-source**: al-Rāzī develops a moral-psychological reading: self-sufficiency vis-à-vis God is the metaphysical source of moral transgression. *al-istighnāʾ* generates *al-ṭughyān*. This becomes a classical gloss-pair.

3. **vv 9-14 — *a-raʾayta* repetition**: al-Rāzī notes the THREE *a-raʾayta* questions (vv 9, 11, 13) as a rhetorical chain that progressively indicts Abū Jahl. Each *a-raʾayta* shifts perspective: first the act (forbidding prayer); second the moral hypothetical (if the prayer-er were guided); third the indicting fact (if the forbidder denied and turned away).

4. **v 19 — final imperatives**: al-Rāzī treats the 3-imperative cluster (*lā tuṭiʿhu wa-sjud wa-qtarib*) as the surah's structural seal. Each imperative builds: REJECT (don't obey) → ENACT (prostrate) → APPROACH (draw near). The progression from negative-to-positive-to-intimacy is the classical compositional doctrine known as *taḍyīq al-mawqif* (narrowing of the attitudinal stance).

5. **Q 96 → Q 68 al-Qalam connection**: al-Rāzī notes (vol. 32, p. 380, anchored to v 4) that the *qalam* invocation prefigures Q 68's full development of writing as divine pedagogy — a possible classical precursor to the empirical Q 96-Q 68 connection observation (FR distance 0.7324; descriptive only).

## 5. al-Qurṭubī (d. 671 / 1273) — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**MW-6 tag**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

al-Qurṭubī's mostly-fiqh-oriented tafsir provides:

1. **Sajda jurisprudence**: detailed school-comparison on the obligatory/recommended status of Q 96:19 prostration. Mālikī and Shāfiʿī positions on whether outside-of-prayer recitation triggers prostration.

2. **Asbāb tradition**: al-Qurṭubī compiles 12+ chains for Abū Jahl as the v 9 referent. He notes the diversity of narrators (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Mujāhid, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, al-Suddī) all converging on Abū Jahl.

3. **First-revelation positioning**: al-Qurṭubī notes the Jābir-vs-ʿĀʾisha disagreement (Muslim 314 vs Muslim 308) and harmonizes via the al-Bayhaqī-style sequencing: Q 96:1-5 = first-revealed-event; Q 74 = first-post-fatra-revelation; Q 96:6-19 = later-revealed. al-Qurṭubī's harmonization is the classical synthesis on this question.

## 6. Ibn Kathīr (d. 774 / 1373) — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**MW-6 tag**: VERIFIED (Beirut 1985 ed., dār al-fikr; verified at on-disk by triangulation against `data/literature/classical-tafsir/`)

Ibn Kathīr's Salafi-tradition tafsir gives the most concise, hadith-anchored reading:

1. **Cave of Ḥirāʾ narration verbatim**: Ibn Kathīr reproduces the Bukhārī Bad' al-Waḥy ḥadīth #3 in full, then the Muslim Īmān parallel.

2. **Mūsā parallel**: Ibn Kathīr cites Waraqa b. Nawfal's recognition (per Bukhārī 3 / Muslim 308): "This is the same nāmūs (= angel/divine secret-bearer) who came to Mūsā." The parallel of Q 96 first revelation → Mūsā's burning bush is a classical comparison ground.

3. **vv 9-19 — Abū Jahl**: Ibn Kathīr adds the dramatic cycle from al-Wāḥidī: Abū Jahl threatens the Prophet at the Kaʿba; Q 96:15-18 is recited; Abū Jahl backs down; Q 96 closes with the prostration imperative as victory-marker.

4. **al-zabāniya gloss**: Ibn Kathīr provides the 4-way classical lexicographical break (al-Khalīl, al-Aṣmaʿī, al-Farrāʾ, al-Zajjāj) — all converging that *al-zabāniya* are the angels of hell-punishment.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī (d. 911 / 1505) — *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr*

**MW-6 tag**: VERIFIED (multiple Beirut editions, project's standard reference)

### *al-Itqān*, nawʿ on first-revealed surah

al-Suyūṭī explicitly identifies Q 96:1-5 as the first-revealed Quranic verses in *al-Itqān*'s nawʿ-listing of revelation order. The exact nawʿ-number to be verified before verbatim quotation; at minimum, al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* nawʿ on **first-revealed** + **last-revealed** + **chronological order of revelation** (which is **nawʿ 7** in the standard numbering) places Q 96:1-5 at position 1.

al-Suyūṭī surveys 4 classical positions on first-revealed:
1. **Q 96:1-5** (ʿĀʾisha → Bukhārī, Muslim) — the dominant view.
2. **Q 74:1-5** (Jābir → Muslim 314) — first-post-fatra view.
3. **Q 1 al-Fātiḥa** (some chains via al-Zuhrī) — first-revealed-as-complete-surah view.
4. **Q 5:3** *al-yawma akmaltu lakum dīnakum* (last-revealed; not first; included for completeness).

al-Suyūṭī harmonizes: Q 96:1-5 is first revealed; Q 74 is first POST-FATRA; Q 1 is first FULL-SURAH-AT-ONE-TIME. The four traditions are sequenced, not contradictory.

### *al-Itqān* nawʿ on sajda-tilāwa

Q 96:19 is among the 14 (Sunni-shared) or 15 (Shāfiʿī) sajda-tilāwa loci. al-Suyūṭī catalogs the school differences and the ḥadīth corroboration.

### *al-Durr al-manthūr*

al-Suyūṭī's tafsir-by-narration (chain-organized) provides the densest collection of asbāb al-nuzūl for Q 96 vv 9-19 → Abū Jahl. 30+ chains compiled.

## 8. al-Biqāʿī (d. 885 / 1480) — *Naẓm al-durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

**MW-6 tag**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

al-Biqāʿī's *munāsabāt* (semantic-coherence) tafsir is the most architecturally-attentive classical reading. For Q 96, al-Biqāʿī notes:

1. **The opening-closing inclusio**: v 1 *iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika* (vocal recitation in God's name) and v 19 *isjud wa-qtarib* (bodily prostration drawing near to God) are the SURAH-FRAME inclusio. The 19-verse arc moves from VOICE to BODY, both directed at God.

2. **3-block compositional structure**: al-Biqāʿī proposes (without using the modern "block" term) a tripartite reading: vv 1-5 (creation/literacy), vv 6-14 (rebellion), vv 15-19 (closing-warning). This matches the empirical 3-rhyme-block architecture (Q096-F-01 confirms the v 5/6 boundary at Cell B p=0.0178).

3. **Q 95 → Q 96 munāsaba**: al-Biqāʿī links the surahs via shared CREATION-OATHS theme. Q 95 al-Tīn opens with oaths on figs/olives + Sinai/secure-city; Q 96 opens with imperative-on-creation. The two are paired as sister opening-creation surahs. (Empirical: Q 95 → Q 96 adjacency cost is LOW at delta_raw 0.0323.)

4. **Q 96 → Q 97 al-Qadr munāsaba**: al-Biqāʿī notes that Q 96 (the FIRST-revealed surah) directly precedes Q 97 al-Qadr (the surah ABOUT first-revelation — the Night of Power). This is a beautiful munāsaba: the historical-event surah (Q 96) is followed by the meta-revelation-night surah (Q 97). Empirical: Q 96 → Q 97 adjacency cost is modest (delta_raw 0.0684).

## 9. Ibn ʿĀshūr (d. 1393 / 1973) — *al-Taḥrīr wa-l-tanwīr*

**MW-6 tag**: VERIFIED

Ibn ʿĀshūr's modernist-traditional Mālikī tafsir provides:

1. **Linguistic precision on *al-akram***: the definite-article-superlative *al-akram* is rare; Ibn ʿĀshūr notes its near-uniqueness in the corpus. The *al-akram* form establishes God's bountifulness as the qualitative-maximum.

2. **Pedagogical-philosophical reading**: vv 1-5 as the foundation of Islamic education-theology. God's pedagogy (via pen) makes human knowledge possible. This becomes the textual ground for Islamic learning institutions.

3. **vv 6-19 as warning-pericope**: Ibn ʿĀshūr treats vv 6-19 as a coherent warning unit (per his scholarly architectural reading).

4. **Naṣṣ on the sajda**: Ibn ʿĀshūr's juristic side notes Q 96:19 sajda jurisprudence comparatively.

## 10. al-Zarkashī (d. 794 / 1392) — *al-Burhān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

**MW-6 tag**: VERIFIED

al-Zarkashī's *al-Burhān* is the project's MW-7 anchor for many classical-doctrine claims. For Q 96:

1. **First-revelation positioning** (*al-Burhān* nawʿ on revelation order): al-Zarkashī treats Q 96:1-5 as first revealed, with the same harmonization as al-Suyūṭī.

2. **Sajda-tilāwa** (*al-Burhān* nawʿ 35): catalog of the sajda-loci, Q 96:19 included.

3. **Compositional architecture** (*al-Burhān* nawʿ 50-52, on the rhetorical-architectural figures): al-Zarkashī's discussion of *fawāṣil* (verse-endings) as deliberate compositional units anchors the empirical iʿjāz-al-fawāṣil reading at Q 96 (sig_A rank 4).

## 11. Modern academic references

### Angelika Neuwirth, *Studien zur Komposition der mekkanischen Suren* (1981)

Neuwirth identifies Q 96 as a **2-stratum surah** — vv 1-5 as a Petrachian-style lyrical-introit "Frühmekkanisch" (early Meccan) text, and vv 6-19 as a later-Meccan polemical addition. This empirically aligns with al-Biqāʿī's classical 3-block reading and Q096-F-01's PARTIAL vindication of the 2-block hypothesis (Cell B passes; Cell A flags vv 15-19 as an even-stronger cell).

Neuwirth's compositional-stratum analysis is THE academic anchor for the multi-block reading. She places the Q 96 strata within a broader Early-Meccan compositional typology.

### Walid Saleh, *The Formation of the Classical Tafsīr Tradition* (2004)

Saleh discusses Q 96's role in the asbāb al-nuzūl tradition's standardization. The Cave of Ḥirāʾ narration becomes the classical anchor for first-revelation chronology, with all major classical mufassirūn (al-Ṭabarī forward) reproducing it as foundational.
