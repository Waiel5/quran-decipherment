# Q 96 al-ʿAlaq — Content Analysis

## Block A — vv 1-5: Creation, recitation, the pen (THE FIRST REVELATION)

### v 1 — اقرأ باسم ربك الذي خلق

*iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika alladhī khalaq*
"Recite in the name of your Lord who created."

**Imperative**: 2MS *iqraʾ* (root q-r-ʾ; QAC `{qora>o`). The first command revealed.

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī** (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān*, ad loc.): the imperative *iqraʾ* may mean "recite" (the recitation context — the Prophet receives a recited revelation) OR "read" (engagement with the text). The Bukhārī-Muslim hadith of the Cave of Ḥirāʾ favors "recite," since the angel commands a recitation event, not a reading of a written text. The Prophet's response *mā anā bi-qāriʾ* — "I am not a reciter / I cannot read" — indicates both are possible meanings.
- **al-Zamakhsharī** (*al-Kashshāf*, ad loc.): *bi-smi rabbika* is the means/instrument by which the recitation is performed — "with the name of your Lord" / "by invocation of your Lord's name." This is the ground for the bismillah formulary as a recitation-frame.
- **al-Rāzī** (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, vol. 32, ad loc.): the *alladhī khalaq* is left absolute (without object) — "the One who created" — to encompass ALL creation. The qualifying object emerges in v 2.
- **Ibn Kathīr** (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*, ad loc.): cites Bukhārī's Cave of Ḥirāʾ hadith verbatim. The *bi-ism* construction sets the tone: human action under divine name.

**Empirical note**: This verse contains 2 of the corpus's 6 IMPV-qrA tokens (the other in v 3 below). Word 1 *iqraʾ* + word 2 *bi-smi* (with prefix *bi-* + *ism* root smw) form a corpus-rare compound — the imperative *iqraʾ bi-smi* construction occurs only in Q 96 v 1.

### v 2 — خلق الإنسان من علق

*khalaqa al-insāna min ʿalaq*
"He created man from a clinging clot."

**Lexicology**:
- *al-insān* (root Ans): "the human/man" — corpus frequency 65 tokens.
- *min ʿalaq* (root Elq): "from a clot" — corpus frequency **7 tokens** (rare). 6 of 7 occurrences are in embryonic-creation contexts.

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: *ʿalaqa* is a clinging clot of blood, the second stage of embryonic development per the Quranic creation-cycle (*nuṭfa → ʿalaqa → muḍgha → ʿiẓām → laḥm*) — the same cycle elaborated in Q 22:5 and Q 23:14.
- **al-Bayḍāwī** (*Anwār al-tanzīl*): the singular *ʿalaq* (not *ʿalaqa* with tāʾ marbūṭa) is generic; the species, not the individual instance.
- **al-Qurṭubī** (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*): the verse establishes the dignity-from-humility theme — God elevates the literacy-recipient (v 1 imperative) from his origin in the lowliest substance.

**Empirical note**: This verse gives the surah its name. The verse pairs *al-insān* (human) with *ʿalaq* (clot) — the latter occurring only 7 times in the entire Quran.

### v 3 — اقرأ وربك الأكرم

*iqraʾ wa-rabbuka al-akram*
"Recite, and your Lord is the Most-Bountiful."

**Imperative**: 2nd corpus *iqraʾ* (the second of Q 96's two — and 1 of 6 corpus IMPV-qrA tokens).

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Rāzī** (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*): the repetition of *iqraʾ* in v 3 has 4 possible exegetical functions: (i) emphasis — restating the command for impact; (ii) shift of object — v 1 about whom-to-recite-on (in name of God), v 3 about the act itself; (iii) consolation — the angel reassuring the trembling Prophet; (iv) layered command — the first *iqraʾ* establishes the principle, the second authorizes Prophetic teaching to others.
- **Ibn ʿĀshūr** (*al-Taḥrīr wa-l-tanwīr*): *al-akram* is the superlative of *karīm*; the comparative reading is "the More Bountiful" (more bountiful than what the man can imagine). Pairs the imperative with a self-disclosure of God's attribute.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: the *wa-* connects the imperative to a circumstantial clause — "Recite, AND ALSO know your Lord is Most-Bountiful." The *wa-rabbuka al-akram* is the GROUND of the imperative — recitation is possible because the Lord is generous.

**Empirical note**: *al-akram* is the only Quranic occurrence of the superlative *al-akram* form (the comparative *akram* and the simple *karīm* occur frequently, but the definite-superlative *al-akram* is hapax-form). This is descriptive, not part of any formal hapax test.

### v 4 — الذي علم بالقلم

*alladhī ʿallama bi-l-qalam*
"He who taught by the pen."

**Lexicology**:
- *ʿallama* (root Elm, form II): "He taught" — caused-knowledge.
- *al-qalam* (root qlm): "the pen" — corpus frequency **4 tokens** total (Q 3:44, Q 31:27, Q 68:1, Q 96:4).

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: the verse establishes the WRITING-INSTRUMENT (*al-qalam*) as the paradigmatic teaching-tool. This is the foundation for the muqaṭṭaʿāt-as-book-introduction tradition (cross-finding-008): the Quran inscribes its own pedagogical-logos via the pen.
- **al-Suyūṭī** (*al-Itqān*, nawʿ on first-revealed surah, also nawʿ on writing-references): cites this verse as the foundational kitāb-paradigm verse — God teaches through the pen, the human acquires writing through divine pedagogy.
- **al-Biqāʿī** (*Naẓm al-durar*, ad loc.): the *bi-l-qalam* is in instrumental ʿbāʾ; reads with v 5 — God taught the human by means of the pen, things he didn't know before.
- **Ibn Kathīr**: the verse vindicates literacy as a divine gift; classical exegetes use it as the ground for elevating writing/scholarship within Islam.

**Empirical note**: 1 of 4 corpus *qalam* tokens. Pair-mate Q 68:1 *Nūn wa-l-qalam* is the OPENING oath of Sūrat al-Qalam. The classical *qalam* attestation pairs:
- Q 96:4 + Q 68:1 = short-Meccan opening-cluster pair.
- Q 3:44 + Q 31:27 = long-surah mid-text pair.

Q096-F-03 tested whether Q 96 + Q 68 form a Fisher-Rao structural mirror; result NULL-BROKEN — the link is semantic only.

### v 5 — علم الإنسان ما لم يعلم

*ʿallama al-insāna mā lam yaʿlam*
"He taught man what he did not know."

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Rāzī**: the *mā lam yaʿlam* is comprehensive — encompasses all human ignorance redeemed by divine teaching. The verse closes the literacy-pedagogy block.
- **al-Bayḍāwī**: *al-insāna* is the human qua human (not Adam specifically) — the species. God's pedagogy elevates the species.
- **Ibn ʿAṭiyya** (*al-Muḥarrar al-wajīz*): the verse parallels Q 2:31 (Adam taught the names of all things). The two verses bookend the species-pedagogy theme: Adam was taught names (ontology); human is taught what-he-didn't-know (epistemology).

**Empirical note**: This is the conclusion of the FIRST-REVEALED block per the strongest classical tradition. The block ends with rhyme letter م, a clean termination. The next verse begins block B with a topical and rhyme break.

## Block B — vv 6-14: Rebellion of the ungrateful

### v 6 — كلا إن الإنسان ليطغى

*kallā inna al-insāna la-yaṭghā*
"Nay! Man indeed transgresses."

**Particle**: *kallā* (root k-l-l) — the corpus's emphatic-negative refrain. **Q 96 contains 3 of these** (vv 6, 15, 19). The corpus has approximately 33 *kallā* tokens; Q 96 has 9% of them in 19 verses (the densest *kallā* concentration corpus-wide on a per-verse basis).

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: *kallā* opens the second register — the moral/eschatological warning section. It functions as a rhetorical pivot, marking that the prior block (vv 1-5, blessing) is over and the warning block begins.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: *yaṭghā* (root Tgy, "to transgress") is a verb-form X-prefix verb in non-pause/imperfect — it describes the persistent disposition of unredeemed humanity. The *la-* particle is emphatic: "indeed certainly transgresses."

**Asbāb al-nuzūl** (al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-nuzūl*; al-Qurṭubī ad loc.): vv 6 onward refer to **Abū Jahl** ʿAmr b. Hishām, the Prophet's primary Meccan opponent. Multiple narrators (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima) place the immediate context as Abū Jahl's repeated attempts to stop the Prophet's prayer at the Kaʿba.

### v 7 — أن رآه استغنى

*an raʾāhu istaghnā*
"That he sees himself self-sufficient."

**Lexicology**: *istaghnā* (root gny, form X) = "to deem oneself self-sufficient / not in need."

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Rāzī**: *istighnāʾ* is the spiritual condition that produces *ṭughyān* (transgression). The verse identifies self-sufficiency-vis-à-vis-God as the root of moral rebellion.
- **al-Suyūṭī** (*al-Itqān* nawʿ on rhetorical figures): the construction *an raʾāhu istaghnā* is an example of *iʿrāḍ* (turning away) — the man's gaze turning inward, disconnecting from divine-recipient mode.

### v 8 — إن إلى ربك الرجعى

*inna ilā rabbika al-rujʿā*
"Indeed to your Lord is the return."

**Lexicology**: *al-rujʿā* (root rjE) = "the returning" — corpus frequency 104 (rjE root) but the substantive *al-rujʿā* with definite article is rare.

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: the verse interrupts the warning to remind of eschatological return. Function: pivotal middle-verse of block B; sets up the back-pressure for vv 9-14.
- **Ibn ʿAbbās** (per al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr*): "the return is to Him alone" — emphasis on the unique-returnable destination.

### vv 9-10 — أرأيت الذي ينهى عبدا إذا صلى

*a-raʾayta alladhī yanhā ʿabdan idhā ṣallā*
"Have you seen the one who forbids a servant when he prays?"

**Particle**: *a-raʾayta* (interrogative + verb) — Q 96 has **3 instances** of *a-raʾayta* (vv 9, 11, 13). This rhetorical-question chain is a stylistic signature of vv 9-13.

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī** + **al-Wāḥidī** *Asbāb al-nuzūl*: the "one who forbids" is **Abū Jahl**. The "servant who prays" is the Prophet Muḥammad. Multiple chains (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿIkrima, Saʿīd b. Jubayr) corroborate.
- **al-Qurṭubī**: the verse functions as a rhetorical taunt — Abū Jahl's threats are publicly mocked. The Prophet's prayer at the Kaʿba is the immediate occasion.
- **al-Rāzī**: the indefinite *ʿabdan* + the conditional *idhā ṣallā* together ridicule Abū Jahl's specificity — he forbids a particular servant in a particular act, exposing his pettiness.

### vv 11-12 — أرأيت إن كان على الهدى أو أمر بالتقوى

*a-raʾayta in kāna ʿalā al-hudā aw amara bi-l-taqwā*
"Have you seen, if he were on guidance, or commanded piety?"

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: rhetorical-conditional — IF the prayer-er is on guidance and commanding piety, then the forbidder's transgression is doubly egregious.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: the conditional *in kāna* is hypothetical from the Abū-Jahl perspective; from the Quran's perspective the apodosis is fact.

### v 13 — أرأيت إن كذب وتولى

*a-raʾayta in kadhdhaba wa-tawallā*
"Have you seen, if he denies and turns away?"

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: the third *a-raʾayta* parallel; this one indicts the FORBIDDER (not the prayer-er). *kadhdhaba wa-tawallā* are key root-pair: rejection (form II *kadhdhaba* of root k*b "to lie") + turning-away (form V *tawallā* of root wly).

### v 14 — ألم يعلم بأن الله يرى

*a-lam yaʿlam bi-anna Allāha yarā*
"Did he not know that Allah sees?"

**Closing of block B**: the rhetorical question closes the rebellion-block by invoking divine surveillance. The block-final ى rhyme completes the 9-verse monorhyme stretch (vv 6-14).

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: the verse asks whether the forbidder has FORGOTTEN that God sees — implying that the forbidder once knew. The transgression is willful.
- **Ibn Kathīr**: parallels Q 75:14 (*bal al-insānu ʿalā nafsihī baṣīra*) — the human knows; transgression is in spite of knowing.

## Block C — vv 15-19: Closing warning + sajda

### v 15 — كلا لئن لم ينته لنسفعا بالناصية

*kallā la-in lam yantahi la-nasfaʿan bi-l-nāṣiya*
"Nay! If he does not desist, We will surely seize him by the forelock."

**Lexicology** (corpus-distinctive cell):
- *la-nasfaʿan* (root sfE, form I + emphatic nūn): **corpus-hapax** — sole Quranic occurrence.
- *bi-l-nāṣiya* (root nSy): "the forelock." Q 96:15 + Q 96:16 contain 2 of 4 corpus nSy tokens (the others Q 11:56, Q 55:41).

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: *sfE* is "to seize/grab violently"; the *la-* + emphatic *-an(a)* is the strongest verbal threat-form in the corpus. The *nāṣiya* (forelock) is the classical Arabic seat of submission — to seize the forelock is to take total control.
- **al-Zamakhsharī**: the threat is direct to Abū Jahl. Multiple aṣbāb traditions (al-Wāḥidī) record: when Abū Jahl heard this verse and threatened to crush the Prophet's neck if he found him praying, the Prophet recited the verse in response and Abū Jahl backed off.
- **al-Rāzī**: the two parts of vv 15-16 (subjunctive *la-nasfaʿan* + adjectival cataphora *nāṣiyatin kādhibatin khāṭiʾa*) form one of the corpus's tightest threat-formulas.

**Empirical note**: This verse has **TWO corpus-distinctive lexical items in 5 words**: *la-nasfaʿan* (corpus-hapax) + *nāṣiya* (rare-root, only 4 corpus occurrences). The lexical density of corpus-rare items in this verse is 40% — extreme.

### v 16 — ناصية كاذبة خاطئة

*nāṣiyatin kādhibatin khāṭiʾa*
"A lying, sinning forelock."

**Lexicology**: 3 indefinite-feminine nouns/participles in apposition.

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: the apposition *nāṣiyatin kādhibatin khāṭiʾa* describes Abū Jahl's character — his speech (lying) and his action (sinning) are localized at the forelock metonym.
- **al-Suyūṭī** (*al-Itqān* nawʿ on metonymy): example of *majāz mursal* with *ṣafat* alaqa (causal metonymy) — the forelock stands for the WHOLE PERSON; the lies and sins are not literally located in the hair.

### v 17 — فليدع ناديه

*fa-l-yadʿu nādiyah*
"So let him call upon his council."

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: the *naḍin* / *al-nādī* (root ndw) is the Quraysh tribal council at the Dār al-Nadwa near the Kaʿba. The verse mocks Abū Jahl's tribal allies — call them, see if they save you.
- **al-Wāḥidī** (*Asbāb al-nuzūl*): the verse responds to Abū Jahl's threat to use his tribal connections against the Prophet.

### v 18 — سندع الزبانية

*sa-nadʿu al-zabāniya*
"We will summon the brutes [of hell]."

**Lexicology**: *al-zabāniya* (root zbn) — **corpus-hapax**. Sole Quranic occurrence.

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: *al-zabāniya* are the angels of hell who carry out punishment. Classical lexicographers (al-Khalīl, al-Aṣmaʿī) gloss as "the savage/brutal angels," "the ones who push down."
- **al-Rāzī**: the verse parallels v 17 in syntax (verb + object) but inverts the subject: where the wicked calls upon his council, GOD will summon HIS council (the angels of hell). The parallel is rhetorical *muqābala*.
- **Ibn Kathīr**: cites a long lexicographical excursus on *zabāniya* — "those who push the disbelievers into hell."

**Empirical note**: 1 of 2 Q 96 corpus-hapax. **The verse-final position** of the hapax confirms H-NEW-23's active-placement pattern (corpus-hapax verbatim placed at verse-final z = +10.61).

### v 19 — كلا لا تطعه واسجد واقترب ۩

*kallā lā tuṭiʿhu wa-sjud wa-qtarib*
"Nay! Do not obey him; prostrate and draw near."

**THREE imperatives in 5 words**:
- *lā tuṭiʿhu* (root TwE, form IV, jussive negative) — "do not obey him."
- *wa-sjud* (root sjd, form I, imperative) — "prostrate." THIS IS THE SAJDA-MARKER.
- *wa-qtarib* (root qrb, form VIII, imperative) — "draw near."

**Sajda-tilāwa**: the sajdah symbol ۩ in the canonical mushaf marks this verse as a place of recitational prostration. Q 96 is 1 of 14 Sunni-shared sajda-surahs.

**Classical commentary**:
- **al-Ṭabarī**: the imperative *wa-qtarib* (root qrb form VIII = *iqtariba*, "to draw close, approach intimately") inverts the closing of the rebellion-block; the prayer-servant draws close while the forbidder is dragged off by the zabāniya.
- **al-Suyūṭī** (*Itqān*, nawʿ on sajdat al-tilāwa): catalogs Q 96:19 in the obligatory/recommended sajda list. The schools differ on the obligatory/recommended status but ALL agree it is a sajda-locus.
- **al-Biqāʿī** (*Naẓm al-durar*): the closing imperative *wa-qtarib* is the structural counterpart to v 1's *iqraʾ bi-smi rabbika*. The surah opens commanding recitation in God's name and closes commanding nearness via prostration. The 19-verse arc is a movement from VOCAL-RECITATION to BODILY-PROSTRATION — the full liturgical-act mapped onto the surah arc.
- **Ibn Kathīr**: cites Bukhārī Sujud al-Quran chapter (Kitāb 17) and Muslim Mosques chapter (idInBook 1201) for the sajda attestation. The sajda is performed at this verse during recitation.

**Empirical note**: 3 imperatives in 5 words is the corpus's densest imperative-cluster verse. The closing is strongly active-volitional, not passive-doxological.

## Synthesis — the surah's narrative arc

Q 96 traces a 3-block arc:
1. **A (vv 1-5) — Affirmation**: God CREATES the human (from clot), TEACHES the human (by pen), GIVES the imperative (recite!). The human is recipient.
2. **B (vv 6-14) — Inversion**: The human REBELS, sees himself as self-sufficient, FORBIDS others from worshiping. The receptive-creature inverted into the obstructive-creature.
3. **C (vv 15-19) — Restoration**: God's threat (zabāniya) overrides the human's tribal-council threat; the obedient prostrate and draw near. Restoration of order.

The arc is liturgical: it begins with the imperative *iqraʾ* (recitation) and ends with the imperative *isjud wa-qtarib* (prostration + drawing near). Verses 1 and 19 frame the surah with **active 2MS commands to the recipient-human**; verses 6-14 interrogate the breakdown when the human refuses these commands.

This 3-block narrative-liturgical structure is what the empirical 3-block rhyme architecture (ق-م / ى / ة-ه-ب) tracks at the formal level. Content, rhyme, and tradition all align on the 3-block reading.
