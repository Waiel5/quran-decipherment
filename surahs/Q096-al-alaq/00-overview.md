## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-ʿAlaq** after the noun *ʿalaq* at v 2 (*khalaqa al-insāna min ʿalaq* — "He created man from a clinging clot"). The Arabic *ʿalaq* is from the root ع-ل-ق (*ʿ-l-q*, "to cling, to attach") and refers to a clot of clinging blood / embryonic-adhesion stage of human development per classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī).

**Alternative classical names**:
- *Sūrat Iqraʾ* — after the first imperative (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* nawʿ on surah-naming).
- *Sūrat al-Qalam* — disputed/secondary (the more common reference for Q 68).

Per the classical tradition (al-Bukhārī, al-Muslim, al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr), this surah's vv 1-5 (or vv 1-3 per some narrators) form **the first Quranic revelation event** — the foundational scene at the cave of Ḥirāʾ.

## 3. First-revelation status (THE ANCHOR PROPERTY)

Q 96 al-ʿAlaq is universally identified by the strongest classical hadith tradition as **the first surah revealed** (the primary verses being vv 1-5, with vv 6-19 revealed in a later revelation cycle, post-fatra-al-waḥy "pause of inspiration").

### The Jābir-vs-ʿĀʾisha disagreement (Muslim 314)

Muslim's idInBook=314 preserves a contrary tradition from Yaḥyā/Abū Salama → Jābir b. ʿAbdullāh: "I asked Abū Salama what was revealed first from the Qurʾān. He said: *yā ayyuhā al-muddaththir* (Q 74). I said: *or 'iqraʾ'*?" The classical response (al-Suyūṭī *Itqān*, al-Ṭabarī, al-Bayhaqī) **harmonizes**: Q 96:1-5 was the first NUZŪL EVENT (the cave of Ḥirāʾ, the angelic encounter), while Q 74 (al-Muddaththir) was the first POST-FATRA revelation — i.e., the first revelation after the pause when public preaching began. The two are sequenced, not contradictory. Q 96 vv 6-19 are then revealed at some later point during the early Meccan period, completing the surah we now read.

This dual-event architecture explains why Q 96's structure shows TWO register-shifts — the vv 1-5 / vv 6-14 / vv 15-19 three-block structure (verified empirically in Q096-F-01).

## 9. The sajda-tilāwa membership (descriptive only — FR-cluster NULL)

Q 96:19 ends with the imperative *kallā lā tuṭiʿhu wa-sjud wa-qtarib* — "Nay, do not obey him; prostrate and draw near" (sajda marker ۩). Q 96 is one of the 14-surah Sunni-shared sajda-tilāwa list:

{Q 7, 13, 16, 17, 19, 22, 25, 27, 32, 38, 41, 53, 84, 96}

(Shāfiʿī tradition adds Q 22:77 as a 15th; the 14-set is the school-conservative base.)

**Empirical caveat**: Q096-F-04 tested whether the 14 sajda-surahs form a Fisher-Rao cluster. **Result: NULL-BROKEN** (musabbiḥāt PC also failed at p=0.083; mean intra-cluster FR for sajda-set = 0.941, very close to corpus mean 0.923, suggesting the sajda-class is FR-neutral). The sajda-tilāwa class is **liturgical-functional, not structural** — consistent with H-NEW-68's NULL on the analogous Friday-cluster shape-cohesion test.

## 10. The vv 1-5 vs vv 6-19 register-shift (PARTIAL VINDICATION via Q096-F-01)

The classical first-revelation tradition predicts that vv 1-5 (the original revelation event) should be COMPOSITIONALLY DIFFERENT from vv 6-19 (later additions). Q096-F-01 tests this at JS-divergence on root-distribution:

- **Cell A (contiguous-block null)**: vv 1-5 ranks **#2 of 15 contiguous 5-block partitions** of Q 96 (rank-1 is vv 15-19, the closing-warning). FAILED Bonferroni (p=0.133) at α_bon=0.025.
- **Cell B (random non-contiguous null)**: vv 1-5 vs vv 6-19 JS divergence p=0.0178 — **PASSES Bonferroni** at α_bon=0.025.
- **MW-5 PC (Q 19 Maryam vv 1-40 vs vv 41-98)**: PASSES at p=0.0023 ✓

Verdict: **ANOMALOUS-INFORMATIVE**. The vv 1-5 / vv 6-19 partition is structurally distinct (Cell B passes), but it is NOT the SINGLE-MOST-DIVERGENT contiguous boundary in Q 96 — that title goes to vv 15-19 (the closing-warning passage). The surah has a **3-block architecture** (vv 1-5 / vv 6-14 / vv 15-19), where ALL three boundaries are register-discontinuities, not a binary 2-block split.

This is consistent with the classical chronology: vv 1-5 = first revelation event; vv 6-14 = post-fatra continuation; vv 15-19 = even-later closing-warning addition (the *zabāniya* threat against Abū Jahl scenes). The 3-block compositional history hypothesis fits the empirical 3-rhyme-block pattern.

## 11. The Abū Jahl context (vv 9-19)

Classical asbāb al-nuzūl (al-Wāḥidī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr) places vv 9-19 in the period when **Abū Jahl** ʿAmr b. Hishām opposed the Prophet's prayer at the Kaʿba. The "one who forbids a servant when he prays" (v 9-10) is Abū Jahl. The closing *zabāniya* threat (v 18) is a direct retort: "We will summon the angels of hell."

This is corroborated by ʿAbd al-Razzāq's *Muṣannaf* and Ibn Hishām's *Sīra* — Abū Jahl is named explicitly in the asbāb literature for this passage.
