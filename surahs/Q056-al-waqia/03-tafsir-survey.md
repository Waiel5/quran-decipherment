# Q 56 al-Wāqiʿa — Tafsir Survey

Sources verified on disk under `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/`. Each citation links to file path.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* (d. 310/923)

**Source**: `spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/56/{N}.json`

al-Ṭabarī's headline gloss on v 1 (*idhā waqaʿat al-wāqiʿa*): "*yaʿnī taʿālā dhikruhu bi-qawlihi 'idhā waqaʿat al-wāqiʿa': idhā nazalat ṣayḥatu ʾl-qiyāma, wa-dhālika ḥīna yunfakhu fī ʾl-ṣūr li-qiyāmi ʾl-sāʿa*" — al-wāqiʿa is the Resurrection-trumpet-blast. Cites al-Ḍaḥḥāk (*al-ṣayḥa*), Ibn ʿAbbās (*al-wāqiʿa wa-l-ṭāmma wa-l-ṣākhkha wa-naḥwa hādhā min asmāʾi ʾl-qiyāma*) — Q 56's title-word is one of the canonical names of the Day of Resurrection.

On v 75 (*fa-lā uqsimu bi-mawāqiʿ al-nujūm*): al-Ṭabarī collects multiple positions on *mawāqiʿ*: (1) Mujāhid: *masāqiṭu ʾl-nujūm* (the setting-places); (2) al-Ḥasan: rising AND setting positions; (3) Qatāda: *manāzilu ʾl-nujūm* (their orbital positions). al-Ṭabarī himself prefers position (1)-(2). On v 76 he treats *la-qasamun ʿaẓīm* as God magnifying the oath itself.

On the 3-class structure: al-Ṭabarī comments at v 7 (*azwāj thalātha*): the three are explicitly named in vv 8-10, and the contrast *qalīlun mina ʾl-ākhirīn* (v 14) is read with Ibn ʿAbbās's reading that the Sābiqūn class is dominated by pre-Muḥammadan ummahs, with few Muḥammadan-ummah members.

## 2. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (d. 671/1273)

**Source**: `spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/56/1.json` and following.

al-Qurṭubī opens with the **classical disagreement on Meccan/Medinan status**:
- Majority (al-Ḥasan, ʿIkrimah, Jābir, ʿAṭāʾ): wholly Meccan, 96 verses (some count 97 — minor mushaf variation).
- Ibn Qutayba: v 82 (*wa-tajʿalūna rizqakum...*) is Medinan.
- al-Kalbī: vv 81-82 (*afa-bi-hādhā ʾl-ḥadīth* + *wa-tajʿalūna rizqakum*) revealed during a journey to Mecca; vv 13-14 (*thullatun mina ʾl-awwalīn wa-qalīlun mina ʾl-ākhirīn*) revealed during journey to Medina.
- Masrūq: "whoever wishes to know the report of the firsts and lasts, the report of the people of paradise and the people of hellfire, the report of the people of this world and the next world, let him recite Sūrat al-Wāqiʿa."

al-Qurṭubī also reports the famous Ibn Masʿūd-deathbed-recitation hadith (citing Ibn ʿAbd al-Barr's *al-Tamhīd* and Tha'labī), with chains both anchoring it and noting weakness. He records the variant readings of the *abkārā / ʿuruban / atrābā* sequence (vv 35-37) and the multiple readings of *fa-lā uqsimu* / *fa-la-uqsimu*.

## 3. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* (d. 774/1373)

**Source**: `spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/56/1.json` (Arabic) + `spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/56/1.json` (English Darussalam).

Ibn Kathīr's introduction to the surah opens with Tirmidhī's "Hūd, al-Wāqiʿa..." gray-hair hadith (graded *ḥasan gharīb*) and follows immediately with the Ibn Masʿūd-deathbed story:

> *qāla al-Ḥāfiẓ Ibn ʿAsākir fī tarjamat ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd bi-sanadihi ilā ʿAmr b. al-Rabīʿ b. Ṭāriq al-Miṣrī ḥaddathanā al-Sarī b. Yaḥyā al-Shaybānī ʿan Shujāʿ ʿan Abī Ẓabya qāla: mariḍa ʿAbdullāh maraḍahu ʾlladhī tuwuffiya fīhi fa-ʿādahu ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān, fa-qāla: 'mā tashtakī?' qāla: 'dhunūbī.' qāla: 'fa-mā tashtahī?' qāla: 'raḥmata rabbī'... qāla: 'a-takhshā ʿalā banātī ʾl-faqr? innī amartu banātī yaqraʾna kulla layla sūrata ʾl-Wāqiʿa, innī samiʿtu rasūla ʾllāh ﷺ yaqūl: «man qaraʾa sūrata ʾl-Wāqiʿa kulla layla lam tuṣibhu fāqatun abadan»'*

Ibn Kathīr immediately follows this with multiple alternative chains (Ibn ʿAsākir → ʿAbdullāh b. Wahb → al-Sarī b. Yaḥyā; Abū Yaʿlā → Isḥāq b. Ibrāhīm → Muḥammad b. Munīb → al-Sarī b. Yaḥyā). He explicitly notes (*kadhā qāla wa-l-ṣawāb ʿan Shujāʿ kamā rawāhu ʿAbdullāh b. Wahb ʿan al-Sarī*) — i.e., he is recording multiple isnād forms but with awareness that the chain has *ikhtilāf* (variation).

He then cites Imām Aḥmad (*Musnad*) on Jābir b. Samura: the Prophet recited al-Wāqiʿa in *fajr* (*kāna yaqraʾu fī ʾl-fajr al-Wāqiʿa wa-naḥwahā mina ʾl-suwar*).

On v 75-76 (META-OATH): Ibn Kathīr quotes ʿĀʾisha's idiom *lā wa-llāhi* as the linguistic precedent for *lā* as oath-affirmative; cites Ibn Jarīr (al-Ṭabarī) on *fa-lā uqsimu* meaning "the matter is not as you claim — and I swear..." On v 76: *hādhā qasamun ʿaẓīmun aʿẓamuhu allāh ʿalā nafsihi, fa-law taʿlamūna ʿaẓamatu hādhā ʾl-qasam la-ʿalimtum ʿaẓamata ʾl-muqsami ʿalayhi* — "this is a great vow that God has imposed on Himself; if you knew the magnitude of this oath, you would know the magnitude of the subject sworn-to (the Qurʾān)."

## 4. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (d. 606/1209)

**Source**: not on disk in spa5k-api per-verse format for Q 56, but al-Rāzī's positions are reproduced via the project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-99names-extract.md` and via **al-Maarif-ul-Qurʾān** (which heavily quotes al-Rāzī in the Q 56 commentary at `spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran/56/`).

al-Rāzī's distinctive contribution on Q 56 is the **theological architecture of the 3 classes**:
- The *Sābiqūn* are the elect (*al-khāṣṣatu min al-khāṣṣ*) characterized by *qurb* (nearness to God) — the highest spiritual rank.
- The *Aṣḥāb al-Yamīn* are the saved-but-not-elect (*ahl al-najāh*) — the broad mass of the righteous.
- The *Aṣḥāb al-Shimāl* are the damned (*ahl al-shaqāwa*).

al-Rāzī treats the asymmetry *thulla / qalīl* (vv 13-14, *thulla / thulla* in vv 39-40) as evidence that the *Sābiqūn* class is small and concentrated in early ummahs while *Aṣḥāb al-Yamīn* are abundant in both early AND late generations. This becomes a key prooftext in classical disputes about Muḥammadan-ummah-honor (some Muʿtazila and others read v 14 as suggesting the Muḥammadan ummah has fewer Sābiqūn than earlier ummahs; al-Rāzī gives multiple readings to reconcile).

al-Rāzī also discusses the META-OATH (vv 75-76) within his catalog of Quranic *aqsām*. He treats the structure of *qasamun ʿaẓīm* as one of three corpus-rare patterns where God magnifies an oath (the others he identifies are Q 75:1-2 and Q 89:5).

## 5. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-Manthūr*

**Source**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf` + `suyuti-itqan-word-counts.md`.

al-Suyūṭī catalogues Q 56 in:
- **Nawʿ on Meccan/Medinan** (al-Itqān): Q 56 listed as Meccan with the minority-view Medinan-exception verses.
- **Nawʿ on aqsām al-Qurʾān**: Q 56:75-76 cited as the canonical example of the *qasamun ʿaẓīm* pattern.
- **Nawʿ on iḥtibāk** and **nawʿ on the science of the openings of surahs**: Q 56's *idhā waqaʿat* opening grouped with the *idhā*-eschatological cluster.

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-Manthūr* on Q 56 (verse-by-verse compilation of asbāb al-nuzūl and tafsīr-by-tradition) cites multiple chains for the Ibn Masʿūd hadith and notes its weakness (citing al-Bayhaqī's *Shuʿab al-Īmān* as the canonical secondary source for the chain).

## 6. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar* (d. 885/1480)

**Source**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf` (PDF, 738 pp); raw OpenITI extraction at `raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Biqāʿī's contribution is the **munāsabāt-chain Q 55 → Q 56 → Q 57**:
- Q 55 al-Raḥmān is structured as a recurring refrain *fa-bi-ayyi ālāʾi rabbikumā tukadhdhibān* — establishing that the *raḥma* and *ālāʾ* (favors) of God are continuous and undeniable.
- Q 56 al-Wāqiʿa **takes the implicit threat in Q 55** ("if you deny these favors") and **realizes it as the 3-class judgment scenario** — the *mukadhdhibūn al-ḍāllūn* (Q 56:51, 92) are the very *tukadhdhibān* of Q 55.
- Q 57 al-Ḥadīd **resolves** the eschatological tension by establishing God's cosmic sovereignty (*lahu mulku ʾl-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ*, Q 57:5), the iron-as-strength motif (Q 57:25), and the call to belief.

al-Biqāʿī thus reads Q 55 → Q 56 → Q 57 as a **tripartite arc**: refrain-of-favors → judgment-on-deniers → cosmic-resolution. The Q 56 → Q 57 high TSP-cost adjacency (rank 17/113, 2.74% of residual) is the empirical anchor for al-Biqāʿī's qualitative observation.

al-Biqāʿī also notes the surah's INTERNAL ring at v 74 / v 96 (*fa-sabbiḥ bi-smi rabbika ʾl-ʿaẓīm*) — describing it as the *khātima* (closing) repeated at the *waṣl* (joint) between the rational-arguments block (vv 57-74) and the META-OATH block (vv 75-82).

## 7. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf* (d. 538/1144)

**Source**: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zamakhshari-kashshaf` directory.

al-Zamakhsharī is the canonical *muʿtazilī* commentator. On Q 56:75-76, he treats the *fa-lā uqsimu* construction with characteristic balāghī precision: *lā* is the *zāʾida* (extra) particle that signals oath-affirmation by negation-of-rebuttal — i.e., "the matter is NOT as you claim, AND (now) I swear..." On v 76, his reading: the *qasamun ʿaẓīm* is great because the things sworn by (*mawāqiʿ al-nujūm*) are themselves cosmically significant — not because the oath is "about itself." This subtly differs from al-Rāzī and Ibn Kathīr who emphasize the SELF-REFERENCE of the oath. al-Zamakhsharī's reading focuses on the SUBJECT SWORN BY (the stars) rather than the META-LEVEL.

This is a key tafsīr-disagreement: is Q 56:76 a meta-comment on the oath structure ITSELF, or simply a magnification of the cosmic-sworn-objects?

## 8. en-Jalālayn (al-Maḥallī d. 864 / al-Suyūṭī d. 911)

**Source**: `spa5k-tafsir-api/en-al-jalalayn/56/1.json` and following.

Compact verse-by-verse paraphrase. On 75-76: "Nay I swear (*fa-lā uqsimu* — *lā* is extra) by the setting-places of the stars! And indeed it (the swearing by these setting-places of stars) is a tremendous oath, if you only knew — if you were people of deep knowledge you would realise the magnitude of this oath." Notably treats v 76 as META: "the SWEARING is itself the great oath." This aligns with al-Rāzī / Ibn Kathīr against al-Zamakhsharī.
