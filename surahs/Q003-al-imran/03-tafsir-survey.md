# Q 3 Āl ʿImrān — Tafsīr Survey

Five classical commentaries surveyed from the on-disk corpus. Source files cited per passage. The central
exegetical loci are (i) the muqaṭṭaʿāt of v 1, (ii) the muḥkam/mutashābih typology of v 7 — the foundational
proof-text of the whole ʿulūm-al-Qurʾān discipline, and (iii) the occasion of revelation (the Najrān delegation).

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (on Q 3:7)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/3/7.json`.

al-Ṭabarī defines the **muḥkamāt** as the verses "made firm by clear exposition and detail (*uḥkimna
bi-l-bayāni wa-l-tafṣīl*), whose proofs and evidences are established for what they were made evidence of — of
ḥalāl and ḥarām, promise and threat, reward and punishment, command and prohibition, report and parable,
admonition and lesson." He glosses *hunna umm al-kitāb* as "they are the FOUNDATION of the Book (*aṣl
al-kitāb*) in which lies the support of the religion and the obligations and the legal limits (*ḥudūd*) and
all that creation needs of its religious matters." He explains the name *umm* by Arab usage: "the Arabs call
the comprehensive bulk of a thing its *umm*" — the muḥkamāt are the bulk and the *mafzaʿ* (refuge) of the
Book's people when they need it. The **mutashābihāt** are the verses whose wording admits more than one face.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf* (on Q 3:1-7)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` lines 7765-7858.

al-Zamakhsharī's surah-header reads *"madaniyya wa-hiya miʾatā āya"* (Medinan, two hundred verses). On the
*mīm* of *alif-lām-mīm* (v 1) he gives a long grammatical treatment: its fatḥa is "the vowel of the hamza
[of *Allāh*] thrown onto it when the hamza was elided for lightening" — defending ʿĀṣim's reading and refuting
the "two-quiescents (*iltiqāʾ al-sākinayn*)" explanation. On *al-furqān* (v 4) he offers four readings:
(1) the genus of revealed Books (each is a *furqān* distinguishing truth from falsehood); (2) the three named
Books; (3) a fourth Book (the Zabūr of Dāwūd); or (4) a repetition praising the Qurʾān as the discriminator.
On v 6 (*huwa alladhī yuṣawwirukum fī al-arḥām*) he reports Saʿīd b. Jubayr's reading: it is an argument
against those who claimed ʿĪsā was Lord — the One formed in the womb is a servant like others. On v 7 he
glosses *muḥkamāt* as "verses whose expression has been *made firm* (*uḥkimat ʿibāratuhā*) by being protected
from ambiguity and confusion," and *mutashābihāt* as "ambiguous, bearing multiple senses, to which the
muḥkamāt are the *umm* (mother) — the ambiguous are carried back to and resolved by them," giving as examples
the anthropomorphic-seeming verses (*lā tudrikuhu al-abṣār* vs *ilā rabbihā nāẓira*).

## 3. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (on the muqaṭṭaʿāt and v 7)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` (muqaṭṭaʿāt discussion,
lines ~980-1060; munāsaba threads passim).

al-Rāzī treats the muqaṭṭaʿāt at length (drawing on his Q 2 treatment), surveying the views that they are
(a) God's secret, (b) names of the surahs, (c) oaths, (d) abbreviations of divine names, or (e) phonetic
challenges. On the *munāsaba* he relates the disconnected letters to the surah's content by noting (line
~1057) that "there is a correspondence (*munāsaba*) between them [the letters] and [the surah], and were it
not for this correspondence the letters would have admitted iʿrāb [grammatical inflection]" — i.e. their
indeclinability marks them as a special sign-class. al-Rāzī's hallmark is the dense *masāʾil* enumeration: he
poses the metaphysical question of how a verse can be "ambiguous" if the Qurʾān is *mubīn* (clear), and
answers via the *rāsikhūn fī al-ʿilm* — whether the *wāw* in *wa-l-rāsikhūn* is conjunctive (the firm-grounded
DO know the taʾwīl) or a new sentence (only God knows it), which is the great qirāʾa-and-theology crux of v 7.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (on Q 3:1, 7)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/3/{1,7}.json`.

**On v 1 (the muqaṭṭaʿāt):** al-Qurṭubī reports that "the people of taʾwīl differed over the letters at the
heads of the sūras." He cites **al-Shaʿbī, Sufyān al-Thawrī, and a group of traditionists**: "they are God's
secret in the Qurʾān; God has in every one of His Books a secret — they are of the *mutashābih* that God alone
knows; one must not speak about them, but believe in them and recite them as they came." This view he traces
to **Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq and ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib**, and via al-Samarqandī to **ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, and Ibn Masʿūd**:
"the disconnected letters are of the concealed (*al-maktūm*) which is not interpreted." He links them to
al-Rabīʿ b. Khuthaym's saying that God "kept to Himself the knowledge of part of the Qurʾān" — making the
muqaṭṭaʿāt a *test* (*ikhtibār / imtiḥān*) of faith in the unseen.

**On v 7:** al-Qurṭubī opens with **Muslim's ḥadīth from ʿĀʾisha**: the Prophet recited *huwa alladhī anzala
ʿalayka al-kitāba minhu āyātun muḥkamāt …* and said, "When you see those who follow what is ambiguous of it,
those are the ones God named [the deviant] — so beware of them (*fa-ḥdharūhum*)." He then relates Abū Umāma's
encounter with the heads of the Khārijites set on the steps of the Damascus mosque, and Abū Umāma's
application of *wa-lā takūnū ka-lladhīna tafarraqū* to them — making v 7 the locus-classicus for warning
against the *ahl al-zaygh* who weaponise the ambiguous verses.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (on Q 3:1-2)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/3/1.json`;
Arabic at `.../ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/3/`.

Ibn Kathīr's section is headed "Which was revealed in Al-Madina." He fixes the **occasion of revelation**:
"Surah Āl ʿImrān was revealed in al-Madīnah, as evident by the fact that the first eighty-three āyāt relate to
the **delegation from Najrān** that arrived in al-Madīnah in the ninth year of Hijra; we will elaborate when
we explain the āya about the **Mubāhala** (3:61)." On the faḍāʾil he refers back to his Q 2 introduction (where
he cites the *al-Zahrāwān* ḥadīth, Muslim). On v 2 (*Allāhu lā ilāha illā huwa al-ḥayy al-qayyūm*) he writes:
"We mentioned the ḥadīth in the Tafsīr of Āyat al-Kursī (2:255) that mentions that **Allāh's Greatest Name
(al-ism al-aʿẓam)** is contained in these two āyāt" — explicitly bridging Q 3:2 to Q 2:255 (the basis of
Tirmidhī #3562; see `04-hadith-corpus.md`).

## 6. Points of agreement / disagreement

| Question | al-Ṭabarī | al-Zamakhsharī | al-Rāzī | al-Qurṭubī | Ibn Kathīr |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| muqaṭṭaʿāt (v 1) | (treated in Q2) sign-class | grammatical (mīm vowel) | God's-secret / names / oaths survey | **God's secret** (al-Shaʿbī, al-Thawrī) | (refers to Q2) |
| muḥkam = | clear/decisive, *aṣl al-kitāb* | firm expression, the *umm* | the unambiguous *mubīn* | clear, the refuge | clear |
| mutashābih = | multi-faced wording | ambiguous, resolved by muḥkam | the great *wāw* crux | followed by ahl al-zaygh | — |
| occasion | — | — | — | (v 7 → Khārijites) | **Najrān delegation, year 9** |
| Greatest Name (v 2) | — | — | — | — | **= Āyat al-Kursī (2:255)** |

The defining exegetical fault-line of Q 3 is the **v-7 *wāw* of *al-rāsikhūn***: does the sentence read "and
none knows its interpretation except God — and the firmly-grounded in knowledge say 'we believe'" (taʾwīl
reserved to God) or "except God AND the firmly-grounded in knowledge [who] say 'we believe'" (the scholars
share the taʾwīl)? This single conjunction governs the entire Sunnī/rationalist debate on the limits of
interpretation. The mufassirūn agree on the *function* of muḥkam/mutashābih (the clear verses anchor the
ambiguous) but split on this syntactic-theological crux.
