# Q 66 al-Taḥrīm — Tafsīr Survey

Five classical commentaries surveyed from the on-disk corpus. Source files cited per passage. The
central exegetical question is the *sabab al-nuzūl* of v 1 (what lawful thing did the Prophet forbid
himself?) — on which the tradition splits two ways. I have read the Arabic of all five.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (on Q 66:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/66/1.json`.

al-Ṭabarī glosses v 1 as: "[O Prophet] forbidding to yourself what Allāh has made lawful for you, seeking
thereby the pleasure of your wives." He then explicitly reports the scholarly disagreement
(*wa-khtalafa ahl al-ʿilm fī al-ḥalāl alladhī kāna Allāh aḥallahu li-rasūlihi fa-ḥarramahu ʿalā nafsihi*):

- **Position 1 (Māriya):** "It was Māriya his Coptic bondwoman; he forbade her to himself by an oath that
  he would not approach her, seeking the satisfaction of Ḥafṣa bint ʿUmar his wife, because she had become
  jealous when the Messenger was alone with her [Māriya] on Ḥafṣa's day and in her chamber." al-Ṭabarī cites
  this via **Zayd b. Aslam** (isnād: Muḥammad b. ʿAbd al-Raḥīm al-Barqī → Ibn Abī Maryam → Abū Ghassān →
  Zayd b. Aslam) and via **al-Shaʿbī ← Masrūq** ("the Prophet forbade his bondwoman and swore off her, so
  he made the lawful forbidden"). al-Ṭabarī adds Zayd's legal note that the formula *anta ʿalayya ḥarām*
  ("you are forbidden to me") is *laghw* — idle, of no binding force in itself; the binding element is the oath.

The *taḥilla* of v 2 is glossed (via al-Shaʿbī ← Masrūq) as the command to expiate (*al-kaffāra*) the oath.

**Empirical correlate:** the Māriya/Ḥafṣa jealousy frame matches the al-Wāḥidī *Asbāb* isnād (Ibn ʿAbbās ←
ʿUmar) on disk (see `04-hadith-corpus.md`).

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf* (on Q 66:1-2)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` lines 66958-67008.

al-Zamakhsharī narrates the **Māriya version** in full ("the Messenger was alone with Māriya on ʿĀʾisha's
day; Ḥafṣa learned of it; he said 'keep my secret — I have forbidden Māriya to myself — and I give you
glad tidings that Abū Bakr and ʿUmar will govern my community after me'; she told ʿĀʾisha, the two being
confidantes"), then gives the **honey version** ("he drank honey in the house of Zaynab bint Jaḥsh; ʿĀʾisha
and Ḥafṣa colluded and said 'we smell maghāfir on you' … so he forbade the honey").

Theologically he is sharp: forbidding the lawful "was a lapse (*zalla*) on his part, for it is not for
anyone to forbid what Allāh has made lawful, since Allāh made lawful only by a wisdom and benefit He knew
in His making-it-lawful; so when he forbade it, that was turning a benefit into a corruption" — hence
*ghafūr* ("He has forgiven you what you slipped in") *raḥīm* ("He has had mercy on you and not taken you
to task"). On *taḥilla* he gives two senses: (1) the legal *istithnāʾ* ("in shāʾ Allāh") that prevents the
oath from binding, and (2) dissolution by *kaffāra* (expiation), citing the ḥadīth *lā tamūt li-rajul
thalāthat awlād … illā taḥillat al-qasam* and a line of Dhū al-Rumma.

He surveys the legal disagreement on *taḥrīm al-ḥalāl* (Abū Ḥanīfa: it is an oath in everything; al-Shāfiʿī:
not an oath but a cause for *kaffāra* in the case of wives only; and the variant ṣaḥāba positions — Abū
Bakr, ʿUmar, Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, Zayd: it is an oath; ʿAlī: triple [divorce]; ʿUthmān: *ẓihār*).
On whether the Prophet expiated: al-Ḥasan says he did not (being already forgiven), it was a *teaching*
for the believers; Muqātil says he freed a slave for the forbidding of Māriya.

## 3. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (on Q 66:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` lines 243800-243859.

al-Rāzī opens with the **munāsaba** (relation to the preceding surah): "Its connection to what precedes
[Q 65 al-Ṭalāq]: they share the rulings specific to women (*al-aḥkām al-makhṣūṣa bi-l-nisāʾ*), and the
address by divorce at the head of that surah parallels the address by prohibition at the head of this one,
since divorce in most or all of its forms involves forbidding what Allāh made lawful." He also ties the
*end* of al-Ṭalāq (which proclaims Allāh's majesty and power) to the opening reproach here (forbidding the
lawful runs against that omnipotence).

al-Rāzī **explicitly copies al-Kashshāf** ("qāla fī al-Kashshāf …") for the Māriya/honey narrations — our
on-disk comparison confirms his text matches al-Zamakhsharī's near-verbatim. He attributes the Māriya-reading
to "al-Ḥasan, Mujāhid, Qatāda, al-Shaʿbī, Masrūq, and Thābit's report from Anas."

He then poses **three masāʾil** (al-baḥth al-awwal/thānī/thālith):
1. **Is v 1 reproach (*ʿitāb*) or alert (*tanbīh*)?** Reproach seems to clash with the prophetic honor in
   *yā ayyuhā al-nabī*; al-Rāzī concludes "the apparent sense is that this address is NOT by way of reproach
   but by way of alerting (*tanbīh*) that what issued from him was not as it ought to have been."
2. **How can he "forbid what Allāh made lawful" — that is metaphysically impossible (a thing cannot be both
   weighted-to-lawful and weighted-to-forbidden)?** al-Rāzī: the *taḥrīm* meant is *abstention from use*,
   not *belief that it is forbidden* — the Prophet abstained while still believing it lawful; "whoever
   believes that this is the forbidding of what Allāh made lawful in itself has disbelieved."
3. **What is the legal ruling of forbidding the lawful?** He lays out Abū Ḥanīfa's vs al-Shāfiʿī's positions
   (matching al-Kashshāf) and defers the ṣaḥāba disagreement to al-Kashshāf.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (on Q 66:1, 11, 12)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/66/{1,11,12}.json`.

**On v 1:** "Sūrat al-Taḥrīm, Medinan by all accounts (*madaniyya fī qawl al-jamīʿ*), and it is twelve verses,
also called *Sūrat al-Nabī*." He then leads with the **honey version** from **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim** (ʿĀʾisha:
"he used to stay with Zaynab bint Jaḥsh and drink honey there; Ḥafṣa and I colluded that whichever of us he
entered upon would say 'I smell maghāfir on you' … so it was revealed *lima tuḥarrimu* … *idh asarra al-nabī*
referring to 'I drank honey'"). al-Qurṭubī treats v 1 under **five masāʾil**.

**On v 11 (Āsiya):** al-Qurṭubī names the wife of Firʿawn as **Āsiya bint Muzāḥim** and cites **Yaḥyā b.
Sallām**: "His saying *ḍaraba Allāh mathalan li-lladhīna kafarū* is a parable Allāh struck to warn ʿĀʾisha
and Ḥafṣa over their opposition when they backed each other against the Messenger; then He struck for them
a parable of the wife of Firʿawn and Maryam bint ʿImrān — encouraging clinging to obedience and steadfastness
in religion." **This is the project-relevant reading**: the dual-exemplar seal (block D) is exegetically a
*return* to the wives' episode (block B), the female exemplars functioning as direct admonition.

**On v 12 (Maryam):** he discusses *aḥṣanat farjahā* and the *jayb* (collar) reading of the breathing-of-the-Spirit
(citing the Ubayy variant *fa-nafakhnā fī jaybihā*), cross-referencing the end of Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (on Q 66:1, 9-12)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/66/{1,10}.json`;
Arabic at `.../ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/66/`.

**On v 1:** Ibn Kathīr's section-title is "Allāh censures His Prophet for Prohibiting Himself from what He
has allowed for Him." He leads with **al-Bukhārī's** honey narration (ʿUbayd b. ʿUmayr ← ʿĀʾisha: "he used
to stay with Zaynab bint Jaḥsh and drink honey … so I and Ḥafṣa decided …"), giving the Prophet's reply
verbatim: *lā, bal sharibtu ʿasalan ʿinda Zaynab bint Jaḥsh wa-lan aʿūda lah* ("No, I drank honey at Zaynab's
and will never return to it"). He identifies *idh asarra al-nabī ilā baʿḍi azwājihi ḥadīthan* (v 3) as the
secret "I drank honey," and *in tatūbā* (v 4) as referring to ʿĀʾisha and Ḥafṣa.

**On v 9:** "The Command for Jihād against the Disbelievers and the Hypocrites — Allāh orders His Messenger
to perform jihād against the disbelievers with weapons and the hypocrites by establishing Allāh's penal
code"; *wa-ghluẓ ʿalayhim* "in this life," *wa-maʾwāhum jahannamu* "in the Hereafter." (Ibn Kathīr does not
flag the v 9 / Q 9:73 verbatim identity — that is the project-internal Q066-F-01 Arm A finding.)

**On vv 10-12:** "The Disbeliever shall never benefit from his Believing Relative … and the Believer is not
harmed by living with a disbeliever" — the wife of Nūḥ and the wife of Lūṭ as the negative exemplars; Āsiya
and Maryam as the positive. He stresses the lesson is the **independence of each soul's fate** from marital
proximity (a disbelieving wife of a prophet is not saved; a believing wife of a tyrant is saved).

## 6. Points of agreement / disagreement

| Question | al-Ṭabarī | al-Zamakhsharī | al-Rāzī | al-Qurṭubī | Ibn Kathīr |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Forbidden thing | **Māriya** (primary) | Māriya + honey | Māriya (primary; cites al-Kashshāf) | **honey** (leads w/ Muslim) | **honey** (leads w/ Bukhārī) |
| v 1 = reproach? | reproach-tone | *zalla* (lapse) | **tanbīh, not ʿitāb** | aḥkām-focused | "censure" |
| *taḥilla* (v 2) | kaffāra | istithnāʾ + kaffāra | kaffāra | aḥkām al-aymān | kaffāra |
| Did Prophet expiate? | — | al-Ḥasan no / Muqātil yes | — | — | — |
| Dual-exemplar seal | exemplar parable | — | — | **admonition to ʿĀʾisha+Ḥafṣa (Yaḥyā b. Sallām)** | independence-of-fate |

The **two-version split** (Māriya vs honey) is the defining exegetical fault-line: the earlier/Kūfan-leaning
chains (al-Shaʿbī ← Masrūq, Zayd b. Aslam) favor Māriya; the Ṣaḥīḥayn honey-narration (ʿUbayd b. ʿUmayr ←
ʿĀʾisha) is what al-Qurṭubī and Ibn Kathīr lead with. Several mufassirūn hold both events occurred. The
empirically-relevant exegetical claim is al-Qurṭubī's **ring-reading** (block D ← block B), which motivates
Q066-F-01 Arm B.
