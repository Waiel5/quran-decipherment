## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Infiṭār** (sometimes *Idhā al-Samāʾu Infaṭarat* by its opening incipit). Arabic *infaṭara* is the form-VII (reflexive) of the root *f-ṭ-r*, which carries the dual semantic of (a) cleaving/splitting and (b) creating/originating ex nihilo (the same root produces *fāṭir al-samāwāt* = "the Originator/Cleaver of the heavens"). The form-VII *infaṭara* denotes a passive-spontaneous cleaving — "to split apart of itself" — which is the precise eschatological imagery: the sky tearing itself open by divine fiat at the End.

The verbal-noun *infiṭār* is the abstract noun for this passive-spontaneous splitting. Classical naming-tradition (al-Bukhārī, Muslim, al-Tirmidhī, al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī) uses both *Sūrat al-Infiṭār* and *Sūrat Idhā al-Samāʾu Infaṭarat*. The surah's name foregrounds the cosmic-cleaving image — a single morphological signature carrying both eschatological-violence (sky-splits) and creation-doctrine (the same Creator who made it now splits it).

The root *f-ṭ-r* is one of the most theologically-loaded roots in the Quran: it produces *al-Fāṭir* (a divine name appearing 6× in the corpus), *fiṭra* (the primordial nature, Q 30:30), and *infaṭara* (the cosmic-cleaving, Q 82:1, Q 73:18). The choice of *infaṭara* over the more common eschatological roots *sh-q-q* (cleave/split, Q 84:1) or *t-w-y* (rolling-up, Q 81:1) is rhetorically deliberate: it inscribes the End onto the same ontological axis as the Beginning — what was *fuṭira* (originated) shall be *infaṭara* (split apart), by the same Originator-Cleaver.

## 4. Opening formula — idhā-cosmic-event-opener

Q 82:1-5 (no-tashkeel):

| v | Text | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | إذا السماء انفطرت | *When the heaven is cleft asunder* |
| 2 | وإذا الكواكب انتثرت | *and when the stars are scattered* |
| 3 | وإذا البحار فجرت | *and when the seas are made to gush forth* |
| 4 | وإذا القبور بعثرت | *and when the graves are turned upside down* |
| 5 | علمت نفس ما قدمت وأخرت | *each soul shall know what it has sent forward and what it has held back* |

The 5-verse opening is the corpus's PUREST form of the eschatological "*idhā* cascade" — four sequential cosmic-event protases (vv. 1-4) leading to a human-knowledge apodosis (v. 5). Each protasis follows the same morphological skeleton: *idhā* + DEFINITE-NOUN + form-VII or form-V intransitive verb (*infaṭarat / intatharat / fujjirat / buʿthirat*). The four cosmic-events are: heaven (sky), stars (heavenly bodies), seas (waters), graves (earth-interior) — a four-element COSMIC INVENTORY: heaven / heavenly bodies / earth's surface-water / earth's interior.

## 5. ⭐ Q 82:17-18 — the corpus-UNIQUE doubled *wa-mā adrāka mā* construction

The surah's most architecturally-distinctive feature is in vv. 17-18:

| v | Text | Gloss |

| 17 | وما أدراك ما يوم الدين | *And what made you realize what the Day of Judgment is?* |

### Classical balāgha on the doubling

al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb*, Q 82:17-18) reads the doubling as INTENSIFICATION-VIA-REPETITION (*al-takrīr li-l-tafkhīm*): the meta-question is repeated to underscore the ineffability of *yawm al-dīn* — the human cannot grasp the Day even through one meta-question, requiring a second iteration.

al-Zamakhsharī (*al-Kashshāf*, Q 82:17-18) reads the doubling as a RHETORICAL TURN-OF-AMPLIFICATION: the *thumma* in v. 18 is not strictly temporal but discursive — "then [furthermore] what made you realize" — signaling that the answer to v. 17's meta-question reveals an even deeper unanswerability in v. 18.

al-Bāqillānī (*Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*) lists the Q 82:17-18 doubling as one of the corpus's *iʿjāz al-fawāṣil* exemplars: the rhetorical figure of *takrīr al-istifhām* (repetition of meta-question) across consecutive verses with the same NP creates a uniquely Quranic rhetorical signature — a feature the doubling produces nowhere else in the corpus.
