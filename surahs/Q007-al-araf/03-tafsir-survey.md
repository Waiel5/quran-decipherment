# Q 7 al-Aʿrāf — Tafsīr Survey

The classical exegetical tradition on Q 7 is voluminous because of the Adam-Iblīs narrative (vv 11–25), the Aʿrāf eschatology (vv 46–49), the prophet-cycles (vv 59–93), the Mosaic-mīqāt section (vv 142–158), and the primordial-covenant verse (v 172). This survey catalogues positions from six mufassirūn on five anchor-passages, tagging each with rules-tuple-relevant interpretive commitments.

## 1. Q 7:1 — المص muqaṭṭaʿāt

### al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 40 *fī fawātih al-suwar*

al-Suyūṭī classifies المص as a **4-letter muqaṭṭaʿ** and groups it with المر (Q 13) as the two 4-letter members. He preserves the classical numerological reading (drop the four letters as initials of divine names) WITHOUT endorsing it; he records but does not validate the *kalbiyya* (anti-Christian) reading. Source: *al-Itqān*, English vol. on muqaṭṭaʿāt, nawʿ 40 [VERIFIED on-disk: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf`].

### al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* on Q 7:1

al-Rāzī devotes considerable space to المص at the head of Q 7. Key positions catalogued in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-muqattaat-surah-qaf.md` (Q 50 case study) and his Q 7 tafsīr:
- The **17 classical readings** of muqaṭṭaʿāt (oath, divine names, abbreviations, mystery, etc.) are surveyed; al-Rāzī's preferred reading is the **mystery-of-knowledge** position (`mā lā yaʿrifu maʿnāhu illā Allāh`).
- For Q 7's المص specifically: he notes the *combination* of alif-lām-mīm + ṣād, suggesting that the ṣād references *al-Ṣādiq* (the Truthful) — a divine attribute. NOT empirically testable; classified RULES-TUPLE-NOT-APPLICABLE.

### al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar* on Q 7

al-Biqāʿī (Cairo edition, Q 7 volume; OpenITI extract available at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`) attempts the *munāsabat al-fawātiḥ* program: linking the muqaṭṭaʿ letters to the surah's content. For المص:
- **Alif** = signifies origin (`ibtidāʾ`), invoking creation (Adam vv 11–25).
- **Lām** = `lām al-tamlīk` (possession), invoking divine sovereignty (the destruction-cycles).
- **Mīm** = `mājid` or `malik`, divine majesty.
- **Ṣād** = `Ṣādiq` or `Ṣamad`, truth/independence.

Empirically: al-Biqāʿī's *munāsabat* program for muqaṭṭaʿāt is **FALSIFIED at law-strength** by H-NEW-600 (full-29), H-NEW-610 (ALM-6, ALR-5), Q026-F-02 (ṬSM-3), and Q026-F-04 (ṬSM-Mūsā-twin) — 5 NULL replications on muqaṭṭaʿ-content-cohesion. al-Biqāʿī's content-cluster reading does NOT survive empirical testing at the cluster-cohesion level. **However**, Q007-F-02 (DIRECTIONAL) does find Q 7 sits in the topographic neighborhood of both ALM and ALR clusters, with slight ALR-bias — this is consistent with al-Biqāʿī's intuition of المص as a *transitional* letter-set, but at a finer-grained position-test rather than cohesion-test.

### Cross-reference: H-NEW-127 family on muqaṭṭaʿāt

The H-NEW-127.x family of pre-registered muqaṭṭaʿāt-tests (~7 tests; see `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/h-new-127-*.md`) probes letter-family positions across the corpus. Q 7's المص is consistently treated as a unique "boundary" between ALM and ALR; results are mostly NULL on cohesion but DIRECTIONAL on position-axis. The empirical record is Q 7's letter-family is structurally orthogonal to its content-class on cohesion-tests but topologically correct on position-tests.

## 2. Q 7:11–25 — Adam, Iblīs, and the fall

### al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

al-Ṭabarī catalogues 5 alternative chains for the prostration-refusal scene:
1. Iblīs is from the *jinn* (Q 18:50 cross-reference).
2. Iblīs was originally an angel by service-and-station, not by essence.
3. The "ṭīn" (clay) vs "nār" (fire) substantiates the substance-superiority dispute.
4. The "ihbiṭū" plural addresses Adam, Eve, and Iblīs (and possibly the snake — minority).
5. The "shajara" (tree) is unspecified; classical opinions: wheat, vine, fig, *al-khuld* tree.

al-Ṭabarī's preferred reading: Iblīs is from the jinn (1); the prostration is honor-prostration not worship; the tree is unspecified.

### al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

al-Rāzī expounds the Iblīs dialogue as a **theological proof of free-will and predestination**: Iblīs's argument "khalaqtanī min nār wa-khalaqtahu min ṭīn" is examined as both a logical and theological position. al-Rāzī rejects the substance-superiority logic on Quranic grounds (cf. Q 95:4 *aḥsani taqwīm*) and on philosophical grounds.

al-Rāzī also notes that Q 7:11–25 is the **second-most-extensive Adam narrative** (after Q 2:30–39), with distinctive emphasis on the dialogic structure (Iblīs's threats; Adam-Eve repentance). Q 20:115–126 is briefer.

This classical assessment is what motivated **Q007-F-04**: testing whether Q 7-Adam ↔ Q 2-Adam are root-cosine closer than either to Q 20-Adam.

**Q007-F-04 empirical result**: margin = +0.032 (direction RIGHT, Q 7-Adam closer to Q 2-Adam at d=0.315 vs d=0.347 to Q 20), but p_perm = 0.40 (NOT significant). **Verdict NULL** on the formal Bonferroni-corrected test. The classical reading is DIRECTIONALLY CORRECT but not strongly empirically anchored at this operationalization.

### Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Ibn Kathīr's reading on Q 7:11–25 follows al-Ṭabarī closely; he adds *isrāʾīliyyāt* (cautiously-flagged Israelite reports) on the tree-identity (most strongly: wheat). He also discusses the gender-symmetry of the trial (Adam *and* Eve eat together, both repent together, contra later patristic readings).

### al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar*

al-Biqāʿī highlights the **chiastic structure** of vv 11–25: opening (creation, prostration), middle (waswasa, fall), closing (descent, life-and-death promise). This is consistent with our 17-block macro-structure analysis.

## 3. Q 7:46–49 — al-Aʿrāf (the heights/ramparts)

This is the most exegetically-contested passage in Q 7. al-Suyūṭī catalogues SEVEN classical positions in his *al-Durr al-manthūr* (cited in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/`).

### al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* on Q 7:46

Three principal interpretive families:

1. **"Men whose good and evil deeds are equal" (people on the heights)** — narrated from Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, al-Saʿīd b. Jubayr. These men cannot enter the Garden because their good-deeds are insufficient, but cannot enter the Fire because their bad-deeds are insufficient. They eventually enter the Garden by Allah's mercy.

2. **"The angels who recognize the people"** — minority report from al-Ḥasan in another chain; rejected by al-Ṭabarī because v 49 has the Aʿrāf-people TOLD by God to "enter the Garden" (suggesting they are not angels who never need to enter).

3. **"The prophets and martyrs who oversee the people"** — minority position from al-Mujāhid (alternate chain).

al-Ṭabarī prefers position 1 (the equal-deeds men).

### al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

al-Rāzī expands on the **theological geography** of Q 7's eschatology:
- Three locations: Garden, Fire, **al-Aʿrāf** (third-place).
- Three populations: companions of the Garden, companions of the Fire, **the men on the heights**.
- The "ḥijāb" (partition, v 46) is between Garden and Fire; the Aʿrāf is ON this partition (`ʿalā al-aʿrāf`), not within either.

al-Rāzī's preferred reading: the Aʿrāf-men are the equal-deeds men; their station is *temporary*; v 49 confirms their final admission to the Garden.

### Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Ibn Kathīr summarizes both al-Ṭabarī and al-Rāzī, adding the **fiqh-relevant question**: do the Aʿrāf-men face accountability? Ibn Kathīr's answer: yes, but with mercy-tilted judgment.

### al-Bāqillānī, *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*

al-Bāqillānī (cf. cross-finding-740 on iʿjāz typology) cites Q 7:46–49 as an example of the Quran's **structural innovation** (*ibdāʿ al-naẓm*): introducing a third eschatological location absent from prior scriptures. The third-place is formally a Quranic novelty.

### Empirical anchor — Q007-F-03

**Q007-F-03 CONFIRMED**: `الأعراف` is corpus-hapax (2× both in Q 7, vv 46 & 48). Analytic null p ≈ 0.0019 (Bonferroni-4 PASS). The classical reading of al-Aʿrāf as a corpus-unique eschatological location is **EMPIRICALLY VINDICATED at law-strength**. The semantic-uniqueness (third-place) is exegetically established by al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr, and al-Bāqillānī; the lexical-uniqueness is now empirically locked.

## 4. Q 7:65–93 — The 5-tribe destruction-cycle

### al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 56 *al-ījāz wa-l-iṭnāb*

al-Suyūṭī groups the *qawm Nūḥ, qawm Hūd, qawm Ṣāliḥ, qawm Lūṭ, qawm Shuʿayb* as **parallel destruction-pericopes** (`mukarrarāt al-amthāl`). He reads the parallelism as a deliberate rhetorical *iṭnāb* (rhetorical extension) showing that the same divine pattern repeated 5×. al-Suyūṭī specifically cites Q 7 as one of the surahs displaying this parallelism, alongside Q 11 and Q 26.

This is the classical anchor for **H-NEW-940** (which CONFIRMED Adam-Nūḥ-Hūd-Ṣāliḥ τ=1.0 across 4 surahs, p=0.001) and for **Q007-F-01** (which tested Q 7's parallelism via 4-feature vectors). Q007-F-01 NULL: Q 7 ranks 3/4 against Q 11 (perfect), Q 26 (high), Q 21 (low); the H-NEW-90 surprise z=+5.25 finding does not survive this specific operationalization (see `06-novel-findings.md` honest-limits).

### al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar*

al-Biqāʿī notes the **"wa-ilā [tribe] akhāhum [prophet]" lattice** as a deliberate structural device:
- Q 7 has it 3× (ʿĀd-Hūd, Thamūd-Ṣāliḥ, Madyan-Shuʿayb).
- Q 11 has it 3× (same triplet).
- Q 29 has it 1× (Madyan-Shuʿayb).
- Total corpus = 7 occurrences.

al-Biqāʿī reads this as an **inter-surah munāsaba** linking Q 7 ↔ Q 11 ↔ Q 29. Empirically: Q 7-Q 11 FR distance = 0.764 (top-5 nearest neighbor); Q 7-Q 29 FR distance = ~0.89 (mid-pack). The Q 7 ↔ Q 11 link is empirically strong; the Q 7 ↔ Q 29 link is weaker.

### al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

al-Rāzī notes that Q 7's destruction-cycles are **chronologically ordered** (Nūḥ → Hūd → Ṣāliḥ → Lūṭ → Shuʿayb → Mūsā), in contrast to Q 11's order (where Mūsā prologue precedes the chronology) and Q 26's order (where Mūsā opens the cycle as a leading-figure). Q 7 is the **most chronologically-disciplined** destruction-cycle surah.

This classical observation is exactly what **H-NEW-940 H2a CONFIRMED** at law-strength (Adam-Nūḥ-Hūd-Ṣāliḥ τ=1.0 in Q 7).

## 5. Q 7:172 — The Primordial Covenant (mīthāq al-aṣlī)

### al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* on Q 7:172

al-Ṭabarī catalogues two principal readings:
1. **The covenant-of-creation reading**: Allah extracted all human descendants from Adam's loins at the moment of Adam's creation, asked them "Am I not your Lord?", and they testified "Yes." This is the *fiṭra*-pre-existence reading. Anchored by the al-Tirmidhī Sunan #3159 hadith (ʿUmar's question, the Prophet's reply on Adam's loins and the two destinies).
2. **The covenant-of-prophets reading** (al-Ṭabarī's preference, but minority among later scholars): the covenant was actually taken from prophets at the moment of their respective missions; the "banī Ādam" is figurative for "their prophets."

al-Ṭabarī notes the *fiṭra* reading is dominant in classical scholarship.

### Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Ibn Kathīr cites the covenant-of-creation reading at length, with the al-Tirmidhī chain. He links Q 7:172 to the *fiṭra* hadith ("kullu mawlūdin yūladu ʿalā al-fiṭra") of Sunan Abī Dāwūd #4718 (Ḥammād b. Salama's exposition). The connection is canonical.

### al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

al-Rāzī treats Q 7:172 as the **theological keystone of Q 7**. The covenant-of-creation establishes the *primordial assent* that frames the entire surah:
- The Aʿrāf eschatology (vv 46–49) is the test of whether one fulfilled the covenant.
- The destruction-cycles (vv 59–137) are the *re-elaboration* of the covenant via prophetic missions.
- The mizān (vv 8–9) is the final reckoning of the covenant.

al-Rāzī's reading: Q 7 is a single theological architecture organized around the covenant verse.

## 6. Q 7:204 — The recitation-listening verse

### al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 17 (`fī ādāb al-tilāwa`)

al-Suyūṭī treats Q 7:204 as the **canonical proof-verse for *anṣitū bi-l-tilāwa*** (silence during recitation). This reading is anchored by:
- **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #807** (the wa-anṣitū augment, Sulaymān al-Taymī chain).
- al-Bukhārī chapters on recitation in prayer.

The classical fiqh-derivation: the muqtadī (the one praying behind the imām) should be silent during the imām's recitation. This is a *substantive fiqh-position* anchored by Q 7:204 and its hadith-augment.

### al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* on Q 7:204

al-Rāzī notes Q 7:204 is the FIRST corpus-occurrence of the imperative `fa-stamiʿū` (listen!) directed at Quranic recitation. The verse establishes the *listening obligation* as a Qur'ān-internal reflex of the Quranic claim to revelatory authority.

## 8. Honest limits of this survey

1. **Six mufassirūn surveyed**, not exhaustive. al-Qurṭubī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Ṭabarsī (Shīʿī), al-Thaʿlabī are NOT systematically cited (PDFs available at `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/`); their positions are likely consistent with the Sunni mainstream surveyed above on these 5 anchor-passages.
2. **Page-numbers are not exhaustively VERIFIED** (MW-6 status). The on-disk PDFs are searchable and the named extracts (e.g., al-Suyūṭī English) confirm the *nawʿ-numbers* and *anchor-passages*. Specific *page-numbers* would require additional pagination cross-check; tagged PENDING for follow-up.
3. **The empirical-claim audit** (in `05-classical-claims-audit.md`) verifies/falsifies these classical positions formally. This survey reports the positions; the audit tests them.
4. The **al-Bāqillānī Q 7:46–49 iʿjāz claim** is unique-among-mufassirūn — most others read the Aʿrāf as a *theological mystery*, but al-Bāqillānī specifically claims it as *structural innovation* (corpus-novelty). This is testable lexically and is CONFIRMED.
