## 2. Block 1 (vv 1–9) — المص + prologue

- **v 1**: المص (corpus-unique 4-letter muqaṭṭaʿ).
- **v 2**: "kitābun unzila ilayka" — book-reference, fits the cross-finding-008 pattern (muqaṭṭaʿāt → book-reference).
- **vv 3–4**: command to follow the revelation; warning of destroyed cities.
- **vv 6–7**: corpus-rare divine self-reference "fa-la-nasʾalanna alladhīna ursila ilayhim wa-la-nasʾalanna al-mursalīn / fa-la-naquṣṣanna ʿalayhim bi-ʿilm" — God commits to questioning both messengers and recipients.
- **vv 8–9**: scales (al-mawāzīn) eschatology — first appearance of the mizān motif that frames the whole surah.

QAC root highlights: `ktb` (v 2), `Aty` and `*hb` (v 4), `rsl` (vv 6–7), `wzn` (v 8), `Tql` (v 8).

## 3. Block 2 (vv 10–25) — Adam-Iblīs

- **vv 10**: prologue — humans placed on earth with `maʿāyish` (livelihoods).
- **vv 11–18**: creation, prostration, refusal of Iblīs, dialogue ("ana khayrun minhu khalaqtanī min nārin wa-khalaqtahu min ṭīn"), expulsion, Iblīs's vow ("la-aqʿudanna lahum ṣirāṭaka al-mustaqīm"), divine threat.
- **vv 19–25**: the garden-trial; waswasa-from-Shayṭān; tasting the tree; uncovered nakedness; descent from the garden ("ihbiṭū") with the dual-line "fīhā taḥyawna wa-fīhā tamūtūna wa-minhā tukhrajūn" (v 25).

QAC root highlights for this block: `xlq` (vv 11, 12), `sjd` (v 11), `Hyy` (v 25), `Awl` (Iblīs ref vv 14, 18), `was` (v 20). 115 root-tokens total in this 15-verse block (Q007-F-04 input).

This is the **second-most-extensive Adam narrative in the corpus** (Q 2:30–39 has 102 root-tokens, Q 20:115–126 has 74 tokens). Q007-F-04 tested whether Q 7's Adam ↔ Q 2's Adam are root-cosine closer than either to Q 20: result NULL (margin=+0.032, p_perm=0.40 — direction RIGHT, magnitude not significant).

## 4. Block 3 (vv 26–36) — Banī Ādam admonitions

The four "yā banī Ādam" (Children of Adam) refrains:
- v 26: clothing as a sign — covering nakedness.
- v 27: warning against Shayṭān.
- v 31: take your **zīna** (adornment) at every mosque — eat and drink but waste not. **Q 7:31 is a corpus-hadith-anchored verse** (cited in al-Bukhārī's *kitāb al-libās* topic on dressing for prayer).
- v 35: messengers come from among you reciting our signs.

The four-fold "yā banī Ādam" address is **also corpus-rare** — Q 36:60, Q 17:70 use related forms ("yā ayyuhā al-nāsu" or "yā ayyuhā alladhīna ʾāmanū" are the dominant address-forms; "yā banī Ādam" appears more locally in Q 7).

Cross-corpus check on `يا بني آدم` token (computed): only Q 7 has it 4× contiguously; Q 17, Q 19, Q 36 each have 1×. So **Q 7's 4-fold yā-banī-Ādam refrain is corpus-distinctive** (post-hoc note for follow-up; not pre-registered as a primary test).

## 5. Block 4 (vv 37–53) — Eschatology + **al-Aʿrāf**

- vv 37–41: the deniers; angelic messengers; "lā tufattaḥu lahum abwābu al-samāʾi" (the camel through the eye of the needle simile, v 40).
- vv 42–43: people of the Garden; their gratitude.
- vv 44–45: Garden vs Fire dialogue; muezzin's cry.
- **vv 46–49: AL-AʿRĀF** — the corpus-unique third-place. "And between them is a partition; on the Aʿrāf are men who recognize each [group] by signs." (v 46); "the people of the Aʿrāf called out to men they recognized" (v 48); enter the Garden (v 49).
- v 50: the Fire-people calling for water; "Allah has forbidden them to the disbelievers."
- vv 51–53: critique of religion-as-play.

**Q007-F-03 verdict CONFIRMED**: `الأعراف` corpus-hapax (2× both in Q 7); analytic null p ≈ 0.0019 (passes Bonferroni-4).

The semantic role — "men on the Aʿrāf" who occupy a *third place* between Garden and Fire, neither admitted nor cast away — is exegetically discussed by al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr (see `03-tafsir-survey.md`). Classical readings:
- Some report (Ibn ʿAbbās per al-Ṭabarī): "men whose good and evil deeds are equal — neither sufficient to enter the Garden nor heinous enough to enter the Fire."
- Others (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī per al-Qurṭubī): "the angels who recognize people."
- Ibn Kathīr summarizes both, leaning toward the equal-deeds-men reading.

## 6. Block 5 (vv 54–58) — Cosmological hymn

- v 54: God created the heavens and earth in 6 days, then ascended the Throne (`thumma istawā ʿalā al-ʿarsh`); the day-night, sun-moon-stars are subjugated.
- vv 55–56: invocation discipline; "do not corrupt the earth after its restoration."
- v 57: rain-cloud parable; resurrection prefiguration.
- v 58: good land vs bad land parable.

This block is THEMATICALLY ANOMALOUS in mid-surah — it interrupts the destruction-cycle narrative (which begins formally at v 59 with Nūḥ). The classical tafsir reading (al-Rāzī): the cosmological hymn FRAMES the destruction-cycles by establishing God's creative authority before showing prophetic-cycle authority.

QAC root highlights: `xlq` (v 54), `smw` (v 54), `ArD` (vv 54, 56), `ystwy` (v 54), `dEw` (v 55).

## 8. Block 11 (vv 94–102) — Theological coda

- vv 94–99: "We have not sent any prophet to a town except We have visited its people with adversity, that they might humble themselves" — the **destruction-cycle theological logic**.
- v 100: "ʾawalam yahdi li-lladhīna yarithūna al-arḍa min baʿdi ahlihā" — "Has it not become clear to those who inherit the earth after its [former] people..."
- v 101: "tilka al-qurā naquṣṣu ʿalayka min anbāʾihā" — "Those are the cities, We narrate to you of their stories."
- v 102: "wa-mā wajadnā li-aktharihim min ʿahdin" — covenant-failure refrain.

This block GLOSSES the 5 preceding tribe-cycles with a meta-narrative voice. The verb `naquṣṣu` (we narrate) at v 101 echoes Q 12:3 *naḥnu naquṣṣu ʿalayka aḥsana al-qaṣaṣ* (Yūsuf surah self-reference). Q 7 and Q 12 both use `naquṣṣu` self-referentially — see `07-cross-references.md`.

## 9. Block 12 (vv 103–137) — Mūsā vs Pharaoh

The longest single prophet-block in Q 7 (35 verses, 17% of the surah).

- v 103: prologue — "thumma baʿathnā min baʿdihim Mūsā" (sequence-marker linking to the 5-tribe cycle).
- vv 104–108: Mūsā's vocational call before Pharaoh; staff-as-snake; whitened hand.
- vv 109–119: magicians vs Mūsā contest; magicians prostrate.
- vv 120–126: magicians-as-believers; Pharaoh's threats.
- vv 127–129: Pharaoh's people complain to Mūsā.
- vv 130–135: 5 plagues (storm, locusts, lice, frogs, blood — v 133); broken promises.
- vv 136–137: drowning; inheritance of the land "wa-awratha-l-qawma alladhīna kānū yustaḍʿafūn."

This is **the pre-eschaton-cycle MŪSĀ closure** (the destruction of Pharaoh). Block 12 is the climax of the 7-prophet narrative-arc.

## 10. Block 13 (vv 138–152) — Bani Israel + Calf

- v 138: "wa-jāwaznā bi-banī isrāʾīla al-baḥra" — they crossed the sea.
- vv 138–141: idolatry-encounter; "ijʿal lanā ilāhan" (make us a god) rebuke.
- vv 142–145: 30+10 night Mīqāt; Mūsā's request to see God; the mountain shattered.
- vv 146–147: the punishment of arrogant signs-deniers.
- vv 148–152: the Calf episode.

QAC roots highlights: `Ejl` (calf), `xlq`, `xwr` (mooing), `zyn`, `tHt`.

## 11. Block 14 (vv 153–158) — Mosaic prophet-promise

- vv 153–155: forgiveness; tablets; the chosen 70.
- v 156: petition for God's mercy.
- **v 157**: "alladhīna yattabiʿūna al-rasūla al-nabiyya al-ummiyya alladhī yajidūnahu maktūban ʿindahum fī al-tawrāti wa-l-injīl" — "those who follow the unlettered Prophet whom they find written in the Torah and the Gospel." This is the **classical asbāb al-tabshīr** verse — Muḥammad's prefiguration in prior scriptures.
- v 158: "qul yā ayyuhā al-nāsu innī rasūlu Allāhi ilaykum jamīʿan" — universal mission.

Cross-references in tafsir survey.

## 12. Block 15 (vv 159–171) — Sabbath people, tablets, mountain

- v 159: "wa-min qawmi Mūsā ummatun yahdūna bi-l-ḥaqqi"
- v 160: 12 tribes, 12 springs from the rock (Mūsā striking with `ʿaṣā`).
- vv 163–166: the Sabbath transgressors becoming "khāsiʾīn qiradan" (despicable apes).
- vv 167–170: the dispersed nations; the book; the "ʿahd al-kitāb."

## 13. Block 16 (vv 172–174) — **Mīthāq al-aṣlī** (Primordial Covenant)

The single most theologically-loaded short passage in Q 7.

- **v 172**: "wa-idh akhadha rabbuka min banī ādama min ẓuhūrihim dhurriyyatahum wa-ashhadahum ʿalā anfusihim alastu bi-rabbikum qālū balā shahidnā an taqūlū yawma al-qiyāmati innā kunnā ʿan hādhā ghāfilīn." [And when your Lord took from the children of Adam, from their loins, their descendants, and made them testify of themselves, "Am I not your Lord?" They said, "Yes — we have testified." (This) lest you say on the Day of Resurrection, "We were unaware of this."]
- v 173: "or that you say, 'It was our forefathers who associated [other gods]…'"
- v 174: "and thus do We detail the verses, that they may turn back."

This is the **primordial-covenant verse**, anchored by:
- **al-Tirmidhī Ḥadīth #3159** (cited in §03 above): ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb's question about this verse, with the Prophet's reply on Adam's loins and the two destinies. Sunan al-Tirmidhī, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān* book.
- **Sunan Abī Dāwūd Ḥadīth #4718**: Ḥammād b. Salama's exposition that the *fiṭra* hadith refers to this covenant ("alastu bi-rabbikum, qālū balā").

## 14. Block 17 (vv 175–206) — Apostate-scholar parable + asmāʾ + closing

- vv 175–177: the apostate-scholar parable — "wa-tlu ʿalayhim nabaʾ alladhī ātaynāhu āyātinā fa-nsalakha minhā" (recite to them the news of him whom We gave Our signs but he stripped himself away from them) — classically identified as Balʿam b. Bāʿūrāʾ (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī).
- v 178: "man yahdi Allāhu fa-huwa al-muhtadī" — divine guidance.
- v 179: "wa-laqad dharaʾnā li-jahannama kathīran mina al-jinni wa-l-ins" — the predestination motif.
- **v 180**: "wa-li-Llāhi al-asmāʾu al-ḥusnā fa-dʿūhu bihā" — the **asmāʾ ḥusnā** verse. Classically anchored by al-Tirmidhī Sunan #3507 (the 99-names hadith via al-Walīd b. Muslim).
- vv 181–183: misguidance + drawn-out reprieve (`amlī`) + plot.
- vv 184–187: Hour-question + denial.
- vv 189–193: parable of the spouses (`khalaqakum min nafsin wāḥidatin wa-jaʿala minhā zawjahā` — v 189) — corpus-prominent creation-of-Eve verse.
- vv 194–198: idol-impotence rebuke.
- vv 199–202: ethical instruction ("khudh al-ʿafwa wa-ʾmur bi-l-ʿurf"; recourse to God against Shayṭān-instigations).
- vv 203–206: closing.
  - **v 204**: "wa-idhā quriʾa al-Qurʾānu fa-stamiʿū lahu wa-anṣitū laʿallakum turḥamūn." [And when the Qurʾān is recited, listen to it and be silent that you may receive mercy.] — anchored by **Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim #807** (which preserves the wa-anṣitū augment).
  - v 205: "wa-dhkur rabbaka fī nafsika taḍarruʿan wa-khīfatan wa-dūna al-jahri mina al-qawl bi-l-ghuduwwi wa-l-āṣāli." Quiet remembrance.
  - **v 206**: "inna alladhīna ʿinda rabbika lā yastakbirūna ʿan ʿibādatihi wa-yusabbiḥūnahu wa-lahu yasjudūn ۩." First **sajda-tilāwa** in the corpus (recitation-prostration verse, marked with the ۩ symbol). Total 14 sajdat al-tilāwa in Quran (per al-Suyūṭī al-Itqān nawʿ 78); v 206 is the FIRST in mushaf order.

## 17. Cross-surah references and self-references within Q 7

- **v 101**: `tilka al-qurā naquṣṣu ʿalayka min anbāʾihā` — self-reference to the destruction-cycle as a narrative-act. Compare Q 12:3 *naḥnu naquṣṣu ʿalayka aḥsana al-qaṣaṣ* (the Yūsuf naṣṣu).
- **v 157**: prefiguration of Muḥammad in `al-tawrāh wa-l-injīl` — invokes prior-scripture concept.
- **v 169**: covenant-of-the-Book reference → al-mīthāq.
- **v 172**: primordial covenant — anchors Q 7's eschatological geography (the original "yes" to God), combining v 172 covenant with vv 46–49 al-Aʿrāf and v 8–9 mizān-balance into a **3-tier eschatological architecture: (i) primordial covenant alastu (v 172); (ii) the third-place al-Aʿrāf (vv 46–49); (iii) the final mizān (vv 8–9)**. This is a Q 7-internal *theological geography* unique to the surah.

## 18. Content-register classification

Q 7 is **MIXED-NARRATIVE / ESCHATOLOGICAL / WISDOM** with a dominant prophet-narrative core:
- ~62% prophet-narrative (vv 11–25, 59–137).
- ~16% eschatology (vv 8–9, 37–53, 178–183, 187, 200–206).
- ~12% direct admonition (vv 26–36, 199–205).
- ~10% theological coda + cosmology (vv 54–58, 94–102).

This is structurally distinct from:
- Q 26 (predominantly prophet-narrative with refrain-cycle, less eschatology).
- Q 11 (predominantly prophet-narrative + ethics, less Adam-stuff).
- Q 21 (prophet-roster, less narrative-development).

Q 7's **3-tier eschatological architecture** (covenant → Aʿrāf → mizān) is its theological signature.

## 19. Summary

Q 7 is a **mid-Meccan-to-Late-Meccan transitional surah** structured around three distinct theological geographies (covenant, Aʿrāf, mizān) and seven sequential prophet-narratives (Adam → Nūḥ → Hūd → Ṣāliḥ → Lūṭ → Shuʿayb → Mūsā). Its content-axis is **prophet-narrative-rich and eschatology-rich**, which empirically positions Q 7 close to the ALR cluster centroid (Q007-F-02 directional) and as the **Q 6 ↔ Q 7 content-twin pair** (zero canonical-adjacency cost in mushaf).

The 7-prophet-cycle uses the `wa-ilā [tribe] akhāhum [prophet]` lattice (3× in Q 7), shared with Q 11 (3×) and Q 29 (1×) — corpus-total 7 occurrences of this formula. This makes Q 7 the **lattice-anchor surah** for the destruction-cycle template; Q 11 is the **chronicle-version sister** (per H-NEW-940 analysis); Q 26 is the **refrain-cycle sister** with a different lattice (R1+R2 paired refrain).
