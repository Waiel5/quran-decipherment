## 1. The famous *sabʿat aḥruf* episode (Hishām b. Ḥakīm + ʿUmar) — the central Q 25 reception-history hadith cluster

## 2. *fadāʾil* / virtues of recitation traditions

Beyond the *sabʿat aḥruf* cluster, the 9 canonical books contain **NO ḥadīth specifically extolling the virtue of reciting Q 25 al-Furqān** in the way that Q 36 Yāsīn, Q 18 al-Kahf, Q 67 al-Mulk, Q 112 al-Ikhlāṣ, or Q 113-114 al-Muʿawwidhatān are extolled. Q 25 lacks the *al-Mānīʿa* / *al-Munjiya* / *thuluth al-Qurʾān* type of dedicated virtues-tradition that elevates other surahs.

This is an **empirically informative null**: despite Q 25's high architectural significance (UAS rank 13/114, top-15), its *recitation-virtue-prominence* in the canonical hadith corpus is modest. This contrasts with Q 67's pattern (where high recitation-virtue is paired with modest UAS) and Q 25's pattern (where high UAS is paired with modest recitation-virtue) — the two *tabāraka alladhī* opener surahs sit on opposite sides of the recitation-virtue-vs-architectural-significance dimension.

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* preserves some sub-canonical fadāʾil traditions (from al-Ḥākim *al-Mustadrak*, al-Ṭabarānī *al-Muʿjam al-kabīr*, and ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Mubārak's *al-Zuhd*) attributing virtue to the recitation of vv 63-77 (*ʿibād al-Raḥmān*), but **none of these survive to the 9-book canonical corpus**.

## 3. The Q 25:68-70 + Q 4:93 abrogation hadith cluster

The second-most-prominent hadith cluster around Q 25 concerns vv 68-70 — the *lā yaqtulūna n-nafsa llatī ḥarrama llāhu* + *lā yaznūna* + *wa-man yafʿal dhālika yalqa athāmā* + the *illā man tāba* exception. This block is the locus of a foundational *uṣūl al-fiqh* and *qatl-al-muʾmin* discussion.

### The central exchange — Saʿīd b. Jubayr ← Ibn ʿAbbās:

**al-Bukhārī 3855, 3858** (chains independently verified via the ahmedbaset-json corpus):
> *qultu li-bni ʿAbbāsin: a-li-man qatala mu'minan muta'ammidan min tawbatin? qāla: lā... wa-qaraʾtu ʿalayhi l-āyata llatī fī l-Furqāni* — "I said to Ibn ʿAbbās: 'Is there repentance for one who intentionally kills a believer?' He said: No... and I recited to him the verse from al-Furqān [Q 25:68-70 with its *illā man tāba* exception]."

The narrative continues: Ibn ʿAbbās replies that the Q 25:68 Meccan verse was abrogated by the Medinan Q 4:93 *wa-man yaqtul mu'minan muta'ammidan fa-jazāʾuhu jahannamu khālidan fīhā*. Saʿīd b. Jubayr's pointed question (and Ibn ʿAbbās' answer) is the classical locus of the abrogation-vs-takhṣīṣ debate.

### Replicated across 9 books:

- al-Bukhārī: #3855, 3858, 4590, 4762, 4763, 4764, 6863, 6885, 6896, 7308 (10+ isnāds)
- Muslim: #7351
- Abū Dāwūd: #4273, 4274
- al-Nasāʾī: #4011, 4016, 4017, 4018, 4875

This is a **multi-isnād convergence** on a single exegetical-legal question, anchored in Q 25:68-70. The classical resolution (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr) is *takhṣīṣ* (Q 4:93 is the specific case, Q 25:70's *illā man tāba* the general repentance-exception): see [`03-tafsir-survey.md`](03-tafsir-survey.md) §3 (al-Rāzī) and §5 (Ibn Kathīr).

### Zayd b. Thābit's sequence-of-revelation note:

**Abū Dāwūd 4273 + Nasāʾī 4016-4018**: Zayd b. Thābit reports that **Q 4:93 was revealed six (or eight, per Nasāʾī's variant) months AFTER the Q 25:68 verses** in Mecca. This is a sequence-of-revelation note that is empirically interesting: it implies that the question of whether Q 25:68 was abrogated by Q 4:93 is a question of subsequent revelation, not contemporaneous-edit-of-text.

## 4. The Q 25:60 sajdah verse hadith

- **Ibn Mājah 790** (Abū al-Dardāʾ tradition): the 11-sajdah-locations enumeration, including the Q 25:60 sajdah location. This is the only canonical-hadith corpus-witness to the specific Q 25:60 sajdah recognition. The four-madhhab discussion on its obligatoriness (al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, vol. 13 pp. 68-71) draws on this thin but explicit canonical attestation.

- The 9-book corpus is otherwise SILENT on specific Q 25:60 sajdah practice (unlike the more famous Q 53 al-Najm sajdah, which has multiple Bukhārī-Muslim attestations).

## 5. The *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* (vv 63-77) — hadith reception

The 9-book canonical corpus contains **NO specific named-hadith tradition celebrating or commenting on the *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* catalog as such**. The block is treated in tafsir (al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr) at length but does not generate a canonical-hadith reception-tradition.

The 9-book corpus DOES have many tangentially-related ḥadīth on individual attributes:
- Night-prayer (*yabītūna li-rabbihim sujjadan wa-qiyāmā*, v 64) — extensive *qiyām al-layl* corpus.
- Balanced spending (*lam yusrifū wa-lam yaqturū*, v 67) — extensive *al-iqtiṣād* corpus.
- The *qurrata aʿyun* prayer (v 74) — multiple traditions on it being a model duʿāʾ.

But none cite v 63-77 as a unified block.

## 6. The asbāb-al-nuzūl traditions (minimal canonical attestation)

The 9-book canonical corpus contains **limited specific asbāb-al-nuzūl traditions for Q 25**. The major traditions are:
- vv 27-29 (the *yawma yaʿaḍḍu* / *yā-laytanī lam attakhidh fulānan khalīlā*): the *fulān* identified by some traditions as Uqba b. Abī Muʿayṭ or Ubayy b. Khalaf. The canonical hadith corpus is largely silent on this; the asbāb tradition comes from al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān* ad loc.), Ibn Kathīr (citing al-Wāḥidī *Asbāb al-nuzūl*), and the Sīra (Ibn Hishām).
- vv 68-70 (the homicide-and-adultery prohibitions): see §3 above. Multi-book canonical attestation.

The general pattern: Q 25's hadith corpus is **dominated by the *sabʿat aḥruf* cluster (§1) and the Q 25:68-70 abrogation cluster (§3)**. Beyond these two clusters, the canonical hadith corpus has limited Q 25-specific content.

## 8. Honest limits

- The ahmedbaset-json corpus is one of multiple canonical-hadith databases (it does not include all sub-canonical or weak-chain traditions); the *fadāʾil-of-Q-25* NULL is robust within the 9-book canonical set but does NOT preclude sub-canonical traditions in al-Bayhaqī's *Shuʿab al-īmān*, al-Dhahabī's *Mīzān*, or al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr*.
- The Hishām+ʿUmar+Q-25 *sabʿat aḥruf* episode is **historically the iconic Q 25 hadith**, but it is NOT specific to Q 25 in content — the same Hishām-and-ʿUmar narrative could have happened with any surah; the *sabʿat aḥruf* doctrine is corpus-general, not Q 25-specific. The historical fact that the episode happened with Q 25 is contingent (al-Ṭabarī notes other surah-specific recitation-conflicts in his asbāb tradition).
- The Q 25:68-70 + Q 4:93 abrogation cluster is empirically multi-attested but theologically/legally contested (Ibn ʿAbbās's no-repentance reading vs. Saʿīd b. Jubayr's challenge); classical consensus on *takhṣīṣ* rather than *naskh* is itself a post-canonical scholarly synthesis, not a unanimous hadith-doctrine.
- Hadith counts here are isnād-based (counting distinct chains in the 9 books); the underlying matn (text-content) tradition is more concentrated.
