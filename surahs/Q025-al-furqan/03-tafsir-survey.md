# Q 25 al-Furqān — Tafsir survey

Survey of major classical commentators on Q 25 al-Furqān, organized by mufassir and by topic. All citations include scholar + work + (where on disk) volume/page or passage reference. The aim is to capture the **major exegetical positions, classical disagreements, and where empirical correlates exist or are absent**.

Sources on disk: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/` — al-Suyūṭī *Itqān* PDF + OpenITI raw; al-Zarkashī *Burhān* PDF; al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar* PDF + OpenITI raw; al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* OpenITI raw; Ibn Kathīr (Arabic OpenITI + English Darussalam); al-Ṭabarī *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* OpenITI; al-Qurṭubī *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām* OpenITI; al-Zamakhsharī *Kashshāf* OpenITI; al-Ṭabarsī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Suyūṭī *Durr*.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān fī taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*, vol. 19 pp. 233–326)

The foundational tradition-aggregating treatment.

### Key contributions:

- **Wajh-enumeration on *al-furqān*** (ad loc. Q 25:1): catalogs at least 4 distinct glosses — (i) *al-Qurʾān* itself, named *al-furqān* because it discriminates *ḥaqq* from *bāṭil*; (ii) the Mosaic scripture analogized from Q 2:53, 21:48; (iii) the *yawm al-furqān* of Badr (analogized from Q 8:41); (iv) the criterion-of-discrimination-of-belief-from-unbelief in a general sense. al-Ṭabarī's preferred wajh: *al-furqān = al-Qurʾān* because the immediate frame *nazzala...ʿalā ʿabdihi li-yakūna li-l-ʿālamīna nadhīrā* names *ʿabdihi* = Muḥammad and *al-ʿālamīna* = all peoples — i.e., the revelation under discussion.

- **Disbeliever-objection block (vv 4–10)**: catalogs Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, al-Ḍaḥḥāk traditions on the polytheist mockery, especially the *iftirāʾ* charge (*in hādhā illā ifkun iftarāhu wa-aʿānahu ʿalayhi qawmun ākharūn*, v 4) — al-Ṭabarī reports the asbāb-al-nuzūl tradition that this verse rebukes a specific group of Meccans who alleged that the Prophet was helped by Christian / Jewish informants in Mecca (the *yasāʾalūnaka* / Salmān al-Fārisī traditions). al-Ṭabarī presents these as informative but not isnādically tight.

- **The *yaʾkulu al-ṭaʿāma* polemic (vv 7–8, 20)**: al-Ṭabarī notes the **structural pairing** between v 7 (disbeliever objection) and v 20 (*wa-mā arsalnā qablaka mina l-mursalīna illā innahum la-yaʾkulūna ṭ-ṭaʿāma wa-yamshūna fī l-aswāq*) — the divine reply at v 20 is the corpus's first explicit prophet-typology argument: previous prophets also ate food and walked the markets. This is the canonical anchor for the al-bashar-as-prophet doctrine (anti-Christological-Christology / anti-Manichaean-prophet-as-pure-spirit).

- **The 3 *tabāraka* refrains (vv 1, 10, 61)**: al-Ṭabarī notes the lexical repetition but does not develop a *naẓm*-organizational reading; this becomes al-Zamakhsharī's contribution.

- **The *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* catalog (vv 63–77)**: al-Ṭabarī reads the 15-verse block as a unified "portrait of the *muʾminīn*" tightly parallel to Q 23:1-11 and Q 70:22-35. He records (from al-Ḍaḥḥāk and Ibn ʿAbbās via Saʿīd b. Jubayr) the tradition that vv 68-70 (the homicide-and-adultery prohibition + the *illā man tāba* exception) was the subject of a famous early Companion exchange — preserved across multiple Bukhārī isnāds — about the abrogation/exception relation between Q 25:70 (*illā man tāba* → forgiveness possible) and Q 4:93 (intentional homicide → eternal Hellfire). al-Ṭabarī's preferred resolution: Q 25:70 is the Meccan general-clause; Q 4:93 the Medinan particular-clause; both stand, and the *illā man tāba* exception applies to Q 4:93 by *takhṣīṣ*, not *naskh*.

- **The sajdah at v 60**: al-Ṭabarī reports the standard recitation-tradition of prostration at this verse + the Companion-attestations.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī (*al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq ghawāmiḍ al-tanzīl*, vol. 3 pp. 263–303)

Muʿtazilite *balāgha* treatment. The dominant lens is *naẓm* (compositional coherence) + lexical-rhetorical exegesis.

### Key contributions:

- **The 3 *tabāraka* refrains as compositional pivots**: al-Zamakhsharī is the **first major mufassir to read the three *tabāraka alladhī* attestations (vv 1, 10, 61) as the structural triple-buttress of the surah's rhetorical architecture**. He argues that they organize the surah into three theological-axis movements:
  1. *tabāraka alladhī nazzala al-furqān* (v 1) — divine-blessedness of REVELATION;
  2. *tabāraka alladhī in shāʾa jaʿala laka khayran min dhālika* (v 10) — divine-blessedness of ESCHATOLOGICAL-VINDICATION (gardens-and-palaces possible for the Prophet);
  3. *tabāraka alladhī jaʿala fī s-samāʾi burūjan* (v 61) — divine-blessedness of COSMOLOGICAL-SIGNS.
- This is the classical anchor for what the project empirically confirms: the 3 *tabāraka* refrains do correspond to the 3 structural-pivot points where the surah switches register-mode.

- **The *atakhadha ilāhahu hawāhu* polemic (v 43)**: al-Zamakhsharī develops the Muʿtazilite reading that *hawā* (whim, passion) is the central enemy of *ʿaql* (reason); the polytheist has taken his own passion as deity, which is the most absurd of all polytheisms. This connects to the Muʿtazila's broader anti-rationalist-determinism / pro-reasoned-faith doctrine.

- **The *al-furqān* gloss**: al-Zamakhsharī's preferred reading: *al-furqān* = al-Qurʾān, because it discriminates between *al-ḥaqq wa-l-bāṭil*, between *al-ḥalāl wa-l-ḥarām*, between *al-īmān wa-l-kufr*. He notes the verb *nazzala* (form II, *taḍʿīf*-emphasis on PHASED descent) vs. *anzala* (form IV, descended-all-at-once) — and reads the verb-choice as anticipating v 32 (*lawlā nuzzila ʿalayhi l-qurʾānu jumlatan wāḥidatan*) and v 33 (*ka-dhālika li-nuthabbita bihi fuʾādaka wa-rattalnāhu tartīlā*). The *nazzala*-vs-*anzala* distinction is rhetorically loaded: Q 25:1 *nazzala* implicitly affirms phased-revelation as theologically correct, immediately before v 32-33's defense of phased-revelation explicitly.

- **The *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* catalog (vv 63–77)**: al-Zamakhsharī catalogs the 12-attribute structure as a compressed Muʿtazilite ethical framework, with vv 63-65 covering *al-akhlāq* (manners/character), vv 67-68 covering *al-ʿadl* (justice in money + ḥudūd), vv 72-73 covering *al-ʿilm* (refusing falsehood + receptive to revelation), and vv 74 covering *al-uns al-ʿāʾilī* (family-affection-as-piety).

## 3. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-kabīr*, vol. 24 pp. 49–125)

The most exhaustive classical treatment. al-Rāzī's lens is *uṣūl* + *kalām* + *naẓm* + *uṣūl al-fiqh* + *balāgha* (the full Ashʿarite-encyclopedic synthesis).

### Major contributions:

- **5-wajh on *al-furqān*** (vol. 24 p. 50; cited in §2 of `00-overview.md`).

- **The 3 *tabāraka* refrains as theological-axis organizers**: al-Rāzī develops al-Zamakhsharī's reading by adding the doctrinal dimension. The 3 refrains correspond to the 3 attributes of God-as-Creator + God-as-Eschatological-Judge + God-as-Cosmological-Sovereign — the foundational tripartite of *uṣūl al-dīn*.

- **The *yaʾkulu al-ṭaʿāma* polemic (vv 7–9, 20)**: al-Rāzī reads this in conjunction with Q 21:8 (*wa-mā jaʿalnāhum jasadan lā yaʾkulūna ṭ-ṭaʿām*) and Q 5:75 (*kānā yaʾkulāni ṭ-ṭaʿām* of Maryam + ʿĪsā) as a project — across Q 5, Q 21, Q 25 — to dismantle the divine-prophet doctrine (i.e., the Christological-or-Manichaean-influenced Meccan opposition's expectation that a true prophet should not eat food).

- **The *jumlatan wāḥidatan* objection (v 32)**: al-Rāzī develops a major *uṣūl al-fiqh* discussion: the wisdom of phased revelation is (i) progressive abrogation (early-Meccan rulings progressively replaced by Medinan rulings); (ii) calibrated psychological-strengthening (*nuthabbita bihi fuʾādaka*); (iii) responsiveness to occasions-of-revelation (asbāb al-nuzūl); (iv) sustained recitation-and-memorization (Q 17:106 *qurʾānan faraqnāhu li-taqraʾahu ʿalā n-nāsi ʿalā mukthin wa-nazzalnāhu tanzīlan*).

- **The *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* catalog (vv 63–77) as a compressed *uṣūl al-fiqh* curriculum** (vol. 24 pp. 105–120): al-Rāzī enumerates 12 attributes mapped to 12 *furūʿ* (legal-ethical branches) — humility, peaceful-non-engagement-with-fools, night-prayer, eschatological-fear, balanced-spending, monotheism, no-unjust-killing, no-fornication, no-false-witness, dignified-bypassing-of-frivolity, attentive-receptivity-to-revelation, prayer-for-righteous-family. This is the most systematic classical reading of vv 63-77 as a structured ethical-legal catalog.

- **The *hawā-as-deity* polemic (v 43)**: al-Rāzī develops the connection between *hawā* + *nafs ammāra* (Q 12:53 *inna n-nafsa la-ammāratun bi-s-sūʾ*) + *iblīs as instigator* (Q 17:64) into a full nafsological doctrine.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, vol. 13 pp. 1–82)

Legal-jurisprudential emphasis treatment. al-Qurṭubī's lens is *aḥkām* + *fiqh* across the four Sunnī madhāhib.

### Major contributions:

- **The sajdah at v 60**: al-Qurṭubī catalogs the four-madhhab positions on the obligatoriness of *sajdat al-tilāwah* for Q 25:60. The Hanafīs hold it obligatory; the Mālikīs hold it recommended-but-not-obligatory; the Shāfiʿīs and Ḥanbalīs hold it strongly-recommended. The verse-frame *wa-idhā qīla lahumu sjudū li-r-Raḥmāni qālū wa-mā r-Raḥmānu* is itself a NAMING of the polytheist refusal to prostrate, which the believer's prostration symmetrically inverts.

- **The Q 25:68-70 homicide-and-adultery prohibitions + abrogation exception**: al-Qurṭubī's most extensive contribution. He treats these verses as foundational *ḥudūd* texts and catalogs the classical debate on (i) the *qatl al-muʾmin muta ʿammidan* / Q 4:93 abrogation question; (ii) the *zinā* punishment + repentance; (iii) the four-madhhab positions on the *illā man tāba* exception clause. al-Qurṭubī's preferred resolution mirrors al-Ṭabarī's: *takhṣīṣ*, not *naskh*.

- **The marriage-and-water cosmology (v 53–54)**: al-Qurṭubī treats this as a foundational *nikāḥ*-related text on the doctrine that humans are created from water and given two types of relation — *nasab* (blood-line) and *ṣihr* (marriage-line). The *barzakh wa-ḥijran maḥjūrā* of v 53 he reads as a cosmological-and-symbolic boundary between sweet and salt water that has both physical reality (oceanographic) and symbolic-ethical (boundaries-must-be-respected) meaning.

- **The *qurrata aʿyun* prayer (v 74) as model duʿāʾ**: al-Qurṭubī treats this as the canonical prayer for righteous family and includes it in his *adʿiyat al-anbiyāʾ* catalog.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*, vol. 6 pp. 79–135 of Dār Ṭayba ed.)

Hadith-anchored treatment.

### Major contributions:

- **The al-Bukhārī *sabʿat aḥruf* hadiths** (#2322, 4785, 4836, 6679, 7264 + Muslim 1791, 1792, 7351 + Tirmidhī 2958, 3026 + Abū Dāwūd 1476 + Nasāʾī 938-940 + Mālik 482 + Aḥmad 152, 266, 281): the Hishām b. Ḥakīm + ʿUmar episode. Ibn Kathīr places this at the head of his Q 25 commentary as the central reception-history tradition for the surah. The hadith reports that ʿUmar heard Hishām reciting Q 25 *ʿalā ḥurūfin kathīratin lam yuqriʾnīhā rasūlu Allāhi*; after intervening conflict, both ʿUmar and Hishām recited their versions to the Prophet, who confirmed BOTH as legitimate, declaring *hākadhā unzilat / inna hādhā l-Qurʾāna unzila ʿalā sabʿati aḥruf, fa-qraʾū mā tayassara minhu*. **This makes Q 25 the iconic *sabʿat aḥruf* surah in classical hadith reception.**

- **The Saʿīd b. Jubayr / Ibn ʿAbbās Q 25:68-70 dossier** (Bukhārī 3855, 3858, 4590, 4762-4764, 6863, 6885, 6896, 7308 + Muslim 7351 + Abū Dāwūd 4273-4274 + Nasāʾī 4011, 4016-4018, 4875 etc.): the Companion-exchange on whether the *qātil muʾmin muta ʿammid* can repent — Ibn ʿAbbās says No (citing Q 4:93 as Medinan; pretending Q 25:70 is abrogated); Saʿīd b. Jubayr challenges with Q 25:70's *illā man tāba*. Ibn Kathīr's resolution: *takhṣīṣ*, not *naskh*; Q 4:93 is a particular about the *muʿtazil-or-warrior-who-kills-his-companion-for-religion*; Q 25:70 is the general about ordinary repentance. The Ibn ʿAbbās *non-repentance* reading is treated as a stricter-pedagogical-stance, not as legal doctrine.

- **The *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* fadāʾil traditions**: Ibn Kathīr cites multiple traditions (some weak chains) celebrating the recitation and embodiment of the vv 63-77 attributes.

- **The asbāb al-nuzūl on vv 27-29** (the *yawma yaʿaḍḍu ẓ-ẓālimu ʿalā yadayhi* / Uqba b. Abī Muʿayṭ traditions): Ibn Kathīr collects the asbāb-traditions that the *fulān* (the "so-and-so") in v 28 refers to Uqba b. Abī Muʿayṭ or Ubayy b. Khalaf — specific Quraysh opponents who once held the Prophet in dialogue but were turned by other Meccan companions.

## 6. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*, vol. 14 pp. 1–86)

*Munāsaba* (sequence-coherence) treatment.

### Major contributions:

- **The Q 24 → Q 25 → Q 26 tight rhetorical triad** (vol. 14 pp. 1–10): al-Biqāʿī reads the three surahs as a rhetorical-thematic triad bound by:
  - Q 24 al-Nūr — divine LIGHT + revelation-mediating + modesty;
  - Q 25 al-Furqān — divine CRITERION + revelation-discriminating + warning;
  - Q 26 al-Shuʿarāʾ — POETS (false-revelation contrast) + prophet-cycle defense.

  al-Biqāʿī argues that the three share an axis of "revelation-and-its-recognition-by-people," with Q 24's modesty-and-light → Q 25's discrimination-and-criterion → Q 26's prophet-cycle-vs-false-poetry. The tight-triad claim is a major *munāsabah* tradition.

  **Empirical audit (see [`05-classical-claims-audit.md`](05-classical-claims-audit.md) §1)**: the al-Biqāʿī triad is RULES-TUPLE-FRAGILE — VINDICATED on Q 25 → Q 26 (cheap seam, fraction-residual 0.67%, h-new-720), FALSIFIED on Q 24 → Q 25 (top-15 expensive seam, fraction-residual 3.49%). The triad's content-axis cohesion is asymmetric.

- **The 3 *tabāraka* refrains as compositional pivots**: al-Biqāʿī develops al-Zamakhsharī's reading further, mapping the three refrains to: (i) revelation-axis at v 1; (ii) recompense-axis at v 10; (iii) cosmology-axis at v 61. The 3-refrain reading is thus a multi-mufassir tradition (al-Zamakhsharī → al-Rāzī → al-Biqāʿī).

- **The Q 25 → Q 26 → Q 27 → Q 28 prophet-narrative-density cluster**: al-Biqāʿī notes that Q 25 introduces the prophet-typology compactly (vv 35-40), and Q 26, 27, 28 then unfold the prophet-cycle in extensive narrative form. Q 25 is the *condensed-table-of-contents* for the four-surah prophet-narrative-cluster that follows.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Durr al-manthūr* + *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*)

Aggregate-tradition treatment.

### Major contributions:

- **Chronology** (*Itqān* nawʿ 1): Q 25 is mid-late Meccan; revelation-order 42 of 114 in the dominant Suyūṭī sequence. Some traditions place vv 68-70 (the homicide-adultery prohibitions) as Medinan-revealed embedded in a Meccan surah.

- **The *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* faḍāʾil and asbāb**: al-Suyūṭī catalogs the faḍāʾil-recitation traditions for the surah and the asbāb for individual verses.

- **The *al-furqān* etymology and *iʿjāz al-faṣāḥa*** (*Itqān* nawʿ 60–70): al-Suyūṭī treats Q 25 as one of the surahs with notable *al-faṣāḥa al-balāghīya* — particularly the 3-*tabāraka*-refrain architecture, the *unẓur kayfa ḍarabū laka l-amthāl* (v 9) ridicule-of-similitudes formula, and the *yaʿaḍḍu ẓ-ẓālimu ʿalā yadayhi* (v 27) corporeal-eschatological image.

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī (*Majmaʿ al-bayān fī tafsīr al-Qurʾān*)

Shīʿī tafsir; treats Q 25 with attention to (i) the *ahl al-bayt* readings — particularly v 74's *qurrata aʿyunin* prayer is sometimes read with reference to Imāmī sayings about Fāṭima and the descendants; (ii) the *tabāraka* refrain as theological-supremacy formula; (iii) the *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* portrait as the universal-Muslim ethical catalog without sectarian narrowing.

## 9. al-Thaʿlabī (*al-Kashf wa-l-bayān*)

Lesser-known but historically important; al-Thaʿlabī preserves alternative traditions on the asbāb of vv 4-5 (the *iftirāʾ* and *asāṭīr* objections) including the identities of the alleged Meccan-foreign informants (al-Sāʾib, ʿAddās, etc.) — these traditions are weakly attested but classically preserved.

## 10. Synthesis — where mufassirūn agree, where they disagree, where empirical correlates exist

### Agreement (5+ mufassirūn):

- *al-furqān* in Q 25:1 = the Qurʾān itself as criterion-of-discrimination (al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Biqāʿī, al-Suyūṭī).
- 3 *tabāraka* refrains as compositional pivots organizing the surah's architecture (al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī).
- The *yaʾkulu al-ṭaʿāma* polemic (vv 7-8, 20) as the canonical anti-divine-prophet-Christology argument (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, Ibn Kathīr).
- The *ʿibād al-Raḥmān* catalog as the corpus's organized believer-attribute-portrait, parallel to but distinct from Q 23:1-11 and Q 70:22-35 (all 5+ mufassirūn).
- The Q 25:68-70 + Q 4:93 abrogation question resolved via *takhṣīṣ* (specification), not *naskh* (abrogation) (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr).

### Disagreement:

- The asbāb-al-nuzūl of vv 4-5 — al-Ṭabarī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Suyūṭī preserve multiple incompatible chains (varying named informants).
- Whether the *fulān* (v 28) is Uqba b. Abī Muʿayṭ or Ubayy b. Khalaf — Ibn Kathīr presents both with weighted preference for Uqba.
- The legal force of the sajdah at v 60 (Hanafīs vs. other madhāhib).
- Whether vv 68-70 are entirely Meccan or contain a Medinan-revealed core (al-Suyūṭī cites both traditions).
