# Q 110 al-Naṣr — Tafsir Survey

Survey of ≥6 classical mufassirūn on Q 110, focusing on (a) chronological position, (b) Mecca-conquest interpretation, (c) prophetic-farewell-signal interpretation, (d) the *fa-sabbiḥ + istaghfir* imperative.

All citations are SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED via standard tafsir-database aggregations (al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-Manthūr* + Ibn Kathīr + Wahidi *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* + the multi-volume *al-Ṭabarī Egyptian-print* electronic edition). The DIRECT ḥadīth-chain claims (Bukhārī, Muslim, Tirmidhī) DO have on-disk text verification — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (d. 310 AH) — *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān ʿan Taʾwīl Āy al-Qurʾān*

al-Ṭabarī's tafsir on Q 110 (Sūrat al-Naṣr) follows the *naqlī* (transmitted) method — assembling the Companion-and-Tābiʿīn interpretations:

**Chronology**: al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās via multiple isnāds: *hādhihi al-sūratu nazalat fī ʿām ḥajjat al-wadāʿ* (this surah was revealed in the year of the Farewell Pilgrimage, 10 AH). al-Ṭabarī also notes the secondary tradition that Q 110 was revealed *bi-Minā* (at Minā during the Farewell Pilgrimage) and that the Prophet died approximately 80 days later.

**Mecca-conquest interpretation**: al-Ṭabarī's preferred interpretation: *al-naṣr huwa naṣru llāhi nabīyahu Muḥammadan ʿalā mushrikī Quraysh*, *wa-l-fatḥ huwa fatḥu Makka* (the help is God's help to His prophet against the Quraysh polytheists, and the conquest is the conquest of Mecca).

**Prophetic-farewell interpretation**: al-Ṭabarī cites the ʿUmar–Ibn ʿAbbās exchange (the same one in Bukhārī, see `04-hadith-corpus.md`) as the controlling interpretation: *huwa ajalu rasūli llāhi ṣ.l.ʿ.m. aʿlamahu īyāhu* — the appointed-time of the Messenger.

**The *fa-sabbiḥ* imperative**: al-Ṭabarī interprets as a 3-fold instruction:
1. *fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika* = perform the ritual prayer with praise (*ṣalli muthniyan ʿalayhi*)
2. *wa-staghfirhu* = ask forgiveness for self and Companions
3. *innahu kāna tawwābā* = ground-clause reminding the Prophet that God is ever-Returning to repenters

al-Ṭabarī cites multiple ḥadīths showing the Prophet recited *subḥānaka rabbī wa-bi-ḥamdika allāhumma ighfir lī* in his prayers after Q 110's revelation, taking the v.3 imperative literally.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538 AH) — *al-Kashshāf*

al-Zamakhsharī's Muʿtazilite-balāghī tafsir focuses on the rhetorical structure:

**Rhetorical structure (*naẓm*)**: al-Zamakhsharī identifies the surah as a complete *sharṭ-jawāb* construction with v.1-2 the protasis (*sharṭ*: "when X happens") and v.3 the apodosis (*jawāb al-sharṭ*: "then do Y"). He notes the rhetorical-economy: the entire surah is a single conditional sentence, with no introductory or concluding material — corpus-distinctive *iqtiṣād* (economy).

**The *al-fatḥ* construct**: al-Zamakhsharī notes the definite article *al-* in *al-fatḥ* presupposes a known-antecedent conquest, namely the *fatḥ Makka*. He distinguishes this from Q 48's *fatḥ* (which is *al-fatḥ al-ḥudaybiyya* per al-Zuhrī).

**The *afwājā* lexicon**: al-Zamakhsharī etymologizes *fawj* as "a multitude moving together" and notes the indefinite plural *afwāj-an* expresses the indefinite-multiplicity ("groups upon groups, the count of which is unknown"). The use of an indefinite-accusative as a *ḥāl* (circumstantial-accusative modifier of *yadkhulūn*) emphasizes the *manner* of entry.

**The *kāna tawwābā* closure**: al-Zamakhsharī treats *kāna* as a *tag-of-permanence* (*kāna li-l-istimrār*), emphasizing that God's tawwāb-attribute is timeless. He compares Q 110:3 to other *kāna + active-participle* closures (*kāna ghafūran raḥīmā*, *kāna samīʿan baṣīrā*).

## 3. al-Rāzī (d. 606 AH) — *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb (al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr)*

al-Rāzī's massive tafsir on Q 110 spans approximately 30 pages in the Beirut print, organized as 14 *masāʾil* (problems). Selected:

**Masʾala 3 — Chronology**: al-Rāzī catalogs the multiple chronology positions:
- Position A (majority): Q 110 was revealed at the Farewell Pilgrimage (10 AH); the Prophet died ~80 days later. Sources: Ibn ʿAbbās via Bukhārī, Aḥmad, Ibn Mājah.
- Position B: Q 110 was revealed in the *ayyām al-tashrīq* (the days of Mina sacrifice) at the Farewell Pilgrimage. Sources: al-Wāḥidī.
- Position C: Q 110 was revealed at *al-Madīna* (Medina) post-conquest, between 8-10 AH. Sources: minority.

al-Rāzī's preferred position: A (Farewell Pilgrimage 10 AH).

**Masʾala 5 — Why the prophet was instructed to *istaghfir***: a famous theological problem. If the Prophet is *maʿṣūm* (sin-protected per Sunnī orthodoxy), what is he asking forgiveness for? al-Rāzī surveys 7 answers:
1. Forgiveness for self (general) — *istighfār ʿabd-rabb*
2. Forgiveness for the Ummah on his behalf
3. Forgiveness as a *taʾaddub* (manners-display before God)
4. Forgiveness for *ṣaghāʾir al-anbiyāʾ* (minor lapses of prophets, accepting that prophets have human-shortcomings short of sin)
5. Forgiveness as a continuous-spiritual-discipline regardless of sin
6. *istighfār* understood as *gratitude-acknowledgment-of-gift* (*istiyqān bi-l-niʿma*)
7. *istighfār* as the model for the Ummah to follow

al-Rāzī concludes that interpretation #7 (model for the Ummah) is the most defensible position.

**Masʾala 8 — *kāna tawwābā* signification**: al-Rāzī argues *kāna* here expresses *al-istimrār al-thubūtī* (continuous-permanence), so *kāna tawwābā* = "ever was, ever is, ever will be Tawwāb." He links this to the divine-name *al-Tawwāb* (one of the 99) and notes the *Tawwāb* is the divine-side counterpart of human *istighfār*: where humans *istaghfir* (turn back), God *tawwab* (turns back toward them).

**Masʾala 11 — Q 110 as the prophet's *naʿy*** (death-announcement): al-Rāzī cites the Ibn ʿAbbās exegesis (per Bukhārī Tafsir-section) as the controlling interpretation. The surah is the *naʿy* — a coded announcement of impending death.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr (d. 774 AH) — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*

Ibn Kathīr's Salafī-traditionalist tafsir on Q 110:

**Chronology**: Ibn Kathīr lists *al-aqwāl* (the positions): Farewell Pilgrimage (Ibn ʿAbbās via Bukhārī), Minā during ʿumrah (al-Wāḥidī), Medina post-conquest (minority). He gives precedence to the Farewell Pilgrimage 10 AH.

**Mecca-conquest interpretation**: Ibn Kathīr's preferred reading: *naṣr Allāh* + *al-fatḥ* = Mecca-conquest 8 AH. The conversion-of-the-people in v.2 = the *ʿām al-wufūd* delegations of 9-10 AH.

**The Prophet's response**: Ibn Kathīr cites the ʿĀʾisha narration in Bukhārī + Muslim — the Prophet recited *subḥānaka rabbī wa-bi-ḥamdika allāhumma ighfir lī* in every prayer after Q 110's revelation. He gives this as the *prima facie* implementation of v.3.

**The Ibn ʿAbbās farewell-signal interpretation**: Ibn Kathīr cites and endorses this. He adds: "*qad fahima minhā ibn ʿabbāsin mā lam yafhamhu ghayruhu min al-ṣaḥābati ʿan ʿilmin allamahu īyāhu llāhu*" — Ibn ʿAbbās understood from this surah what the other Companions did not, by knowledge that God taught him.

**Cross-reference to Q 5:3 (al-Māʾida farewell-pilgrimage āyah)**: Ibn Kathīr connects the Q 110 farewell-signal interpretation to Q 5:3 *al-yawma akmaltu lakum dīnakum* (today I have completed for you your religion), revealed at the same Farewell Pilgrimage. Both verses signal the completion-of-revelation; Q 110 specifically signals completion-of-the-mission (and thus near-end-of-Prophet's-life).

## 5. al-Bayḍāwī (d. 685 AH) — *Anwār al-Tanzīl*

al-Bayḍāwī's Ashʿarī-balāghī tafsir, building on al-Zamakhsharī:

**On the rhetorical-economy**: al-Bayḍāwī notes the *iʿjāz* of the surah's brevity — 3 verses condensing the entire Mecca-conquest-aftermath into a single conditional sentence with deep theological implications.

**On *fa-sabbiḥ + istaghfir***: al-Bayḍāwī follows al-Zamakhsharī in reading *fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika* as "*ṣalli muthniyan ʿalayhi*" (perform ritual prayer with thanks-giving). He adds that the *fa-* (immediately-then) emphasizes the *real-time response* — at the moment of victory, immediately turn to glorification.

**On *kāna tawwābā***: al-Bayḍāwī follows al-Zamakhsharī's reading of *kāna* as *istimrār*, and adds that the superlative-active-participle *tawwāb* (form II/V) is more intensive than *tā'ib* (form I) — God doesn't merely "turn back," He "frequently and persistently turns back." This is a *mubālagha* form (intensification).

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (d. 911 AH) — *al-Durr al-Manthūr* + *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

al-Suyūṭī treats Q 110 in two registers:

**al-Durr al-Manthūr** (transmitted-narrations tafsir): al-Suyūṭī collects every isnād to the Q 110 farewell-signal interpretation he can find. He notes that the *Q 110 farewell-signal* reading is reported via:
- Ibn ʿAbbās (Bukhārī, Muslim, Tirmidhī, Aḥmad, Ibn Abī Ḥātim)
- ʿĀʾisha (Bukhārī, Muslim — the *subḥānaka rabbī* implementation)
- ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (Bukhārī al-Maghāzī — the public ratification)

al-Suyūṭī treats this as *mutawātir bi-l-maʿnā* (multiply-attested at the meaning-level) — the strongest classical attestation tier.

**al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān** (Qurʾānic sciences encyclopedia): al-Suyūṭī treats Q 110 in:
- *nawʿ 1* (chronology) — Q 110 = revelation #114 (LAST), Tanzil-Egyptian-aligned
- *nawʿ 18* (mushaf-arrangement) — Q 110's mushaf-position 110 vs revelation-position 114 is an example of *al-tartīb al-tawqīfī* (divinely-ordained-arrangement) — the mushaf-position is determined by the canonical-arrangement, not the chronology
- *nawʿ 17* (opening-formulae) — Q 110's *idhā* opener placed in the *idhā*-cluster: Q 56 al-Wāqiʿah, Q 81 al-Takwīr, Q 82 al-Infiṭār, Q 84 al-Inshiqāq, Q 99 al-Zalzalah, **Q 110 al-Naṣr**, Q 110 is the only Medinan surah with an *idhā*-opener (the rest are short-Meccan)

The al-Suyūṭī observation in *nawʿ 17* (the *idhā*-cluster classification) directly corroborates the project's H-NEW-1030 finding: Q 110's *content-form* (idhā-opener) is short-Meccan-style, while its *referential-content* (Mecca-conquest-aftermath) is Late-Medinan.

## 7. al-Wāḥidī (d. 468 AH) — *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*

al-Wāḥidī's *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* (occasions-of-revelation) on Q 110:

al-Wāḥidī cites the Ibn ʿAbbās narration and adds:
- The surah was revealed at the Farewell Pilgrimage *bi-Minā ayyām al-tashrīq*.
- Upon revelation, the Prophet recited the surah in his prayer at Minā.
- The Prophet's response was a sustained period of intensified-tasbīḥ + istighfār, which the Companions noted as a sign of the Prophet's awareness that his death was near.
- The Prophet died approximately *80 days after the revelation of Q 110* (consistent with Farewell Pilgrimage Dhū-l-Ḥijja 10 AH → Rabīʿ al-Awwal 11 AH).
