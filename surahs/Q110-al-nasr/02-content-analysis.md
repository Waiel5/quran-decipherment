# Q 110 al-Naṣr — Content Analysis

## 1. The text

```
بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

إِذَا جَاءَ نَصْرُ اللَّهِ وَالْفَتْحُ
وَرَأَيْتَ النَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا
فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَاسْتَغْفِرْهُ ۚ إِنَّهُ كَانَ تَوَّابًا
```

**Translation (literal-syntactic, with structure preserved)**:

1. When the help of God and the conquest come,
2. and you see mankind entering God's religion in waves,
3. then glorify with the praise of your Lord and seek His forgiveness — surely He is ever-Returning.

## 2. The 3-step rhetorical arc

Q 110 has a clean 3-step *condition → observation → imperative* arc that reads as a single sentence:

| Verse | Function | Syntactic role | Classical category |
|:-:|:--|:--|:--|
| v.1 | **Condition** (*idhā* + perfect) | Conditional protasis | شرط (sharṭ) |
| v.2 | **Observation** (*wa-raʾayta* + imperfect) | Conditional-extension | عطف على الشرط (continuation of protasis) |
| v.3 | **Imperative** (*fa-* + imperative) + **causal-clause** (*innahu* + perfect) | Apodosis + ground | جواب الشرط + علة (apodosis + reason) |

This 3-step structure is **classically schematic** for Qurʾānic *sharṭ-jawāb* constructions; what is **distinctive about Q 110** is that the entire sharṭ-jawāb construction *is* the surah, with no introductory or concluding material around it.

## 3. The lexical-thematic anchors

### *naṣr* (نصر) — the help (v.1)

The root *n-ṣ-r* appears 158 times in the Qurʾān across multiple roots-and-derivatives (per QAC). In Q 110:1 it is in the construct *naṣru llāh* — God's help / God's victory. The classical interpretive consensus (al-Ṭabarī, al-Zamakhsharī, Ibn Kathīr) reads this specifically as the divine help granted to Muḥammad in the lead-up to the Mecca-conquest of 8 AH / 630 CE.

### *al-fatḥ* (الفتح) — the conquest / the opening (v.1)

*al-fatḥ* is itself the title of Q 48 (al-Fatḥ), revealed in 6 AH at Ḥudaybiyya. The classical reading distinguishes the Q 48 *fatḥ* (which al-Zuhrī interpreted as the truce, *fatḥ al-Ḥudaybiyya*) from the Q 110 *fatḥ* (which is unambiguously the actual conquest of Mecca, *fatḥ Makka*, 8 AH).

The use of *al-* (definite article) is grammatically significant: it presupposes a known, expected, prophesied conquest — i.e., the *fatḥ* the audience already knew was coming.

### *al-nās* (الناس) — mankind / the people (v.2)

In Q 110:2 *al-nās* refers specifically to the Arabian-tribes-of-Arabia who entered Islam in the post-Mecca-conquest *ʿām al-wufūd* (Year of Delegations, 9-10 AH). The classical interpretation is *al-ʿarabu jamīʿan* ("the Arabs as a whole"), not all of humanity (*ʿumūm al-nās* would require additional context).

### *yadkhulūna fī dīni llāhi* (يدخلون في دين الله) — entering God's religion (v.2)

The imperfect *yadkhulūna* expresses **continuous-aspect** ("[they were] entering"), not point-in-time. This indicates the conversion was a sustained process across the post-conquest year(s) — corroborating the *ʿām al-wufūd* historical reading.

### *afwājā* (أفواجا) — in waves / in groups (v.2)

The plural of *fawj* (فوج, "a group / a wave / a throng"). The intensive plural *afwājan* (with the *-ā(n)* indefinite-accusative) means *throng-after-throng, group-after-group, wave-after-wave* — emphasizing the volume and pace of the conversion.

The root *f-w-j* appears only **3 times** in the Qurʾān (Q 38:59, Q 78:18, **Q 110:2**) — making *afwājā* a near-hapax of the corpus and the verse-final word a corpus-rare lexical signature. (See Q110-F-novel-finding context in `06-novel-findings.md`.)

### *sabbiḥ + istaghfir* (سبح + استغفر) — glorify and seek forgiveness (v.3)

A 2-imperative compound, both 2nd-person-masculine-singular addressed to the Prophet:
- *fa-sabbiḥ bi-ḥamdi rabbika* — "[then] glorify [your Lord] together with the praise of your Lord" — a tasbīḥ-with-ḥamd formula common in Qurʾānic doxology (compare Q 50:39, Q 87:1, Q 20:130).
- *wa-staghfirhu* — "and seek His forgiveness" (X-form *istaghfara* = "ask-for-forgiveness").

The combination is classically interpreted as *adab al-fātiḥ* (the etiquette of the conqueror): victory triggers humility-before-God + repentance-seeking, not pride.

### *innahu kāna tawwābā* (إنه كان توابا) — surely He is ever-Returning (v.3)

The closing *kāna + active-participle* clause is the *taʿlīl* (causal explanation) for the *fa-staghfirhu* imperative. The active-participle *tawwāb* is intensive ("ever-Returning toward [the repentant]") — an attribute classical theology categorizes among the "names of mercy" (*asmāʾ al-raḥma*).

The entire 3rd verse is read by classical tafsīr as a **mutual-tawba dynamic**: the human-side (*istaghfir*) and the divine-side (*tawwāb*) of the repentance-relation. This is one of only **6 verses in the Qurʾān** ending with *kāna tawwābā(n)* in this exact construction (Q 4:64 *innahu kāna tawwāban* construction; Q 110:3 *innahu kāna tawwāban*; the 4 close cognates).

## 4. Why Companions read this as the prophetic-farewell

Per Bukhārī *Kitāb al-Manāqib* / *Kitāb al-Maghāzī* / *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* and Muslim *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* (see `04-hadith-corpus.md`):

The ʿUmar–Ibn ʿAbbās exegesis: when ʿUmar asked the elder Companions of Badr the meaning of *idhā jāʾa naṣru llāh*, they answered "the future conquest of cities and palaces" (a forward-looking-victory reading). Ibn ʿAbbās said "*ajalu rasūli llāhi ṣallā llāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallama aʿlamahu īyāhu*" — "the appointed-time of God's Messenger, of which God informed him" — i.e., the surah is the *signal* that the Prophet's mission has been completed and his death is near.

The classical reasoning chain:
1. The conquest of Mecca + mass-conversion = the prophet's mission has reached its goal.
2. A goal-completed prophet is one whose appointed-time has come.
3. Therefore *idhā jāʾa naṣru llāhi* + *raʾayta al-nāsa yadkhulūna* = the prophet's death is near.
4. The imperative *fa-sabbiḥ + istaghfir* is the prophet's correct response in the time before his death: glorify + seek forgiveness.

ʿUmar's response: "*mā aʿlamu minhā illā mā taʿlamu*" — "I do not know from this Verse but what you know" — a public ratification of Ibn ʿAbbās's reading.

The Prophet's own confirmation (per ʿĀʾisha narration in Bukhārī *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* + Muslim *Kitāb al-Ṣalāt*): from the day Q 110 was revealed until his death, the Prophet recited *subḥānaka rabbī wa-bi-ḥamdika allāhumma ighfir lī* in every prayer — fulfilling the v.3 imperative directly in his ritual practice.

## 5. The al-Tawdīʿ (الْتَوْدِيع) classical name

Some classical scholars (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān*, al-Wāḥidī *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*) record the name **al-Tawdīʿ** ("the farewell") for Q 110, distinct from its canonical title *al-Naṣr*. The name is justified by the prophetic-farewell-signal interpretation. (al-Suyūṭī also records *al-Tawbīkh* — "the rebuke" — as a minority alternative, applied if the surah is read as exhorting the late-converting tribes; this is a minority position not supported by the classical mainstream.)

The name *al-Naṣr* itself is from v.1 *naṣru llāh*, the surah's first nominal noun. The classical naming convention (*tasmiyat al-suwar*) overwhelmingly uses the surah's first vivid lexical anchor.

## 6. Connection to the Q 109 al-Kāfirūn polemical-disengagement

Q 109 (immediately preceding) reads:

> Q 109:1-6: *qul yā ayyuhā al-kāfirūn* … *lakum dīnukum wa-liya dīn* — "Say: O disbelievers … to you your religion, to me my religion."

Q 110 then opens with *idhā jāʾa naṣru llāhi … wa-raʾayta al-nāsa yadkhulūna fī dīni llāh* — "When the help of God comes … and you see the people entering God's religion."

The classical *munāsabah* (al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar*, see `05-classical-claims-audit.md` §3):
- Q 109 = the prophet's individual position vis-à-vis the disbelievers ("to you your religion, to me my religion") — polemical disengagement, accepting the religious-pluralist status quo.
- Q 110 = the divine response ("My help and conquest will come, and you'll see them entering MY religion in waves") — the *takhṣīṣ-tabdīl* inversion: what looked like permanent religious-disengagement turns out to be temporary; the *fatḥ* converts the resistant to entrants.

This explains the **clamped-zero** mushaf-adjacency cost (rank 6 of 13 seamless seams per H-NEW-1240): the classical *munāsabah* reads the two surahs as a single pivotal-pair at the rhetorical level.

## 7. Connection to Q 111 al-Masad (the Abū Lahab cursing)

Q 111 (immediately following Q 110) curses *Abū Lahab* — Muḥammad's paternal uncle who opposed him from the beginning of the Meccan revelation. The mushaf-adjacency Q 110 → Q 111 is at delta=0.0170 (rank 21/113, very-cheap).

The classical thematic-pivot reading:
- Q 110 = mass-conversion of the previously-resistant.
- Q 111 = the persistent-resistant individual who will NOT convert (Abū Lahab).

Q 110-Q 111 thus dramatize the **post-Mecca-conquest splitting of the Arabs** into two camps: those who entered Islam in waves (Q 110:2) and those who refused unto death (Q 111). This is the classical *taqsīm al-nās* reading.

## 8. Connection to Q 1 al-Fātiḥa (the wrap-around)

Per cross-finding-013 (mushaf as topological ring): Q 1 al-Fātiḥa wraps to TERMINAL_TRIAD {Q 112-114} via FR-content closure. Q 110 is *also in the terminal-triad neighborhood* (FR-rank 14 = Q 1 enters as Q 110's 14th nearest neighbor at distance 0.353).

Classical balāgha treats this as the *iftitāḥ–ikhtitām* cycle (opening-closing): the corpus opens with *bismi llāhi* (Q 1) and closes with *idhā jāʾa naṣru llāh* (Q 110) — both naming God explicitly, both invoking the divine-presence in human-affairs, both short. The wrap-around is empirically strong (cross-finding-013) and classically thematic (al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ on *taʾwīl al-mushaf*).

## 9. Continuous-aspect prophetic-tense vs. perfect-tense narrative

Q 110:1's *idhā jāʾa* uses the perfect verb *jāʾa* in the *idhā*-conditional, where it is grammatically read as imminent-future ("when [there] comes" = "when [it] does come"). This is **prophetic-perfect** usage — describing future events as if already accomplished.

Q 110:2's *yadkhulūna* uses the imperfect ("[they] enter") in a continuous/durative aspect — capturing the *ongoing* mass-conversion.

The **tense-shift from perfect (*jāʾa*) to imperfect (*yadkhulūna*)** is rhetorically functional: the *fatḥ* is a single completed event; the *afwāj* is an unfolding process. The grammar tracks the historical-phenomenology.

## 10. Repetition: *Allāh* twice, no other repetition

The only repeated word in Q 110 is *Allāh* (in v.1 *naṣru llāhi* and v.2 *dīni llāhi*). All 17 other content-words are unique. This is corpus-distinctive: a 19-word surah with 18 distinct content-words and only 1 (Allāh) repeating — a maximally lexically-diverse short surah.

The double-*Allāh* placement is also rhetorically deliberate:
- *naṣru **llāh***: God as the source of help.
- *dīni **llāh***: God as the destination of the converted.

The two *Allāh*s frame the source-and-destination of the historical movement — divine help → divine religion. The 3rd verse's *rabbika* (your Lord) is then the *2nd-person addressing of Allāh*, completing the v.1 (3rd-person source) → v.2 (3rd-person destination) → v.3 (2nd-person directly-addressed) progression.

This is corpus-distinctive intra-surah *iltifāt* (rhetorical-shift) on the *Allāh* root: 3rd → 3rd → 2nd-person address in 3 verses, as identified in the Abdel-Haleem iltifāt-catalog (see `data/literature/classical-tafsir/abdel-haleem-iltifat-catalog.md`).

## 11. Summary

Q 110 al-Naṣr is a 19-word, 80-letter, 3-verse Medinan surah that compresses the entire Mecca-conquest-aftermath into a *condition-observation-imperative* arc. Its content vocabulary is short-Meccan-tail-style (which produces the architectural dissociation from chronology); its referential content is uniquely Late-Medinan-historical (Mecca-conquest 8 AH + ʿām al-wufūd 9-10 AH); its rhetorical function is canonically the *prophetic-farewell-signal* (per multiply-attested Bukhārī + Muslim ḥadīths from Ibn ʿAbbās).
