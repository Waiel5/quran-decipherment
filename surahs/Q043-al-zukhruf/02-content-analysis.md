## 2. Q 43:31 — the *rajulin min al-qaryatayni ʿaẓīm* objection

The Meccan socio-economic objection: Quraysh wanted a Prophet from a powerful patrician family, not from a clan that had lost its head-of-protection. Per Ibn Kathīr ad Q 43:31, the two named candidates were:
- al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra (Mecca)
- ʿUrwa b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī (al-Ṭāʾif)

The verse responds: *a-hum yaqsimūna raḥmata rabbik* — "Are they the ones to apportion their Lord's mercy?" — God selects the messenger by *raḥma*, not by social status. This is one of the **canonical Meccan polemic verses** on prophetology.

## 3. Q 43:33-35 — the *zukhruf* / wealth-critique passage

The grammatical structure (Q 43:33): *wa-law-lā an yakūna al-nāsu ummatan wāḥidatan la-jaʿalnā li-man yakfuru bi-l-Raḥmān…* — *"and were it not that all people would become a single community [of disbelievers], We would have made for those who disbelieve in al-Raḥmān…"*. The conditional is theologically distinctive: God *limits* the wealth-distribution to disbelievers because total prosperity would corrupt all to disbelief. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*, ad loc., elaborates this as the *ḥikmah* (wisdom) of God's provisional inequality.

The surah's name *al-Zukhruf* derives from v. 35 ("…and gold (zukhruf)…") — gold is named as the most-tempting wealth-form, the surah-name a critique-marker.

## 4. The ʿĪsā passage (Q 43:57-65)

Most extended Meccan ʿĪsā christological discussion:

| Verse | Content |
|:--|:--|
| 57-58 | Quraysh argue: "Are our gods better, or he?" — testing the Prophet on idol-rejection vs ʿĪsā-veneration |
| 59 | *In huwa illā ʿabdun anʿamnā ʿalayhi* — "He is only a servant whom We blessed" — anti-divinity |
| 60 | If We willed, We could replace humans with angels — God's omnipotence |
| 61 | *Wa-innahu la-ʿilmun li-l-sāʿati* — "and he is a sign of the Hour" — eschatological-return reading classically anchored |
| 62-64 | Satan must not turn you away; ʿĪsā affirmed *innallāha huwa rabbī wa-rabbukum* — anti-trinitarian |
| 65 | The schism after ʿĪsā; Day of pain |

This block is the **Christological-engagement core of the Meccan corpus** — alongside Q 19 (Maryam) and Q 5 (Medinan, table-of-feast). Classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr ad Q 43:61) ties v. 61 to the Mahdi/Second-Coming traditions; the *ʿalāmāt al-sāʿa* tradition reads ʿĪsā's return as Q 43:61's referent.

## 5. Q 43:81 — *qul in kāna li-l-Raḥmāni waladun fa-anā awwalu al-ʿābidīn*

"Say: if al-Raḥmān had a son, I would be the first of the worshipers." This conditional-counterfactual is a classical *mushkil*: how can the Prophet say "I would be the first to worship" a hypothetical son? Classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.; al-Rāzī ad loc.) reads this as a *baligh-style* refutation: the very impossibility of God having offspring is shown by hypothetical assent. Some readings (al-Zamakhsharī, *Kashshāf*) read *in* as negative ("if NOT", reading the conditional as denial).

## 6. Vocabulary distinctness

- ز-خ-ر-ف (zḫrf) "gold/ornament" — title-root; appears 1× (Q 43:35), but the surah-name-bearing reference
- ع-ج-ز / ʿ-ṣ-w / ʿ-i-y / ʿ-y-y (ʿyy) "incompetent" (v. 52: "*mahīnun wa-lā yakādu yubīn*") — Pharaoh's slur on Mūsā; unusual root
- ر-ح-م-ن (Raḥmān) — appears 7× in Q 43, dense usage of the divine name *al-Raḥmān*; Q 43 is among the *Raḥmān-densest* surahs corpus-wide

## 8. Asbāb al-nuzūl

- Q 43:31 *rajulin min al-qaryatayni*: per Ibn Kathīr ad loc., revealed in response to Quraysh's persistent demand for a high-status Prophet. Specific Companion-tradition: al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra and ʿUrwa b. Masʿūd named via Mujāhid (in al-Ṭabarī ad loc.).
- Q 43:57: per Ibn Kathīr ad loc., revealed when Ibn al-Zibaʿrā argued that Q 21:98 (*innakum wa-mā taʿbudūna min dūnillāhi ḥaṣabu jahannam*) condemns ʿĪsā along with idols, since Christians worship him. The verse responds by setting ʿĪsā apart as a willing servant.

## 9. Refrains and rhetorical structure

- *Yā ʿibādī…* (Q 43:68) — direct vocative to believers
- *Wa-mā arsalnā fī qaryatin min nadhīrin illā qāla mutrafūhā…* (Q 43:23) — echoes Q 34:34
- *In huwa illā…* (v. 59, the ʿĪsā servant-claim formula)
- *Wa-lakin akthara-kum li-l-ḥaqqi kārihūn* (v. 78) — closing-warning marker

## 10. Honest limits

1. The "ʿĪsā as sign of Hour" (v. 61) reading depends on which classical isnād is selected; multiple readings exist.
2. Q 43's dense *Raḥmān* usage is descriptive; not yet tested for *Raḥmān-cluster* significance corpus-wide.
3. Q 43:31's "two cities" identification (al-Walīd / ʿUrwa) is one classical tradition; others exist.
