## 3. Distinctive features

### 3.1 Q 43:31 — *the rich-man hypothesis*
Quraysh's classic objection: *law-lā nuzzila hādhā al-Qurʾān ʿalā rajulin min al-qaryatayni ʿaẓīm* — "If only this Qurʾān had been sent down upon a great man from the two cities (Mecca and al-Ṭāʾif)". The verse names two specific candidates Quraysh had in mind (al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra, ʿUrwa b. Masʿūd al-Thaqafī) per Ibn Kathīr ad Q 43:31. This is the **paradigmatic Meccan objection to Muḥammad** based on socio-economic status.

### 3.2 The *zukhruf* / wealth-critique passage (Q 43:33-35)
*And were it not that all people would become a single nation [of disbelievers], We would have made for those who disbelieve in the Most Merciful — for their houses — silver ceilings and stairways… and **zukhruf** (gold/adornment)…* — the source of the surah's name. Critique of wealth-as-divine-favor reasoning.

### 3.3 ʿĪsā passage (Q 43:57-65)
The most extended ʿĪsā christological passage in the Meccan corpus:
- Q 43:57 — *wa-lammā ḍuriba bnu Maryama mathalan idhā qawmuka minhu yaṣiddūn* — "When the son of Maryam was set forth as an example, your people scoff"
- Q 43:59 — *in huwa illā ʿabdun anʿamnā ʿalayhi* — "He is only a servant whom We blessed"
- Q 43:61 — *wa-innahu la-ʿilmun li-l-sāʿati* — "He is a sign of the Hour" (eschatological-return reading classically anchored here)
- Q 43:63-64 — ʿĪsā says: *innallāha huwa rabbī wa-rabbukum* — "God is my Lord and your Lord" — anti-trinitarian formulation

This is one of the project's flagged Christological-engagement verses; classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.; Ibn Kathīr ad loc.) ties Q 43:61 to the *ʿalāmāt al-sāʿa* (signs of the Hour) tradition.

### 3.4 The truth-burdens warning (Q 43:78)
*Wa-lakin akthara-kum li-l-ḥaqqi kārihūn* — "but most of you are averse to the truth". Classic eschatological-judgment formula used in many post-Q 43 surahs.

### 3.5 Polytheism critique (Q 43:9, 43:81-89)
"If you ask them who created the heavens and the earth, they will say 'al-ʿAzīz al-ʿAlīm'" (Q 43:9) — empirical observation that pagan Quraysh acknowledge a creator-God; the surah uses this to argue the contradiction of their idolatry.
