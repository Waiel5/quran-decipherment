## 2. Block A: doxology of dominion (vv. 1-2)

> تبارك الذي بيده الملك وهو على كل شيء قدير . الذي خلق الموت والحياة ليبلوكم أيكم أحسن عملا وهو العزيز الغفور

(v. 1) "Blessed is He in whose hand is the dominion, and He is over all things ever-Powerful.
(v. 2) He who created death and life to test you — which of you is best in deed; and He is the Mighty, the Forgiving."

**Notable**: v. 1 is the only Quranic occurrence of the 2-word phrase *bi-yadihi al-mulk* (computed from `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json` corpus-wide search; see `06-novel-findings.md` Q067-F-03). The five *tabāraka alladhī* surah-openings of the Quran (Q 25:1, 25:10, 25:61, 43:85, 67:1) form a small cluster: Q 25 al-Furqān (3×) is the corpus's other *tabāraka*-rich surah; Q 43 and Q 67 each have one occurrence. The *tabāraka* + *bi-yadihi* + *al-mulk* tri-element compound is unique to Q 67:1.

The v. 2 formulation "*khalaqa al-mawta wa-l-ḥayāta li-yabluwakum*" places the creation of death BEFORE the creation of life — a classical exegetical observation (al-Qurṭubī Q67:2, al-Rāzī Q67:2). The order reverses the natural intuition (life-then-death) to emphasize that death is itself a created reality, not merely the cessation of life. Companion-tradition (al-Bukhārī Kitāb al-tawḥīd; cited in al-Qurṭubī Q67:2) glosses *al-mawt* as *al-nuṭfa* (the seminal drop) and *al-ḥayāt* as the breath-of-life — i.e., the death-life pair refers to pre-existence and existence as an embryological-soteriological complex.

The fāṣila pair *qadīr* (v. 1) / *al-ʿazīz al-ghafūr* (v. 2) opens the surah's *-īr / -ūr* rhyme-family.

## 3. Block B: cosmological dominion + visual return (vv. 3-5)

> الذي خلق سبع سماوات طباقا ما ترى في خلق الرحمن من تفاوت فارجع البصر هل ترى من فطور . ثم ارجع البصر كرتين ينقلب إليك البصر خاسئا وهو حسير . ولقد زينا السماء الدنيا بمصابيح وجعلناها رجوما للشياطين وأعتدنا لهم عذاب السعير

(v. 3) "He who created seven heavens *ṭibāq* — you see no flaw in al-Raḥmān's creation; so return the gaze: do you see any fissure?
(v. 4) Then return the gaze a second time: the gaze will return to you humbled and exhausted.
(v. 5) And We have adorned the lowest heaven with lamps and made them projectiles for the devils, and have prepared for them the punishment of the Saʿīr."

**Cross-corpus check** (computed):
- The phrase *sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqan* appears at exactly **2 verses**: Q 67:3 and Q 71:15 (al-Suyūṭī's *ālā tarawna kayfa khalaqa Allāhu sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqan*). The *ṭibāq* construction is a Q 67/Q 71 pair-signature.
- *fa-rjiʿi al-baṣar* (with *fāʾ* prefix) is a **corpus-singleton** at Q 67:3.
- *irjiʿi al-baṣar karratayn* (Q 67:4) is also unique.

al-Rāzī (`razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:3-4) reads the *fa-rjiʿi al-baṣar … thumma rjiʿi karratayn* sequence as a structured **double-iteration argument from cosmological observation**: the first return-of-gaze finds no flaw; the second-iteration return finds the gaze itself exhausted. The argument is empirical-then-introspective: the first iteration tests creation; the second iteration tests the limits of human perception. al-Biqāʿī (`biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:4) reads the *karratayn* not as *exactly two times* but as *iteratively* (i.e., however many times you look) — al-Qurṭubī (Q67:4 masʾala) sides with al-Rāzī's literal *exactly twice* reading.

v. 5 transitions from cosmological-observation to angelic-projectile-warfare: the *maṣābīḥ* (lamps) of the lowest heaven serve double-duty as decoration and as *rujūm li-l-shayāṭīn* (projectiles for the devils). al-Ṭabarī (`tabari-jami-bayan.openiti.raw.txt` Q67:5) collects multiple isnads on the *rujūm* tradition — the meteoric streams understood as celestial defense against eavesdropping demons (cf. Q 15:18, Q 37:10, Q 72:8).

## 4. Block C: Saʿīr — reception of disbelievers (vv. 6-11)

> وللذين كفروا بربهم عذاب جهنم وبئس المصير . إذا ألقوا فيها سمعوا لها شهيقا وهي تفور . تكاد تميز من الغيظ كلما ألقي فيها فوج سألهم خزنتها ألم يأتكم نذير . قالوا بلى قد جاءنا نذير فكذبنا وقلنا ما نزل الله من شيء إن أنتم إلا في ضلال كبير . وقالوا لو كنا نسمع أو نعقل ما كنا في أصحاب السعير . فاعترفوا بذنبهم فسحقا لأصحاب السعير

(v. 6) "And for those who disbelieved in their Lord, the punishment of Jahannam — and wretched is the destination.
(v. 7) When they are thrown into it, they will hear from it a roaring as it boils.
(v. 8) Almost will it burst with rage. Each time a *fawj* (group) is thrown into it, its keepers ask them: did no warner come to you?
(v. 9) They will say: yes, a warner did come to us, but we denied and said: God has not sent down anything; you are in great error.
(v. 10) And they will say: had we listened or used reason, we would not have been among the companions of the Saʿīr.
(v. 11) Then they will admit their sin — so far away [from mercy] for the companions of the Saʿīr."

**Structural feature**: the dialogue between hellfire's keepers (*khazana*) and the disbelievers (vv. 8-10) is one of the Quran's most-developed eschatological-dialogues. The *khazana* questioning *a-lam yaʾtikum nadhīr* recurs four times across the corpus (Q 39:71, 67:8) — the Q 67 instance is the more elaborated. The triplet *naẓarnā / sami'nā / ʿaqalnā* (we looked / heard / reasoned) covers the three classical Aristotelian-style faculties of discernment, posthumously endorsed.

The closing fāṣila *fa-suḥqan li-aṣḥābi al-saʿīr* (v. 11) — "so far away [from mercy] for the companions of the Saʿīr" — is a verb-form curse-phrase, rare in the corpus.

## 5. Block D: believers-in-the-unseen + earth-as-tame (vv. 12-15)

> إن الذين يخشون ربهم بالغيب لهم مغفرة وأجر كبير . وأسروا قولكم أو اجهروا به إنه عليم بذات الصدور . ألا يعلم من خلق وهو اللطيف الخبير . هو الذي جعل لكم الأرض ذلولا فامشوا في مناكبها وكلوا من رزقه وإليه النشور

(v. 12) "Indeed those who fear their Lord *bi-l-ghayb* (in the unseen) — for them is forgiveness and a great reward.
(v. 13) And speak your word secretly or openly — He is knower of what is in the breasts.
(v. 14) Does He not know what He created, and He is the Subtle, the Acquainted.
(v. 15) He it is who made the earth tame for you — so walk in its tracts and eat of His provision; and to Him is the resurrection (*nushūr*)."

**Lexical signature**: the term *bi-l-ghayb* (in the unseen / beyond perception) at v. 12 appears 7× in the corpus; this is one of the canonical "fear God in the unseen" verses (cf. Q 50:33, Q 35:18, Q 21:49, Q 36:11). al-Rāzī (Q67:12) treats this verse as the canonical *taqwā fī al-ghayb*-formula.

**Earth-as-tame**: the term *dhalūl* (tame, submissive, broken-in) at v. 15 is one of two corpus-occurrences (the other at Q 2:71 where it describes the cow). The earth-as-tame metaphor is unique to Q 67 in the cosmological context. al-Qurṭubī Q67:15 reads *manākib* (literally "shoulders / sides") as referring to mountains and well-trodden paths — the earth's stable parts. The fāṣila *al-nushūr* (resurrection) in v. 15 is the earliest *nushūr*-attestation in the surah and prepares for the eschatological re-emphasis at v. 22 onwards.

## 6. Block E: cosmic terror + birds-with-outstretched-wings (vv. 16-19)

> أأمنتم من في السماء أن يخسف بكم الأرض فإذا هي تمور . أم أمنتم من في السماء أن يرسل عليكم حاصبا فستعلمون كيف نذير . ولقد كذب الذين من قبلهم فكيف كان نكير . أولم يروا إلى الطير فوقهم صافات ويقبضن ما يمسكهن إلا الرحمن إنه بكل شيء بصير

(v. 16) "Have you secured against Him-who-is-in-the-heaven that He should swallow you up by the earth, then it will tremble?
(v. 17) Or have you secured against Him-who-is-in-the-heaven that He should send upon you a stone-storm? You will then know how was My warning.
(v. 18) Indeed those before them denied — how then was My disapproval?
(v. 19) Have they not seen the birds above them, outspreading their wings and folding them? None holds them but al-Raḥmān; indeed, He is, of every thing, ever-Sighted."

**Structural feature**: the *aʾamintum* / *am amintum* doublet at vv. 16-17 is one of the corpus's clearest two-fold rhetorical-doublets (cf. Q 53:33-34, Q 75:36-39). al-Zamakhsharī (`zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` Q67:16) reads this as al-Bāqillānī-style *iʿjāz al-fawāṣil* in interrogative-form: the doubled rhetorical question creates two distinct paths to the same conclusion (you have not secured against either earth-swallowing OR projectile-storm).

The **birds-with-outstretched-wings** (vv. 19) is one of the corpus's most-cited cosmic-doxology images. al-Biqāʿī (`biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` Q67:19) reads vv. 16-19 as a four-fold cosmic-evidence-cluster: terror-from-below (v. 16), terror-from-above (v. 17), historical-warning (v. 18), beauty-from-above (v. 19) — the visual evidence-cluster for the dominion-doctrine. al-Rāzī (Q67:19) emphasizes *yaqbiḍna* (and-they-fold) as the parsimonious flight-mechanism evidence: birds maintain altitude with minimal effort, a sign of al-Raḥmān's holding-power.

## 7. Block F: disbelievers' delusion — tropic interrogatives (vv. 20-22)

> أمن هذا الذي هو جند لكم ينصركم من دون الرحمن إن الكافرون إلا في غرور . أمن هذا الذي يرزقكم إن أمسك رزقه بل لجوا في عتو ونفور . أفمن يمشي مكبا على وجهه أهدى أمن يمشي سويا على صراط مستقيم

(v. 20) "Who is this that is an army for you who would aid you besides al-Raḥmān? Indeed the disbelievers are in nothing but delusion.
(v. 21) Or who is this that would provide for you if He withheld His provision? Rather they persist in arrogance and aversion.
(v. 22) Is then he who walks falling on his face better-guided, or he who walks upright on a straight path?"

**Structural feature**: triple *amman hādhā alladhī* anaphora at vv. 20, 21, 22 — three rhetorical questions challenging the disbelievers' alternative-power-source. al-Rāzī (Q67:20-22) treats this as the *dialectic-by-counterfactual*: the disbelievers must specify what alternative defender / provider they propose; the rhetorical absence of an answer is the argument's force.

The closing v. 22 introduces the *walking-upright* / *walking-on-face* metaphor — one of the corpus's most-physical embodiments of the *guidance / misguidance* contrast. The classical commentators (al-Qurṭubī Q67:22, Ibn Kathīr `ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt` Q67:22) report that this metaphor was so vivid the early-Madinan converts asked the Prophet "how does one walk on one's face?" and were told "the One who made him walk on his feet can make him walk on his face on the Day of Resurrection" — Bukhārī #4760 in the ahmedbaset-json idInBook convention (verified in `04-hadith-corpus.md` §5).

## 8. Block G: first-batch *qul* + sense-organs + Day's terror (vv. 23-27)

> قل هو الذي أنشأكم وجعل لكم السمع والأبصار والأفئدة قليلا ما تشكرون . قل هو الذي ذرأكم في الأرض وإليه تحشرون . ويقولون متى هذا الوعد إن كنتم صادقين . قل إنما العلم عند الله وإنما أنا نذير مبين . فلما رأوه زلفة سيئت وجوه الذين كفروا وقيل هذا الذي كنتم به تدعون

(v. 23) "Say: He it is who created you and made for you the hearing, sight, and hearts; little do you give thanks.
(v. 24) Say: He it is who scattered you in the earth, and to Him you will be gathered.
(v. 25) And they say: when is this promise [fulfilled], if you are truthful?
(v. 26) Say: the knowledge is only with God, and I am only a clear warner.
(v. 27) But when they see it [the punishment] approaching, the faces of those who disbelieved are saddened, and it is said: this is what you used to call for."

**Structural feature**: triple *qul* anaphora at vv. 23, 24, 26 — three prophetic-instructions. The first two are creational (creation + dispersal in the earth); the third is epistemic (knowledge-only-with-God). The fourth and fifth *qul* anaphora come at vv. 28, 29 (and v. 30 if "qul" includes the verse's *qul aʾraʾaytum*). The fivefold *qul* anaphora across vv. 23-30 is one of the corpus's high-density imperative-prophetic-instruction zones.

al-Rāzī (Q67:23) emphasizes the order *al-samʿ wa-l-abṣār wa-l-afʾida* — hearing first, then sight, then hearts/intellects — as the developmentally-ordered sequence of human cognitive-capacities (cf. Q 16:78 same triplet, same order). The "little do you give thanks" closes with one of the corpus's standard *qalīlan mā tashkurūn* rebukes.

## 9. Block H: final-three-*qul* — fate / Lord / water (vv. 28-30)

> قل أرأيتم إن أهلكني الله ومن معي أو رحمنا فمن يجير الكافرين من عذاب أليم . قل هو الرحمن آمنا به وعليه توكلنا فستعلمون من هو في ضلال مبين . قل أرأيتم إن أصبح ماؤكم غورا فمن يأتيكم بماء معين

(v. 28) "Say: have you considered — if God destroys me and those with me, or has mercy on us, who will protect the disbelievers from a painful punishment?
(v. 29) Say: He is al-Raḥmān; we believe in Him and rely upon Him. You will know who is in clear error.
(v. 30) Say: have you considered — if your water were to become sunken into the ground, who could bring you flowing water?"

**Structural feature**: bookend *qul aʾraʾaytum* anaphora at vv. 28 and 30 — two existential-conditional questions, each answerable only by reference to divine power. v. 29 between them affirms the speaker's faith in al-Raḥmān, the central doxological name of Q 67 (also the divine-name in vv. 3, 19, 20, 29 — five occurrences in 30 verses, one of the corpus's high-density al-Raḥmān concentrations).

The closing v. 30 — *fa-man yaʾtīkum bi-māʾin maʿīn* (who could bring you flowing water?) — is the surah's hydrological-eschatology bookend: the water-question matches the cosmological-question of vv. 3-4 (the heavens-question). The surah opens with vertical cosmology (heavens) and closes with horizontal hydrology (groundwater) — both serving as evidence of dominion. al-Biqāʿī (Q67:30) reads this closure as the *full circle of dominion-evidence* — heavens and earth-and-water united as one demonstration.

- Q 67:1 *bi-yadihi al-mulk* ↔ Q 3:26 *Allāhumma mālika al-mulk* — the divine-king-and-dominion classical pair (linked in classical recitation: al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 70 lists Q 3:26 as the *du'ā al-mulk*; Q 67:1 as the doxology).

The *closest Quranic siblings* of Q 67 (by content + register, not by FR distance) are:
- **Q 71 Nūḥ** — shares *sabʿa samāwātin ṭibāqan* + cosmological-evidence rhetorical mode.
- **Q 32 al-Sajda** — Tirmidhī (idInBook 2975) and Dārimī (idInBook 2667) explicitly pair Q 32 + Q 67 as the Prophet's *nightly recitation pair*. FR distance = 0.7534 (Q 67's nearest mid-mufaṣṣal neighbour). The classical pairing is empirically vindicated.
- **Q 36 Yāsīn** — *al-Khaṭṭābī faḍāʾil* tradition's other anchor; Q 36 and Q 67 both have grave-protection / nightly-recitation tradition.

## 13. Register summary

Q 67 is **predominantly doxological-eschatological-creedal** (blocks A, C, E, F) with an embedded **legal-creedal-ethical injunction** (block D, vv. 12-15) and a closing **prophetic-imperative-stance** (blocks G, H, vv. 23-30). The proportions: doxological-cosmological (~12 verses), eschatological (~8 verses), prophetic-imperative (~8 verses), creedal-ethical (~2 verses) ≈ 40:27:27:6.

This is a **canonical Meccan-mufaṣṣal-awsāṭ surah**: roughly equal allocation across cosmological-evidence (creation, heavens, birds), eschatological-warning (Saʿīr, Day-terror), and prophetic-imperative (the *qul* cluster). The classical reading — Q 67 as a comprehensive *minor-Quran* compendium of all the dominant Meccan rhetorical modes — is descriptively accurate.

The classical naming convention (al-Mulk = opening word, NOT thematic-content) is consistent with this register-distribution: the surah does not have a single dominant content-cluster (light, night-journey, ʿĀd-Thamūd, etc.) that would license thematic-naming. *al-Mulk* is the surah's *opening-image* and *recurring-theme* (dominion-evidence), but the *mulk*-stem itself appears only once. The surah is named for its *thematic-frame* (dominion), enacted through *opening-word naming* (the standard convention for surahs without dense single-cluster vocabulary).
