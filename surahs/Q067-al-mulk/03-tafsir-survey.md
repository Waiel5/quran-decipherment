## 1. Surah-level openings: what each mufassir says about Q 67 as a whole

### 1.1 al-Qurṭubī's surah-purpose

al-Qurṭubī opens his Q 67 commentary (`qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt` Q67 marker): the surah is **Meccan by consensus** (مكية في قول الجميع), 30 verses, named *Sūrat al-Mulk*, also known as *al-Mānīʿa* and *al-Munjiya* and *al-Wāqīʿa al-Mujādila* (the disputant). al-Qurṭubī cites Ibn ʿAbbās via Tirmidhī and Abū Dāwūd for the *al-Mānīʿa* tradition. He frames the surah's *maqṣūd* as **dominion-evidence-and-grave-protection**: a doxological-eschatological framework whose primary classical use is recitation-protection of the dead.

### 1.2 al-Thaʿlabī's verse-letter-word counts

al-Thaʿlabī (`thaclabi-kashf-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67 opening) gives explicit counts for the surah. The classical figures (under the al-Thaʿlabī Uthmani-orthographic convention) are typically: **30 verses**, ~330 words, ~1,330 letters. (Project-computed counts: 30 verses ✓, 333 words [no-tashkeel-orthographic, mushaf-marks-stripped], 1,347 letters [no-spaces]. The 0.9% / 1.3% deltas are within the rules-tuple-discipline tolerance documented for al-Thaʿlabī's classical empirical figures; see Q 24 audit 8 in `surahs/Q024-al-nur/05-classical-claims-audit.md` for the Q 24 parallel.)

The verse-count of **30** is empirically verified — it matches Hafs-Kufan and aligns with the famous Tirmidhī #2890 ḥadīth (*sūratun min al-Qurʾāni thalāthūna āyatan shafaʿat li-ṣāḥibihā* — "a surah of the Quran of thirty verses pleads for its companion"). See audit 2 in `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for the full verification.

### 1.3 al-Ṭabarsī's Shīʿī perspective

al-Ṭabarsī (`tabarsi-majma-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67 opening) records the same Meccan-30-verse consensus. He preserves a Shīʿī tradition (via Imām al-Ṣādiq) recommending Q 67 recitation for grave-protection — the tradition is parallel to the Sunnī Tirmidhī chain and demonstrates the **cross-sectarian persistence** of the *al-Mānīʿa* tradition. See audit 3 in `05-classical-claims-audit.md` for the al-Tirmidhī chain quality audit.

### 1.4 al-Suyūṭī's revelation-order placement

Per `data/revelation-order.csv` and al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* nawʿ 1, Q 67 al-Mulk is revelation-order **#77 of 114** — middle-Meccan (post-early-Meccan-eschatology, pre-late-Meccan-prophetic-narrative). Internal asbāb-al-nuzūl support: the surah's content is doxological-cosmological-eschatological with no historical-event-anchored verse, consistent with middle-Meccan abstraction-period.

## 2. Q 67:1 — *tabāraka alladhī bi-yadihi al-mulk*

### 2.1 The *tabāraka*-cluster

al-Rāzī (`razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:1) treats *tabāraka* as a **special divine-blessing-superlative** that is only used of Allāh and His name. He cites the corpus's other 4 *tabāraka alladhī* attestations (Q 25:1, 25:10, 25:61, 43:85) as parallel divine-doxology openings. al-Rāzī's classical observation matches the project's empirical computation (5 corpus occurrences, computed; see `01-empirical-profile.md` §7).

### 2.2 The *bi-yadihi al-mulk* construction

al-Zamakhsharī (`zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:1) raises the classical question: is *bi-yadihi al-mulk* literal (anthropomorphic) or metaphorical? He sides firmly with the **majāz** (metaphorical) reading: the *yad* (hand) of Allāh is metaphor for the *qudra* (power) of Allāh. This is the standard Muʿtazilī-influenced reading.

al-Rāzī (Q67:1, after lengthy meta-commentary) reads *bi-yadihi* as **the locus of dispensation** — Allāh's "hand" is the locus from which dominion is given out. He cites Q 3:26 *Allāhumma mālika al-mulk tuʾtī al-mulka man tashāʾu* as the operational gloss: dominion is given from Allāh's "hand" to whomever He wills. The *tuʾtī al-mulka man tashāʾu* clause makes *yad* the giving-locus.

al-Qurṭubī (Q67:1) records the salaf reading (al-Bukhārī's bi-lā kayf principle): the *yad* is affirmed as Allāh's attribute *bi-lā kayf* (without specifying how) — neither literally anthropomorphic nor merely metaphorical. al-Qurṭubī's gloss: "the *yad* of Allāh is real but not like human hands; we believe in it without describing it."

The three classical positions (Muʿtazilī majāz / Razian dispensation / salaf bi-lā kayf) are theological-philosophical and not empirically resolvable on the project's methods. The empirical observation is that *bi-yadihi al-mulk* is a **corpus-singleton 2-word phrase** (Q067-F-03 in `06-novel-findings.md`) — its specific construction is unique to Q 67:1, which intensifies the theological stakes of the interpretive question.

## 3. Q 67:2 — death-before-life ordering

al-Qurṭubī (Q67:2) collects classical commentary on the verse's **death-before-life** order:
- Position 1 (Ibn ʿAbbās via Mujāhid): *al-mawt* = *al-nuṭfa* (the seminal drop, pre-life), *al-ḥayāt* = the breath-of-life. The order is then natural-developmental: pre-life → life.
- Position 2 (Qatāda): *al-mawt* = first death (pre-existence), *al-ḥayāt* = the worldly life. Same developmental order.
- Position 3 (al-Ḍaḥḥāk): *al-mawt* = death of the worldly life, *al-ḥayāt* = the resurrection. The order is then teleological: dying-then-resurrected.

al-Rāzī (Q67:2) endorses Position 1 with caveat: the verse's *li-yabluwakum ayyukum aḥsanu ʿamalan* makes worldly conduct the test, so position 1's ante-natal-then-life reading is contextually strongest.

## 4. Q 67:3-4 — the 7-heavens *ṭibāq* + visual return

### 4.1 *Ṭibāq*: layered or matched?

al-Ṭabarī (`tabari-jami-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:3) collects two readings:
- Position A (Mujāhid, al-Ḥasan): *ṭibāq* = layers, one above another (vertical-stacking, the "seven heavens stacked like floors").
- Position B (Ibn ʿAbbās minority): *ṭibāq* = matching/conforming to one another (i.e., each heaven matches the one below in structure).

al-Rāzī (Q67:3) sides with position A but notes the Arabic verb *ṭabaqa* is bidirectional — both *to layer* and *to match*. The two readings are not mutually exclusive: the heavens are *both* stacked AND mutually conforming. al-Biqāʿī (`biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:3) reads the *ṭibāq* construction as a structural cosmological term that corresponds to the verse's evidential argument: stacked AND matching = no-flaw evidence.

### 4.2 *Fa-rjiʿi al-baṣar* — the imperative

al-Zamakhsharī (Q67:3) reads *fa-rjiʿi al-baṣar* as **rhetorical imperative addressed to any beholder** — not specifically to the Prophet. The verse invites empirical testing of the claim: look, find no flaw, then look again, find your own gaze defeated.

al-Rāzī (Q67:3-4, his characteristic philosophical-exegetical mode) reads the *fa-rjiʿi … thumma rjiʿ … karratayn* as a **two-stage epistemic argument**:
1. First-iteration return-of-gaze: tests the cosmological claim (no flaw is found).
2. Second-iteration return-of-gaze: tests the limits of human perception (the gaze itself becomes exhausted *khāsiʾan*).

The argument's force, on al-Rāzī's reading, is that **even when human perception is given full opportunity to disconfirm the cosmological claim, it cannot — and the perception itself confesses its limits**. This is one of the classical Quranic-rhetorical-style proofs that al-Rāzī treats as foundational for *kalām* (theology) argumentation.

### 4.3 *Karratayn*: literal or iterative?

al-Qurṭubī (Q67:4) records the dispute:
- Position A (al-Rāzī, al-Ṭabarī majority): *karratayn* = exactly two times. The dual-form is precise.
- Position B (al-Biqāʿī, al-Khaṭṭābī minority): *karratayn* = repeated iteration, an idiomatic for "again and again." The dual is rhetorical (cf. *labbayka* "twice-here-am-I" = "fully here").

al-Qurṭubī sides with Position A (literal). al-Biqāʿī's *iterative* reading would soften the claim from "exactly two iterations" to "iterated trial." The classical majority is literal.

## 5. Q 67:5 — *maṣābīḥ* and *rujūm*

al-Ṭabarī (`tabari-jami-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:5) collects multiple isnads on the *rujūm* (projectiles for the devils) tradition, tracing back through al-Suddī from Ibn ʿAbbās. The *rujūm*-doctrine — that meteoric streams are part of celestial defense against eavesdropping demons — appears at Q 15:18 (*illā man istaraqa al-samʿ fa-atbaʿahu shihābun mubīn*), Q 37:10, Q 72:8-9. Q 67:5 is one of the four canonical attestations.

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* (`suyuti-durr-manthur.openiti.raw.txt`, Q67:5) preserves multiple Companion-traditions on the *maṣābīḥ* / *rujūm* dual-function: the same celestial bodies that *adorn* the heavens also *defend* against the demons. al-Rāzī (Q67:5) raises the philosophical-cosmological question: are these the *same* lamps, or different objects? He concludes they are the same — visible from earth as adornment, functional in the heavens as missiles.

## 6. Q 67:8-11 — the Hellfire-keeper dialogue

### 6.1 *Hādhā khazana al-saʿīr*

al-Qurṭubī (Q67:8) reads the *khazana* (keepers) as the angels of hellfire (cf. Q 96:18 *sa-na-dʿu al-zabāniya* — the zabāniya angels of fire). The questioning *a-lam yaʾtikum nadhīr* is one of the corpus's only dialogues *between angels-of-hell and the damned* — most other eschatological dialogues are between Allāh and the damned, or between Allāh and the prophets.

al-Rāzī (Q67:8) reads the *fawj-by-fawj* arrival ("each time a *fawj* is thrown into it") as evidence of the **continuous-arrival eschatology**: hellfire is fed in batches, each batch questioned, each batch confessing. The structure is **forensic-judicial**: the *fawj* is asked the *defense question* (did you receive a warning?), the disbelievers' answer is the *confession* (yes, but we denied), and the closing *fa-ʿtarafū bi-dhanbihim* (then they admitted their sin) is the **explicit-acknowledgment-of-guilt** clause that closes the forensic-eschatological case.

### 6.2 Q 67:10 — the triplet *naẓarnā / sami'nā / ʿaqalnā*

al-Biqāʿī (Q67:10) reads the Hellfire-confession's reference to *al-samʿ* (hearing) and *al-ʿaql* (reasoning) — but NOT to *al-baṣar* (sight) — as deliberate. The disbelievers confess they failed to listen and reason; they do NOT confess they failed to look. al-Biqāʿī's gloss: the *fa-rjiʿi al-baṣar* of v. 3 is *evidence already presented*, the disbelievers cannot deny it; they retreat to the *internal-faculty* failure (we did not hear with our hearts; we did not use our reason). This is one of al-Biqāʿī's clearest examples of his *naẓm* (sequential-coherence) doctrine: the surah's argument is internally cross-referenced.

## 7. Q 67:15 — earth-as-tame and *al-nushūr*

### 7.1 The *dhalūl* metaphor

al-Qurṭubī (Q67:15) records the classical position: *dhalūl* (tame, broken-in) describes the earth as an obedient mount. The earth is configured for human use — habitable, walkable, fertile. The metaphor uses the same root that describes the cow at Q 2:71 (*lā dhalūlun tuthīru al-arḍ wa-lā tasqī al-ḥarth* — neither tamed-to-plow nor irrigating-the-tilth). The earth-as-tame metaphor is unique to Q 67 in the cosmological-evidence context.

### 7.2 *Al-nushūr* (resurrection) — first occurrence in surah

al-Rāzī (Q67:15) treats the closing fāṣila *wa-ilayhi al-nushūr* as a **structural pivot**: the surah's first half (vv. 1-15) has been doxological-cosmological; from v. 16 onward it pivots into eschatological-judgmental. The *nushūr* term anticipates the resurrection-judgment that will be implied throughout vv. 16-30.

## 8. Q 67:23 — the sense-organ triplet

al-Rāzī (Q67:23) emphasizes the **developmental order** of *al-samʿ wa-l-abṣār wa-l-afʾida* — hearing first, then sight, then hearts/intellects. This order matches the prenatal-developmental sequence: hearing forms in utero before sight; intellect develops post-natally. The same triplet in the same order appears at Q 16:78 (*ja'ala lakum al-samʿa wa-l-abṣār wa-l-afʾida*), Q 23:78, Q 32:9, Q 46:26 — five-time corpus-cluster.

al-Biqāʿī (Q67:23) reads the verse-closing *qalīlan mā tashkurūn* as the **rebuke that bridges sense-organ and gratitude**: the divine gift of cognitive-faculty deserves reciprocal gratitude.

## 9. Q 67:28-30 — the closing triple-*qul*

### 9.1 The structural function

al-Biqāʿī (Q67:28-30) reads the closing three *qul* statements as the **final-prophetic-rhetoric**:
- v. 28 (fate-question): the disbelievers cannot evade *ʿadhāb alīm* whether the Prophet is destroyed or saved.
- v. 29 (Lord-affirmation): explicit declaration of belief in al-Raḥmān; reliance upon Him.
- v. 30 (water-question): the surah's hydrological-eschatology bookend.

The bookending of vv. 28 and 30 with *qul aʾraʾaytum* anaphora is one of the corpus's clearest small-scale chiastic frames: faith-affirmation in v. 29 is bracketed by two *aʾraʾaytum* questions.

### 9.2 *Bi-māʾin maʿīn*

al-Qurṭubī (Q67:30) notes that *māʾun maʿīn* (flowing water; literally "water-running-from-the-eye-of-the-spring") appears 3× in the corpus (Q 67:30, Q 23:50, Q 37:45). The term names *spring-water* specifically, distinct from rain-water (*māʾ al-samāʾ*) or ocean (*baḥr*). The closing v. 30 thus invokes the most-fundamental hydrological resource — groundwater spring-fed potable water — as the surah's final dominion-evidence.

## 11. al-Biqāʿī on the Q 66 → Q 67 / Q 67 → Q 68 *tanāsub*

### 11.1 Q 66 (al-Taḥrīm) → Q 67 (al-Mulk)

al-Biqāʿī (`biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` Q67 opening) reads the Q 66 → Q 67 transition as **household-discipline** → **cosmological-doxology**. Q 66 closes with the Pharaoh-wife / Maryam exemplars (vv. 11-12); Q 67 opens with the *tabāraka* dominion-doxology. al-Biqāʿī's reading: the household-righteous-women of Q 66 are the *micro-instance* of the dominion-righteousness that Q 67 unfolds at *macro* scale.

This classical *tanāsub* claim is descriptively coherent; the empirical canonical-adjacency cost of Q 66-Q 67 is 0.0780 length-units (rank 47/113 — middle-of-pack), suggesting the transition is content-natural, not register-mismatched (cf. `01-empirical-profile.md` §3).

### 11.2 Q 67 (al-Mulk) → Q 68 (al-Qalam)

al-Biqāʿī reads Q 67 → Q 68 as **dominion-evidence-shown** → **the-pen-that-records-the-dominion-evidence**. Q 67 closes with *qul aʾraʾaytum in aṣbaḥa māʾukum ghawran* (the water-question); Q 68 opens with *nūn wa-l-qalami wa-mā yasṭurūn* (the pen and what they write). al-Biqāʿī's reading: the closing rhetorical-question of Q 67 is *answered* by the writing-recording instrument of Q 68 — the *qalam* records both the question AND the answer for posthumous-eschatological reference.

The empirical Q 67 → Q 68 adjacency cost is 0.0962 (rank 36/113 — also middle-of-pack). The al-Biqāʿī tanāsub is consistent with the empirical content-naturalness of the transition.

## 13. One-paragraph synthesis

Q 67's classical reception clusters on three axes: (i) the **doxological-dominion** (Q 67:1's *tabāraka alladhī bi-yadihi al-mulk*, with its theological-philosophical disputes about the *yad*), (ii) the **cosmological-evidence-and-visual-return** (Q 67:3-4's *fa-rjiʿi al-baṣar*, with disputes about *karratayn* and *ṭibāq*), and (iii) the **eschatological-Hellfire** (Q 67:6-11's Saʿīr-keeper-dialogue, with the developmental triplet *naẓarnā / sami'nā / ʿaqalnā*). Across all three axes, classical mufassirūn — including al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Biqāʿī, al-Suyūṭī — converge on Q 67 as a **doxological-cosmological-eschatological compendium**, structurally arranged as an arched argument from creation-evidence to eschatological-implication. The surah's classical *al-Mānīʿa* / *al-Munjiya* naming traditions (audited in `04-hadith-corpus.md` and `05-classical-claims-audit.md`) are integrated with this exegetical reading: the surah's *grave-protection* function is the *practical-recitation* application of its *cosmological-eschatological* content. The empirical findings — Q 67's UAS rank 102/114 (low), corpus-singleton phrases (high), FR-nearest-neighbour to Q 32 al-Sajda (vindicating the classical pair-recitation tradition), and mlk-stem density NULL (refuting the name-tracks-vocabulary hypothesis) — refine the classical reading: **Q 67's distinctness lives at the level of TOKEN-LEVEL phrase-uniqueness and RECITATION-TRADITION USE, not in fawāṣila-virtuosity or structural-architectural disrupter status**.
