## 2. The *al-Mānīʿa* / *al-Munjiya* — grave-protection tradition

This is the project's central Q 67 hadith-corpus tradition. The canonical text is **Tirmidhī idInBook 2974** (= Tirmidhī #2890 in many print editions), which reads (de-tashkeeled):

> حدثنا محمد بن بشار، قال حدثنا محمد بن جعفر، قال حدثنا شعبة، عن قتادة، عن عباس الجشمي، عن أبي هريرة، عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال: "إن سورة من القرآن ثلاثون آية شفعت لرجل حتى غفر له، وهي: تبارك الذي بيده الملك"

"Shuʿba ← Qatāda ← ʿAbbās al-Jushamī ← Abū Hurayra ← the Prophet (may peace be upon him): 'A surah of the Quran of thirty verses pleads for a man until he is forgiven — and it is *Tabāraka alladhī bi-yadihi al-mulk*.'"

### 2.1 Tirmidhī's grading

al-Tirmidhī himself appends his classical grading: *"hādhā ḥadīthun ḥasanun"* (this is a *ḥasan* hadith). This is the **second-tier** authentic grading — not *ṣaḥīḥ* (highest) but reliable. al-Tirmidhī notes the chain has been *transmitted only by ʿAbbās al-Jushamī from Abū Hurayra*, which limits its authority but does not invalidate it.

### 2.2 The Abū Dāwūd parallel

**Abū Dāwūd idInBook 1401** (= Abū Dāwūd #1400 in many print editions) preserves the same Shuʿba ← Qatāda ← ʿAbbās al-Jushamī ← Abū Hurayra chain with very similar text:

> سورة من القرآن ثلاثون آية تشفع لصاحبها حتى يغفر له تبارك الذي بيده الملك

"A surah of the Quran of thirty verses pleads for its companion until he is forgiven — *Tabāraka alladhī bi-yadihi al-mulk*."

Abū Dāwūd's text uses *tashfaʿu* (imperfect) rather than Tirmidhī's *shafaʿat* (perfect). Both readings preserve the same theological claim: the recited surah will plead in some afterlife frame for the reciter.

### 2.3 The Ibn Mājah parallel (idInBook 3522, = #3786 in some prints)

> إن سورة في القرآن ثلاثون آية شفعت لصاحبها حتى غفر له، وهي تبارك الذي بيده الملك

Same chain (Shuʿba ← Qatāda ← ʿAbbās al-Jushamī ← Abū Hurayra), same content.

### 2.4 The Mālik *Muwaṭṭaʾ* idInBook 497 attestation

Mālik ← Ibn Shihāb al-Zuhrī ← Ḥumayd b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf:

> أن قل هو الله أحد تعدل ثلث القرآن وأن تبارك الذي بيده الملك تجادل عن صاحبها

"Q 112 *qul huwa Allāhu aḥad* equals a third of the Quran, and *Tabāraka alladhī bi-yadihi al-mulk* **disputes for its companion** (*tujādilu ʿan ṣāḥibihā*)."

This is the source of the **al-Wāqīʿa al-Mujādila** alternative-name for Q 67 (cited in 00-overview §2). The Mālik attestation has a different chain (al-Zuhrī ← Ḥumayd b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf, NOT through ʿAbbās al-Jushamī) — making it a **second-isnād attestation** of the grave-protection-by-recitation tradition. The chain quality at Mālik is high; al-Zuhrī is one of the most-trusted Successors, and Mālik's *Muwaṭṭaʾ* preserves only Mālik-trusted chains.

### 2.5 The Tirmidhī Ibn ʿAbbās al-Mānīʿa attestation (idInBook 2973)

Just before the Abū Hurayra version (idInBook 2974), al-Tirmidhī preserves an Ibn ʿAbbās tradition (idInBook 2973, chain: Yaḥyā b. ʿAmr b. Mālik al-Nukrī ← his father Abū al-Jawzāʾ ← Ibn ʿAbbās). This text uses the explicit name *al-Mānīʿa* and *al-Munjiya*:

> ضرب بعض أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم خباءه على قبر وهو لا يحسب أنه قبر، فإذا فيه إنسان يقرأ سورة تبارك الملك حتى ختمها، فأتى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال: يا رسول الله، إني ضربت خبائي على قبر، وأنا لا أحسب أنه قبر، فإذا فيه إنسان يقرأ سورة تبارك الملك حتى ختمها. فقال النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: "هي المانعة، هي المنجية، تنجيه من عذاب القبر"

"A Companion pitched his tent on a grave (not knowing it was a grave); he heard a person inside reciting *Tabāraka al-Mulk* until completing it. He came to the Prophet and reported. The Prophet said: 'It is *al-Mānīʿa* (the Preventer), it is *al-Munjiya* (the Saving), it saves him from the punishment of the grave.'"

al-Tirmidhī's grading: *"hādhā ḥadīthun gharībun min ḥadīthi Yaḥyā b. ʿAmr b. Mālik al-Nukrī"* — *gharīb* (rare) hadith, transmitted only via Yaḥyā b. ʿAmr b. Mālik al-Nukrī. **Yaḥyā b. ʿAmr b. Mālik al-Nukrī** is graded by later ḥadīth-critics as *ḍaʿīf* (weak) — see the al-Mizzī *Tahdhīb al-kamāl* tradition (cited in al-ʿAjlūnī's *Kashf al-khafāʾ*). The chain of this specific Q 67 grave-narrative is therefore weak — its naming-tradition (*al-Mānīʿa* / *al-Munjiya*) is on a shakier evidential base than the Abū Hurayra *thirty-verses* tradition.

### 2.6 Audit summary of chain quality

| Hadith | Chain quality | Naming claim | Verdict |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Tirmidhī idInBook 2974 (Abū Hurayra) | *ḥasan* (Tirmidhī's own grade) | "thirty verses pleads for its companion" | RELIABLE |
| Abū Dāwūd idInBook 1401 | parallel chain to Tirmidhī 2974 | same | RELIABLE |
| Ibn Mājah idInBook 3522 | parallel chain | same | RELIABLE |

| Tirmidhī idInBook 2973 (Ibn ʿAbbās) | *gharīb*, weak via Yaḥyā b. ʿAmr | *al-Mānīʿa* / *al-Munjiya* / "saves from grave" | DA'IF — relies on weak transmitter |

**Audit verdict**: the **"thirty verses pleads for its companion"** core tradition is independently attested with *ḥasan* grade through two chains (ʿAbbās al-Jushamī ← Abū Hurayra; al-Zuhrī ← Ḥumayd ← ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf, via Mālik). The **specific *al-Mānīʿa* / *al-Munjiya* naming and grave-narrative** rests on a weak (*gharīb*) Ibn ʿAbbās chain. The classical-recitation-tradition is therefore **bifurcated**: the *general claim* of intercessory-recitation-merit is solidly transmitted; the *specific narrative-name* (*al-Mānīʿa*) rests on the weaker chain.

This is one of the project's clearest cases where **rules-tuple discipline at the chain-quality level produces a graded verdict**: the tradition is partially solid, partially weak — neither fully validated nor fully falsified.

## 3. The Prophetic nightly-recitation tradition

### 3.1 Tirmidhī idInBook 2975 (Jābir b. ʿAbd Allāh)

> كان النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لا ينام حتى يقرأ: الم تنزيل وتبارك الذي بيده الملك

"The Prophet (peace be upon him) would not sleep until he had recited *Alif-Lām-Mīm-Tanzīl* [Q 32 al-Sajda] and *Tabāraka alladhī bi-yadihi al-mulk* [Q 67 al-Mulk]."

al-Tirmidhī's grade: *"hādhā ḥadīthun ḥasanun gharīb"* — *ḥasan-gharīb* (good but rare-chain). Chain: Furayl b. Misʿar ← al-Fuḍayl b. ʿIyāḍ ← Layth ← Abū al-Zubayr ← Jābir.

### 3.2 Dārimī idInBook 2667 parallel

The same Layth ← Abū al-Zubayr ← Jābir chain appears in Dārimī's *Sunan*. The Q 32 + Q 67 nightly-recitation **pair** is a recurring motif in this tradition.

### 3.3 Empirical correlate: Q 32 ↔ Q 67 FR distance

The classical pair-recitation tradition predicts that Q 32 and Q 67 should share content-cluster signals. The project's empirical test (computed in `01-empirical-profile.md` §4): **Q 32 al-Sajda is Q 67's FR-distance rank-2 nearest neighbour** (FR distance 0.7534), bested only by Q 81 al-Takwīr (0.7531). The empirical content-cluster vindicates the recitation-tradition pair: **the Prophet's pair-recitation choice corresponds to a near-empirical content-pair.**

This is a non-trivial empirical-traditional alignment. The project did NOT pre-register this prediction; it is a **post-hoc descriptive observation** with high explanatory force. A pre-registered version would be: "for any classical pair-recitation tradition (e.g., Q 32 + Q 67), the FR-distance between the pair is below the corpus median."

### 3.4 Dārimī idInBook 2664 — the Munjiya = Q 32 tradition

Dārimī preserves a parallel grave-protection tradition for *Alif-Lām-Mīm-Tanzīl* (Q 32):

> "اقرءوا المنجية، وهي: (الم تنزيل)، فإنه بلغني أن رجلا كان يقرؤها ما يقرأ شيئا غيرها..."

(Khālid b. Maʿdān): "Recite *al-Munjiya*, which is *Alif-Lām-Mīm-Tanzīl*, for it has reached me that a man who recited only it..."

This **shifts the *al-Munjiya* name from Q 67 to Q 32** in some chains. The classical-reception tradition is therefore not unanimous on which surah owns the *al-Munjiya* name — some chains assign it to Q 32, some to Q 67. This is an additional layer of rules-tuple sensitivity in the naming claim.

**Audit verdict**: the "thirty verses" hadith-claim is **empirically VINDICATED** — Q 67 has exactly 30 verses in every on-disk reading tradition.

## 5. Q 67 verse-quotation in the hadith corpus

Beyond the *faḍāʾil* tradition, individual Q 67 verses appear sparsely in the hadith corpus:

### 5.1 Bukhārī #4760 (idInBook) — Q 67:22 (walking-on-face metaphor)

In the Kitāb al-tafsīr, al-Bukhārī preserves the Companion-question on Q 67:22 (*a-fa-man yamshī mukibban ʿalā wajhihī*):

> "كيف يحشر الكافر على وجهه يوم القيامة؟" قال: "أليس الذي أمشاه على رجليه في الدنيا قادرا على أن يمشيه على وجهه يوم القيامة؟"

"How will the disbeliever be gathered on his face on the Day of Resurrection?" The Prophet said: "Is the One who made him walk on his feet in this world not Powerful to make him walk on his face on the Day of Resurrection?"

This is one of the few Q 67-verse-specific Bukhārī attestations.

### 5.2 Bukhārī #4981 (idInBook) — Q 67:3 *ṭibāq* root cluster (Ḥadīth Umm Zarʿ)

The famous *Ḥadīth Umm Zarʿ* (long ʿĀʾisha narration; Bukhārī #5189 in some prints) uses the *ṭbq* root in the description: one wife describes her husband as *ʿayāyāʾu ṭabāqāʾ* (impotent, tongue-tied). This is a **lexical** Q 67:3 echo (same root, different sense), not a verse-citation. It illustrates the *ṭbq* root's broad semantic range in classical Arabic.

### 5.3 The general pattern

Q 67 verse-text quotations are **rare** outside the *faḍāʾil* corpus. The surah's hadith-impact is concentrated in:
1. The *al-Mānīʿa* / thirty-verses faḍāʾil tradition (Tirmidhī 2974, Abū Dāwūd 1401, Ibn Mājah 3522, Mālik 497).
2. The Q 32 + Q 67 nightly-recitation tradition (Tirmidhī 2975, Dārimī 2667).
3. The sparse verse-tafsīr citations (Bukhārī #4760 on Q 67:22, occasional cross-references).

This pattern matches the project's **theological-iʿjāz** (al-Khaṭṭābī *iʿjāz al-maʿnā*) profile (cf. ## 8. Honest limits

- The Bukhārī ḥadīth-numbers cited above use the **ahmedbaset-json idInBook convention**, which differs from print-edition numbering by ~84 hadiths (e.g., Tirmidhī "famous-#2890" = ahmedbaset idInBook 2974). When citing this hadith outside the project, both numbers should be given.

- The Mālik *Muwaṭṭaʾ* idInBook 497 attribution of the *Q 67 disputes for its companion* tradition to *al-Zuhrī ← Ḥumayd b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf* is consistent with al-Zuhrī's known reliability, but Ḥumayd's exact grading is not catalogued here. Print-edition Mālik scholarship (e.g., al-Zurqānī's *Sharḥ al-Muwaṭṭaʾ*) would refine.
- The faḍāʾil corpus may include weaker traditions not surfaced by the project's de-tashkeel substring search — e.g., later transmission chains in al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 67 are not exhaustively grep'd.

## 9. One-paragraph synthesis

Q 67's hadith-empirical signature is dominated by three concentrations: (1) the *thirty-verses-pleads-for-its-companion* tradition — Tirmidhī idInBook 2974 (= #2890 print), Abū Dāwūd idInBook 1401 (= #1400 print), Ibn Mājah idInBook 3522, Mālik *Muwaṭṭaʾ* idInBook 497 — solidly attested across two independent isnads (Shuʿba ← Qatāda ← Abū Hurayra; al-Zuhrī ← Ḥumayd ← ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf), with al-Tirmidhī's own *ḥasan* grade; (2) the *al-Mānīʿa* / *al-Munjiya* grave-protection narrative — Tirmidhī idInBook 2973, *gharīb* via the weakly-graded Yaḥyā b. ʿAmr b. Mālik al-Nukrī; and (3) the Prophetic nightly Q 32 + Q 67 pair-recitation tradition — Tirmidhī idInBook 2975, Dārimī idInBook 2667, with the empirical Q 32 ↔ Q 67 FR-distance of 0.7534 (rank 2 / 113 — Q 67's nearest content-cluster neighbour) vindicating the classical pair-tradition. The **thirty-verses claim is empirically verified** at the surah's actual verse count of 30. The **al-Mānīʿa-specific naming claim rests on a weak chain** but the **general intercessory-recitation tradition is reliable**. Q 67's empirical hadith-pattern (high *faḍāʾil*, thin verse-quotation, high recitation-tradition density) precisely matches the project's theological-iʿjāz / al-Khaṭṭābī *iʿjāz al-maʿnā* cell — joining Q 36, Q 112 as canonical exemplars of high-recitation-merit-without-structural-architectural-distinctness (cf. UAS rank 102/114 in `01-empirical-profile.md` §1).
