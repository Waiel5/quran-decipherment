# Q 37 al-Ṣāffāt — Classical Tafsīr Survey

Survey of 6 classical mufassirūn on the major exegetical loci of Q 37: (a) the triple-oath opener Q 37:1-3; (b) the sacrifice-of-Ishmael narrative Q 37:99-113; (c) the prophet-cycle structure; (d) the *salāmun ʿalā [PROPHET]* refrain.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*)

al-Ṭabarī's discussion of Q 37 (per the OpenITI raw text, available indirectly via al-Qurṭubī's and Ibn Kathīr's citations):

### Q 37:1-3 referent
al-Ṭabarī catalogs the major Companion+Successor positions:
- **Angels in worship-rows** (Ibn ʿAbbās, Ibn Masʿūd, ʿIkrima, Saʿīd b. Jubayr, Mujāhid, Qatāda) — the dominant view.
- **Cloud-driving angels** for *al-zājirāt* (al-Suddī).
- **The Quranic verses themselves** (one of the al-Rāzī "vajh-IV" Quran-self-reflexive readings; al-Ṭabarī notes this less prominently).
- **Birds in flight** (a less-cited reading; cf. Q 67:19 *al-ṭayru ṣāffātin*).

### Q 37:99-113 sacrifice
al-Ṭabarī famously catalogs **both positions** on the *dhabīḥ*'s identity:
- **Ismāʿīl** (preferred by majority Sunni view): the natural-language sequence in the surah indicates the announced *ghulām ḥalīm* (v. 101) is the son of the sacrifice; the announcement of *Isḥāq* in v. 112 follows the resolution of the sacrifice, indicating a separate (later-born) son.
- **Isḥāq** (the *qawl thānī*, attributed to several Companions including ʿUmar, ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, al-ʿAbbās in some chains; al-Ṭabarī gives this view substantial space).

al-Ṭabarī's editorial position leans toward Ismāʿīl but presents both with their isnad-chains.

## 2. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`, lines 204252-204700+ (the Q 37 section).

### Q 37:1-3 — al-Rāzī's exhaustive multi-vajh enumeration

al-Rāzī (vol. 26 p. 313, OpenITI) provides what is arguably the corpus's most thorough analysis of the trio's referent. He divides the question into TWO scenarios:

**Scenario I**: the three are attributes of ONE subject (*ṣifātan thalāthatan li-mawṣūfin wāḥidin*):
- **Vajh 1**: angels in worship-rows.
- **Vajh 2**: angels with cosmic-causal influence on terrestrial events (a metaphysical reading reflecting al-Rāzī's three-tier cosmology: (i) Pure Cause — God; (ii) Pure Effect — bodies; (iii) intermediate Spiritual-substances that both cause and are caused — angels). The *ṣāffāt* signal angels' standing-in-rank in their adoration-mode; *zājirāt* signal their causal-influence on bodies (driving clouds, repulsing demons, urging human souls); *tāliyāt* signal their cognitive-discursive activity (recital of remembrance).
- **Vajh 3**: the worshippers-in-prayer-rows (humans). *Ṣāffāt* = the rows in *jamāʿa*-prayer; *zājirāt* = the *aʿūdhu bi-llāh* invocation that repulses Satan during prayer; *tāliyāt* = the recitation of the Quran in prayer. al-Rāzī notes a hadith where the Prophet ﷺ heard Abū Bakr reciting softly and ʿUmar loudly, and asked each their reason — Abū Bakr: "the listener is Hearing, Knowing"; ʿUmar: "I awaken the drowsy and repel Satan."
- **Vajh 4**: warriors in battle-array. *Ṣāffāt* = battle-rows (cf. Q 61:4 *innallāha yuḥibbu al-ladhīna yuqātilūna fī sabīlihi ṣaffā*); *zājirāt* = the war-cry; *tāliyāt* = the warriors' recitation of Allah's name during combat.
- **Vajh 5**: the Quran's own verses. *Ṣāffāt* = Quranic verses arranged in their canonical order; *zājirāt* = the verses that prohibit; *tāliyāt* = the verses that exhort to good actions.

**Scenario II**: three distinct subjects (*ashyāʾan thalāthatan mutabāyinatan*):
- *Ṣāffāt* = birds in flight (per Q 24:41 *wa-l-ṭayru ṣāffāt* and Q 67:19); *zājirāt* = "anything that repulses from disobedience to God"; *tāliyāt* = "anything that recites from the Book of God."
- *Ṣāffāt* = bodies (the corporeal realm in cosmological ranks); *zājirāt* = the spirit-substances acting upon bodies; *tāliyāt* = the highest spirit-substances absorbed in remembrance of God.

### Q 37:99-113 sacrifice
al-Rāzī follows the majority-Sunni Ismāʿīl identification and catalogs the v. 101 (*ghulām ḥalīm*) → v. 112 (*Isḥāq*) sequencing as decisive: the announced son must be Ismāʿīl, and the Isḥāq announcement is a separate, later annunciation.

al-Rāzī also stresses the *theodicy of the test*: the test is NOT a divine cruelty; the test functions as an *iẓhār li-faḍīlat al-ʿabd* (manifestation of the servant's excellence). He cites v. 106 *inna hādhā lahuwa al-balāʾu al-mubīn* as the explicit Quranic theodicy-frame.

### Q 37 prophet-cycle structure
al-Rāzī notes that Q 37's prophet-vignettes follow a uniform 4-element template: (a) *wa-laqad-naʿma* / *inna [PROPHET] la-min al-mursalīn* opener; (b) prophet-action narrative; (c) *wa-taraknā ʿalayhi fī al-ākhirīn*; (d) *salāmun ʿalā [PROPHET]* + *innā kadhālika najzī al-muḥsinīn*. al-Rāzī comments on the variation in salām-form (singular *ibrāhīm*, dual *mūsā wa-hārūn*, plural-marked *ilyāsīn*).

## 3. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt`, lines 134023+.

al-Qurṭubī catalogs:
- the qirāʾāt variations on Q 37:1-3 (al-Naḥḥās's three objections to Ḥamza's idghām reading).
- The 5+ exegetical positions on the *ṣāffāt/zājirāt/tāliyāt* referent (matching al-Rāzī's catalog).
- The astronomy-cosmology of v. 5 *rabbu al-mashāriq* (the 365-mashriq tradition; the *kawwāt* — sun-windows — that the sun rises through across the year).
- al-Qurṭubī cites a hadith on Umayya b. Abī al-Ṣalt's poetry being "believing in his speech, disbelieving in his heart."
- For Q 37:147 *miʾat alf aw yazīdūn*, al-Qurṭubī catalogs the variant numerical opinions (some early sources said 130,000 or 150,000; the Quranic *aw yazīdūn* makes the figure indeterminate-upper).

## 4. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`, lines 101581+.

Ibn Kathīr opens the surah with a hadith from al-Nasāʾī via Ibn ʿUmar:
> *kāna rasūlu allāhi ṣallā allāhu ʿalayhi wa-sallam yaʾmurunā bi-l-takhfīf wa-yaʾummunā bi-l-ṣāffāt* — "The Messenger ﷺ used to command us to lighten our prayer, and used to lead us in prayer with [Sūrat] al-Ṣāffāt." (al-Nasāʾī solo; *tafarrada bihi al-Nasāʾī*.)

This grounds Q 37 as a **Prophetic-recitation-favored** surah — not despite its 182-verse length but specifically as a *moderate-length* prayer-recitation. Ibn Kathīr's extended discussion of Q 37:99-113 is pivotal: he gives a robust **defense of the Ismāʿīl identification** against the Isḥāq-tradition (which he attributes to Jewish-source contamination via *aḥbār ahl al-kitāb*). His arguments:
- The Hebrew Bible itself records that Ismāʿīl was Abraham's eldest son (born when Abraham was 86) and that Isḥāq was born when Abraham was 99. The "sacrifice-of-the-only-son" frame indicates Ismāʿīl (the only son between Ismāʿīl-birth and Isḥāq-birth, a 13-year window).
- The Quranic narrative-sequence v.101 (*ghulām ḥalīm*) → sacrifice → v.112 (*Isḥāq* annunciation) requires the dhabīḥ to be the v.101 son (Ismāʿīl).
- The Hebrew Bible's "your only son" (*yaḥīdaka*) was — in Ibn Kathīr's view — a polemical rewriting that substituted "Isḥāq" for "Ismāʿīl" because Isḥāq is the Israelite ancestor while Ismāʿīl is the Arab ancestor.

Ibn Kathīr also catalogs:
- The *kabsh* (ram) of v. 107: a white-eyed, horned ram, descended from Paradise (per Saʿīd b. Jubayr from Ibn ʿAbbās), grazing in Paradise for 40 years. al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī gave it the name *Jarīr*.
- The ram-head was preserved at the Kaʿba's mīzāb (drainpipe) until the time of Ibn ʿAbbās.
- The classical-uṣūl debate: this verse is cited as evidence for *naskh qabla al-tamakkun min al-fiʿl* (abrogation before-execution-becomes-possible), against a Muʿtazila position. Ibrāhīm was commanded to sacrifice; the command was abrogated mid-act and substituted with the ram.

## 5. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`, multiple references to al-Ṣāffāt.

al-Biqāʿī's specialty is munāsabāt — the inter-surah and intra-surah connections. For Q 37:
- **Q 36 → Q 37 munāsabah**: al-Biqāʿī links Yāsīn (Q 36) to al-Ṣāffāt (Q 37) via the **closing of Q 36 + opening of Q 37**: Q 36:79-83 closes with God's creative power and resurrection-affirmation (*qul yuḥyīhā al-ladhī anshaʾahā awwala marra*); Q 37:1-5 opens with the cosmic oath + monotheism (*inna ilāhakum la-wāḥid*) + creation-affirmation (*rabbu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ*). The seam is **theological-creational**: from "He resurrects the dead" (Q 36 close) to "your God is one, Lord of heavens and earth" (Q 37 open). EMPIRICAL CORRELATE: Q 36→Q 37 fraction_residual = 0.0080 (very low); the seam is empirically smooth.
- **Q 37 → Q 38 munāsabah**: al-Biqāʿī notes both surahs share a prophet-cycle compendium structure. EMPIRICAL CORRELATE: Q 37→Q 38 fraction_residual = 0.0000 (clamped — seamless). One of 13 clamped-zero adjacencies in the corpus. Empirical seam-validation of al-Biqāʿī's munāsabah.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr*)

al-Suyūṭī classifies Q 37 in *al-Itqān* nawʿ 67 (al-aqsām) as one of the major oath-opener surahs. The opening *wa-l-ṣāffāti ṣaffā* is catalogued among the angelic-host oaths.

In nawʿ 1 (chronology), Q 37 is placed at revelation order 56/114 — mid-Meccan, after Q 36 Yāsīn (rev. 41) and before Q 38 Ṣād (rev. 38, which by mushaf-position adjacency comes AFTER but by revelation order PRECEDES — a structural discontinuity that classical scholars noted).

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 37:99-113 collects the multiple traditions on the dhabīḥ's identity and the kabsh's descent.
