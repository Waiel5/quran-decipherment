# Q 5 al-Māʾida — Classical Tafsir Survey

This file surveys ≥ 5 classical mufassirūn on Q 5. Each citation gives scholar + work + passage anchor.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*)

Source path: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabari-jami-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Ṭabarī's signature on Q 5:
- **Q 5:1 — *al-ʿuqūd***: al-Ṭabarī catalogs four interpretations of *ʿuqūd*: (a) the covenants God made with humanity at the beginning; (b) the covenants of Islam (prayer, zakat, etc.); (c) contracts between people; (d) the contracts of the Pact between God and Banī Isrāʾīl. He prefers (c) — the broadest contractual-fulfillment reading — citing al-Suddī.
- **Q 5:3 — completion of religion**: al-Ṭabarī reports the Bukhārī #45 / Tirmidhī #3043 ʿArafāt context with multiple isnāds; affirms the verse was revealed at ʿArafāt during the Farewell Pilgrimage. He reports that the Prophet died 81 (or 82, per other isnāds) days after this verse was revealed.
- **Q 5:6 — wuḍūʾ**: al-Ṭabarī surveys the disputes over WHETHER the feet are washed or wiped — settles on washing being the sound reading per the recitation tradition, while wiping (*masḥ*) is the Shīʿī tradition. The disputed *qirāʾāt* over *arjulakum* (acc.) vs *arjulikum* (gen.) tracks exactly this dispute.
- **Q 5:32 — universal life**: al-Ṭabarī notes the maxim is "written upon Banī Isrāʾīl" — addressing the Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5 parallel ("whoever destroys a single soul is considered to have destroyed an entire world"). The Quranic universalization is achieved in the second half of the verse: the maxim is given to humanity through them.
- **Q 5:51 — walāyah**: al-Ṭabarī surveys multiple asbāb al-nuzūl: (a) ʿUbāda b. al-Ṣāmit's withdrawal from his pact with Jews of Medina; (b) the Banū Qaynuqāʿ episode. He treats *awliyāʾ* as "political-allies / protectors", not "personal friends" — the legal context is military-political alliance.

al-Ṭabarī's general approach: *naqlī* (transmissive) — every interpretation is grounded in transmitted tradition with isnāds. This is reflected in Q 5's detailed asbāb al-nuzūl coverage.

## 2. al-Rāzī (*Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-kabīr*)

Source path: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Rāzī's introduction to Q 5 (kalāmu Llāh fī awwali sūrati l-māʾida) frames the whole surah as **"khiṭāb al-yahūd wa-l-naṣārā"** — address to the People-of-the-Book. al-Rāzī is the strongest classical voice on this characterization. From his commentary on Q 5:1, he argues:

> "هذه السورة افتُتحت بأمرٍ عام بالوفاء بكل ما عقدوه على أنفسهم، ثم تخصصت في عقود الميثاق مع بني إسرائيل وأهل الإنجيل، ثم بأحكام أهل الكتاب في الزواج والطعام، ثم بمناقشاتهم العقدية، فهي سورة الميثاق المتعدد"

(translation: "This surah opens with a general command to fulfill every contract, then specifies the covenants of Banī Isrāʾīl and the people of the Gospel, then the rulings on People-of-the-Book in marriage and food, then their theological disputes — it is the surah of the multiple covenant.")

This is the empirical pre-flight for **Q005-F-01 (PoTB-density)** and **Q005-F-04 (covenants-density)**.

Other al-Rāzī signatures on Q 5:
- **Q 5:3 (completion)**: al-Rāzī devotes ~30 pages to the completion-of-religion declaration. He argues the *kamāl* refers to the COMPLETENESS OF JURISPRUDENCE — that no further legal rulings would be revealed after this. This makes Q 5:3 a candidate for "the last legal verse" in his framework, even if not "the last verse simpliciter."
- **Q 5:55 (walāyah)**: al-Rāzī surveys the Imāmī Shīʿī interpretation that the verse refers to ʿAlī's rukūʿ-alms-giving — but argues against it on grammatical grounds (the plural *al-ladhīna āmanū* cannot refer to a single individual without explicit constraint). He treats the verse as referring to the believers in general.
- **Q 5:67 (tablīgh)**: al-Rāzī catalogs multiple asbāb: (a) revealed early in Mecca regarding the Prophet's protection from harassment; (b) revealed in Medina regarding hostility from People-of-the-Book; (c) revealed at Ghadīr Khumm regarding ʿAlī (Shīʿī tradition). He prefers (b).

## 3. al-Qurṭubī (*al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*)

Source path: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Qurṭubī is the **legal-rulings** mufassir; his commentary on Q 5 is the corpus' richest legal-jurisprudence treatment (Q 5 is the legal-densest surah after Q 2). Headlines:
- **Q 5:6**: extensive jurisprudence on wuḍūʾ — the 6 obligatory acts (face, hands, head, feet, niyya, tartīb), the disputes over the order, the legal status of socks/khuffāyn, etc.
- **Q 5:33**: the legal categories of ḥirāba — surveying Mālikī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanafī, Ḥanbalī positions on what counts as ḥirāba and the sequential applicability of the four punishments (kill / crucify / cross-amputate / exile).
- **Q 5:38**: theft punishment — the niṣāb threshold, what counts as a "theft" (e.g., embezzlement), the protection of repentance (Q 5:39: *fa-man tāba min baʿdi ẓulmihi wa-aṣlaḥ fa-inna llāha yatūbu ʿalayh*).
- **Q 5:51**: walāyah — al-Qurṭubī clarifies the sharp distinction between *muwālāh* (alliance / protection-pact) and *muʿāwana* (cooperation in good); the verse forbids the FIRST in religious-protection terms but does not forbid the second (cf. Q 60:8).
- **Q 5:90**: khamr — al-Qurṭubī recounts the gradualism of Quranic intoxicant prohibition (Q 2:219 → Q 4:43 → Q 5:90-91), arguing Q 5:90-91 is the FINAL absolute.

al-Qurṭubī confirms the late-Medinan dating: cites the Asmāʾ bint Yazīd tradition that Q 5 was the LAST surah revealed (al-Tirmidhī's recension).

## 4. Ibn Kathīr (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*)

Source path: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`.

Ibn Kathīr's signature on Q 5 — the *naṣṣ-ḥadīth* approach:
- He gives the **Bukhārī #45** (ʿUmar / Jew at ʿArafāt) tradition for Q 5:3 in full.
- He gives multiple isnāds for the māʾida-from-heaven episode (Q 5:114): one tradition (via ʿAmmār b. Yāsir from the Prophet) that the table descended with seven roasted fish and seven loaves; another (via Salmān) that a man offered a meal each day; another that the table came AND was withdrawn after some doubted; another that it descended on a Sunday — establishing the Christian-Sunday connection. Ibn Kathīr cautions that all these narrations are *isrāʾīliyyāt* and the Quran is silent on whether the table actually descended — the wording *innī munazziluhā ʿalaykum* (Q 5:115) is interpreted variously: "I am sending it down" or "I am ABOUT to send it down." Ibn Kathīr gives both readings and abstains from settling.
- **Q 5:32** universal-life: Ibn Kathīr cites the parallel ḥadīth (al-Bukhārī #6914-15) that "whoever takes the life of a single soul wrongfully, it is as if he killed all of humanity"; emphasizes the Quranic verse universalizes a Mishnaic principle.
- **Q 5:55** walāyah: Ibn Kathīr summarizes the Shīʿī interpretation but firmly endorses the general-believer reading; he traces the rukūʿ-alms-by-ʿAlī tradition to weak isnāds (specifically al-Hamadānī's chain).

## 5. al-Suyūṭī (*al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr*)

Source paths: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt` and `suyuti-durr-manthur.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Suyūṭī's signatures on Q 5:
- **Chronology** (*al-Itqān* nawʿ 1): "Asmāʾ bint Yazīd reported: I was at the side of the she-camel of the Messenger of God when sūrat al-māʾida was revealed; the camel almost broke under the heaviness of it... and the Messenger said *innahā nazalat fī ḥajjat al-wadāʿ wa-hiya ākhiru sūratin nazalat*" (al-Tirmidhī #3063). This is the strongest hadith-tradition for Q 5 = LAST surah revealed. al-Suyūṭī harmonizes with Q 9 (which is sometimes claimed) by attributing the dispute to "what reached each Companion."
- **The four "last verses"** (*al-Itqān* nawʿ 8): Q 9:128-129 (Ubayy b. Kaʿb tradition), Q 4:176 (al-Barāʾ tradition), Q 2:281 (Ibn ʿAbbās), Q 5:3 (Sufyān b. ʿUyayna). Q 5:3 is presented as the LAST LEGAL verse ("ākharu mā nazala min al-aḥkām").
- **al-Durr al-manthūr** is al-Suyūṭī's compendium of asbāb al-nuzūl with isnāds; for Q 5:67 (tablīgh) he records ≥ 6 distinct asbāb chains (including the Ghadīr Khumm narration with full isnād — although al-Suyūṭī himself does not endorse the Ghadīr identification as the cause of revelation).

## 6. al-Zamakhsharī (*al-Kashshāf*)

Source path: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Zamakhsharī's *balāgha-rhetorical* lens on Q 5:
- **Q 5:3 (akmaltu / atmamtu / raḍītu)**: the triple sequence is a canonical example of *iltifāt* and gradation in al-Kashshāf. al-Zamakhsharī notes the three verbs are NOT synonymous: *akmaltu* refers to ENABLING the religion's completeness; *atmamtu* to the completion of FAVOR (niʿmah); *raḍītu* to acceptance — three logical stages of perfection.
- **Q 5:32 (universal-life maxim)**: the *fa-ka-annamā qatala l-nāsa jamīʿan* is the strongest classical *balāgha* example of *tashbīh by hyperbole-equating-the-singular-to-the-multiple* — al-Zamakhsharī treats it as one of the Quran's foundational moral-theological hyperboles.
- **Q 5:48 (sharʿa wa-minhāj)**: al-Zamakhsharī unpacks the legal-pluralism reading: *sharʿa* is the entry to law; *minhāj* is the broader path; both are willed by God for each community. This is the foundational *balāgha* analysis of the legal-pluralism verse.
- **Q 5:51 vs Q 5:5**: al-Zamakhsharī addresses the apparent tension between *lā tattakhidhū l-yahūda wa-l-naṣārā awliyāʾ* (5:51) and *ṭaʿāmu l-ladhīna ūtū l-kitāba ḥillun lakum* (5:5) — argues that the prohibition is on ALLIANCE-walāyah (political-protection), not on commerce, food-sharing, or marriage with chaste women. The tension dissolves at the level of category-of-relation.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*)

Source path: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf` (full text); raw: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Biqāʿī is the *munāsabāt* (inter-coherence) mufassir. His Q 5 signatures:
- **Q 4 → Q 5 transition**: al-Biqāʿī argues Q 4 ends with mushaf-position rulings on inheritance (Q 4:176 is *kalāla*), and Q 5 opens with *awfū bi-l-ʿuqūd* — the *kalāla* covenants flow naturally into the general fulfillment of all covenants. This explains the smooth Q 4 → Q 5 transition that empirically registers as **rank 102/113 — the cheapest non-zero canonical adjacency** (H-NEW-720).
- **Q 5 → Q 6 transition**: Q 5 ends with God's Day-of-Resurrection scene with prophets; Q 6 opens with *al-ḥamdu lillāhi l-ladhī khalaqa l-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* — the eschatological closing of Q 5 transitions to the cosmic-praise opening of Q 6. al-Biqāʿī treats this as a sequential thematic-coherence chain.
- **Internal munāsabāt**: al-Biqāʿī documents the **8-block structure** of Q 5 (closely aligning with our `02-content-analysis.md` blocks A-H). He treats the māʾida-episode (Block H) as the *naḍad al-khātima* — the closing knot tying the surah's title to its theological climax.

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī (*Majmaʿ al-bayān*) — Imāmī Shīʿī

Source path: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabarsi-majma-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Ṭabarsī (d. 548 AH) is the major Imāmī Shīʿī mufassir for Q 5. Headlines:
- **Q 5:3 (completion)**: al-Ṭabarsī endorses an Imāmī tradition that the verse was revealed at Ghadīr Khumm (rather than at ʿArafāt) — referring to the appointment of ʿAlī as successor. He surveys the Sunnī tradition (al-Bukhārī #45) but offers the Imāmī alternative as concurrent.
- **Q 5:55 (walāyah)**: al-Ṭabarsī endorses the rukūʿ-alms tradition; the verse refers specifically to ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib in his giving alms while bowing. This is the canonical Imāmī interpretation, contrasting with the Sunnī majority's general-believer reading.
- **Q 5:67 (tablīgh)**: al-Ṭabarsī endorses the Ghadīr Khumm tradition as the cause of revelation.

The walāyah and tablīgh divergences at Q 5:55 and Q 5:67 are the **canonical inter-confessional dispute points** for Q 5 between Sunnī and Shīʿī tafsir.

## 9. Convergences (≥ 4-tafsir consensus)

- **Late-Medinan dating**: al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī, al-Biqāʿī all confirm Q 5 is among the LAST surahs revealed (Asmāʾ bint Yazīd hadith).
- **Q 5:3 = ʿArafāt + Friday + Hajjat al-Wadāʿ (Sunnī)**: al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī all converge.
- **Q 5:6 establishes wuḍūʾ as obligatory**: universal classical consensus.
- **Q 5:90-91 is the FINAL absolute prohibition of khamr**: al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr converge on the gradualism (Q 2:219 → Q 4:43 → Q 5:90-91).
- **al-māʾida is the surah's defining episode**: all 7 mufassirūn surveyed name the surah after the table-from-heaven (Q 5:112-115).
- **al-Rāzī's PoTB-density characterization** of Q 5 is implicitly endorsed by al-Biqāʿī's munāsabāt and al-Suyūṭī's chronology framing.
