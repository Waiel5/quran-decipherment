# Q 4 al-Nisāʾ — Tafsīr Survey

Five classical commentaries surveyed from the on-disk corpus. Source files cited per passage. The central
loci are (i) the Medinan/Meccan classification debate (driven by the *yā-ayyuhā al-nās* opening), (ii) the
polygamy/orphan ruling of v 3, and (iii) the inheritance shares (farāʾiḍ) of v 11.

## 1. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (on Q 4:1, 3)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/4/{1,3}.json`.

**On the classification (v 1):** al-Qurṭubī surveys the debate: the surah is "Medinan except one āya revealed
at Mecca in the year of the Conquest concerning ʿUthmān b. Ṭalḥa al-Ḥajabī (Q 4:58, *inna Allāha yaʾmurukum
an tuʾaddū al-amānāt*)." He reports al-Naḥḥās's claim that the surah is Meccan and the view that "every
*yā-ayyuhā al-nās* is Meccan" (ʿAlqama and others), then **refutes** it: *"the sound view is the first
[Medinan], for in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī from ʿĀʾisha: 'Sūrat al-Nisāʾ was not revealed except while I was with the
Messenger of God' — meaning after he had consummated marriage with her, and there is no disagreement that he
consummated with ʿĀʾisha in Medina."* He demolishes the "*yā-ayyuhā al-nās* = Meccan" rule by the Q 2
counterexample (al-Baqara is Medinan and contains *yā-ayyuhā al-nās* twice).

**On v 3 (polygamy):** al-Qurṭubī treats it under **fourteen masāʾil**. The first establishes the conditional:
*"if you fear you cannot be just … then marry."* He cites **the ḥadīth (wording from Muslim) from ʿUrwa ←
ʿĀʾisha**: *"O son of my sister, this is the orphan girl who is in the care of her guardian, sharing in his
property, and her wealth and beauty please him, so her guardian wishes to marry her without being equitable in
her dowry … so they were forbidden to marry them unless they dealt justly and gave them the highest customary
dowry."* The verse thus restricts, not licenses — it caps marriages at four and conditions them on justice.

## 2. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (on Q 4:11)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/4/11.json`.

al-Ṭabarī glosses *yūṣīkumu Allāhu fī awlādikum* as "**your Lord enjoins (*yaʿhad*) upon you** concerning your
children": when one dies leaving sons and daughters, the whole estate is theirs, *li-l-dhakari mithlu ḥaẓẓi
al-unthayayn* (to the male a share like that of two females), young and old, male and female alike, "when there
is no heir besides them." He explains the grammar: *mithl* is raised by the *lām* of *li-l-dhakar* (it is a
predicate), and the construction is *not* governed by *yūṣīkum* directly, because *waṣiyya* here is a "covenant
and notification with the force of speech (*ʿahd wa-iʿlām bi-maʿnā al-qawl*)," and speech does not govern the
named things reported about. al-Ṭabarī treats the inheritance shares as a fixed divine apportionment.

## 3. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf* (on Q 4:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` lines 10950-10985.

al-Zamakhsharī's surah-header reads *"madaniyya, wa-hiya miʾatun wa-sittun wa-sabʿūna āya"* (Medinan, one
hundred and seventy-six verses) — matching the on-disk Hafs count exactly. On v 1 he asks the grammatical
question: onto what is *wa-khalaqa minhā zawjahā* ("and created from it its mate") conjoined? He gives two
faces: (1) onto an elided verb (*min nafsin wāḥidatin* [He originated] *and created from it its mate*) — the
mate being Ḥawwāʾ created from a rib of Ādam; or (2) onto *khalaqakum*, with *al-nās* addressed as the
Prophet's contemporaries descended from Ādam. He then asks **why the creation-from-one-soul motivates *taqwā***
(the command of v 1): "because it points to the immense power (*al-qudra al-ʿaẓīma*) — whoever can do the like
can do anything, and among the things-in-His-power is the punishment of the disobedient; so reflecting on it
leads one to fear the One able to do it." Alternatively, he says, the *taqwā* is specifically the guarding of
mutual rights (*ḥifẓ al-ḥuqūq*) among kin who spring from one stock — *"and this sense matches the meanings of
the surah"* (i.e. its family-and-rights theme), which he notes explicitly.

## 4. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (on Q 4:1 and the Q 3 → Q 4 link)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` (al-Nisāʾ block; the
Q-3-to-Q-4 cross-references at lines ~18687, 28218).

al-Rāzī ties the opening of al-Nisāʾ to its predecessor: Āl ʿImrān closes on the believing community's
*ṣabr/ribāṭ* charge (Q 3:200) and the family of ʿImrān; al-Nisāʾ opens on the creation of mankind from one
soul and the rights of kinship — the move from *the family of a prophet* to *the law of the family in general*.
On v 1 he expands the *al-arḥām* (the wombs/kinship) clause as the surah's organising principle: the surah is
"about the rights that arise from kinship and marriage," so it opens by grounding all such rights in the single
origin of humankind. (al-Rāzī's characteristic *masāʾil* enumeration treats the qirāʾāt of *tasāʾalūna bihi
wa-l-arḥāma* — whether *al-arḥām* is in the accusative [an oath/object] or the genitive [conjoined to the
pronoun in *bihi*], a grammatical-theological crux about swearing by kinship.)

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (on Q 4:1)

Source: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/spa5k-tafsir-api/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir/4/1.json`;
Arabic at `.../ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/4/`.

Ibn Kathīr's section is headed "Which was revealed in Al-Madina — Virtues of Surat An-Nisāʾ, A Madinan
Surah." He cites **al-ʿAwfī ← Ibn ʿAbbās** that al-Nisāʾ was revealed in al-Madīna, with parallels from
ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr and Zayd b. Thābit (via Ibn Marduwayh). He then records **Ibn Masʿūd**'s ranking (via
al-Ḥākim's *Mustadrak*): *"There are five āyāt in Sūrat al-Nisāʾ that I would prefer to the world and all that
is in it,"* listing among them *inna Allāha lā yaẓlimu mithqāla dharra* (Q 4:40) and *in tajtanibū kabāʾira mā
tunhawna ʿanhu* (Q 4:31) — a faḍīla testimony for specific al-Nisāʾ verses. On v 1 he stresses the
single-origin of humanity and the command of God-consciousness and kinship (*al-arḥām*).

## 6. Points of agreement / disagreement

| Question | al-Qurṭubī | al-Ṭabarī | al-Zamakhsharī | al-Rāzī | Ibn Kathīr |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Classification | **Medinan** (refutes Meccan) | Medinan | **Medinan, 176 āyāt** | Medinan | **Medinan** (Ibn ʿAbbās) |
| v 3 polygamy = | restriction (orphan-girl occasion) | — | — | rights-grounded | — |
| v 11 farāʾiḍ = | divine apportionment | **fixed divine ʿahd** | grammar of fractions | kinship-rights | — |
| v 1 *taqwā* motive | kinship-rights | — | **power + ḥuqūq** (matches surah) | the *al-arḥām* organising principle | single-origin |
| Faḍāʾil | — | — | — | — | **Ibn Masʿūd: 5 preferred āyāt** |

The mufassirūn AGREE the surah is Medinan (refuting the "*yā-ayyuhā al-nās* = Meccan" heuristic by the Q 2
counterexample and ʿĀʾisha's testimony) and read it as the Qurʾān's family-law surah grounded in the
single-origin of humankind. The grammatical cruxes (*wa-l-arḥāma* case; the conjunction of v 1) and the
polygamy-as-restriction reading are the chief discussion points. al-Zamakhsharī's note that the
kinship-rights reading "matches the meanings of the surah" is the most directly thematic — it aligns with the
empirical profile (Q 4 is the family-and-social-law surah).
