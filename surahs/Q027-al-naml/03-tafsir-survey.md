## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabari-jami-bayan.openiti.raw.txt` (the Q 27 section opens at search hit "سورة النمل" near offset 11,942,706; v. 30 commentary near offset 11,986,869).

### On Q 27:30 (the second basmala)

al-Ṭabarī collects asbāb-traditions on the letter to Bilqīs. From Qatāda (via Maʿmar ← Abū Sufyān):

> "She [Bilqīs] is a woman called Bilqīs, daughter of Sharāḥīl — one of her parents was of the jinn; the back of one of her feet was like a hoof of a beast; she was of a royal house, with three hundred and twelve counselors each over ten thousand men; she was in a land called Maʾrib, three days from Ṣanʿāʾ. When the Hudhud brought her news to Sulaymān b. Dāwūd, he wrote a letter and sent it with the Hudhud; the Hudhud went and the doors were locked — she would lock her doors and place the keys under her head; the Hudhud entered through a window and dropped the page on her, and she read it: '*innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm. allā taʿlū ʿalayya wa-ʾtūnī muslimīn*' [Q 27:30-31]. Thus the prophets used to write — they did not lengthen; they wrote brief sentences."

al-Ṭabarī then transmits via Ibn Jurayj that "Sulaymān did not exceed what God recounts in His book — '*innahu* and *innahu*'" (i.e., the letter consisted only of the two clauses Q 27:30 reports).

al-Ṭabarī also explains the grammar: "the kasra on *inna* in both occurrences is on the *radd* (predicative apposition) to *innī* in 'I have been delivered an honored letter' [v. 29]. The meaning: 'O chiefs, an honored letter has been delivered to me, and it is from Sulaymān.'"

### On Q 27:18-19 (the ant)

al-Ṭabarī (search "وادي النمل") preserves multiple isnāds locating the *Wādī al-Naml* — most identifying it as in Sham (Syria) or in Ṭāʾif. Various traditions on the namla's species and Sulaymān's "smile in laughter" (v. 19) — most read this as Sulaymān understanding the ant's speech and being delighted at God's gift of *manṭiq al-ṭayr*.

### On Q 27:82 (al-Dābba — the Beast)

al-Ṭabarī (multiple sites; ~7 hits on دابة من الأرض) gathers traditions: the Beast emerges at end-time; carries Sulaymān's seal-ring and Mūsā's staff (cf. Tirmidhī ḥadīth #3271 in `04-hadith-corpus.md`); marks believer's face with light, disbeliever's nose with blackness.

## 2. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` (multiple hits on "إنه من سليمان وإنه").

### Why does Sulaymān precede his own name to God's?

This is al-Rāzī's most extensive Q 27:30 discussion (the famous "11th and 12th points" in his tafsīr's opening section on the basmala — al-Rāzī treats Q 27:30 as a primary text on the basmala's status):

> "*Question*: Why did Sulaymān (peace be upon him) put his own name before God's name in his statement '*innahu min Sulaymān*'? *Answer*, on multiple grounds:
>
> **First**: When Bilqīs found that letter placed on her pillow, with no one having access to her, and saw the Hudhud standing on the edge of the wall, she knew the letter was from Sulaymān. She took the letter and said 'It is from Sulaymān' — when she opened it and saw '*bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*', she said: 'and it is — *bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*.' So the words '*innahu min Sulaymān*' are Bilqīs's speech, not Sulaymān's.
>
> **Second**: Perhaps Sulaymān wrote on the *envelope* (*ʿunwān al-kitāb*) 'innahu min Sulaymān', and inside the letter began with '*bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*' as is the custom of all letters. So when Bilqīs took the letter, she read what was on the envelope and said 'It is from Sulaymān', then opened the letter and read 'In the Name of God…' and said 'and it is — In the Name of God…'.
>
> **Third**: Bilqīs was a disbeliever. Sulaymān feared she would curse God when she looked at the letter, so he placed his own name before God's, that the curse — if any — would fall on him, not on God."

### On the basmala's miraculous power

al-Rāzī (just before the Q 27:30 passage) reads Q 27:30 as the textbook proof that the basmala-formula carries unique sanctification:

> "Sulaymān (peace be upon him) attained the kingdoms of this world and the next by saying '*innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*' [al-Naml: 30]. So if the servant says it, he too will gain the kingdom of this world and the next."

This pairs with al-Rāzī's earlier example of Nūḥ saying "*bismi llāhi majrāhā*" in Q 11:41 — found salvation by half this formula. Q 27:30 is the second canonical proof.

### Iʿjāz al-qaṣr (concision)

al-Rāzī treats Q 27:30 as exemplary of *ījāz al-qaṣr* — though his most extended treatment is in the Q 27 commentary itself. The same point is made by al-Suyūṭī (see §7 below).

## 3. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt` (Q 27:30 in al-Qurṭubī's introductory section on the basmala, ~position with "إنه من سليمان وإنه").

### The basmala's status — Q 27:30 as legal evidence

al-Qurṭubī uses Q 27:30 as **the legal anchor for the consensus that the basmala is at minimum part of the Qurʾān in this verse**:

> "There is no disagreement among them that the basmala is a verse of the Qurʾān in *Sūrat al-Naml*."

He continues by reporting the chronology of basmala-writing in pre-revelation Arabia:

> "It is reported via al-Shaʿbī, al-Aʿmash, [Abū Mālik], Qatāda, and Thābit b. ʿUmāra: the Prophet (peace be upon him) used to write '*bi-smika allāhumma*' until he was commanded to write '*bismi llāhi*'; so he wrote it. When [Q 17:110] '*qul udʿū llāha aw udʿū al-raḥmāna*' was revealed, he wrote '*bismi llāhi al-raḥmān*'; when [Q 27:30] '*innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*' was revealed, he wrote that. In Abū Dāwūd's *Muṣannaf*: 'al-Shaʿbī, Abū Mālik, Qatāda, and Thābit b. ʿUmāra say: the Prophet did not write *bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm* until *Sūrat al-Naml* was revealed.'"

This establishes Q 27 as **the chronological-revelation anchor for the canonical basmala formula**. Before Q 27, the Prophet wrote shorter forms; from Q 27 onwards, the full formula. (Cross-validate: Tha'labī also has this — see §6.)

al-Qurṭubī then surveys the four classical positions on whether the basmala is a Qurʾānic verse in surahs other than Q 1 / Q 27, citing Mālik, Shāfiʿī, Ibn al-Mubārak, and the Mālikī majority.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`.

### On Q 27:30

Ibn Kathīr narrates the Hudhud's flight to Bilqīs's palace; the secluded chamber where she retires alone; the Hudhud drops the letter through a window:

> "She came to the letter, took it, broke its seal, and read it: '*innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm. allā taʿlū ʿalayya wa-ʾtūnī muslimīn*'. So she gathered her chiefs, viziers, and the great ones of her state, and said: '*yā ayyuhā al-malaʾu innī ulqiya ilayya kitābun karīm*' [v. 29] — meaning by *karīm*: what she had seen of its marvelous matter, that a bird brought it and dropped it before her, then turned aside out of decorum. This is something no king is able to do, none can attain it. Then she read it to them: '*innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm. allā taʿlū ʿalayya wa-ʾtūnī muslimīn*'. They knew it was from God's prophet Sulaymān, and that they had no power against him.
>
> **This letter is at the height of eloquence (*balāgha*), brevity (*wajāza*), and clarity (*faṣāḥa*) — it conveyed the meaning in the shortest and best phrasing.**
>
> The scholars say: no one wrote '*bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*' before Sulaymān (peace be upon him)."

Ibn Kathīr then narrates a ḥadīth from Ibn Burayda (via his father) — the Prophet:

> "I know a verse that has not been revealed to any prophet after Sulaymān b. Dāwūd."

(Cross-validate this ḥadīth in `04-hadith-corpus.md` — a strong claim of basmala-uniqueness anchored to Q 27:30.)

### On Q 27:18-19 (the ant)

Ibn Kathīr remarks that the Prophet forbade the killing of four animals: the ant, the bee, the hoopoe, and the shrike — citing the Abū Dāwūd / Ibn Mājah ḥadīth (see `04-hadith-corpus.md`). The Prophet is alluding to Q 27:18 (ants) and Q 27:20 (hudhud) — *the surah's ecology is preserved in Prophetic law*.

## 5. al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Biqāʿī's project is *munāsabāt* (verse-and-surah inter-connection). For Q 27 his concern is:
- The relationship between Q 26 al-Shuʿarāʾ (ṬSM) and Q 27 al-Naml (ṬS) — the abbreviated muqaṭṭaʿāt is interpreted by al-Biqāʿī as marking a *dimensional reduction*: Q 26 names Mūsā extensively across many polemic encounters; Q 27 takes the same Mūsā material and *contracts* it into 8 verses (vv. 7-14), reserving the surah's narrative bulk for Sulaymān.
- The nūn-rhyme of Q 27 is taken by al-Biqāʿī as a *fawāṣil* unification with Q 26 (which is also predominantly nūn-final). Empirically: see `01-empirical-profile.md` — Q 27 nūn = 90.3%; Q 26 also nūn-dominant, supporting al-Biqāʿī's *munāsaba* claim at the rhyme axis.

## 6. al-Thaʿlabī, *al-Kashf wa-l-bayān*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/thaclabi-kashf-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Thaʿlabī preserves a key chronology-of-basmala ḥadīth via Yazīd al-Naḥwī ← ʿIkrima + al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī:

> "The Prophet — in the beginning of the matter, on the convention of Quraysh — used to write '*bi-smika allāhumma*'. When [Q 11:41] '*wa-qāla rkabū fīhā bismi llāhi majrāhā wa-mursāhā*' was revealed, he wrote '*bismi llāhi*'. When [Q 17:110] '*qul udʿū llāha aw udʿū al-raḥmāna*' was revealed, he wrote '*bismi llāhi al-raḥmān*'. When [Q 27:30] '*innahu min Sulaymāna wa-innahu bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*' was revealed, he wrote it like that. The Prophet boasted of this verse — and rightly so."

al-Thaʿlabī then narrates a separate ḥadīth from Ibn Burayda (← his father) — the Prophet:

> "Shall I not inform you of a verse that was not revealed to anyone after Sulaymān b. Dāwūd except me?" I [Burayda] said: "Yes." He said: "With what do you open the recitation of the Qurʾān?" I said: "*bismi llāhi al-raḥmāni al-raḥīm*." He said: "It is, it is."

This ḥadīth (*hiya hiya* — "it is, it is") is one of the strongest hadith-evidences that the basmala IS a Qurʾānic verse, anchored explicitly to Q 27:30.

al-Thaʿlabī then makes a sharp observation:

> "This ḥadīth is evidence that the *tasmīya* is a complete verse from al-Fātiḥa and the *fawātiḥ* of the surahs — for the Prophet pronounced the verse in full, and the one in Sūrat al-Naml is *not* a [full] verse, but only part of one verse. *Wa-bi-llāhi al-tawfīq*."

This is a **theologically subtle distinction**: the basmala in Q 1 is a *standalone verse* (in al-Thaʿlabī's reading), while the basmala in Q 27:30 is *part* of v. 30, embedded within Sulaymān's letter. The two basmalas are textually identical but grammatically distinct.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-manthūr*

### *al-Itqān*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt`.

Q 27:30 appears in the *Itqān* in two locations:
1. **In the chapter on the preposition *min*** (the kind that "begins the limit of place/time/etc.") — al-Suyūṭī gives Q 27:30 as the standard example: "*innahu min Sulaymān*."
2. **In the *ījāz al-qaṣr* chapter (concision-iʿjāz)** — al-Suyūṭī cites al-Ṭībī's *Tibyān*:

   > "*ījāz al-qaṣr* (concise concision) is when the wording is condensed onto its meaning; like His statement '*innahu min Sulaymān*' through to '*wa-ʾtūnī muslimīn*' — *which combines in a few letters the address-line, the body of the letter, and the demand* (*jamaʿa fī aḥrufin al-ʿunwāna wa-l-kitāba wa-l-ḥājata*). It has been said in praise of an eloquent man: 'his words were vessels for his meanings.' This is the view of those who include *musāwāt* (parity-of-letter-and-meaning) within *ījāz*."

This identifies **Q 27:30-31 as the textbook locus of al-iʿjāz al-qaṣr in the entire Quran**. The basmala-letter is canonically the most concise Quranic text-act.

al-Suyūṭī also uses this passage in his chapter on Meccan-Medinan classification, listing al-Naml (with its right-flank Q 28 al-Qaṣaṣ, Q 29 al-ʿAnkabūt) as Meccan with possible Medinan minor-verse intrusions in al-Qaṣaṣ — Q 27 is purely Meccan in al-Suyūṭī's chronology.

### *al-Durr al-manthūr*

**Source**: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-durr-manthur.openiti.raw.txt`.

al-Suyūṭī collects narrations from Ibn ʿAbbās (via Ibn Abī Shayba, ʿAbd b. Ḥumayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abī Ḥātim, al-Ḥākim — declared ṣaḥīḥ) on:
- *How did Sulaymān miss the Hudhud?* — Ibn ʿAbbās: "Sulaymān stopped at a station and could not tell where the water was; the Hudhud used to indicate the water for him; when Sulaymān wished to ask him, he found him absent."
- *How could the Hudhud see water under the earth yet be trapped by simple traps?* — Ibn ʿAbbās: "When fate comes, sight departs (*idhā jāʾa al-qaḍāʾu dhahaba al-baṣar*)."

These are illustrative of the *al-Durr*'s methodology: ḥadīth/āthār collation rather than verse-by-verse exegesis.

## 9. Disagreements / divergences in tafsīr

1. **Where Sulaymān put "min Sulaymān"** — body of letter, envelope, or both? al-Rāzī gives 3 alternatives; al-Ṭabarī's report from Qatāda implies it was the letter-text proper.
2. **Identity of Bilqīs's parentage** — al-Ṭabarī preserves the "one parent of jinn" tradition (likely Israelite-isrāʾīlīyāt). Ibn Kathīr is quieter on this; al-Thaʿlabī repeats it.
3. **The *throne-bringer*'s identity** (v. 39 *qāla alladhī ʿindahu ʿilmun min al-kitāb*) — Ibn ʿAbbās: Āṣaf b. Barakhya (Sulaymān's vizier); others: an angel; al-Suyūṭī collects multiple isnāds in *al-Durr*.
4. **The basmala's verse-status outside Q 1 and Q 27** — Mālikī (no), Shāfiʿī (yes for Q 1, hesitant for others), Ḥanafī. The dispute is **legal**, not narrative — and it always exempts Q 27:30 from the dispute (everyone agrees Q 27:30 contains the basmala-string as part of revealed scripture).
