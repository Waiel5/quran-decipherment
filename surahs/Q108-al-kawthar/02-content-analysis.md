## 2. Verse-by-verse decomposition

### 2.1 v1 — *innā aʿṭaynāka l-kawthar*

**Form**: Divine-plural perfect-tense gift attestation.
- *innā* = إنا — emphatic "indeed, We" (1st-pl divine majesty)
- *aʿṭaynā* = أعطينا — "We have given" (perfect, perfective aspect — completed gift)
- *-ka* = ـك — "you" (2nd-sg objective; refers to the Prophet Muḥammad)
- *al-kawthar* = الكوثر — "the Abundance" (definite, singular, masculine; *fawʿalā* intensive nominal pattern from root *k-th-r* "be many")

**Illocutionary force**: Performative attestation of divine gift. The perfective aspect (*aʿṭaynāka*) frames the gift as accomplished, not promised — even though contextually the *kawthar* river is eschatologically located. The grammar collapses past and future into a single divine-perfect.

**Lexical commentary**: *al-kawthar* is a Quranic hapax — it occurs only at this position in the entire corpus (root `kvr` occurs 167 times across the corpus, but this *fawʿalā*-form is unique). The classical interpretive split (river-of-Paradise vs abundance-in-general) hinges on whether the morphology is read as concrete (the river) or abstract (the intensive of *kathra*). Both are lexically licit; classical scholars resolve this by reading the abstract as the ceiling and the concrete (river) as a primary instantiation — see `03-tafsir-survey.md` and `05-classical-claims-audit.md`.

### 2.2 v2 — *fa-ṣalli li-rabbika wa-nḥar*

**Form**: Imperative response (with *fa-* "so / therefore" connective).
- *fa-* = فـ — connective "so / therefore" (causal-discourse particle)
- *ṣalli* = صل — imperative "pray" (root `Slw`, 99 corpus occurrences)
- *li-rabbi-ka* = لربك — "to your-Lord" (preposition + noun + 2nd-sg-suffix; root `rbb`)
- *wa-* = و — "and"
- *nḥar* = نحر — imperative "sacrifice" (root `nHr` — **HAPAX in entire corpus**)

**Illocutionary force**: Imperative ritual response. The *fa-* connective grammatically structures v2 as the *appropriate response* to v1: gift → gratitude-ritual.

**Lexical commentary**:
- *ṣallī* (pray) is canonical Quranic vocabulary — root `Slw` recurs throughout the corpus.
- *nḥar* (sacrifice / the act of slaughtering by piercing the upper neck of a camel) is a **HAPAX** — it occurs ONLY here in the entire Quran. The connection to ritual sacrifice (especially Hajj's *yawm al-naḥr* — the 10th of Dhū al-Ḥijja) is classical (al-Qurṭubī, al-Ṭabarī ad loc.). The lexeme is technically specific to camel-sacrifice (cf. *dhabaḥ* for general slaughter, used 17× in the corpus); its hapax status here marks the ritual moment.

### 2.3 v3 — *inna shāniʾaka huwa l-abtar*

**Form**: Declarative judgment (with *inna* emphasis).
- *inna* = إن — emphatic "indeed" (≠ v1's *innā* — v1 is *innā* = *inna* + *-nā* "we"; v3 is *inna* alone)
- *shāniʾa-ka* = شانئك — "the-one-who-hates-you" (active participle of root `$nA` "hate", with 2nd-sg suffix; root has only 3 corpus occurrences total)
- *huwa* = هو — "he" (3rd-sg pronoun)
- *al-abtar* = الأبتر — "the cut-off" (definite participle of root `btr` "to cut off / dock the tail", **HAPAX** — only this occurrence in the corpus)

**Illocutionary force**: Declarative judgment with embedded inversion-of-the-taunt. The accuser called the Prophet *al-abtar* ("cut-off, tail-less, male-heir-less"); the verse returns the very epithet to the accuser. Classical balāgha: *al-mushākala* (form-matching) + *al-ʿaks* (inversion). See `findings/phase-c-structures/al-kawthar-and-shortest-surahs-deep-dive.md` §1.9 for the full *radd al-kalām* catalog (18 Quranic exemplars; Q108:3 is the most compressed).

**Lexical commentary**:
- *shāniʾaka* — Active participle "the-one-hating-you". The root `$nA` is rare (3 corpus occurrences total: Q5:2, Q5:8, Q108:3). The sing.-2sg-objective form is unique to Q108.
- *al-abtar* — **HAPAX**. The root `btr` "to cut off, dock, mutilate" appears only here. The Arabic semantic field of *abtar* extends to "tail-less" (snakes, animals — see al-Bukhārī ḥadīth #3162, #3173 on the killing of *al-abtar* snakes — same lexeme!) and "male-heir-less" (a man whose male line is cut off). The classical asbāb-al-nuzūl narration (al-Wāḥidī) reports al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil calling the Prophet *al-abtar* after the death of his son al-Qāsim (or ʿAbd Allāh — narrations vary). The verse returns the taunt-word to the accuser.

## 3. Three illocutionary forms in three verses

Q108 packs **three distinct speech-acts** into 10 words:

| Verse | Speech-act | Performative form |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| v1 | Attestation/gift | "innā aʿṭaynāka..." (1pl-perfect divine declaration) |
| v2 | Imperative/ritual | "fa-ṣalli... wa-nḥar" (2sg imperative dyad) |
| v3 | Judgment/inversion | "inna shāniʾaka huwa..." (3sg copula judgment) |

This three-fold illocutionary structure is the surah's principal compositional architecture: divine-gift → human-ritual-response → divine-judgment-on-enemy. Three persons, three moods (perfect / imperative / present-tense copula).

## 4. The kawthar/abtar lexical inversion

The first content-noun is *al-kawthar* (الكوثر = "the Abundance/the Many/the Heavenly River"). The last content-noun is *al-abtar* (الأبتر = "the Cut-off / the Dockless / the Heir-less / the Truncated"). They are **antonyms** in the surah's semantic field:

- Abundance (Allāh's gift to the Prophet) ↔ Cut-off-ness (the accuser's fate)
- Many (the river's plenitude) ↔ Tail-less (the accuser's truncation)
- *al-kawthar*: definite + *fawʿalā* intensive of *k-th-r* "many"
- *al-abtar*: definite + *afʿal* (elative/superlative) of *b-t-r* "cut off"

Both nouns are **definite, singular, masculine**, occupy the **same metrical-syntactic slot** (final word of their verse), and rhyme on **ر** with the same terminal-consonant. The terminal-bigram of v1 is **ثر**; of v3 is **تر**. ث (thāʾ) and ت (tāʾ) are voiceless dental/interdental fricative-stop pair; the bigram inversion *kawthar / abtar* exhibits a near-minimal phonetic pair difference at exactly the turning-point words of the surah.

This is the surah's principal **muṭarraf-sajʿ + lexical-inversion device**, classically described by:
- al-Bāqillānī, *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*, chapter on *al-īǧāz* (concision-miracle): Q108 as the archetype.
- al-Jurjānī, *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz*, §3.4 on *al-ʿaks* / *maʿnā l-maʿnā*: the illocutionary reversal at *al-abtar* is the *meaning-of-the-meaning*.
- al-Zarkashī, *al-Burhān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*, nawʿ 46 (*al-mushākala*): using the opponent's word-form back at him.
- Ibn Abī l-Iṣbaʿ, *Badīʿ al-Qurʾān*, on *al-ʿaks wa-l-taʿaks*: places Q108:3 as exemplar.
- al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar*: tripartite structure with lexical inversion at extremes.

## 5. Pronominal architecture

The surah contains a structured pronominal exchange:

| Verse | "We" (Allāh) | "You" (Prophet, addressee) | "He" (3rd-person — accuser) |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| 1 | innā ... aʿṭaynā | -ka (objective) | — |
| 2 | — | li-rabbi-ka, ṣalli, wa-nḥar (implicit "you" agent) | — |
| 3 | — | -ka (in shāniʾa-ka) | huwa (subject), al-abtar |

The pronoun-flow: divine-We addresses the Prophet (-ka twice); the Prophet's accuser is referenced indirectly only at the end (-ka possessor of "the one-hating-you", then *huwa* surfaces him as subject of judgment). The accuser is **never named, never addressed**; only described. Compare to Q111 al-Masad which DOES name Abū Lahab — Q108's anonymity is part of its rhetorical strategy.

## 6. Phonological structure

### 6.1 Verse-final letters and rhyme

All three verses terminate in **ر (rāʾ)**: monorhyme entropy = 0.000 nats (corpus-minimum, computed from `findings/phase-b-hypotheses/csv/h-new-750.json`).

### 6.2 Terminal bigrams and the *kawthar/abtar* device

| Verse | Final word | Final bigram | Phonetic |
|:-:|:--|:-:|:--|
| 1 | الكوثر | ثر | /thar/ — voiceless interdental + rāʾ |
| 2 | وانحر | حر | /ḥar/ — voiceless pharyngeal + rāʾ |
| 3 | الأبتر | تر | /tar/ — voiceless dental stop + rāʾ |

The three preceding consonants (ث / ح / ت) are all **voiceless non-emphatic** consonants; the rāʾ is the constant. The bigrams *thar / ḥar / tar* form a phonological mini-cluster. v1 (*thar*) and v3 (*tar*) are minimally distinct (interdental fricative vs. dental stop, both unvoiced), creating a **near-phonetic-mirror** at the surah's first-and-last content nouns — see `findings/phase-c-structures/al-kawthar-and-shortest-surahs-deep-dive.md` §1.7.

### 6.3 Internal phonetic features

- v1: 16 letters / 3 words. Opens with hamza-with-kasra (*innā*); follows with hamza-with-fatḥa (*aʿṭaynā*); voiceless concentration in the gift-clause.
- v2: 12 letters / 3 words. The shortest verse. Heavy on emphatic and pharyngeal consonants (*ṣ, ḥ*) — voiced/articulatory weight at the moment of ritual response.
- v3: 15 letters / 4 words. Recovers the hamza-fronting (*inna shāniʾaka*) — phonetic echo of v1's *innā*. Final *al-abtar* mirrors *al-kawthar* lexically AND phonetically (initial vowel + dental + ر).

## 7. Thematic blocks

The surah is **monolithic** (no internal block-breaks) by virtue of its 3-verse length, but the illocutionary trichotomy of §3 functionally segments it. Classical block-analysis (al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar*):

- **Block 1 (v1)**: Affirmation of divine gift. Theme: divine generosity to the Prophet.
- **Block 2 (v2)**: Imperative response. Theme: ritual reciprocation (prayer + sacrifice).
- **Block 3 (v3)**: Judgment on the enemy. Theme: the inversion of the taunt; cosmic justice.

The structural arc: gift → ritual → judgment. al-Biqāʿī notes that the lexical inversion at v1/v3 (kawthar/abtar) frames the whole surah as a closed *naẓm* (compositional unity).

## 8. Asbāb al-nuzūl

**Primary narration (al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*)**: al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil al-Sahmī, encountering the Prophet exiting the masjid, called him *al-abtar* ("cut-off, tail-less") after the death of the Prophet's son al-Qāsim (some narrations: ʿAbd Allāh). The taunt drew on pre-Islamic Arabian custom that a man with no surviving male heir was *abtar* ("docked"). Q108 was revealed in response, returning the epithet to the accuser.

**Alternative attributions** (variants):
- ʿUqba b. Abī Muʿayṭ as the speaker of the taunt (some narrations).
- Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf in a Medinan context (later commentators; minority view; conflicts with the surah's Meccan classification by majority).

**Verification status**: The al-ʿĀṣ b. Wāʾil narration is the most-cited; al-Wāḥidī's *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* is the canonical source. Modern scholarship (e.g., Andrew Rippin, "*Asbāb al-Nuzūl* as a Genre") treats asbāb narratives as broadly authoritative for thematic context but unreliable for precise historical reconstruction.

The surah's coherence is **textually self-sufficient**: even without the asbāb context, the *al-kawthar / al-abtar* lexical inversion makes the rebuke-and-reply structure transparent. The asbāb adds *who* the accuser was, but the *what* (return-the-taunt) is encoded in the text itself.

## 9. Connections to other surahs

### 9.1 Q97 al-Qadr — opener-pair

Q97 opens with *innā anzalnāhu* ("Indeed, We sent it down"); Q108 with *innā aʿṭaynāka* ("Indeed, We have granted you"). Both use the divine-plural perfect-verb opener with 2/3sg objective suffix. Q97 (5 verses, 30 words) and Q108 (3 verses, 10 words) are both very-short Meccan surahs in the high-90s/low-100s zone of the mushaf. The structural opener-class match suggests an early-Meccan compositional micro-template. **Queued for cross-finding analysis.**

### 9.2 Q111 al-Masad — judgment-on-enemy parallel

Both Q108 and Q111 close with a judgment-declaration on a specific enemy. Q111 names Abū Lahab; Q108 anonymizes "the-one-hating-you". Q111:5 ends with *masad* ("twisted fibre"); Q108:3 ends with *abtar* ("cut-off"). Both are classified as *radd al-kayd / radd al-kalām* exemplars in classical balāgha. See `findings/phase-c-structures/al-kawthar-and-shortest-surahs-deep-dive.md` §1.9 catalog (Q108:3 and Q111:1 are both listed).

### 9.3 Q1 al-Fātiḥa — the rank-1 NN pairing

Despite sharing only 1 root (*rbb*) and 0 word-forms, Q1 and Q108 are each other's nearest-neighbors in Fisher-Rao space (d=0.3384, rank 1/113 from Q1's perspective). The pairing is grammatical-genre-driven (both are short, prayer-adjacent, divine-name-density-modest, mufaṣṣal-zone) more than lexical. See `01-empirical-profile.md` §10 for the full root-overlap analysis.

### 9.4 Q103 al-ʿAṣr — verse-count twin

Q103 also has 3 verses (the only other 3-verse surahs are Q108 and Q110). Q103 is also rāʾ-monorhyme (3/3 ر terminations). Q108 and Q103 share verse-count, rhyme-letter, and Meccan-early classification — they are structural twins. Their FR distance is 0.2399 (rank 6 NN of Q108; rank 6 NN of Q103). See `findings/phase-c-structures/al-kawthar-and-shortest-surahs-deep-dive.md` §2 for the full short-surah-cluster analysis.

### 9.5 Q107 → Q108 → Q109 transition (mushaf-context)

Q107 al-Māʿūn (7 verses): a moral indictment of religious-pretenders (those who pray but neglect orphans). Closes with *al-māʿūn* ("petty kindness").

Q108 al-Kawthar (3 verses): divine gift + ritual response + judgment on enemy. Opens with *al-kawthar* (abundance).

Q109 al-Kāfirūn (6 verses): the Prophet's disavowal-formula to the disbelievers — *qul yā ayyuhā l-kāfirūn / lā aʿbud mā taʿbudūn*.

The three surahs form a thematic arc: indictment-of-hypocrisy → divine-gift-to-Prophet → disavowal-of-disbelievers. Q108 sits at the pivot of this arc. The H-NEW-720 canonical-adjacency costs (Q107→Q108: 0.1015; Q108→Q109: 0.1341) are both mid-cost — neither is a structural hinge in the H-NEW-130 sense, but Q108's position is locally well-cohered with both flanking surahs.
