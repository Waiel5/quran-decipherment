## 1. The eight thematic blocks (4 narratives + 4 frame/bridge)

Q 18 is structured as **four narrative cores** separated by **four discursive frames/bridges**. Block boundaries follow the H-NEW-268 locked four-narrative segmentation (vv. 9-26, 32-44, 60-82, 83-98) supplemented by classical block markers (al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar*, Q18 commentary; al-Qurṭubī's masāʾil divisions).

**Block-boundary verification**: the four-narrative-core block-starts (9, 32, 60, 83) and block-lengths (18, 13, 23, 16) match the H-NEW-268 locked segmentation. Bridge-A's natural break is at v. 31 (al-Biqāʿī Q18:32 marks "and strike for them the parable of two men"), bridge-B at v. 59 (al-Qurṭubī Q18:60 marks the Mūsā-narrative opening), narrative-4 ends at v. 101 by classical reading (al-Ṭabarī), at v. 98 by the H-NEW-268 conservative endpoint. Frame-closing block D opens definitively at v. 102 in both readings.

**Block-end fāṣila echo (the *aḥadan* mirror)**: narrative-1 (v. 26) and frame-closing (v. 110) both close on the same word *aḥadan* ("anyone"). v. 26: *wa-lā yushriku fī ḥukmihi aḥadan* — "He associates none in His judgment". v. 110: *wa-lā yushrik bi-ʿibādati rabbihi aḥadan* — "let him associate no one in the worship of his Lord". The two *aḥadan* fāṣilas are 84 verses apart but use the same verb *yushrik* (associate) and the same fāṣila-noun *aḥadan*. This is one of the cleanest single-word *iltifāt-ḥakīm-resolution* patterns in the corpus — see [[Q018-al-kahf/05-classical-claims-audit|audit 4]] and the empirical pre-reg `Q018-F-04`.

## 2. Block A: frame opening (vv. 1-8)

### 2.1 Q 18:1-2 — *al-ḥamdu lillāh* + *al-kitāb*

> الحمد لله الذي أنزل على عبده الكتاب ولم يجعل له عوجا • قيما لينذر بأسا شديدا من لدنه ويبشر المؤمنين الذين يعملون الصالحات أن لهم أجرا حسنا

"Praise be to God who sent down upon His servant the Book and made it not crooked, [making it] straight, to warn of severe punishment from Him and to give good tidings to the believers who do righteous deeds — that for them is a goodly reward."

**Notable**: 
- Q 18 is the third of five *al-ḥamdu lillāh*-opening surahs (Q 1, 6, 18, 34, 35).
- *ʿiwaj* (root *ʿwj*, crookedness) is a corpus-rare root: 6 occurrences total, of which 1 is in Q 18 — a rare root deployed at the surah's opening signature.
- *qayyiman* ("straight" — implicit asyndetic continuation, "straight" qualifying "the Book") is one of the most-debated grammatical points in classical Arabic exegesis. al-Zamakhsharī (`zamakhshari-kashshaf` Q18:2) reads it as a *ḥāl* (circumstantial accusative) qualifying *al-kitāb*; al-Rāzī (`razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt` Q18:2) endorses this and notes the rhetorical-pivot function — the *ʿiwaj* (crookedness) is *negated*, then immediately followed by *qayyiman* (straightness asserted) — *the same content stated in two complementary syntactic forms*. This is one of al-Sakkākī's *iqāʿ* (rhythmic-doubling) examples in classical balagha; for the empirical correlate see [[h-classic-48-sakkaki-iqa|H-classic-48]].

### 2.2 Q 18:3-5 — *fīhi abadan* refrain

vv. 3-5 specify the *abadan* (eternally) fāṣila motif:
- v. 3: *māki thīn-a fīhi abadan* — "abiding in it eternally"
- v. 5: *kabura kalimatan takhruju min afwāhihim* — "great is the word coming from their mouths"

The *abadan* appears 7× in Q 18 (vv. 3, 20, 35, 57, 108, 110 — one of the highest *abadan*-densities in the corpus, comparable only to Q 4:57 / Q 64:9).

### 2.3 Q 18:6-8 — Prophet's grief + reduction to barren plain

v. 6: *fa-laʿallaka bākhiʿun nafsaka ʿalā āthārihim in lam yuʾminū bi-hādhā al-ḥadīth-i asafā* — "Perhaps you would kill yourself in grief over their tracks if they do not believe this report".

v. 7-8: the world's adornment is a test; "We will surely make whatever is on it [as] barren ground (*ṣaʿīdan juruzan*)".

The *bākhiʿun* + *asafan* combination is corpus-rare: only Q 18:6 and Q 26:3 share *bākhiʿun nafsaka*. The semantic anchor is the Prophet's distress at unbelievers' rejection of the *ḥadīth* (here = the Quran). v. 7-8 then resolves the distress by relativizing the world: ultimately *we* (the Reciter) reduce the world to a barren plain.

## 3. Block N1: Aṣḥāb al-Kahf (vv. 9-26) — narrative 1

### 3.1 Opening signature: *am ḥasibta*

v. 9: *am ḥasibta anna aṣḥāba al-kahfi wa-al-raqīmi kānū min āyātinā ʿajaban* — "or do you consider that the companions of the cave and al-Raqīm were a wonder among Our signs?"

The *am-ḥasibta* opening is rhetorical: the implicit answer is "no, the cave-companions are NOT the wondrous sign — the *real* sign is the world's history at large". This is one of three *am ḥasibta* / *am ḥasibtum* openings of major narrative blocks in the Quran (others: Q 2:214, Q 3:142). al-Rāzī (Q18:9) reads this as *al-tasallī al-mubāshir* (direct consolation): the Prophet has just been distressed at believers' rejection in vv. 6-8; now the *am ḥasibta* insists that the cave-tale is one sign among many — the rejection is not unique.

### 3.2 Verse-by-verse summary

- v. 9: rhetorical question (above).
- v. 10: *idh awā al-fityatu ilā al-kahfi fa-qālū rabbanā ātinā min ladunka raḥmatan wa-hayyiʾ lanā min amrinā rashadan* — "when the youth took refuge in the cave and said: 'Our Lord, give us mercy from You and arrange right-guidance for us in our affair'". The youths' prayer is the surah's most-often-quoted single line in the *istighātha* (refuge-seeking) liturgical tradition.
- v. 11: *fa-ḍarabnā ʿalā ādhānihim fī al-kahfi sinīna ʿadadan* — "We struck their ears in the cave for a numbered count of years" (the sleep miracle).
- v. 12: *thumma baʿathnāhum li-naʿlama ayyu al-ḥizbayni aḥṣā li-mā labithū amadan* — "then We resurrected them to know which of the two parties had counted longer the time they remained".
- vv. 13-16: narrative recap with *naḥnu naquṣṣu ʿalayka* ("we narrate to you"); they were youth who believed and increased in guidance.
- v. 17: detailed visual description — *wa-tarā al-shamsa idhā ṭalaʿat tazāwaru ʿan kahfihim dhāta al-yamīn wa-idhā gharabat taqriḍuhum dhāta al-shimāl* — "you would see the sun when it rose, slanting from their cave to the right; and when it set, passing them on the left".
- v. 18: physical description in the cave — *taḥsabuhum ayqāẓan wa-hum ruqūd* ("you would think them awake but they were asleep"); *kalbuhum bāsiṭun dhirāʿayhi bi-l-waṣīd* ("their dog stretching its forelegs at the threshold"). The dog of the cave is one of the corpus's three named-animal-in-narrative references (others: Sulaymān's hoopoe, Q 27:20; the camel of Ṣāliḥ, Q 7:73, etc.).
- v. 19: dialogue among the awakening companions — *kam labithtum* ("how long have you stayed?"); *yawman aw baʿḍa yawm* ("a day or part of a day"); the dispatched-coin commission.
- vv. 20-21: city-discovery + city's mass-discovery of them.
- vv. 22-26: the **number-of-companions** dispute, the Prophet instructed not to be drawn into it; *qul rabbī aʿlamu bi-ʿiddatihim mā yaʿlamuhum illā qalīl* (v. 22); the **In shāʾa Allāh** verse (v. 23-24) — *wa-lā taqūlanna li-shayʾin innī fāʿilun dhālika ghadan illā an yashāʾa Allāh* — the Quranic foundation of the *In shāʾa Allāh* discipline.
- v. 25: *wa-labithū fī kahfihim thalātha miʾatin sinīna wa-zdādū tisʿan* — "they remained in their cave three hundred years and increased nine [years]" — the corpus's only multi-century single-event temporal specification.
- v. 26: closing: *qul Allāhu aʿlamu bi-mā labithū lahu ghaybu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi abṣir bihi wa-asmiʿ mā lahum min dūnihi min waliyyin wa-lā yushriku fī ḥukmihi aḥadan* — closing on the *aḥadan* fāṣila that mirrors v. 110.

### 3.3 Lexical signature

The 18-verse Companions block uses **6 occurrences of root *khf*** (cave: vv. 9, 10, 11, 16, 17, 25). All 6 corpus-occurrences of *khf* are in Q 18; this is the project's clearest case of a single-narrative root being a single-surah hapax-cluster. See `06-novel-findings.md` Q018-F-03 for the lexical-isolation test.

The narrative is also the only surah-block deploying *al-raqīm* (root *rqm*, "the inscribed/inscription") in Q 18:9. *al-raqīm* is a corpus-hapax — appearing exactly once. Classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī Q18:9, al-Qurṭubī Q18:9 masāʾil) is divided on whether *al-raqīm* is the cave's name, the youths' inscribed names, or a separate event entirely.

## 4. Block B: bridge A (vv. 27-31)

vv. 27-28: *wa-tlu mā ūḥiya ilayka min kitābi rabbika* — recite what is revealed; do not seek to alter; be patient with believers among the poor.

vv. 29-31: warning to the abandoned (the *ẓalimūn* will be tormented in molten-metal-water); reward for believers (gardens-of-eternity, gold-bracelets, silk-garments, reclining on couches).

The block's function: *iltifāt* (turn-of-attention) from the cave-narrative to the dual fate of believers/disbelievers, preparing the next narrative (the two gardens) which is itself a parable of pride-vs-belief.

## 5. Block N2: the two gardens (vv. 32-44) — narrative 2

### 5.1 Setup (vv. 32-36)

v. 32: *wa-ḍrib lahum mathalan rajulayni jaʿalnā li-aḥadihimā jannatayni min aʿnābin* — "and strike for them a parable of two men: We made for one of them two gardens of grapes". 

vv. 33-34: the rich man's two gardens both yielded fruit; he had wealth and progeny.

vv. 35-36: pride-of-wealth narrative — *wa-dakhala jannatahu wa-huwa ẓālimun li-nafsih* — "he entered his garden being unjust to himself"; *mā aẓunnu hādhihi tabīda abadan* (v. 35); *wa-mā aẓunnu al-sāʿata qāʾimatan* (v. 36).

### 5.2 The believing companion's response (vv. 37-43)

vv. 37-41: the believing companion challenges him; *kafarta bi-l-ladhī khalaqaka min turābin thumma min nuṭfatin thumma sawwāka rajulan* (v. 37); *lākinnā huwa Allāhu rabbī wa-lā ushriku bi-rabbī aḥadan* (v. 38) — *aḥadan* fāṣila echo. v. 39: *wa-lawlā idh dakhalta jannataka qulta mā shāʾa Allāhu lā quwwata illā billāh* — the second-major *In shāʾa Allāh* admonition.

v. 42: *wa-uḥīṭa bi-thamarihi fa-aṣbaḥa yuqallibu kaffayhi ʿalā mā anfaqa fīhā wa-hiya khāwiyatun ʿalā ʿurūshihā* — "his fruit was encompassed [by destruction], and he turned over his palms [in regret] over what he had spent on it, while it had collapsed upon its trellises".

v. 43: *wa-lam takun lahu fiʾatun yanṣurūnahu min dūni Allāhi wa-mā kāna muntaṣiran* — "no party defended him besides God, nor was he defended".

v. 44: *hunālika al-walāyatu lillāhi al-ḥaqq huwa khayrun thawāban wa-khayrun ʿuqban* — "there, sovereignty is for God, the Truth — He is best in reward and best in outcome".

### 5.3 Lexical signature

The two-gardens narrative uses **8 occurrences of root *jnn*** (gardens, vv. 32, 33, 35, 36, 39, 40, 42). The *jnn* density in Q 18 (8 in this block + scattered elsewhere) is moderate; the surah's true *jnn*-density is in this block alone.

## 6. Block C: bridge B (vv. 45-59)

### 6.1 The water-and-vegetation parable (v. 45)

v. 45: *wa-ḍrib lahum mathalan ḥayāti al-dunyā ka-māʾin anzalnāhu min al-samāʾi fa-khtalaṭa bihi nabātu al-arḍ fa-aṣbaḥa hashīman tadhrūhū al-riyāḥu wa-kāna Allāhu ʿalā kulli shayʾin muqtadirā* — "strike for them the parable of the worldly life: like water We sent down from the sky, then it mixed with the vegetation of the earth, then it became chaff scattered by the winds. And God is over all things competent."

This is Q 18's most-cross-referenced verse outside the four narratives. Q 24:39 ("kāfir deeds like a mirage in a plain") and Q 14:18 ("their deeds like ash in a wind") are the parallel chiasms; Q 18:45 is the *water-mixing-then-chaff* version.

### 6.2 Day of Judgment vignettes (vv. 47-49)

vv. 47-49: mountains pulverized; deeds-record book — *fa-tarā al-mujrimīna mushfiqīna mimmā fīhi* (v. 49) — "you see the criminals afraid of what is in [the record]"; *yā waylatanā mā li-hādhā al-kitābi lā yughādiru ṣaghīratan wa-lā kabīratan illā aḥṣāhā* (v. 49).

### 6.3 Iblīs's apostasy (vv. 50-51)

v. 50: *illā Iblīsa kāna min al-jinn fa-fasaqa ʿan amri rabbihi* — "except Iblīs; he was of the jinn and disobeyed his Lord's command". This is the corpus's most explicit identification of Iblīs as *of the jinn* (vs. some classical tafsīrs that classed him as a fallen angel). al-Qurṭubī (Q18:50 masāʾil) treats this verse as the decisive textual evidence for Iblīs's jinn-classification.

vv. 50-51: warning against taking Iblīs and his offspring as protectors.

### 6.4 Closure (vv. 52-59)

vv. 52-53: the Day they call upon their false partners and find no response.

vv. 54-56: the Quran's clear-explanation of every-parable (*wa-laqad ṣarrafnā fī hādhā al-Qurʾāni li-l-nāsi min kulli mathal* — v. 54); this is one of the corpus's two explicit references to *this* Quran's own self-explanatory nature (the other is Q 17:89).

vv. 57-58: those reminded who turn away.

v. 59: catalogue of past doomed civilizations as object-lessons.

## 7. Block N3: Mūsā and al-Khaḍir (vv. 60-82) — narrative 3

### 7.1 The journey to *majmaʿ al-baḥrayn* (vv. 60-65)

v. 60: *wa-idh qāla Mūsā li-fatāhu lā abraḥu ḥattā ablugha majmaʿa al-baḥrayni aw amḍiya ḥuqubā* — "when Mūsā said to his servant-boy: 'I will not cease until I reach the meeting of the two seas, even if I have to go for ages'".

v. 61: at the *majmaʿ* (the place where the seas meet), the fish slipped away into the sea.
v. 62: Mūsā ate breakfast; said "we have suffered fatigue".
v. 63-64: the boy reported the fish-event; this was the sign — *dhālika mā kunnā nabghi*.
v. 65: *fa-wajadā ʿabdan min ʿibādinā ātaynāhu raḥmatan min ʿindinā wa-ʿallamnāhu min ladunnā ʿilman* — "they found a servant of Our servants, to whom We had given mercy from Our presence and taught from Our knowledge". The Khaḍir is referred to ONLY as *ʿabd* — no proper name in the verse.

### 7.2 The three trials (vv. 66-78)

- **Trial 1** — the boat (vv. 71, 79): Khaḍir holes a boat. Mūsā objects. Explanation: "It belonged to poor men working in the sea; I wanted to defect it because there was a king behind them seizing every ship by force."
- **Trial 2** — the boy (vv. 74, 80-81): Khaḍir kills a young boy. Mūsā objects. Explanation: "His parents were believers; we feared he would burden them with rebellion and disbelief; we wanted his Lord to give them in his place one better in purity."
- **Trial 3** — the wall (vv. 77, 82): Khaḍir straightens a wall in a town that refused them food. Mūsā objects ("you could have taken a wage"). Explanation: "It belonged to two orphan boys in the city; under it was a treasure for them; their father was righteous; your Lord wanted them to reach maturity and extract their treasure as a mercy from your Lord — I did not do it of my own accord; that is the interpretation of what you could not bear with patience."

### 7.3 Departure (v. 82 ending)

The Khaḍir's closing line: *dhālika taʾwīlu mā lam tasṭiʿ ʿalayhi ṣabran* — "that is the interpretation of what you could not endure with patience".

### 7.4 Lexical signature

Mūsā-Khaḍir uses:
- *ṣbr* (patience, 8× in the surah, mostly here): vv. 67, 68, 69, 72, 75, 78, 82.
- *ʿabd* (servant, of God) at v. 65 — the Khaḍir as *ʿabd*-of-God parallels the Prophet as *ʿabd* in v. 1.
- *ʿilm* (knowledge), *raḥma* (mercy), *ladun* (presence-of) — the Sufi theological lexicon of *al-ʿilm al-ladunī*.

The Khaḍir's identity is unnamed in the Quran; the proper-noun *al-Khaḍir* is given in the hadith (Bukhārī #3262: "Khaḍir was named so because he sat over a barren white land, it turned green with plantation after his sitting" — explaining the name from root *xDr*, "verdure"). The QAC does NOT have *al-Khaḍir* as a lemma in Q 18 (only the unrelated common-noun *al-khuḍr* "verdure" appears at v. 31, in a different block — `data/morphology/quranic-corpus-morphology-0.4.txt` line `(18:31:17:1) STEM|POS:N|LEM:xuDor|ROOT:xDr|M|INDEF|ACC`).

## 8. Block N4: Dhū al-Qarnayn (vv. 83-101) — narrative 4

### 8.1 The questioner (v. 83)

v. 83: *wa-yasʾalūnaka ʿan Dhī al-Qarnayn qul sa-atlū ʿalaykum minhu dhikran* — "they ask you about Dhū al-Qarnayn; say: 'I will recite to you about him a [memorable] account'". The verse is the only Quranic reference to *Dhū al-Qarnayn* — three occurrences of *qrn* in dual form (vv. 83, 86, 94), all in this block.

### 8.2 The three journeys (vv. 84-98)

- **Journey 1 — west** (vv. 86-88): Dhū al-Qarnayn reaches the place of the sun's setting (*tagrubu fī ʿaynin ḥamiʾa* — "in a muddy spring"). He finds a people; God gives him discretion to punish or reward them; he punishes the unjust and rewards the believers.
- **Journey 2 — east** (vv. 89-91): he reaches the place of the sun's rising; finds people for whom no shelter has been provided.
- **Journey 3 — between two barriers** (vv. 92-98): finds people who barely understand speech; they ask for protection from **Yājūj wa-Mājūj** — *yaʾjūj wa-maʾjūj* — *mufsidūn fī al-arḍi*. He builds a barrier of iron-blocks (*zubar al-ḥadīd*) and molten-copper (*qiṭr*); the barrier holds Yājūj-Mājūj until *ḥattā idhā jāʾa waʿdu rabbī jaʿalahu dakkāʾ* (v. 98) — "until my Lord's promise comes, He will make it leveled".

### 8.3 The eschatological clause (vv. 99-101)

v. 99: *wa-taraknā baʿḍahum yawmaʾidhin yamūju fī baʿḍ wa-nufikha fī al-ṣūr fa-jamaʿnāhum jamʿan* — "We will leave them on that Day surging into one another; the Trumpet will be blown and We will gather them in a [single] gathering". This is the Quran's most explicit description of *al-fitna al-ākhira* (the final-fitna eschatological scene); it transitions from Dhū al-Qarnayn's barrier-history to the universal Last-Day.

vv. 100-101: Hell prepared for the disbelievers; eyes-blinded-from-remembrance.

### 8.4 Lexical signature

Dhū al-Qarnayn uses:
- *qrn* (3× — corpus-rare for Q 18 specifically; note that the broader *qarn* "century" appears elsewhere).
- *yaʾjūj wa-maʾjūj* — corpus-hapax pairing in Q 18:94 (the only other Quranic occurrence is Q 21:96).
- *zubar* (iron-blocks), *qiṭr* (molten-copper) — corpus-rare technical-metalwork roots.
- *mawj* (surge, v. 99) — the eschatological-surge image.

## 9. Block D: frame closing (vv. 102-110)

v. 102: warning against taking other-protectors-than-God.
v. 103-105: those whose deeds were lost will be told they are *al-akhsarīna aʿmālan*.
v. 106: their reward is Hell because of their disbelief.
v. 107-108: believers' Paradise — *jannātu al-firdawsi nuzulan* — *khālidīna fīhā lā yabghūna ʿanhā ḥiwalan* (v. 108).
v. 109: *qul law kāna al-baḥru midādan li-kalimāti rabbī la-nafida al-baḥru qabla an tanfada kalimātu rabbī wa-law jiʾnā bi-mithlihi madadan* — "say: were the sea ink for the words of my Lord, the sea would be exhausted before my Lord's words were exhausted, even if We brought the like of it as supply".

The v. 109 *baḥr-as-ink* parable is the Quran's most explicit self-reference to the inexhaustibility of revelation. It mirrors Q 31:27's *seven-seas-as-ink* parable (the only other corpus-locus of this trope).

### 9.1 The terminal *qul innamā anā basharun mithlukum* (v. 110)

v. 110: *qul innamā anā basharun mithlukum yūḥā ilayya annamā ilāhukum ilāhun wāḥidun fa-man kāna yarjū liqāʾa rabbihi fa-l-yaʿmal ʿamalan ṣāliḥan wa-lā yushrik bi-ʿibādati rabbihi aḥadan*

"Say: I am only a man like you, to whom it is revealed that your god is one God; so whoever expects to meet his Lord let him do righteous deeds and associate no one in the worship of his Lord."

The closing fāṣila *aḥadan* mirrors v. 26's closing *aḥadan* (84 verses apart). Both use the verb *yushrik* (associate); v. 26 has it in the negative-third-person about God ("He associates none in His judgment"), v. 110 has it in the imperative-second-person about humanity ("let him associate none in worship"). The mirror is exact at the lexical level: *wa-lā yushriku fī ḥukmihi aḥadan* (v. 26) ↔ *wa-lā yushrik bi-ʿibādati rabbihi aḥadan* (v. 110).

This is one of the cleanest single-word-fāṣila ring-closures in the corpus. The four-narrative arc is bracketed by an *aḥadan* fāṣila at its first-narrative-close (v. 26, end of N1) and at its surah-close (v. 110, end of D); the entire 4-narrative arc + bridges + closing frame is enclosed within this fāṣila ring.

## 12. Cross-surah references

Internal Quranic citations / parallels from Q 18:
- The Mūsā-Khaḍir narrative is **uniquely concentrated in Q 18** — Mūsā's other narrative-blocks (Q 7, Q 20, Q 26, Q 28) do NOT contain this story; it is corpus-hapax in narrative position.
- The Dhū al-Qarnayn / Yājūj-Mājūj barrier is paralleled in Q 21:96 (the *minor* Yājūj-Mājūj reference): *ḥattā idhā futiḥat yaʾjūju wa-maʾjūj* — "until Yājūj-Mājūj are released, they descend from every height". Q 18 has the *building* of the barrier; Q 21 has the *release*.
- The two-gardens parable (vv. 32-44) thematically parallels Q 68:17-33 (the garden-owners who plotted to harvest without saying *In shāʾa Allāh*); both narratives center on pride-of-wealth + non-attribution-of-causation-to-God.
- The Aṣḥāb al-Kahf narrative has no clear Quranic parallel; the Christian "Seven Sleepers of Ephesus" tradition is the documented pre-Islamic context per al-Ṭabarī Q18:9 (asbāb of the questioner — Najrān Christian elders).
- Q 18:45's *māʾ-yansab-min-al-samāʾ* parable parallels Q 24:39 (*kāfir deeds like mirage*), Q 14:18 (*deeds like ash in wind*).

## 13. Register summary

Q 18 is **predominantly narrative** (4 distinct narrative cores covering 73 verses = 66% of the surah), with discursive frames and bridges (35 verses = 32%) and a final 2-verse hortatory closure (v. 109-110). The legal-imperative content is essentially zero (no *ḥadd*-rules, no civil legislation, no inheritance law); Q 18 is a pure prophet-narrative + parable + eschatology surah.

The 4-narrative architecture creates **lexical-disjoint blocks**: each narrative's vocabulary is largely closed within its block. *khf* (cave) lives only in N1. *jnn* (gardens) is concentrated in N2. *ṣbr* (patience) is concentrated in N3. *qrn* (Two-Horned) is exclusive to N4. This is the project's clearest case of **narrative-block-lexical-isolation in a single surah** — see `06-novel-findings.md` Q018-F-04 for the empirical hapax-test on the Mūsā-Khaḍir block.

This 4-narrative architecture is the project's empirical contrast to Q 12 Yūsuf — Q 12 is the corpus's single-narrative-archetype (one continuous Yūsuf story), Q 18 is the corpus's multi-narrative-archetype (four distinct stories sharing a single rhyme). The two surahs are the cleanest corpus-poles for the question "how does the Quran organize narrative?"
