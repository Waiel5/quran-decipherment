## 1. The *shaqq al-ṣadr* / chest-opening (the v-1 *sharḥ al-ṣadr* prophetic correlate)

al-Qurṭubī (on Q 94:1) cites, "fī al-Ṣaḥīḥ," the **Isrāʾ chest-opening** narration of **Anas b. Mālik ←
Mālik b. Ṣaʿṣaʿa**: the Prophet, between sleep and waking, has his breast opened (*fa-shuriḥa ṣadrī*), his
heart washed with Zamzam water from a basin of gold and refilled with faith and wisdom. This is the
ḥadīth-corpus anchor for the v-1 *sharḥ al-ṣadr* favor (the mufassirūn split on whether v 1 *refers* to
this miracle or to spiritual illumination — see `03-tafsir-survey.md`). The Mālik-b.-Ṣaʿṣaʿa Isrāʾ
narration is attested across the Ṣaḥīḥayn and the Sunan:

**Key text (Muslim #321 / Tirmidhī #3430, Anas ← Mālik b. Ṣaʿṣaʿa):** *"… a basin of gold containing
Zamzam water; then He opened my breast (*fa-sharaḥa ṣadrī*) up to such-and-such … then my heart was
extracted, washed with Zamzam water, restored to its place, and filled with faith and wisdom."* The
verb *sharaḥa ṣadrī* in this ḥadīth is the same root (š-r-ḥ) + collocation (ṣadr) as Q 94:1's *a-lam
nashraḥ laka ṣadrak* — which is why al-Qurṭubī cites it on v 1. The Tirmidhī placement under **Tafsīr**
makes this an exegetically-linked ḥadīth, not merely an Isrāʾ report.

## 2. *Lan yaghliba ʿusrun yusrayn* (the v 5-6 "two-eases" report)

The famous statement attached to vv 5-6 — *"one hardship will not overcome two eases"* — is the
load-bearing ḥadīth/athar for the two-eases reading. **On disk, its status is split:**

| Locus | idInBook / file | Isnād | Status |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Mālik, *al-Muwaṭṭaʾ*** | **#1007** (Book 21, *Kitāb al-Jihād*) | Mālik ← **Zayd b. Aslam** | **VERIFIED.** ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb's letter replying to **Abū ʿUbayda b. al-Jarrāḥ** about the Roman armies: *"… mā yanzilu bi-ʿabdin muʾminin min manzili shiddatin illā jaʿala Allāhu baʿdahu farajan, wa-innahu **lan yaghliba ʿusrun yusrayn**, wa-anna Allāha yaqūlu fī kitābihi {yā ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū iṣbirū…}."* This is a **mawqūf** report (ʿUmar's words in a letter), not marfūʿ. |
| al-Ṭabarī *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (Q 94:5) | spa5k `ar-tafsir-al-tabari/94/5.json` | al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (Yūnus, ʿAwf, Maʿmar chains); Qatāda | **mursal** marfūʿ-attribution ("the Prophet came out laughing, saying lan yaghliba…") — al-Ḥasan does not name a Companion. |
| Ibn Kathīr *Tafsīr* (Q 94:5) | spa5k `ar-tafsir-ibn-kathir/94/5.json` | al-Ḥasan / Qatāda (mursal); + Abū Hurayra (other text) | quotes the al-Ḥasan mursal; explicitly notes it is *"rawāhu … ʿan al-Ḥasan mursalan."* |

**FLAGGED.** A 9-book search (all 9 collections, diacritics-stripped match on *يغلب عسر* / *عسر يسرين* /
*يسرين*) returns **no marfūʿ ḥadīth** of *lan yaghliba ʿusrun yusrayn* connected to the Prophet through a
Companion isnād. The only 9-book attestation of the phrase is **Mālik Muwaṭṭaʾ #1007**, where it is
**ʿUmar's mawqūf statement** in his letter to Abū ʿUbayda (= the very ʿUmar letter al-Qurṭubī cites on
v 5). The marfūʿ form is preserved only in the tafsīr literature (al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr) as a
**mursal** report from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī. This is an important honesty point: the "ḥadīth" most readers
attach to Q 94:5-6 is, in the 9-book corpus, an **athar of ʿUmar**, not a connected prophetic ḥadīth.

*(Incidental non-matches the search surfaced and excluded: Bukhārī #220, #5896, Tirmidhī #147, Abū Dāwūd
#380, Nasāʾī #56, #331 — the *"buʿithtum muyassirīn wa-lam tubʿathū muʿassirīn"* Bedouin-urination ḥadīth,
which shares the y-s-r / ʿ-s-r roots but is a different report; Dārimī #2599 — Ibn Masʿūd's *"luyussira
ʿalā al-Qurʾān"* dream, also unrelated.)*

## 3. The v 7-8 worship-charge (*fa-idhā faraghta fa-nṣab*)

Ibn Kathīr (on vv 7-8) attaches the *muttafaq ʿalayh* prayer-etiquette ḥadīth that bears on "when you are
free, devote yourself": *"lā ṣalāta bi-ḥaḍrati al-ṭaʿām, wa-lā huwa yudāfiʿuhu al-akhbathān"* ("no prayer
in the presence of food, nor while restraining the two foul things") — the principle that worship is
performed once one is *free* of distracting needs. This is a thematic gloss on v 7's *farāgh*-then-*naṣb*
charge, not a surah-specific occasion-of-revelation. (The agreed-upon ʿĀʾisha narration of *lā ṣalāta
biḥaḍrati al-ṭaʿām* is in Muslim, Book of Mosques; not isolated here as a Q-94 citation since Ibn Kathīr
adduces it analogically.)

## 4. Faḍāʾil / recitation

A 9-book search for the surah by name (*a-lam nashraḥ*, *al-inshirāḥ*, *sūrat al-sharḥ*) returns **0
ḥadīth** isolating the recitation-virtue of Q 94. This is consistent with the short-Meccan block
generally (faḍāʾil ḥadīth concentrate on Q 1, 2, 18, 36, 55, 56, 67, 112, 113, 114). **Flagged as a
data-gap, not an absence-of-evidence claim** — a dedicated faḍāʾil sub-collection search (Ibn al-Ḍurays
*Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān*, al-Thaʿlabī's faḍāʾil chains) lies outside the on-disk 9-book set. (The classical
practice of pairing al-Ḍuḥā + al-Sharḥ in a single recitation unit — and the takbīr-from-al-Ḍuḥā Meccan
recitation tradition — is a *qirāʾāt* matter discussed in the tafsīr/qirāʾāt literature, not a 9-book
ḥadīth; noted in `05-classical-claims-audit.md` and `07-cross-references.md`.)
