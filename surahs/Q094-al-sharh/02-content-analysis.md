# Q 94 al-Sharḥ — Content Analysis

Text from `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json` (cross-verified against min- and full-tashkeel variants;
all three Arabic variants agree on the 8-verse text). Register: **consolation / direct second-person
address to the Prophet** — the surah is wholly addressed to *anta* (the *-ka* suffix), with no narrative
or legal material.

## 2. Thematic blocks

**Block I — the three favors (vv 1-4), rhyme-zone -aka.** A rhetorical-affirmative *a-lam* ("did We
not…?", which functions as a strong affirmation: "We surely did") enumerates three completed divine
favors on the addressee: (1) the **expansion of the breast** (*sharḥ al-ṣadr*, v 1); (2) the **removal
of the burden** that "weighed down the back" (vv 2-3 — *wizr* / *anqaḍa ẓahrak*); (3) the **raising of
the remembrance/fame** (*rafʿ al-dhikr*, v 4). All four verses close on the second-person possessive
suffix -ka (ṣadra-ka, wizra-ka, ẓahra-ka, dhikra-ka), making the addressee the rhyme itself — the surah
is, phonologically, *about* "you."

**Block II — the doubled assurance (vv 5-6), rhyme-zone -ā.** The structural pivot. A general law is
stated and immediately reprised near-verbatim: *fa-inna maʿa al-ʿusri yusrā* / *inna maʿa al-ʿusri
yusrā*. The two verses are identical save the leading fāʾ of v 5 (see §3). This is the surah's emotional
and rhetorical center: the completed past favors (Block I) ground a present-and-future assurance.

**Block III — the discharge charge (vv 7-8), rhyme-zone -ab.** A two-clause imperative turning the
consolation into a directive: *fa-idhā faraghta fa-nṣab* ("when you are free [of one task], labor on [at
the next / at worship]") and *wa-ilā rabbika fa-rghab* ("and to your Lord direct your longing"). The
surah closes by re-naming the addressee's Lord (*rabbika*) — the same *rabb* that closes al-Ḍuḥā
(Q 93:11 *wa-ammā bi-niʿmati rabbika fa-ḥaddith*).

## 3. The near-verbatim reprise (vv 5-6) — the surah's signature

This is the project's per-surah landing of the **near-verbatim adjacent reprise** (full detail in
`06-novel-findings.md`, test Q094-F-01). Three facts:

1. **One-character delta.** v 5 = `فإن مع العسر يسرا`; v 6 = `إن مع العسر يسرا`. They are
   character-identical except v 5's leading connective fāʾ (فإن "for truly") which v 6 drops (إن "truly").
   Character (Levenshtein) edit distance = **1**.

2. **Corpus-singular tightness.** Across all 5,821 substantive (≥3-word) adjacent same-surah verse pairs
   in the Quran, Q 94:5-6 has the **globally minimum** edit distance (1) — the single tightest
   near-verbatim adjacency in the entire corpus. There are **zero** byte-identical adjacent verse pairs
   anywhere, so Q 94:5-6 is as close to verbatim succession as the Quran ever places two verses. It is
   also the **unique** adjacent pair differing by only a single leading connective particle.

3. **Why the verbatim refrain census misses it.** H-NEW-2310's exact-string census (24 intra-surah
   verbatim repeats) does *not* list Q 94:5-6, because the strings differ by the fāʾ. Q 94 thus adds a
   distinct repetition type — *near*-verbatim reprise — to the project's repetition ladder (within-verse
   reduplication H-NEW-2100 → verse-initial anaphora H-NEW-2140 → distributed verbatim refrain H-NEW-2310).

**The grammatical reading (definite ʿusr / indefinite yusr).** *al-ʿusr* ("the hardship") carries the
definite article الـ in both verses; *yusr* ("ease") is indefinite (يسرا, accusative tanwīn) in both.
Classical grammarians read the repeated **definite** noun as one-and-the-same referent (one hardship) and
the repeated **indefinite** noun as two distinct referents (two eases) — hence *lan yaghliba ʿusrun
yusrayn* ("one hardship will not overcome two eases"). Q094-F-01 Arm C verifies that this asymmetry is a
genuine orthographic fact (not an interpretive imposition): العسر in both verses, يسرا in both, and the
v5/v6 root-Jaccard is exactly 1.0 (same proposition reprised). The *theological* inference about "two
eases" is out of scope (Protocol §10); only the orthographic substrate is verified here. al-Qurṭubī
additionally reads the dropped fāʾ of v 6 as the syntactic marker of an independent *ibtidāʾ* (fresh
restart) rather than a coordinated continuation — i.e. the surface single-character difference carries
the exegetical weight of the "second, distinct ease."

## 4. Content register and vocabulary

- **Register:** pure consolation / hortatory address. No eschatology, no law, no narrative — unusual even
  among short Meccan surahs.
- **Vocabulary distinctness:** the surah's 14 roots are highly concentrated — the *-ka* address saturates
  vv 1-4 and v 8, and the ʿ-s-r / y-s-r pair is doubled (vv 5-6). This concentration drives the high
  H-NEW-750 local_cohesion (2.45, z = +1.27) and the very-low FR mean (0.79): Q 94 is lexically typical of
  its dense short-surah cluster and internally repetitive.
- **The *sharḥ al-ṣadr* motif elsewhere:** v 1's *nashraḥ … ṣadrak* is the only first-person-plural
  divine *expansion* of the Prophet's breast; the same root+ṣadr collocation appears at Q 6:125 (*yashraḥ
  ṣadrahu li-l-islām*), Q 39:22 (*sharaḥa Allāhu ṣadrahu li-l-islām*), Q 16:106 (*sharaḥa bi-l-kufri
  ṣadran*), and — most resonantly — Q 20:25, Mūsā's prayer *rabbi-shraḥ lī ṣadrī* ("my Lord, expand for me
  my breast"). Q 94:1 answers, for Muḥammad, the prayer Mūsā had to make.

## 5. Repetition patterns

- **The vv 5-6 near-verbatim reprise** (the surah's defining feature; §3).
- **The -ka possessive-suffix anaphora** across vv 1-4 (ṣadrak, wizrak, ẓahrak, dhikrak) + v 8 (rabbik) —
  a sustained second-person address run.
- **The fāʾ-initial verses** (vv 5, 7) marking the two consequential turns (assurance, charge).

## 6. Cross-surah content references

- **Q 93 al-Ḍuḥā (the consolation pair):** shares the second-person consolation register, the *rabbuka*
  address, and the dhikr/elevation theme; the Q 93 → Q 94 seam is seamless (asc-rank 10/113, H-NEW-720).
  Both are early-Meccan reassurances to the Prophet during the *fatra* (pause in revelation) period
  reported in the asbāb tradition (see `04-hadith-corpus.md` / `05-classical-claims-audit.md`).
- **Q 48 al-Fatḥ:** Ibn Kathīr reads v 2 (*wa-waḍaʿnā ʿanka wizrak*) in light of Q 48:2 (*li-yaghfira laka
  Allāhu mā taqaddama min dhanbika wa-mā taʾakhkhar*) — the burden as the forgiven matter.
- **Q 108 al-Kawthar (nearest FR neighbor, 0.231):** short-Meccan gift/consolation register; both reassure
  the Prophet against the slights of the mushrikūn.

## 7. Honest limits

- The "two eases" / *yusrayn* reading is a *grammatical-exegetical* inference; Q094-F-01 Arm C verifies
  only the orthographic asymmetry (definite ʿusr, indefinite yusr) on which it rests, not the theological
  claim itself, which is out of scope.
- Cross-surah motif links (sharḥ al-ṣadr at Q 6/16/20/39) are lexical co-occurrences of the root š-r-ḥ +
  ṣ-d-r; they are content resonances, not the per-surah structural findings the empirical instruments test.
