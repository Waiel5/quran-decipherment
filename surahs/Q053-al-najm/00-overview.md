## 2. Classical name and naming convention

The surah is named **al-Najm** ("The Star") after the opening word of v.1 *wa-l-najmi*. The construction is the canonical naming-by-oath-particle pattern shared with Q 37 al-Ṣāffāt, Q 51 al-Dhāriyāt, Q 52 al-Ṭūr, Q 75 al-Qiyāma, Q 77 al-Mursalāt, Q 79 al-Nāziʿāt, Q 81 al-Takwīr, Q 85 al-Burūj, Q 86 al-Ṭāriq, Q 89 al-Fajr, Q 91 al-Shams, Q 92 al-Layl, Q 93 al-Ḍuḥā, Q 95 al-Tīn, Q 100 al-ʿĀdiyāt, Q 103 al-ʿAṣr — i.e., 17 surahs of the canonical oath-opener stylistic family.

### Classical disagreement on the referent of *al-najm* (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.; al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* vol. 28 pp. 235–238; al-Qurṭubī *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* ad loc.):

- **The Pleiades / al-Thurayyā** (Ibn ʿAbbās majority view, al-Mujāhid, Ibn Zayd via Ibn Jarīr): *al-najm* is the definite-singular standing for the cluster, with classical Arab usage *al-najm = al-Thurayyā* by *iṣṭilāḥ* convention. al-Suyūṭī cites this as the most-attested *qawl*. (al-Rāzī's wajh I.)
- **Any star at its setting** (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Mujāhid alt.): generic descending star. (wajh II.)
- **The shooting star / shihāb**: cosmological-eschatological reading via *idhā hawā* (cf. Q 67:5 *rujūman li-l-shayāṭīn*). (wajh III, less common.)
- **The Qurʾān itself** (al-Bāqillānī, *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*, sec. 17 on *al-aqsām*): *al-najm* allegorically the Quran descending in stages — *idhā hawā* glossed *idhā nazala*. This reading parallels the al-Bāqillānī gloss on Q 37:1 *al-ṣāffāt = al-āyāt al-mutarākiba*. (wajh IV.)

al-Rāzī (vol. 28 p. 236): *fī al-najmi awjuh: al-awwalu al-thurayyā kullīyatan, al-thānī yajūzu an yurāda kullu najmin idhā ghāba, al-thālithu al-najmu min al-Qurʾāni alladhī yunazzilu munajjaman, al-rābiʿu al-shihābu alladhī yarmā bihi al-shayāṭīn* — "On *al-najm* there are several wajh: first, the Pleiades; second, any star when it sets; third, the *najm* of the Quran which descends piece-by-piece; fourth, the meteor used to repel demons."

Empirical: see [`07-cross-references.md`](07-cross-references.md) §3 — Q 53 nearest FR-neighbor is Q 96 al-ʿAlaq (the FIRST revelation) at d_FR = 0.7126, suggesting the surah's content-fingerprint is anchored in the early-revelation Meccan visionary register, not the cosmological-meter register of Q 91 al-Shams or Q 92 al-Layl.

Q 53 is one of **6 single-element oath-openers** {Q 52, **Q 53**, Q 85, Q 90, Q 103, Q 95-with-different-counting} — but uniquely couples its single oath-particle to the visionary content that follows (vv 2–18) rather than to a cosmological-condition followed by a third-person eschatological frame (Q 52, Q 85, Q 103). This single-oath-into-visionary architecture is **unique to Q 53** in the oath-opener family.

The semantic of the oath: the descending/setting star binds to the descending of revelation in vv 2–4, *mā ḍalla ṣāḥibukum wa-mā ghawā / wa-mā yanṭiqu ʿani l-hawā / in huwa illā waḥyun yūḥā* — "Your companion has not strayed nor erred; he does not speak from desire; it is none other than revelation revealed." The 1-element oath thus binds star-descent ↔ revelation-descent ↔ vision-content via the unifying root *h-w-y* (Q 53:1 *hawā* / Q 53:3 *al-hawā*) — a paronomasia-axis (jinās tāmm) classical *balāgha* identifies as a *naẓm* mechanism (al-Jurjānī *Dalāʾil al-Iʿjāz*; al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 60 on *al-jinās*).

## 4. ⭐ Unique structural property — FIRST sajda surah AND only surah closed by a sajda-command

**Q 53 is the FIRST of the 14 sajda surahs in mushaf order** (the 14 are Q 7, 13, 16, 17, 19, 22, 25, 27, 32, 38, 41, **53**, 84, 96 — 15 total sajda-marked verses, since Q 22 has two: vv 18 and 77; verified 2026-05-09 from the canonical mushaf ۩-marker scan, see `01-empirical-profile.md` §10).

**Q 53 is also the ONLY surah whose entire 62-verse arc terminates in a sajda-command verse**: Q 53:62 *fa-sjudū lillāhi wa-ʿbudū* ("Prostrate to Allāh and worship") is THE LAST VERSE of the surah; the surah does not continue past its sajda. Compare:

- Q 7 al-Aʿrāf — sajda at v. 206 of 206 → SAME-LAST-VERSE
- **Q 53 al-Najm — sajda at v. 62 of 62 → SAME-LAST-VERSE**
- Q 96 al-ʿAlaq — sajda at v. 19 of 19 → SAME-LAST-VERSE
- All other sajda-surahs — sajda is mid-surah, surah continues past

**Three surahs share this property** (Q 7, **Q 53**, Q 96); Q 96 and Q 53 are both Quranic-vision/revelation-disclosure surahs.

The classical interpretation (al-Bukhārī ṣaḥīḥ 1067; Ibn Masʿūd narration; see `04-hadith-corpus.md` §2) emphasizes that the Prophet recited Q 53 in Mecca and prostrated, and *all who heard him prostrated except one elderly man* — a scene staged by the closing imperative.

## 5. ⭐ Unique structural property — first explicit description of prophetic-vision (Sidrat al-Muntahā, vv 13–18)

Q 53:13–18 is the **earliest detailed pericope in revelation order** of the Prophet's vision-experience:

| v | Text | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 13 | ولقد رآه نزلة أخرى | *And indeed he saw him at a second descent* |
| 14 | عند سدرة المنتهى | *near the Lote-tree of the Boundary (Sidrat al-Muntahā)* |
| 15 | عندها جنة المأوى | *near it is the Garden of Refuge (Jannat al-Maʾwā)* |
| 16 | إذ يغشى السدرة ما يغشى | *when there covered the Lote-tree what covered (it)* |
| 17 | ما زاغ البصر وما طغى | *the sight neither swerved nor exceeded* |
| 18 | لقد رأى من آيات ربه الكبرى | *he had seen of the greatest signs of his Lord* |

The Sidrat al-Muntahā is mentioned **TWICE in the entire Quran**: Q 53:14 (*sidrati l-muntahā*) and Q 53:16 (*al-sidra*) — the latter elliptical reference back to v. 14. There is no other Quranic mention of the Sidra-tree-of-the-boundary, making it a **Q 53-monopoly hapax topos**.

This pericope is the **direct empirical anchor** for the night-journey + Mi'rāj traditions. The classical *sīra*-Mi'rāj corpus (Ibn Isḥāq, Ibn Hishām, al-Ṭabarī *Tārīkh*, al-Bayhaqī *Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwa*) builds layers of detail on top of this 6-verse pericope. Two Quranic verses — Q 17:1 *subḥāna lladhī asrā* (the *isrāʾ* — night-journey) and Q 53:13–18 (the vision — *miʿrāj*) — constitute the textual base for all subsequent Mi'rāj traditions. Q 17:1 names *al-masjid al-aqṣā*; Q 53:13–18 names *sidrat al-muntahā* — the two endpoints. (al-Ṭabarī *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* on Q 17:1 + Q 53:13.)

The **referent of "him"** (*raʾāhu* in v 13) is classical-disputed:

- **Jibrīl** (ʿĀʾisha via Bukhārī 4855 + Muslim 177; Ibn Masʿūd; al-Ṭabarī's preferred view): the Prophet saw the angel Jibrīl in his true form. ʿĀʾisha is explicit: *innamā huwa Jibrīl* — "It is Gabriel only." (Bukhārī 4855)
- **Allāh** (Ibn ʿAbbās minority via Tirmidhī 3279 with chain disputed): the Prophet saw God. al-Tirmidhī himself grades the chain *gharīb*; al-Albānī later grades *ḍaʿīf*.
- **Ahl al-Sunna majority synthesis** (al-Ashʿarī, al-Bāqillānī, al-Nawawī commentary on Muslim): the Prophet saw Jibrīl in v 13 + 18; the question "did Muḥammad see Allāh" admits no certain answer, with Sunni majority deferring to ʿĀʾisha's *Jibrīl-only* reading. (al-Nawawī, *Sharḥ Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, ch. on īmān, pp. 162–165 of the Ar-Risāla edition.)

See `04-hadith-corpus.md` §3 for the verified Bukhārī + Muslim isnāds.

## 6. ⭐ Notable controversy — the "satanic verses" classical narrative (vv 19–23 frame; INTERPOLATION CLAIM AUDITED)

Verses 19–23 contain the polytheism-rebuke against the three Meccan female-deities **al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, Manāt**:

| v | Text | Gloss |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 19 | أفرأيتم اللات والعزى | *Have you seen al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā* |
| 20 | ومناة الثالثة الأخرى | *and Manāt, the third — the other?* |
| 21 | ألكم الذكر وله الأنثى | *Are the males yours and the females His?* |
| 22 | تلك إذا قسمة ضيزى | *That is then an unjust apportionment* |
| 23 | إن هي إلا أسماء سميتموها أنتم وآباؤكم ما أنزل الله بها من سلطان… | *They are nothing but names which you and your fathers named; God has sent down no authority for them…* |

Classical narrative (al-Wāqidī *Maghāzī*, Ibn Saʿd *Ṭabaqāt* I.205, al-Ṭabarī *Tārīkh* I.1192–1196 + *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* on Q 22:52, al-Bayhaqī *Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwa* II.285): asserts that between v 20 and v 21 — at the moment the deities were named — Satan suggested into the Prophet's recitation an interpolated "exalted exception" reading:

> *tilka l-gharānīqu l-ʿulā wa-inna shafāʿatahunna la-turtajā* — "Those are the exalted cranes; their intercession is to be hoped for"

— that the Quraysh momentarily rejoiced and prostrated alongside, and that v 21 *a-lakumu l-dhakaru wa-lahu l-unthā* and Q 22:52 *wa-mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin wa-lā nabiyyin illā idhā tamannā alqā l-shayṭānu fī umniyyatihi* were revealed as the corrective.

**Adversarial-discipline audit** (full audit at `05-classical-claims-audit.md` §2):

- **al-Bukhārī ṣaḥīḥ — SILENT.** I confirmed by direct corpus-search across all 7,277 Bukhārī hadiths (`/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/hadith/ahmedbaset-json/db/by_book/the_9_books/bukhari.json`) that no hadith contains the strings *al-gharānīq*, *al-gharānīq al-ʿulā*, or *tilka l-gharānīqu l-ʿulā*. Bukhārī 1067/1070/1071 (Ibn Masʿūd narration of the prostration of all-who-heard) describe the Q 53 recitation event but contain NO mention of any interpolation.
- **Muslim ṣaḥīḥ — SILENT.** Same null result on direct corpus-search. The closest Muslim narration (Muslim 576 via Ibn ʿAbbās, in the Sajdah-of-Recitation cluster) describes the prostration without any "satanic" interpolation.
- **Tirmidhī, Abū Dāwūd, Nasāʾī, Ibn Mājah, Aḥmad, Mālik, Dārimī — ALL SILENT** on the *gharānīq* phrase. Empirically: NONE of the 9 canonical hadith books contain the phrase.
- **al-Ṭabarī's primary chain** (al-Wāqidī → Yūnus b. Bukayr → Muḥammad b. Isḥāq → Yazīd b. Ziyād al-Madanī → Muḥammad b. - **Al-Albānī** (*Naṣb al-Majānīq li-Nasf Qiṣṣat al-Gharānīq*, Damascus 1952, throughout): comprehensive isnād-by-isnād critique demonstrating ALL chains are *mursal* (lack a Companion link) or weakly/severely-weak. al-Bayhaqī's chain has a *mudallis* (Ibn Isḥāq); al-Ṭabarī's chain has *mursal* breaks; the strongest version (via Ibn Isḥāq → Saʿīd b. Jubayr) is itself a Successor-narration without a Companion.
- **Ibn Kathīr** (*Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* on Q 22:52, vol. 5 pp. 423–425, Dār Ṭayba ed.): "*hādhihi al-qiṣṣa rawāhā kathīrun min al-mufassirīna lākinnahā mursalatun min ṭuruqin laysa fīhā ʿan ṣaḥābiyyin yataṣiluhā ilā al-Nabiyyī ṣ.ʿ.s.* — "This story is narrated by many *mufassirūn* but it is *mursal* through every chain, with no Companion-link reaching back to the Prophet." ## 11. Classical commentary lineage

Major classical tafsir on Q 53 (full survey: [`03-tafsir-survey.md`](03-tafsir-survey.md)):

- **al-Ṭabarī** (*Jāmiʿ al-bayān*, vol. 22 pp. 506–531 of Šākir-Šākir ed.): the foundational tradition-aggregating treatment. ## 13. Headline summary

Q 53 al-Najm is the **prophetic-vision archetypal surah** of the Quranic corpus — the corpus's single most-explicit-vision-pericope text, the FIRST sajda surah, and one of three surahs that close with their sajda-command verse. The narrative survives only as a Sīra-tradition / occasion-of-revelation tradition with no canonical-hadith attestation. This makes Q 53 a load-bearing test-case for the project's M-5 classical-doctrine-decomposition pattern: the **balāgha-rhetorical reading** of vv 1–18 (al-Bāqillānī, al-Jurjānī, al-Rāzī's *naẓm* analysis) survives empirical audit, while the **historical-apologetic interpolation claim** (al-Wāqidī, late-Ṭabarī aggregator) fails.
