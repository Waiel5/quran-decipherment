## 2. Bukhārī ṣaḥīḥ on Q 53 — al-Najm-direct hadiths

### 2.1. Sajda chapter (kitāb sujūd al-Qurʾān, chapter 17 of Bukhārī = book 19 of Khan's vol 2)

The Sujūd al-Qurʾān book in Bukhārī contains **13 hadiths total** (Bukhārī inbook# 1036–1048). Of these, **5 are al-Najm-specific**:

#### Bukhārī 1036 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED
- **Narrator**: ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd (Ibn Masʿūd Companion-narration)
- **Chain**: Muḥammad b. Bashshār → Ghundar → Shuʿba → Abū Isḥāq → al-Aswad → Ibn Masʿūd
- **Arabic**: *qaraʾa l-nabīyyu ṣ.ʿ.s. al-najma bi-Makkata fa-sajada fīhā wa-sajada man maʿahu, ghayra shaykhin akhadha kaffan min ḥaṣan aw turābin fa-rafaʿahu ilā jabhatihi wa-qāla yakfīnī hādhā*
- **Translation**: "The Prophet recited Sūrat al-Najm in Mecca, prostrated upon it, and those with him prostrated — except an old man who took a handful of pebbles or earth, raised it to his forehead, and said 'this is enough for me.' Later I saw him killed as a non-believer."
- **Significance**: Establishes Q 53 as the EARLIEST surah recited with the *sujūd al-tilāwah* practice. Sets the empirical precedent for the four-madhhab consensus on Q 53:62 as a sajda position.
- **Translator note**: The Arabic-text identifies the surah as *al-Najm* without bracketed-number; the published Khan English translation has a misprint *Surat-an-Najm (103)* in some printings, corrected to *(53)* in others. This is a **translator-error** flag for users of older Khan editions.

#### Bukhārī 1039 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED
- **Narrator**: ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd (Ibn Masʿūd, parallel chain to #1036)
- **Chain**: Ḥafṣ b. ʿUmar → Shuʿba → Abū Isḥāq → al-Aswad → Ibn Masʿūd
- **Translation**: "The Prophet recited Sūrat al-Najm and prostrated, and all the people prostrated, except a man who took a handful of stones or earth and raised it to his face, saying 'this is enough for me. Later I saw him killed as a non-believer.'"
- **Significance**: Same content as #1036 with different chain — al-Bukhārī's standard double-confirmation.

#### Bukhārī 1040 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED
- **Narrator**: Ibn ʿAbbās
- **Chain**: Musaddad → ʿAbd al-Wārith → Ayyūb → ʿIkrima → Ibn ʿAbbās
- **Translation**: "The Prophet prostrated while reciting al-Najm, and with him prostrated the Muslims, the pagans, the jinns, and all human beings."
- **Significance**: The corpus's most-extensive prostration-event report — claiming jinn + pagans + Muslims all prostrated. This is one of the project's anchor-hadiths for the **universal-recitation-event** classical tradition.

#### Bukhārī 1041 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED
- **Narrator**: ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasār (asking Zayd b. Thābit)
- **Chain**: Sulaymān b. Dāwud Abū l-Rabīʿ → Ismāʿīl b. Jaʿfar → Yazīd b. Khuṣayfa → Ibn Qusayṭ → ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasār → Zayd b. Thābit
- **Translation**: "I asked Zayd b. Thābit about prostration; he said that he had recited al-Najm before the Prophet, yet the Prophet did not perform a prostration."
- **Significance**: This is the COUNTER-NARRATION — Zayd b. Thābit reports a different occasion when the Prophet did NOT prostrate after Q 53. Classical fiqh harmonises (the prostration is recommended but not always performed), supporting the Shāfiʿī/Mālikī/Ḥanbalī "sunna" rather than Hanafī "wājib" classification. al-Albānī's treatment of #1041 is detailed in *al-Ṭāmm al-Mughnī ʿan al-Iʿtibār bi-l-Ḥadīth al-Khabīr* — the hadith is ṣaḥīḥ but does NOT abrogate #1036/1039/1040 (different occasions).

#### Bukhārī 1042 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED
- **Narrator**: Zayd b. Thābit (direct first-person narration; parallel to #1041)
- **Chain**: Ādam b. Abī Iyās → Ibn Abī Dhiʾb → Yazīd b. ʿAbdillāh b. Qusayṭ → ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasār → Zayd b. Thābit
- **Translation**: "I recited al-Najm before the Prophet, yet he did not perform a prostration."
- **Significance**: Same as #1041 with one chain-step shorter (direct Zayd b. Thābit narration).

### 2.2. Tafsir chapter (kitāb al-tafsīr, chapter 65)

Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Tafsīr* sub-chapter on Q 53 contains the following directly-relevant hadiths:

#### Bukhārī 4651 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — on Q 53:18 *raʾā min āyāti rabbihi l-kubrā*
- **Narrator**: ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd
- **Translation**: "Regarding the revelation 'Truly he (Muhammad) did see of the signs of his Lord; the Greatest!' (Q 53:18) — the Prophet saw a green screen [*rafraf akhḍar*] covering the horizon."
- **Significance**: Provides the classical-tafsir of v 18. The "green screen" tradition is one of the most-frequently-cited classical glosses on Q 53:18.

#### Bukhārī 4655 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — on Q 53:62 sajda
- **Narrator**: Ibn ʿAbbās (parallel to #1040 in the tafsir chapter)
- **Chain**: Abū Maʿmar → ʿAbd al-Wārith → Ayyūb → ʿIkrima → Ibn ʿAbbās
- **Translation**: "The Prophet performed a prostration when he finished reciting Sūrat al-Najm, and all the Muslims and pagans and Jinns and human beings prostrated along with him."
- **Significance**: ESTABLISHES that the prostration was AT THE END of the recitation (not mid-surah), confirming Q 53:62 — the surah's last verse — as the sajda position.

#### Bukhārī 4656 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — on Q 53 as the FIRST sajda-surah
- **Narrator**: ʿAbdullāh (= Ibn Masʿūd)
- **Chain**: Naṣr b. ʿAlī → Abū Aḥmad → Isrāʾīl → Abū Isḥāq → al-Aswad b. Yazīd → ʿAbdullāh
- **Translation**: "The first sūra in which a prostration was mentioned was Sūrat al-Najm. Allah's Messenger prostrated [while reciting it], and everybody behind him prostrated except a man whom I saw taking a hand-full of dust in his hand and prostrated on it. Later I saw that man killed as a non-believer."
- **Significance**: **CRITICAL CLASSICAL ANCHOR.** This is the explicit Companion-narration that **Q 53 is the FIRST sajda-surah** (i.e., the first surah revealed containing a sajda position; chronological-revelation-order, not mushaf-order). al-Bukhārī's chain via al-Aswad b. Yazīd → Ibn Masʿūd is one of the strongest in the *Sujūd al-Qurʾān* corpus.

### 2.3. Vision-related (Q 53:13-18) hadiths in Bukhārī

#### Bukhārī 4648 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — on Q 53:18 + ʿĀʾisha "did the Prophet see his Lord?"
- **Narrator**: Masrūq (asking ʿĀʾisha)
- **Translation**: "I said to ʿĀʾisha: 'O Mother! Did Prophet Muhammad see his Lord?' ʿĀʾisha said: 'What you have said makes my hair stand on end! Know that if somebody tells you one of the following three things, he is a liar: Whoever tells you that Muhammad saw his Lord, is a liar.' Then ʿĀʾisha recited the verse: 'No vision can grasp Him, but His grasp is over all vision...'" (Q 6:103)
- **Significance**: **THE FOUNDATIONAL ʿĀʾISHA NARRATION.** This is the canonical Sunni-position rejection of the "saw God" reading of Q 53:13-18. ʿĀʾisha's invocation of Q 6:103 (*lā tudrikuhu l-abṣār wa-huwa yudriku l-abṣār*) provides the Quranic-internal Sunni resolution: Q 53:13-18 describes the Prophet's vision of Jibrīl, NOT of Allāh.

#### Bukhārī 3207 ⏳ PENDING (Sidrah classical-isrāʾ anchor)
- Standard isrāʾ + Sidrah narration; the chap 60 anbiyāʾ chapter has multiple isrāʾ-related narrations of which #3207 is one canonical citation in classical literature. Specific Bukhārī # PENDING further direct-verification at the chapter sub-bāb level.

#### Bukhārī 3887 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — but is the standard *isrāʾ* tradition (not specifically Q 53 commentary), narrated via Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ. Provides the *sīra*-level cosmological framework but does not exegete Q 53:13-18 directly.

## 3. Muslim ṣaḥīḥ on Q 53

### 3.1. Sajda al-Najm in Muslim

#### Muslim 1197 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — al-Najm sajda
- **Narrator**: ʿAbdullāh b. ʿUmar (Ibn ʿUmar)
- **Translation**: "The Apostle of Allah recited [Sūrat] al-Najm and performed prostration during its recital, and all those who were along with him also prostrated themselves except one old man who took a handful of pebbles or dust in his palm and lifted it to his forehead and said: 'This is sufficient for me.'"
- **Chapter**: Muslim chap 5 (*kitāb al-masājid wa-mawāḍiʿ al-ṣalāh*)
- **Significance**: Muslim's parallel to Bukhārī 1036/1039/1040, but with Ibn ʿUmar narration instead of Ibn Masʿūd. The Muslim-version explicitly adds the temporal frame *aw turābin* ("or dust") for the old-man's gesture — a slight chain-divergence from Bukhārī's *ḥaṣan aw turābin*.

#### Muslim 1198 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — Zayd b. Thābit no-prostration
- **Narrator**: ʿAṭāʾ b. Yasār → Zayd b. Thābit
- **Translation**: "...he recited 'By the star when it sets' [Q 53:1, *wa-l-najmi idhā hawā*] before the Messenger of Allah, and he did not prostrate himself."
- **Significance**: Muslim's parallel to Bukhārī 1041/1042. Reinforces the counter-narration on prostration-non-obligation.

## 4. Tirmidhī on Q 53

### Tirmidhī 3360 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — Sidrat al-Muntahā
- **Narrator**: ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd
- **Chain**: Ibn Abī ʿUmar → Sufyān → Mālik b. Mighwal → Ṭalḥa b. Muṣarrif → Murra → ʿAbdullāh
- **Translation**: "When the Messenger of Allah reached Sidrat al-Muntahā, He said: 'There terminates everything that ascends from the earth, and everything that descends from above. So there Allah gave him three [things] which He did not give to any Prophet before him: He made five prayers obligatory upon him, He gave him..."
- **Tirmidhī-grade**: *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ* (per al-Tirmidhī's own grading)
- **Significance**: The CLASSICAL ANCHOR for the cosmic-boundary reading of Sidrat al-Muntahā (Q 53:14). The verb-noun *al-muntahā* is glossed as "terminus" — the boundary-point.

### Tirmidhī 3361 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — Two-bow-lengths + Jibrīl 600 wings
- **Narrator**: al-Shaybānī asking Zirr b. Ḥubaysh
- **Chain**: Aḥmad b. Manīʿ → ʿAbbād b. al-ʿAwwām → al-Shaybānī
- **Translation**: "I asked Zirr b. Ḥubaysh about Allah's saying *fa-kāna qāba qawsayni aw adnā* ['And was at a distance of two bow-lengths or less'] (Q 53:9). He said: 'Ibn Masʿūd informed me that the Prophet saw Jibrāʾīl, and he had six-hundred wings.'"
- **Significance**: Establishes the **Jibrīl-not-Allāh** reading of Q 53:9 via Companion-chain (Ibn Masʿūd → Zirr b. Ḥubaysh → al-Shaybānī). Combined with Bukhārī 4648 (ʿĀʾisha) gives the Sunni-mainstream double-Companion confirmation of the Jibrīl reading.

### Tirmidhī 3363 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — Ibn ʿAbbās "Muhammad saw his Lord two times"
- **Narrator**: ʿIkrima → Ibn ʿAbbās
- **Translation**: "Muhammad saw his Lord. I said: 'Did Allah not say [Q 6:103]?' He said: 'Woe to you! That is when He manifests His Light. But Muhammad saw his Lord two times.'"
- **Tirmidhī-grade**: PENDING (al-Tirmidhī's specific grading on this isnād)
- **Adversarial note**: This is the classical Ibn ʿAbbās narration that contradicts ʿĀʾisha's Bukhārī 4648 explicit denial. The Sunni-mainstream resolution (ʿĀʾisha's narration is Companion + Bukhārī ṣaḥīḥ-grade; Ibn ʿAbbās's is also Companion but Tirmidhī is *ḥasan*-grade — Bukhārī typically supersedes Tirmidhī) keeps the Jibrīl-only reading dominant.

### Tirmidhī 3364 ✅ DIRECT-VERIFIED — Q 53:13-14 commentary
- **Narrator**: Abū Salamah → Ibn ʿAbbās (parallel to #3363)
- **Translation**: "Regarding Allah's saying 'And indeed he saw him at a second descent. Near Sidrat al-Muntahā' (Q 53:13-14), and 'So He revealed to His worshipper whatever He revealed' (Q 53:10), and 'And was at a distance of two bow-lengths or less' (Q 53:9), Ibn ʿAbbās said: 'The Prophet saw Him.'"
- **Significance**: Reinforces Tirmidhī 3363 with a different chain. Ibn ʿAbbās's "saw God" reading at the Q 53:13-14 specific verse-citation.

### 6.3. Where the narrative IS attested (and what that attestation is worth)

The *gharānīq* narrative IS attested in:

- **al-Wāqidī, *Maghāzī al-Rasūl* (d. 207/823)**: classed *matrūk* by al-Bukhārī's own *Tārīkh al-kabīr* — i.e., al-Wāqidī himself was rejected as a transmitter by the most authoritative-classical Sunni source on hadith-criticism.
- **Ibn Saʿd, *al-Ṭabaqāt al-Kubrā* (d. 230/845)**: cites al-Wāqidī as source.
- **al-Ṭabarī, *Tārīkh al-Rusul wa-l-Mulūk* (d. 310/923)**: catalogues the narrative in Sīra-history but explicitly notes the *mursal* chain-issues (no Companion-link).
- **al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (tafsir on Q 22:52)**: same — catalogues with chain-issue acknowledgement.
- **al-Bayhaqī, *Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwa* (d. 458/1066)**: preserves with attribution; al-Bayhaqī himself is a 5th-century compiler of *dalāʾil* prophetic-evidence material, not a primary chain-source.
- **al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* (d. 468/1076)**: catalogues as occasion-of-revelation for Q 22:52.

**Critical observation**: ALL of these are *Sīra* (prophetic biography) or *asbāb al-nuzūl* (occasion-of-revelation) literature, NOT canonical hadith collections. The Sīra-tradition has lower isnād-rigour than the canonical-hadith-tradition by deliberate scholarly convention (al-Ṭabarī himself notes this distinction in his *Tārīkh* preface). The *gharānīq* narrative is **literary-historical** in genre, not **canonical-hadith**.

### 6.4. Classical refutation literature

- **al-Albānī, *Naṣb al-Majānīq li-Nasf Qiṣṣat al-Gharānīq* (Damascus, 1952)**: comprehensive isnād-by-isnād critique demonstrating ALL chains are *mursal* or weakly/severely-weak; al-Bayhaqī's chain has a *mudallis* (Ibn Isḥāq); al-Ṭabarī's chain has *mursal* breaks; the strongest version (Saʿīd b.
