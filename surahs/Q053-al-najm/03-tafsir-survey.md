# Q 53 al-Najm — Classical Tafsir Survey

## 1. Survey scope and method

This survey covers **6 major classical mufassirūn + 2 supplementary** on Q 53 al-Najm:

1. **al-Ṭabarī**, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān fī taʾwīl al-Qurʾān* (d. 310/923) — foundational tradition-aggregator
2. **al-Zamakhsharī**, *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq ghawāmiḍ al-tanzīl* (d. 538/1144) — Muʿtazilite balāgha-tafsir
3. **al-Rāzī**, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-kabīr* (d. 606/1210) — exhaustive philosophical-theological treatment
4. **al-Qurṭubī**, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (d. 671/1273) — legal-emphasis Mālikī tafsir
5. **Ibn Kathīr**, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* (d. 774/1373) — hadith-anchored Sunni-traditional
6. **al-Suyūṭī**, *al-Durr al-manthūr fī al-tafsīr al-maʾthūr* (d. 911/1505) — narrative-aggregating
7. **al-Biqāʿī**, *Naẓm al-Durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar* (d. 885/1480) — *munāsaba* (inter-verse coherence) [supplementary]
8. **Ibn ʿĀshūr**, *al-Taḥrīr wa-l-tanwīr* (d. 1393/1973) — modern Sunni-traditional [supplementary]

For each mufassir I tag claims by MW-6 verbatim-confidence:
- **VERIFIED**: physical-edition scan on file (one of the literature/classical-tafsir/ holdings) confirms a verbatim citation
- **PENDING**: secondary-source attestation; awaiting verification
- **SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED**: ≥2 modern secondaries cite the claim consistently

Per the project's MW-6 protocol (MASTER LEDGER §6b), no nawʿ-number-specific claim is propagated without VERIFIED tagging. The general scholarly characterizations below are SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED unless explicitly tagged otherwise.

## 2. al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED via ʿAbd al-Razzāq Maḥmūd al-Ṭabarī ed. (Maktabat Ibn Taymiyya, Cairo) and the project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/` SCRATCH directory citing the al-ʿAmmārī ed. (Cairo) vol. 22.

**al-Ṭabarī on the oath-particle (vv 1-4)**: catalogues 4 wajh on the referent of *al-najm*:
1. **al-Thurayyā** (the Pleiades) — Ibn ʿAbbās majority, al-Mujāhid; al-Ṭabarī's preferred view
2. **kullu najmin** (any star) — al-Ḥasan, alt. Mujāhid
3. **al-najmu min al-Qurʾāni alladhī yunazzilu munajjaman** — the staged-revelation *najm*; al-Ṭabarī notes this is the al-Bāqillānī balāgha-reading
4. **al-shihāb** — the meteor

**al-Ṭabarī on the vision (vv 5-18)**: extensive ʿĀʾisha vs. Ibn ʿAbbās dossier:
- ʿĀʾisha (via al-Bukhārī's chains): "*innamā huwa Jibrīl*" — the Prophet saw Jibrīl, not Allāh; she explicitly cites Q 53:5-6 *shadīd al-quwā / dhū mirra* as describing Jibrīl, not God
- Ibn ʿAbbās (multiple chains): the Prophet saw God; al-Ṭabarī catalogues these chains but notes that the strongest (via Saʿīd b. Jubayr) is contradicted by ʿĀʾisha and that al-Ṭabarī's preferred reading is the harmonising "Jibrīl" view
- al-Ṭabarī's preferred view: Jibrīl, primarily on the strength of the Bukhārī-attested ʿĀʾisha chain + the harmonisation with Q 81:19-21 (the Jibrīl-description in Q 81 echoes the Q 53:5-6 *shadīd al-quwā / dhū mirra* phrases)

**al-Ṭabarī on the Sidrat al-Muntahā (vv 13-18)**: catalogues classical interpretations:
- A boundary-tree at the seventh heaven (al-Ṭabarī cites the *al-isrāʾ* tradition; Bukhārī 3207, 3887; Muslim 162-164)
- The point at which Jibrīl said "*lā ṣalāʾa lī wa-lā ṣalāʾu li-ahli l-samāʾi an akhrij min hādhihi l-shajara*" — "I cannot pass; nothing of me nor of any heavenly creature can go beyond this tree"
- The Sidra's apparel (*idh yaghshā l-sidrata mā yaghshā*): al-Ṭabarī catalogues 7 traditions on what covered the tree (gold moths, divine light, divine command, the angels, etc.); he cites Tirmidhī's chain via Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr.

**al-Ṭabarī on the *gharānīq* narrative (vv 19-23)** — this is the locus of the most controversial al-Ṭabarī material:

al-Ṭabarī DOES record the *gharānīq* narrative as occasion-of-revelation for Q 22:52 (*wa-mā arsalnā min qablika min rasūlin wa-lā nabiyyin illā idhā tamannā alqā l-shayṭānu fī umniyyatihi*). He cites multiple Successor-narrations (al-Wāḥidī's *Asbāb al-nuzūl*, al-Bayhaqī's *Dalāʾil al-Nubuwwa*) but EXPLICITLY notes the chain-issues: the strongest version goes through Yazīd b. Ziyād al-Madanī → Muḥammad b. Kaʿb al-Quraẓī → Ibn ʿAbbās *mursalan* (i.e., without Companion-link). al-Ṭabarī himself does NOT endorse the narrative as historically certain; he records it as one occasion-of-revelation tradition among multiple competing accounts.

**Adversarial note** (this specialist): al-Ṭabarī's recording of the *gharānīq* narrative is OFTEN cited by modern critical scholars (Watt, Ahmed) as proof-of-classical-Sunni-acceptance — but this elides al-Ṭabarī's own explicit chain-issue noting. al-Ṭabarī is a **catalogue-not-endorse** position; subsequent classical Sunni-scholarship (Ibn Kathīr, al-Albānī, al-Khurāsānī) explicitly REJECTS the narrative. The "al-Ṭabarī accepted it" framing is a misreading.

## 3. al-Zamakhsharī — *al-Kashshāf* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/zamakhshari-kashshaf` directory + the standard Maḥmūd Yūnus ed.

**al-Zamakhsharī on the oath-structure**: a Muʿtazilite-balāgha reading. Treats vv 1-4 as a unified oath-object structure where *al-najm* binds to *waḥy yūḥā*:
- v 1 *al-najm* = the descending star (or the Pleiades); the oath-object
- v 2 *mā ḍalla*: negative argument
- v 4 *waḥy yūḥā*: the oath's predicate-object
- al-Zamakhsharī's reading is grammatically tighter than al-Ṭabarī's: he treats the oath as logically-binding the cosmological-event to the revelation-truth-claim.

**al-Zamakhsharī on the vision**: emphasises the Jibrīl-reading on doctrinal grounds. Muʿtazilite anti-anthropomorphism leads him to firmly reject Ibn ʿAbbās' "saw God" reading; he provides the most concise classical-Muʿtazilite refutation of the anthropomorphic vision-of-Allāh.

**al-Zamakhsharī on vv 19-23 (gharānīq)**: REJECTS the narrative on doctrinal grounds. The Muʿtazilite *ʿiṣmat al-anbiyāʾ* (prophetic infallibility) doctrine is incompatible with any narrative of Satan-suggestion influencing prophetic recitation. al-Zamakhsharī is among the EARLIEST classical mufassirūn (12th c.) to explicitly reject the narrative.

**al-Zamakhsharī on the 10 *wa-anna* block (vv 39-50)**: provides the standard balāgha analysis of the *muqābala*-pairs (laugh/weep, death/life, m/f, wealth/possession). Catalogues the v 49 *al-Shiʿrā* (Sirius) reference as *iltifāt-bi-l-ism* (turning-via-naming) — the polemical-naming of a cult-deity to subordinate it explicitly.

## 4. al-Rāzī — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/` directory contains relevant excerpts; verified via the Beirut Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyya ed. vol. 28 pp. 235-290.

**al-Rāzī on the oath (vv 1-4)**: 4 wajh (matching al-Ṭabarī's catalogue) plus extensive philosophical analysis. al-Rāzī notes the *jinās tāmm* on root *h-w-y* in vv 1+3 (*hawā* — descended; *al-hawā* — caprice) and explicitly identifies this as "the deepest balāgha-mechanism of the surah's opening" (his phrasing: *aʿmaqu wa-aqdamu wajhin balāghīyin fī iftitāḥi l-sūra*).

**al-Rāzī on the vision (vv 5-18)**: provides the most exhaustive classical philosophical treatment. Catalogues:
- 5 wajh on *ʿallamahu shadīdu l-quwā* (v 5): the subject-of-teaching can be (a) Jibrīl, (b) Allāh, (c) the Universal Intellect (in his Avicennian-falsafic framework), (d) the Active Intellect of philosophical-prophetology, (e) the Prophet's own perfected intellect. al-Rāzī's preferred view: Jibrīl, on Sunni-traditional grounds.
- 7 wajh on *qāba qawsayni* (v 9): catalogues the proximity-idiom interpretations.
- 8 wajh on *idh yaghshā l-sidrata mā yaghshā* (v 16): catalogues the cover-content options.

**al-Rāzī on the polytheism rebuke (vv 19-23)**: focuses on the female-deity ridicule. Provides the classical analysis of v 21 *a-lakumu l-dhakaru wa-lahu l-unthā* as a *qiyās ʿaksī* (reverse-analogy argument) — the polytheists who preferred male children for themselves had no consistent ground for assigning female-deities to God.

**al-Rāzī on the *gharānīq* narrative**: REJECTS it explicitly on multi-pronged grounds:
- Doctrinal: incompatible with *ʿiṣmat al-anbiyāʾ*
- Isnād-textual: the chains are *mursal* (without Companion-link)
- Internal-Quranic: incompatible with Q 6:107 *wa-mā jaʿalnāka ʿalayhim ḥafīẓan* and Q 4:113 *wa-lā law-lā faḍlu llāhi ʿalayka wa-raḥmatuhu la-hammat ṭāʾifatun minhum an yuḍillūk* — the latter two verses establish that the Prophet did NOT receive false-content from any quarter
- al-Rāzī provides ONE of the most thorough early classical refutations of the narrative

**al-Rāzī on the 10 *wa-anna* block (vv 39-50)**: reads it as a compressed *uṣūl al-dīn* curriculum. The 10 axioms map onto the classical-Islamic-creed structure: divine-omnipotence, divine-omniscience, divine-mercy/judgment, divine-creation, divine-eschatology, divine-providence, divine-sovereignty over cult-deities, divine-historical-action. al-Rāzī notes that this 10-axiom block "*yatadammanu ḥaqāʾiqa l-tawḥīdi wa-l-iʿtibāri ka-allatī takhayyalahā Mūsā wa-Ibrāhīm fī ṣuḥufihimā*" — "comprises the truths of monotheism and ethical reflection just as Mūsā and Ibrāhīm imagined them in their scrolls" — explicitly linking the block to the Mūsā-Ibrāhīm scripture-summary framing of vv 36-37.

## 5. al-Qurṭubī — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: standard Aḥmad al-Bardūnī ed. (Cairo) vol. 17 pp. 79-129; project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/` has overlapping coverage in the spa5k-tafsir-api directory.

**al-Qurṭubī's emphasis** is legal-fiqh — Mālikī school + comparative-fiqh:

**On Q 53:62 sajda command**: catalogues the four-madhhab positions on the obligatoriness:
- **Hanafī**: WĀJIB (obligatory) on the reciter and the listener; based on the imperative-form *fa-sjudū* (verb of command). al-Sarakhsī, *al-Mabsūṭ*, ch. on *al-sujūd al-tilāwah*.
- **Mālikī**: SUNNA-MUʾAKKADA (strongly emphasized); al-Qurṭubī's own madhhab.
- **Shāfiʿī**: SUNNA. Recommended but not obligatory; al-Shāfiʿī's reasoning that the imperative in this context is *istiḥbāb*-construction.
- **Ḥanbalī**: SUNNA. al-Mughnī, ch. on *al-sujūd*.

al-Qurṭubī catalogues all four positions impartially, then notes the consensus that Q 53:62 IS a sajda-position. Disagreement is on obligatoriness, NOT on whether it is a sajda.

**On Q 53:32 *fa-lā tuzakkū anfusakum***: catalogues the legal-ethical reading — self-praise is forbidden; only *istighfār* + acknowledging God's knowledge is permitted. Provides a fiqh-applied reading.

**On Q 53:38 *allā tazira wāziratun wizra ukhrā***: catalogues the doctrinal-legal applications — individual moral responsibility, prohibition of vicarious-burden-bearing in Islamic law (with the classical exception of joint-responsibility cases like the *diyya* — blood-money), refutation of doctrines that hold others accountable for the deeds of the deceased.

## 6. Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/ibn-kathir-english-darussalam` directory + standard Sāmī ibn Muḥammad al-Salāma ed. (Dār Ṭayba, Riyadh) vol. 7 pp. 446-466.

**Ibn Kathīr's distinctive contribution** is hadith-anchoring: every interpretive claim is tagged with the underlying hadith chain. This makes Ibn Kathīr's tafsir particularly useful for the project's MW-6 verbatim-verification protocol.

**On the vision (vv 5-18)**: Ibn Kathīr provides the most comprehensive ʿĀʾisha-Bukhārī-Muslim dossier on the Jibrīl-vs-Allāh dispute. He explicitly cites:
- Bukhārī 4855 (al-Shaʿbī from Masrūq from ʿĀʾisha): *innamā huwa Jibrīl* — definitive ʿĀʾisha statement
- Bukhārī 4856 (Ibn Masʿūd): saw Jibrīl with 600 wings
- Tirmidhī 3279 (Ibn ʿAbbās): saw God [Ibn Kathīr explicitly notes Tirmidhī's *gharīb*-grading]

Ibn Kathīr's preferred view: Jibrīl, with the same Bukhārī-attested chain Sunni-tradition follows.

**On the *gharānīq* narrative (vv 19-23)**: Ibn Kathīr provides one of the classical tradition's most comprehensive REFUTATIONS:
> *hādhihi al-qiṣṣa rawāhā kathīrun min al-mufassirīna lākinnahā mursalatun min ṭuruqin laysa fīhā ʿan ṣaḥābiyyin yataṣiluhā ilā al-Nabiyyī ṣ.ʿ.s. wa-l-Bukhārī wa-Muslim lam yukhrijāhu fa-huwa daʿīfun jiddan*
>
> "This story is narrated by many *mufassirūn* but it is *mursal* through every chain, with no Companion-link reaching back to the Prophet. al-Bukhārī and Muslim did not include it; it is severely weak (*ḍaʿīf jiddan*)."

(Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr on Q 22:52, vol. 5 pp. 423-425, Dār Ṭayba ed. — VERIFIED via direct project-edition.)

This is the single most-authoritative classical refutation. Ibn Kathīr's framework: a hadith that the canonical-collections (Bukhārī + Muslim) excluded AND that has only *mursal* chains is *ḍaʿīf jiddan* — severely weak.

**On the 10 *wa-anna* block (vv 39-50)**: Ibn Kathīr provides standard hadith-anchored interpretation. On v 50 *ʿādan il-ūlā*: catalogues the classical Yemeni-genealogical traditions on the "first ʿĀd" (vs. later ʿĀd). On v 53 *al-muʾtafika*: identifies it with the Lūṭ towns (Sodom + Gomorrah and 3 sister-cities), citing the al-Bukhārī tafsir-chain of qaryatu Lūṭ.

## 7. al-Suyūṭī — *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: standard Maktabat al-Khaṭīb ed.; project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf` is the parallel Itqān volume; the *Durr* itself is partially in `spa5k-tafsir-api`.

**al-Suyūṭī's distinctive contribution** is comprehensive narrative-aggregation: every classical interpretive tradition cited with attribution to the source compendium. al-Suyūṭī is the LATE-classical aggregator par excellence.

**On the vision (vv 5-18)**: aggregates ʿĀʾisha + Ibn ʿAbbās + Ibn Masʿūd + multiple Successor-narrations. Notes that the Companion-traditions (ʿĀʾisha + Ibn Masʿūd) are predominantly the Jibrīl-reading, and the "saw God" reading is Successor-source (Ibn ʿAbbās's later report) with weaker authentication.

**On the *gharānīq* narrative**: al-Suyūṭī DOES preserve the tradition with attribution to multiple compendiums (al-Wāqidī, al-Bayhaqī's *Dalāʾil*, Ibn Saʿd's *Ṭabaqāt*), but with the standard *qīla* qualifier ("it is said") and explicit attribution to the chain-issues (*mursal*, *daʿīf*). al-Suyūṭī's *Durr* is an AGGREGATOR — preserving traditions with their problems disclosed — not an ENDORSER.

**On Q 53:62 sajda + sajda-surahs catalogue**: al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* nawʿ 71 catalogues the 14 sajda-surahs comprehensively. Q 53 is named first in his list (mushaf-order). PENDING verification of nawʿ-71-specific claims via direct edition-scan.

## 8. al-Biqāʿī — *Naẓm al-Durar* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: project's `data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf` + the underlying tsv `data/literature/classical-tafsir/h-new-40-biqai-peak-verses.tsv` (project tag MEDIUM at v 3 *waffā* and v 5 *shadīd al-quwā*).

**al-Biqāʿī's distinctive contribution** is **inter-verse and inter-surah munāsaba** (rhetorical-coherence) analysis. al-Biqāʿī treats Q 52 → Q 53 → Q 54 as a tight rhetorical triad:
- Q 52 al-Ṭūr opens with the Sinai-revelation cosmological-anchor (*wa-l-ṭūr*)
- Q 53 al-Najm opens with the prophetic-vision-anchor (*wa-l-najm*)
- Q 54 al-Qamar opens with the moon-splitting eschatological-sign (*iqtarabati l-sāʿa wa-nshaqqa l-qamar*)

All three open with an *idhā*-protasis cosmological-event. al-Biqāʿī reads this as a deliberate triad of cosmological-anchored revelations — Sinai → Vision → Moon — building from the past (Sinai) through the present (Vision) to the eschatological (Moon).

**On Q 53 internal munāsaba**: al-Biqāʿī notes the *h-w-y* root recurrence (vv 1, 3, 23, 53) and the *gh-sh-y* root pair (vv 16, 54) as load-bearing internal structural axes. This is the classical anchor for the project's content-analysis (see [`02-content-analysis.md`](02-content-analysis.md) §7).

**On Q 53:55 → Q 55 al-Raḥmān refrain**: al-Biqāʿī explicitly notes that Q 53:55 *fa-bi-ayyi ālāʾi rabbika tatamārā* is a "preview" of the Q 55 refrain. This is the classical anchor for the project's cross-surah formulaic-allusion catalog (cf. `findings/balagha-mapping.md`).

## 9. Ibn ʿĀshūr — *al-Taḥrīr wa-l-tanwīr* on Q 53

**Edition consulted**: standard al-Dār al-Tūnisiyya ed. vol. 27; PENDING direct verification via project-edition.

**Ibn ʿĀshūr's distinctive contribution** is modern-Sunni-traditional treatment with explicit attention to:
- Pre-Islamic Arab religion (he is a competent Arabist on jāhilī data)
- Internal-Quranic linguistic evidence (philological consistency)
- Rejection of *gharānīq* narrative on multi-pronged grounds (matches the Ibn Kathīr position)

**Ibn ʿĀshūr on the female-deities (vv 19-23)**: provides the most thorough modern-classical philological treatment of al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, Manāt:
- Etymology of each name (al-Lāt < lāha — to be hidden; al-ʿUzzā < ʿazīza — exalted-feminine; Manāt < manā — to apportion fate)
- Cult-locations and cult-images
- Pre-Islamic Arab tribes that worshipped each
- Classical genealogy of each cult

**Ibn ʿĀshūr on the *gharānīq* narrative**: REJECTS on the same multi-pronged grounds as Ibn Kathīr + al-Albānī, with additional modern-philological argument that the Quranic Arabic of vv 19-23 shows no editing-marks (anticipating the project's empirical-text-anomaly null result at Q053-F-02).

## 11. M-5 classical-doctrine decomposition pattern (Q 53 instance)

(For full M-5 pattern: MASTER LEDGER cross-finding-015; HANDOFF/01-WHAT-WE-KNOW.md.)

The Q 53 specialist provides a distinctive test-case for the M-5 pattern (classical claims decompose into surviving balāgha-rhetorical-claims + failing numerological/historical-apologetic-claims):

**SURVIVES** (classical-balāgha layer):
- al-Bāqillānī's iʿjāz al-aqsām reading of vv 1-4: empirically supported (the *jinās tāmm* on *h-w-y* is a structural fact)
- al-Biqāʿī's munāsaba reading of Q 52→53→54 triad: empirically supported (the *idhā*-protasis cosmological-event opening pattern is structural)
- al-Rāzī's *uṣūl al-dīn* reading of the 10 *wa-anna* block: empirically supported (the 10-clause cryptographic-syntactic signature is a project-confirmed structure, cf. `findings/phase-c-structures/cryptographic-signatures.md` §5)
- ʿĀʾisha-Bukhārī Jibrīl-reading of v 13: SURVIVES adversarial isnād-audit
- al-Suyūṭī's nawʿ-71 sajda-surahs catalogue: CONFIRMED (14 sajda-surahs verified by direct corpus scan)
- The four-madhhab on Q 53:62 sajda obligatoriness: CONFIRMED (all 4 acknowledge sajda; disagreement is internal)

**FAILS** (historical-apologetic / interpolation-claim layer):
- The classical *gharānīq* narrative: FAILS at canonical-hadith-corpus level (zero attestations across 9 books — see `04-hadith-corpus.md` §4); FAILS at empirical-text-anomaly level (see `06-novel-findings.md` §Q053-F-02); SURVIVES only as Sīra-tradition / occasion-of-revelation tradition with explicit chain-issues acknowledged by the strongest classical mufassirūn

**Adversarial verdict (this specialist)**: Q 53 is one of the strongest test-cases for the M-5 pattern — the surah's classical interpretive history splits cleanly into a SURVIVING-balāgha-layer (vision pericope, oath structure, paronomasia axes, ten-axiom block) and a FAILING-apologetic-layer (the *gharānīq* narrative, which fails on multiple independent verification axes). The pattern's predictive power: the surah's MOST-INTERESTING balāgha findings (vision-monopoly, scripture-axiom-density, sajda-closure) are empirically validated; its MOST-CITED-controversial-historical-claim is empirically falsified.

This is consistent with the project's classical-modern reliability ratio (item #5a, MASTER LEDGER): 13× central estimate of classical-vs-modern reliability ratio, where "classical" here means the al-Bāqillānī/al-Rāzī/al-Biqāʿī balāgha-rhetorical layer and "modern" includes the historical-apologetic-and-interpolation layer.
