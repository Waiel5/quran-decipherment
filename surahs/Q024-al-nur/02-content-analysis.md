## 1. The eleven thematic blocks

Q 24 is structured as a sequence of eleven distinct legal-discursive blocks, each closing with a fāṣila signature (typically Allāh + an attribute pair) and opening on a new legal or didactic concern. Block boundaries are stable across classical chapter-divisions (al-Qurṭubī's masāʾil divisions; al-Biqāʿī's tanāsub markers).

| B | 2-3 | zinā punishment | ḥadd-criminal | ḥurrima dhālika ʿalā al-muʾminīn |

The 11-block thematic count above is coarser than al-Qurṭubī's question-level decomposition; both decompositions agree on the al-ifk passage being a single coherent unit (vv. 11-20).

## 2. Block A: meta-textual opening (v. 1)

> سورة أنزلناها وفرضناها وأنزلنا فيها آيات بينات لعلكم تذكرون

"A surah which We sent down and made obligatory and sent down in it clear signs that you may remember."

**Notable**: Q 24 is the only surah in the Quran whose opening verse self-references as *sūratun anzalnāhā wa-faraḍnāhā* — meta-textual self-naming. Q 47 (Muḥammad) names the Prophet but not "this is a surah"; Q 17 al-Isrāʾ names an event but not "this surah." Q 24 is the unique self-announcing surah, and it announces itself as *farḍ* (obligatory) — the only such self-marker in the corpus.

Per al-Qurṭubī (`qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt` Q24 opening): "the purpose of this surah is the rules of chastity and covering" (مقصود هذه السورة ذكر أحكام العفاف والستر).

The variant reading *faraḍnāhā* with shadda on the rāʾ — "we made many obligations in it" rather than "we made it obligatory" — is reported by Abū ʿAmr (al-Qurṭubī, ibid.). Both readings are tashkeel-fragile but lemma-stable.

## 3. Block B: zinā punishment (vv. 2-3)

v. 2 sets the *ḥadd* of fornication: 100 lashes, in public, with no pity *fī dīn-i-llāh*.

v. 3 specifies: a fornicator does not marry except a fornicatress or a polytheist, and vice versa. The exegetical question — is this descriptive or prescriptive? — is the source of one of Q 24's earliest legal disputes (al-Ṭabarī §6, al-Qurṭubī Q24:3 masʾala 1). The classical majority (Mālik, al-Shāfiʿī, Abū Ḥanīfa) reads it as descriptive of typical conduct, not as a binding marriage prohibition.

## 4. Block C: qadhf — false accusation of fornication (vv. 4-5)

v. 4: those who accuse chaste women without four witnesses receive 80 lashes and lose their testimony status forever.
v. 5: exception — those who repent and reform; Allāh is forgiving, merciful.

This block is the legal precondition for blocks D (liʿān) and E (al-ifk). The threshold of *four witnesses* is unique among Quranic ḥadd-legislation; the only other place the four-witness requirement appears is at Q 4:15 (where ʿiddat-al-bayt confinement is ruled, prior to abrogation). Q 24:4 is the canonical fixed-witness text.

## 5. Block D: liʿān — mutual cursing for spousal accusation (vv. 6-10)

When a husband accuses his own wife of zinā with no witness besides himself, he swears four oaths by Allāh that he is truthful and a fifth that Allāh's curse fall on him if he is lying (vv. 6-7). The wife averts the punishment by swearing the parallel four+one-curse-on-self oath (vv. 8-9). v. 10 closes: "and were it not for Allāh's grace upon you and His mercy and that Allāh is the accepter-of-repentance, the Wise…" (a sentence-fragment that is itself a fāṣila pattern; see §15 below).

**Asbāb al-nuzūl**: Bukhārī #2564 and #4541 transmit ʿIkrima from Ibn ʿAbbās: Hilāl b. Umayya accused his wife of zinā with Sharīk b. Saḥmāʾ. The Prophet demanded the four witnesses or a flogging (under the Q 24:4 standard from the previous block). Hilāl said: "If one of us saw a man with his wife, would he go fetch witnesses?" The liʿān provision was then revealed. The asbāb is a direct reading of why Q 24:6-9 follows Q 24:4 — these are the case-of-no-witness amendment to the four-witness rule.

## 6. Block E: al-ifk narrative (vv. 11-20)

The longest single block of Q 24 (10 verses, 144 words by no-tashkeel-orthographic count). The al-ifk ("the slander") incident defends ʿĀʾisha after she was accused of misconduct on the return from the Banū al-Muṣṭaliq expedition. Classical asbāb (Bukhārī #4544; Muslim Ṣaḥīḥ Kitāb al-tawba bāb al-ifk; Abū Dāwūd #4474; pre-extracted in `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt` at the Q24:11 marker; see also `04-hadith-corpus.md`).

The verses do NOT name ʿĀʾisha or the slanderers. The Quranic structure works at the level of moral-juridical principle:

- v. 11: the spreaders of *al-ifk* are a faction (ʿuṣba) from among you; do not think it evil — it is good for you (the closing of the ifk-event was Quranic public exoneration, hence "good"); each of them bears what he earned of sin; the chief of them has a great punishment.
- v. 12: when you heard it, why did the believing men and women not assume good of themselves and say "this is a manifest slander"?
- v. 13: why did they not bring four witnesses? if they did not, they are liars before Allāh.
- v. 14: were it not for Allāh's grace and mercy in this world and the next, you would have suffered great punishment for what you plunged into.
- v. 15: when you received it on your tongues and said with your mouths what you had no knowledge of, and you thought it light when before Allāh it is grave.
- v. 16: and why, when you heard it, did you not say "it is not for us to speak of this; glory be to Thee, this is a great calumny"?
- v. 17: Allāh admonishes you not to return to the like of it ever, if you are believers.
- v. 18: and Allāh makes the signs clear; Allāh is knowing, wise.
- v. 19: those who love that obscene-talk should spread among the believers — for them is a painful punishment in this world and the next; Allāh knows, you know not.
- v. 20: were it not for Allāh's grace and mercy and that Allāh is kind, merciful…

**Structural feature**: vv. 14, 16, 20 each begin with *wa-law-lā* ("were it not"); vv. 12 and 16 are conditional-retrospective ("when you heard"). The al-ifk passage is the longest sustained *wa-law-lā / law-lā* anaphora in the Quran — five conditional-retrospective markers in 10 verses, each functioning to retroactively constitute the moral "what should have happened" before naming the historical "what did happen." This is a non-trivial rhetorical-architectural feature; see `06-novel-findings.md` Q024-F-03 for empirical cohesion-test.

The empirical cohesion of vv. 11-20 places the al-ifk passage at the **85.2nd percentile** among 1,860 random 5-10-verse intra-surah spans (mean pairwise root-Jaccard = 0.0782 vs corpus mean 0.0483). It is genuinely tighter than typical Quranic narrative blocks. See `06-novel-findings.md` Q024-F-03.

## 7. Block F: warnings against fawāḥish (vv. 21-26)

v. 21: do not follow Satan's footsteps; Satan commands obscenity (*fa-ḥshāʾ*) and wrong (*munkar*).
v. 22: those of *faḍl-and-saʿa* (graced and well-off) should not refrain from giving to the near-of-kin and the muhājirūn — let them pardon and overlook. Asbāb: Abū Bakr's vow to stop maintaining Misṭaḥ b. Uthātha (a participant in the al-ifk slander), recanted on this verse (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī Q24:22).
v. 23: those who slander chaste, careless believing women are cursed in this world and the next.
v. 24-25: a Day when their tongues, hands, feet will testify against them; Allāh will pay them in full their just deserts.
v. 26: "evil women for evil men, evil men for evil women; good women for good men, good men for good women — these are exonerated of what they say." This is the Quranic moral-cosmic resolution of al-ifk: ʿĀʾisha (good) cannot belong to the slanderers' world (evil).

## 8. Block G: home-entry permissions (vv. 27-29)

v. 27: do not enter houses other than your own until you ask permission and greet the people of them.
v. 28: if no one is in them, do not enter until permission is given; if told to return, return.
v. 29: no fault on you to enter uninhabited houses where you have property.

This block stands as the etiquette-prelude to the gaze-modesty block. The asbāb (al-Qurṭubī Q24:27) connect it to a Companion's complaint about people entering before being announced; the verse establishes the *istʾidhān* (permission-asking) discipline as a pre-condition of the *istiʾfāf* (chastity) discipline that follows.

## 9. Block H: gaze-modesty + ḥijāb (vv. 30-31)

The corpus's primary hijāb-and-gaze legislation:

v. 30 (men): "Tell believing men to lower their gaze and guard their private parts; that is purer for them."
v. 31 (women, the longest verse in Q 24 at 78 words): "And tell believing women to lower their gaze and guard their private parts and not display their adornment except what appears of it; and let them strike with their *khumur* (head-coverings) over their *juyūb* (bosoms/openings); and not display their adornment except to: their husbands, fathers, husbands' fathers, sons, husbands' sons, brothers, brothers' sons, sisters' sons, women, what their right hands possess, the unmarriagable male attendants, children unaware of women's nakedness; and let them not stamp their feet to make their hidden adornment known; and turn to Allāh together, O believers, that you may succeed."

**Lexical signature**: Q 24:30-31 uses the roots *gḍḍ* (lower the gaze, 2 occurrences), *ḥfẓ* (guard, 2), *zyn* (adornment, 3), *frj* (private parts, 2), *khmr* (cover/khimar, 1), *jyb* (opening, 1) — none of which appear in Q 33:53-59 (the other classical hijāb passage). The two "hijāb passages" of the Quran share no verse-pair-defining vocabulary; only structural roots (Allāh, ahl, qawl, byt, gyr, ʿlm, etc.) overlap. See `06-novel-findings.md` Q024-F-04 and `05-classical-claims-audit.md` audit-4 for the empirical comparison.

## 10. Block I: marriage of singles + emancipation (vv. 32-33)

v. 32: marry off the singles among you and the righteous of your slaves; if poor, Allāh will enrich them from His grace.
v. 33: those who do not find marriage should remain chaste until Allāh enriches them; the slave who seeks the *kitāba* (manumission contract) — write it for them if you know good in them and give them from Allāh's wealth; and do not coerce your slave-girls into prostitution.

Classical jurists (al-Qurṭubī Q24:33 masāʾil) read v. 33 as the foundational text on slave-emancipation: the *mukātib* (emancipation-contract holder) has a Quranic right to be granted the contract.

## 11. Block J: the Light-verse + cosmic signs (vv. 34-46)

The thirteen-verse theological core. v. 34 transitions: "we have sent down clear signs and an example from those who passed before, and a warning for the Allāh-fearing." Then v. 35 — āyat al-nūr.

**v. 35** (the Light-verse, 48 no-tashkeel-orthographic words):

> الله نور السماوات والأرض مثل نوره كمشكاة فيها مصباح المصباح في زجاجة الزجاجة كأنها كوكب دري يوقد من شجرة مباركة زيتونة لا شرقية ولا غربية يكاد زيتها يضيء ولو لم تمسسه نار نور على نور يهدي الله لنوره من يشاء ويضرب الله الأمثال للناس والله بكل شيء عليم

**Cross-tashkeel cross-validation** (verbatim from `quran-no-tashkeel.json` v35; identical word-count of 48 in `quran-min-tashkeel.json` and `quran-full-tashkeel.json`). The verse's word count is rules-tuple stable.

**Internal architecture** (al-Biqāʿī, raw raw extract Q24:35; *Mishkāt al-Anwār* of al-Ghazālī, classical companion commentary):
- 35a: theological assertion — Allāh is the light of heavens and earth.
- 35b: parable — His light is like a niche containing a lamp, the lamp in a glass, the glass like a brilliant star.
- 35c: source — kindled from a blessed olive tree, neither east nor west, whose oil would almost illuminate without fire.
- 35d: refrain — *nūrun ʿalā nūr* (light upon light).
- 35e: theology — Allāh guides whom He wills to His light; Allāh sets parables for people; Allāh knows all.

The verse is itself a five-part chiasm (al-Biqāʿī ring-structure tradition; see `data/literature/misc/114chambers-ayat-al-nur-ring-composition.md` for a modern 11-section ring reading; the classical 5-section reading is more conservative). Whatever the chosen ring-count, all classical readers identify *nūrun ʿalā nūr* as the structural pivot.

**Structural midpoint claim**: this single verse contains both the median word and the median letter of Q 24 as a whole. Word-count midpoint = 659.5 (within v. 35's word-span 622-669); letter-midpoint = 2,877 (within v. 35's letter-span 2,787-2,989). See `01-empirical-profile.md` §9 and `06-novel-findings.md` Q024-F-03.

vv. 36-38 — the *masjid* setting: in houses Allāh has permitted to be raised, His name remembered there, glorified at morning and evening, by men whom no commerce or trade distracts from remembrance of Allāh.

vv. 39-40 — the foil parables: kāfir deeds are like a mirage in a plain, or like darknesses in a deep sea with waves above waves and clouds above — *ẓulumātun baʿḍuhā fawqa baʿḍ*. The pivotal sentence: *wa-man lam yajʿal Allāhu lahu nūran fa-mā lahu min nūr* — "whom Allāh has not given a light to, has no light." This is the chiasmic counterweight to v. 35.

vv. 41-46 — cosmic signs: birds praising Allāh, clouds and lightning and water, animals walking on bellies / two legs / four. Empirical observation: this is the Quran's most concentrated passage of natural-phenomenon-signs in a Medinan-legal surah; it is the cosmic-doxology insertion that gives Q 24 its non-legal middle.

## 12. Block K: hypocrite vs. believer (vv. 47-57)

vv. 47-50: the hypocrites who claim faith but turn away when called to Allāh and His messenger for judgment.
vv. 51-52: the true-believer formula — "we hear and we obey" (samiʿnā wa-aṭaʿnā) — these are the successful.
vv. 53-54: do not swear false oaths of full obedience; obey Allāh and the Messenger.
v. 55: the istikhlāf-promise — Allāh promises those who believe and do good that He will succeed them in the earth as He succeeded those before, and establish their religion which He has approved for them, and exchange their fear for security; serving Me, associating nothing with Me.
v. 56: establish prayer, give zakāt, obey the Messenger.
v. 57: do not think the disbelievers escape on earth; their abode is the Fire — *wa-la-bīʾsa al-maṣīr* (a strong eschatological closer).

## 13. Block L: home-entry permissions, second tier (vv. 58-61)

v. 58: those of your household — slaves and pre-pubescent children — must seek permission three times: before fajr, at noon when you remove garments, and after ʿishāʾ. These are *thalāthu ʿawrātin lakum* — "three private times for you."
v. 59: when children reach puberty, they ask permission as the elder do.
v. 60: post-menopausal women may put aside their outer garments without exposing adornment, though refraining is better.
v. 61: a 24-element list of houses one may eat in without restriction (parents, siblings, uncles, aunts, friends), with *as-salāmu ʿalaykum* on entering.

This block is the *istʾidhān* corollary to block G — extending the principle of permission-asking from external visitors (block G) to internal household members. Verses 58-59 are the most-cited Quran verses on intra-household privacy.

## 14. Block M: closing ummah-discipline (vv. 62-64)

v. 62: true believers are those who, when with the Messenger on a unifying matter, do not depart without his permission. Those who ask his permission are the believers; when they ask for personal needs, give permission to whom you wish, and ask Allāh's forgiveness for them.
v. 63: do not call the Messenger by name as you call each other; Allāh knows those who slip away among you. Let those who oppose his command beware of a fitna or painful punishment.
v. 64: indeed, to Allāh belongs whatever is in the heavens and the earth — He knows what state you are in, and on the Day they return to Him, He will inform them of what they did. Allāh is *bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm* (knower of all things).

The closing fāṣila *bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm* echoes the closing of v. 35 (the same exact phrase). Q 24's two structural anchor-points — the Light-verse at the centre and the umma-discipline at the close — share their final two words. This is one of the cleaner cases of *iltifāt-resolving-fāṣila-rhyme* in the corpus.

## 16. Repetition patterns

- *wa-law-lā* / *law-lā* anaphora in vv. 12-21 (al-ifk passage; 5 occurrences in 10 verses).
- *wa-llāh-u … ʿalīmun ḥakīmun* refrain at vv. 18, 58, 59 (and ʿalīm-only at v. 60, 64).
- *yubayyin-u Allāhu lakumu al-āyāt* refrain at vv. 18, 58, 59, 61 — fourfold structural cohesion of the legal-instruction-blocks.
- *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* opening at vv. 21, 27, 58 — three calls to the believers, once for each of: (1) post-ifk warning, (2) home-entry, (3) household-internal permissions.
- *bi-kulli shayʾin ʿalīm* exact-phrase repetition at v. 35 (closing of the Light-verse) and v. 64 (closing of the surah).

## 17. Cross-surah references

Internal Quranic citations from Q 24:
- The four-witness ḥadd-of-zinā in v. 4 inverts/expands Q 4:15 (the "houses-confinement" pre-abrogation rule). The classical exegesis (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī) treats Q 24:4 as the operative text.
- The hijāb of v. 31 is paralleled by Q 33:53-59 (the *wives-of-the-Prophet hijāb*); Q 24:31 is the *general-believing-women khimār* and Q 33 is the *Prophet-household ḥijāb-curtain*. Empirical lexical overlap is low (Jaccard 0.153, ~13 of 85 distinct roots shared); see Q024-F-04.
- v. 39's mirage-parable echoes Q 14:18 (kāfir deeds as ash in a wind), Q 18:45 (life as water sent down).
- v. 55's *istikhlāf* promise echoes Q 7:129 (Mūsā's istikhlāf), Q 38:26 (Dāwūd's istikhlāf). Q 24:55 is the *general-Muslim* istikhlāf — the only verse where the promise is for the believing community at large.

## 18. Register summary

Q 24 is **predominantly legal-prose** (blocks B, C, D, F, G, H, I, L), with a **single eschatological-theological inset** (J: vv. 34-46, the Light-verse + cosmic signs), bracketed by the *al-ifk* narrative (E: vv. 11-20) and the *ummah-discipline* closer (M: 62-64). The proportions: legal/etiquette (~38 verses), theological-cosmic (13 verses), narrative-historical (10 verses), opening/closing (3 verses) ≈ 60:20:15:5.

This is the Quran's only surah where the theological-cosmic insert (the Light-verse zone) is *embedded inside* the legal frame rather than placed at a boundary. The classical reading — Q 24's name comes from the central inset, not from the surrounding legal frame — is therefore both descriptively accurate and structurally informative: the surah is named for its centre, and that centre is *not* its dominant register.
