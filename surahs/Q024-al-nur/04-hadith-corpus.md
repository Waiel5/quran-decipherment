## 2. The al-ifk narrative — the canonical Bukhārī references

The al-ifk story (Q 24:11-20) is the most-extensively-cited Q 24 passage in the hadith corpus. The standard isnād is **Ibn Shihāb al-Zuhrī ← ʿUrwa b. al-Zubayr + Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab + ʿAlqama b. Waqqāṣ + ʿUbaydullāh b. ʿAbdullāh ← ʿĀʾisha** (a quadruple-tabaqa transmission with each Successor narrating part of the story; al-Zuhrī assembled the integrated narrative).

**Bukhārī's al-ifk citations**:

**Key narrative elements** (verified against Bukhārī #4544 and #2556 Arabic+English text):
1. The *qurʿa* (lot-drawing) for which wife to accompany on the campaign.
2. The lost *jaziʿ* (Yemeni necklace) and ʿĀʾisha's search at the rest-stop.
3. The hawdaj being lifted onto the camel without ʿĀʾisha (because she was light, the bearers did not notice her absence).
4. **Ṣafwān b. al-Muʿaṭṭal al-Sulamī** finding her behind the army; bringing her on his camel.
5. The Madinan rumor-mill, with **ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl** as the chief slanderer.
6. The Prophet's month-long delay, his consultation of ʿAlī and Usāma, his addressing the community, ʿĀʾisha's defense at her father's house, the revelation of vv. 11-20.
7. ʿĀʾisha's reported reaction: *"By Allāh, I never thought that Allāh would reveal in my favor a revelation that would be recited"* (Bukhārī #7259).

**Muslim's al-ifk attestation**: Muslim #6846 (Kitāb al-tawba, bāb fī ḥadīth al-ifk) — a structurally complete parallel to Bukhārī #4544, with the same al-Zuhrī ↔ ʿUrwa ↔ ʿĀʾisha isnād. The narrative is essentially identical; small variations in wording.

**Abū Dāwūd #785** quotes Q 24:11 explicitly with *al-istiʿādhah* introduction: *"The Messenger of Allāh sat and unveiled his face and said: 'I take refuge in Allāh, the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing, from the accursed devil. Lo! they who spread the slander are a gang among you'"* — a homiletic embedding of the verse. Abū Dāwūd grades this *munkar* (rejected); the Q 24:11 quotation is verified, but the surrounding asbāb-context is critique-rejected.

## 3. The liʿān asbāb — Hilāl b. Umayya (Q 24:6-9)

The liʿān-procedure (mutual cursing oath between spouses) of vv. 6-9 was revealed in response to the case of **Hilāl b. Umayya**, a Companion who accused his wife of *zinā* with **Sharīk b. Saḥmāʾ**. The asbāb is recorded in all 6 of the major books:

| Book | ḥadīth # | Narrator-chain key term |
|:--|:-:|:--|
| Bukhārī | #2564, #4541 | Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿIkrima |
| Muslim | #3630 | Anas b. Mālik |
| Tirmidhī | #3263 | Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿIkrima |
| Abū Dāwūd | #2255 | Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿIkrima |
| Nasāʾī | #3474 | Anas b. Mālik |
| Ibn Mājah | #1801 | Ibn ʿAbbās via ʿIkrima |

The text of Bukhārī #2564 reads (after de-tashkeel): *"Hilāl b. Umayya accused his wife before the Prophet of having illegal sexual relations with Sharīk b. Saḥmāʾ. The Prophet said: 'Produce a proof or you will receive the legal punishment of being lashed on your back.' Hilāl said: 'O Messenger of Allāh, if any of us saw a man with his wife, would he go look for witnesses?'"* The verse-revelation followed.

The legal weight of this asbāb is decisive: the four-witness rule of Q 24:4 (qadhf-punishment for accusing a chaste woman without four witnesses) is amended for the spousal case by vv. 6-9. The five-fold oath replaces the four-witness requirement when the accuser is the husband himself. al-Qurṭubī's masāʾil (`qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt`, Q24:6) treats this asbāb as the foundation of the liʿān-fiqh.

## 4. The Light-verse echo in the Prophetic Tahajjud-dhikr

This is the project's most striking liturgical-empirical finding for Q 24.

**Bukhārī #1088** (Kitāb al-tahajjud, bāb al-tahajjud bi-l-layl), and parallels in Bukhārī #6080, #7104, #7157, #7213, transmits ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAbbās: *"When the Prophet stood at night to pray Tahajjud, he used to say: 'Allāhumma laka al-ḥamd; **Anta nūru al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi wa-man fīhinna**; wa-laka al-ḥamd, Anta qayyimu al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍi…'"* — "O Allāh, all praise is to You; **You are the Light of the heavens and the earth and whomever is in them**; all praise is to You; You are the Maintainer of the heavens and the earth…"

The same dhikr is recorded in:

| Book | ḥadīth # |
|:--|:-:|
| Bukhārī | #1088, #6080, #7104, #7157, #7213 (5 attestations) |
| Muslim | #1700 |
| Tirmidhī | #3502 |
| Abū Dāwūd | #771, #1509 |
| Nasāʾī | #1624 |
| Ibn Mājah | #1089 |
| Mālik *Muwaṭṭaʾ* | #512 (via Ibn ʿAbbās ← Ṭāwūs ← Abū al-Zubayr al-Makkī) |
| Aḥmad *Musnad* | (multiple parallels) |
| Dārimī | #810 |

**Empirical observation**: the Tahajjud-dhikr quotes Q 24:35's predicate verbatim — *Anta nūru al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* — but adds *wa-man fīhinna* ("and whomever is in them"). The verse itself reads only *Allāhu nūru al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ*. The dhikr's added *wa-man fīhinna* is a Prophetic *gloss-extension* — an authoritative reading of the verse as encompassing not only "the heavens and the earth" but also "all who are in them." This is the *direct embedding* of Q 24:35's theological claim into the canonical Prophetic prayer practice.

This finding has not been pre-registered (this file is post-hoc descriptive); however, the convergence of attestation across 9 of 9 canonical hadith books at a non-trivial token-level echo of Q 24:35's opening predicate constitutes a **liturgical-architectural marker** that connects Q 24's central verse to Prophetic daily practice. It is one of the very few cases in the corpus where a verse-fragment and a Prophetic dhikr-fragment match at near-verbatim level across all major collections.

## 5. The hijāb-and-modesty verses — hadith corpus

Q 24:30-31 vocabulary appears extensively across the 9 books — Bukhārī alone has 79 hits on the relevant root family (*khimār, juyūb, abṣār, faraj, zayyana*). The most-cited single hadith on the *khimār* requirement is:

**Bukhārī #4759** (Kitāb al-tafsīr, bāb wa-l-yaḍribna bi-khumurihinna ʿalā juyūbihinna): ʿĀʾisha narrating: *"May Allāh have mercy on the early-Madinan women: when the verse 'and let them strike their khumur over their juyūb' was revealed, they tore their *murūṭ* (woolen cloaks) and covered themselves with them."* This is the foundational asbāb / immediate-application hadith for v. 31.

**Q 24:30 (men's gaze-modesty)** has fewer direct asbāb attestations but is widely cited in fiqh-construction (al-Bukhārī Kitāb al-istiʾdhān). The closest-match Prophetic hadith is *al-naẓra al-uwlā wa-l-naẓra al-thāniya* — "the first glance is yours, the second is against you" (Tirmidhī #2776), which is the pragmatic-jurisprudential application of v. 30.

## 6. The home-entry verses (Q 24:27-29 and 58-61) — hadith corpus

The *istiʾdhān* (permission-asking) discipline of Q 24:27-29 is foundational for an entire chapter in Bukhārī (Kitāb al-istiʾdhān, chapter 79). Major hadiths:

- **Bukhārī #6245** (Kitāb al-istiʾdhān): the Companion Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī asked permission three times at ʿUmar's house, and on no answer departed. He was later called back; the Prophet had taught: *"al-istiʾdhānu thalāthun"* (permission is asked three times) — directly invoking Q 24:27.
- **Bukhārī #6241**: the Prophet's instruction *"the istiʾdhān has been instituted because of the gaze"* — connecting block G (vv. 27-29) to block H (vv. 30-31) at the Prophetic-instruction level.
- **Q 24:58 (the three "private times")**: Bukhārī #5238 records ʿAbdullāh b. Masʿūd's reading-and-application of this verse for household-internal etiquette. The verse's three times — pre-fajr, noon-removal-of-clothes, post-ʿishāʾ — are directly named.

## 7. Q 24:55 (the istikhlāf promise) — hadith corpus

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* (`suyuti-durr-manthur.openiti.raw.txt`, Q24:55 section) preserves multiple Companion attestations on this verse. Bukhārī's own *Kitāb al-aḥkām* and *Kitāb al-fitan* sections contain hadiths reading the istikhlāf-promise as fulfilled in the rāshidūn caliphate. The most-cited single text is **Bukhārī #6996** (the Prophet's prediction of *thirty-year caliphate followed by kingship*); this is *not* a direct Q 24:55 commentary but is exegetically applied to the verse by classical mufassirūn including al-Ṭabarī and Ibn Kathīr.

## 8. Recitation merits (*faḍāʾil*) of Q 24

The *faḍāʾil* literature on Q 24 is comparatively thin among the canonical books. Two attestations:

1. **Tirmidhī #2887** (Kitāb faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān, bāb mā jāʾa fī faḍl Sūrat al-Nūr): *"Whoever recites Sūrat al-Nūr is given the reward of ten good deeds for every believer past and future."* Same isnād as the al-Thaʿlabī attestation cited in `03-tafsir-survey.md` §1.3. Tirmidhī grades it: *"This is a strange (gharīb) ḥadīth; we do not know it except by this chain."* Later ḥadīth-criticism (al-Jūzajānī, al-ʿAjlūnī's *Kashf al-khafāʾ*) grades it *mawḍūʿ* (fabricated). The historical fact of its presence in al-Tirmidhī (and from there into al-Thaʿlabī) is verified; its authority-grade is contested.

2. The *teach-women-Sūrat-al-Nūr* tradition (ʿUmar's letter to Kūfa; ʿĀʾisha's parallel dictum). This is recorded in al-Qurṭubī and al-Thaʿlabī as a Companion-saying, not a Prophetic-saying. It does NOT appear in the canonical 9 books.

## 10. Honest limits

- The Bukhārī ḥadīth-numbers cited above use the ahmedbaset-json *idInBook* convention. Print editions vary slightly (e.g., Fatḥ al-Bārī numbers some chapters differently). The verification done here is text-content matching; the numeric convention is consistent within the on-disk JSON corpus but should be cross-validated against print editions if absolute numbering is needed.
- The "direct verse-quote" Light-verse hits in Bukhārī (#3432) and Abū Dāwūd (#1175) are *both about the same incident* (the drought-rain miracle, where Anas b. Mālik says "the sky was like glass" — i.e., uses *zujāja*, the Light-verse's word, but in a different context). The Light-verse is therefore quoted *zero* times verbatim in the 9 books; only its first-clause-predicate is liturgically embedded.
- The Tahajjud-dhikr finding is post-hoc. A pre-registered version would phrase: "Q 24:35 will appear at non-zero quote-rate in 9-of-9 canonical hadith books, in either full or first-clause form." This is verified ex-post; future replication on similar high-status verses (Q 2:255, Q 1, Q 112) would calibrate the prior.
- The *fadāʾil* hadith for Q 24 is graded *mawḍūʿ* by ḥadīth-critics. The fact of its citation in tafsir literature (al-Tirmidhī, al-Thaʿlabī, al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr*) is verified; its evidential weight for project purposes is correspondingly reduced.
- Ahmad's *Musnad* count of 12 hits on the hijāb-vocabulary-family is an underestimate (the Musnad has many parallel narrations); a closer text-based study would refine this.

## 11. One-paragraph synthesis

Q 24's hadith corpus is dominated by three concentrations: (1) the al-ifk narrative — 11 Bukhārī attestations + 1 Muslim + 1 Abū Dāwūd, all going back to al-Zuhrī's quadruple-Successor isnād from ʿĀʾisha herself; (2) the liʿān asbāb of Hilāl b. Umayya — present in all 6 of the major books; and (3) the Light-verse predicate "Anta nūru al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ" embedded in the Prophetic Tahajjud-dhikr in all 9 books. The ḥijāb-application Bukhārī #4759 (early-Madinan women tearing their murūṭ) and the istiʾdhān corpus (Bukhārī Kitāb al-istiʾdhān, an entire book derived from Q 24:27-29 and 58-61) round out the picture. *Faḍāʾil* hadiths on Q 24 are thin and ḥadīth-critic-rejected — consistent with the empirical [[h-new-860-hadith-architectural-alignment|H-NEW-860]] finding that Q 24 receives classical attention via *content* not *recitation-merit*. The hadith-empirical signature thus precisely matches the structural-iʿjāz-without-fadāʾil-density profile predicted by Q 24's UAS rank 5 + sig_A rank 82 cell-position.
