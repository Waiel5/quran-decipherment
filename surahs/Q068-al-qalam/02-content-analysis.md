## 2. Thematic block structure (per al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar*)

al-Biqāʿī's *munāsabāt* analysis of Sūrat al-Qalam (Naẓm al-Durar, sūra 68) identifies four major blocks:

### Block 1 — Oath / affirmation of the Prophet's character (vv. 1-16)

- **vv. 1-7**: Nūn-oath + qalam-oath; reassurance that the Prophet is not mad (*lasta bi-majnūn*); promise of unbroken reward; affirmation of his *khuluqin ʿaẓīm*; future-vindication promise.
- **vv. 8-16**: Polemical portrait of the unbeliever-opponent: *ḥallāf mahīn* (contemptible swearer), *hammāz mashshāʾin bi-namīm* (slanderer-calumniator), *mannāʿ li-l-khayr muʿtadin athīm* (withholder of good, transgressor, sinful), *ʿutull baʿda dhālika zanīm* (coarse and ignoble), called Q. 15 *asāṭīr al-awwalīn* (legends of the ancients), promise of branding on the snout (*sa-nasimuhu ʿalā al-khurṭūm*).

Classical asbāb al-nuzūl identifies the opponent as al-Walīd b. al-Mughīra (al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*, on Q 68:8-16, citing Ibn ʿAbbās; also al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*, Q 68 entry). The branding-on-the-snout (v.16) was understood by classical scholars (al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ*, on Q 68:16) as a battle-of-Badr prefiguration — al-Walīd's nose was sliced at Badr.

### Block 2 — The garden of the two ḥakam (parable of disinherited orchard-owners, vv. 17-33)

The corpus's most-developed economic-moral parable. A narrative cycle in five movements:

- **v. 17**: framing — *innā balawnāhum ka-mā balawnā aṣḥāba al-jannah* (we tested them as we tested the garden-owners).
- **vv. 18-25**: night-plot — they swore to harvest at dawn (v.17b), did not say "insha'Allah" (v.18), Allah sent a visitation (*ṭāʾif*) while they slept (v.19), the garden became *ka-l-ṣarīm* (black, reaped) (v.20), morning calls (v.21), departure whispering (v.23), bar-the-poor resolve (v.24), early arrival in capable wrath (v.25).
- **vv. 26-31**: discovery — they saw the destruction and recognized themselves as *ḍāllūn* (astray), *maḥrūmūn* (deprived), and confessed *innā kunnā ẓālimīn* (we were wrongdoers).
- **v. 28**: the "most balanced" (*awsaṭuhum*) reminds them they should have glorified Allah (*lawlā tusabbiḥūn*).
- **vv. 31-32**: prayer for replacement — *ʿasā rabbunā an yubdilanā khayran minhā innā ilā rabbinā rāghibūn*.
- **v. 33**: closing — such is punishment; the Hereafter's is greater.

This parable is **lexically isolated** but not corpus-extreme: Q068-F-04 sub-test (a) found Jaccard distances d(Q 68:17-33, Q 18:32-44) = 0.893 and d(Q 68:17-33, Q 36:13-32) = 0.880, both at p>0.83 against the 17-verse-window control distribution. The parable is genre-isolated (planted/garden/dawn/calamity vocabulary not heavily shared with other parables) but the corpus-wide 17-verse-window distribution is even MORE dispersed.

### Block 3 — Judgment-day scene (vv. 34-47)

- **vv. 34-35**: contrast — believers in *jannāt al-naʿīm*, then the rhetorical *afa-najʿalu al-muslimīna ka-l-mujrimīn*.
- **vv. 36-41**: polemical scriptural challenge — "Or do you have a book in which you study?" (v.37); "Or do you have oaths upon us...?" (v.39); "Ask them which is guarantor" (v.40); "Or do they have partners?" (v.41) — five-fold rhetorical *am*-questions.
- **vv. 42-43**: the **leg-uncovering** (*yawma yukshafu ʿan sāq*) scene — a divine theophany or eschatological sign (classical: al-Ṭabarī cites Ibn ʿAbbās on the meaning of *al-sāq*); they are called to prostrate but cannot; their eyes humbled.
- **vv. 44-47**: warning to the Prophet — leave the deniers to Allah (v.44), divine *kayd* / istidrāj (v.44b-45), challenge about debt/unseen (vv. 46-47).

### Block 4 — Yūnus close (vv. 48-52)

- **v. 48**: command to patience and the negative example *lā takun ka-ṣāḥibi al-ḥūt* (be not like the companion of the fish, i.e. Yūnus).
- **vv. 49-50**: the Yūnus rescue — had grace not overtaken him, he would have been cast on the barren ground (*al-ʿarāʾ*), blamed (*madhmūm*); but his Lord chose him.
- **vv. 51-52**: the closing — disbelievers almost slip you with their eyes; they call you mad; but it is nothing but a reminder for the worlds.

The Yūnus reference (vv. 48-50) connects Q 68 to Q 21:87-88 (Yūnus = Dhū al-Nūn), Q 37:139-148 (Yūnus = Yūnus b. Mattā). The thematic logic per al-Biqāʿī: just as the disinherited garden-owners (Block 2) and the future-leg-uncovered deniers (Block 3) failed by impatience and arrogance, the Prophet is warned NOT to repeat Yūnus's haste-and-suppression.

## 3. Content register

**Q 68 is primarily MIXED-MECCAN-RHETORICAL**: oath/affirmation (Block 1), narrative parable (Block 2), judgment-eschatology (Block 3), prophetic counsel (Block 4). It is NOT pure-eschatology (unlike Q 78, Q 81, Q 88), NOT pure-narrative (unlike Q 12 Yūsuf), NOT pure-legal (unlike Q 4, Q 5, Q 24), NOT pure-creedal (unlike Q 112). The mixing is rhetorical-purposive: the four blocks serve a single argumentative arc — *the Prophet is not mad; the deniers will face judgment; here is a cautionary parable; here is a positive counter-example*.

## 4. Vocabulary distinctness

**Writing-vocabulary over-concentration (Q068-F-01 VINDICATED)**: Q 68 over-concentrates at least one writing-vocabulary root (sTr / يسطرون) at Bonferroni-6 significance (p=0.0017), and the joint writing-vocabulary family is significant at p=0.0117. Specifically:

- **qlm** (qalam): 1 token (in v.1) — 65× over uniform-expected rate.
- **sTr** (*yasṭurūn* v.1, *asāṭīr* v.15): 2 tokens — 33× over expected.
- **ktb** (*kitāb* v.37, *yaktubūn* v.47): 2 tokens — 1.6× over expected (NOT significant).
- **sjl, rqm, lwH**: 0 tokens each.

The over-concentration is **NOT** at the v.1 alone (qlm + sTr in the opening formula); the v.15 *asāṭīr* and v.37 *kitāb* + v.47 *yaktubūn* mentions confirm that writing-vocabulary is distributed THROUGHOUT the surah, not just at the muqaṭṭaʿ-opener position. This is the **CONTENT-BEACON** signature predicted by Ibn ʿAbbās's classical gloss of ن.

## 5. Repetition patterns

- **Rhyme ن at 80.8%**: 42 of 52 verses end in ن (final-rāwī). This is the singleton-letter-cohort's only opener-rāwī match (Q050-F-05 cohort table).
- **Polemic-vocative *am***: vv. 35-47 contain five *am* (or-question) markers (vv. 37, 39, 41, 46, 47) — a tight rhetorical block.
- **Garden-owner block lexical concentration**: *ṣarama* (to harvest, vv. 17, 22, 25), *aṣbaḥ-* (morning, vv. 20, 21), *taqāwl-/tanāday-* (call/blame, vv. 21, 30), *ẓālim/ṭāghin* (wrongdoer, vv. 29, 31) — block-internal lexical cohesion is high.

## 6. Cross-surah content references

- **Yūnus (vv. 48-50)** ↔ Q 21:87-88 (Dhū al-Nūn), Q 37:139-148 (Yūnus b. Mattā).
- **Garden parable (vv. 17-33)** ↔ Q 18:32-44 (the Two Garden-Owners parable; lexically distinct per Q068-F-04 d=0.893).
- **Leg-uncovering / prostration scene (v. 42)** is a classical theological *cruxq*; al-Ṭabarī catalogues several Companion-interpretations.
- **Pen-creation theology (v.1)** ↔ Q 96:4 *alladhī ʿallama bi-l-qalam* — the only two surahs where *qalam* is a primary theological motif.

## 8. Sequence-of-thought (al-Biqāʿī munāsabāt arc)

The four-block arc traces an argumentative trajectory:
1. **Affirmation**: the Prophet is firmly upon great character.
2. **Cautionary tale**: the disinherited garden-owners — a generic-prosperity narrative falling to impiety and bar-the-poor selfishness.
3. **Eschatological warning**: the Day of Reckoning, the leg-uncovering theophany, the failed-prostration humiliation.
4. **Patience instruction**: stay patient, do not be like Yūnus; even the Prophet must continue.

The arc moves from *the prophetic character* through *moral failure narrative* to *eschatological judgment* to *prophetic discipline*. al-Biqāʿī reads this as a *Meccan tafsīr*-arc of "the Prophet stands at the boundary between affirmation and counsel; the surah ends as it began — *wa-mā huwa illā dhikrun li-l-ʿālamīn*" (v.52, closing).
