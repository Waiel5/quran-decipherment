## 2. Classical names

- **al-Anʿām** (الأنعام) — "The Cattle" / "The Livestock" — canonical, from the polemic against pre-Islamic livestock-superstitions in vv. 136-145.
- The surah is sometimes called *Sūrat al-Ḥujja* ("the Sura of the Argument") in some classical works, owing to its sustained creedal argumentation against polytheism.

## 3. Meccan-with-exceptions tradition

al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt`, search "الأنعام"): cites Ibn ʿAbbās (via al-Ḥākim, al-Bayhaqī, al-Ṭabarānī, Abū ʿUbayd):

> "سورة الأنعام نزلت بمكة جملة واحدة فهي مكية إلا ثلاث آيات منها نزلن بالمدينة: قل تعالوا أتل إلى تمام الآيات الثلاث"
> ["Sūrat al-Anʿām was revealed at Mecca in a single instalment; it is Meccan except three verses revealed at Madīna: 'Say, come, I will recite' [Q 6:151] to the end of the three verses"]

Variants (also in al-Suyūṭī, citing Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār): some traditions exclude up to 9 verses; al-Kalbī (via Abū al-Shaykh) says "all Meccan except two revealed at Madīna concerning a Jewish man who said *mā anzala llāhu ʿalā basharin min shayʾ*" (Q 6:91). Ibn al-Ḥaṣṣār concludes that "no nine-exception report is properly attested."

**Per the project's revelation-order CSV (al-Suyūṭī chronology): position 55 — mid-late Meccan**, after the early-Meccan creedal surahs (Q 96, 68, 73 etc.) and before the Hijra. This places Q 6 AFTER most of the Meccan corpus and WITHIN the late-Meccan polemic-cluster (Q 6, 7, 10-15, 16-17, etc.).

## 4. Opening formula

Q 6 opens with **الحمد لله** ("Praise be to God") — one of only five surahs to do so:
- Q 1 al-Fātiḥa
- Q 6 al-Anʿām
- Q 18 al-Kahf
- Q 34 Sabaʾ
- Q 35 Fāṭir

al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (Q 6 introduction, `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`) develops a four-fold typology of these *al-ḥamd*-openings: Q 6 honours God for **horizontal creation** (heavens-earth-darkness-light), Q 18 for **vertical revelation** (the Book on the servant), Q 34 for **eschatological dominion**, Q 35 for **angelic mediation**. Q 1 anchors the typology as the prayer-formulation. al-Bāqillānī cites Q 6:1 as a paradigmatic instance of *iʿjāz al-fawāṣil*-style opening (theme + cosmic creation in 12 words; see §5).

## 5. Length classification

Q 6's 165 verses + 3,304 words places it in **al-sabʿ al-ṭiwāl** (the Seven Long Surahs: Q 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, plus Q 9 or Q 10 disputed). Per al-Zarkashī, *al-Burhān* (PDF), Q 6 is the SIXTH long surah. Q 6 is shorter than its Medinan ṭiwāl neighbours (Q 2-5) but longer than mufaṣṣal openers (Q 50+).

## 8. Pre-Islamic religious vocabulary

Q 6 is uniquely the polemic locus against pre-Islamic cattle-sacralization (vv. 136-145). Distinctive vocabulary:

| Term | Occurrence | Role |
|:--|:--|:--|
| *anʿām* (أنعام) | 31× in Q 6 (out of ~33 in surah-corpus); also in Q 16 (5×), Q 5 (multiple) | the eponymous noun |
| *ḥarth* (حرث, "tilth") | 4× in Q 6 vv. 136-141 | paired with *anʿām* in offering-classifications |
| *baḥīra*, *sāʾiba*, *waṣīla*, *ḥām* (the four jāhilī cattle-categories) | **Q 5:103 ONLY** in entire Quran | NOT in Q 6 itself — the named jāhilī categories appear in Q 5 (al-Māʾida); Q 6 develops the polemic with general vocabulary (*ḥajr*, *muḥarram*, *fartharā*, *zaʿm*) |

This is critical: the four named pre-Islamic categories (*baḥīra*, *sāʾiba*, *waṣīla*, *ḥām*) are **Q 5:103 hapaxes**. Q 6 attacks the same JĀHILĪ system but in general terms (*hādhihi anʿāmun ḥajrun*, "this is sacred-cattle"; *anʿām ḥurrimat ẓuhūruhā*, "cattle whose backs are forbidden"; etc.). Q 6 is the polemic; Q 5:103 is the lexical inventory. See `06-novel-findings.md` Q006-F-03 for the corpus-rare hapaxes audit.

## 9. Major hadith-emphasis points

(Provisional, prior to full hadith-corpus build; see `04-hadith-corpus.md` for the chain audit.)

- **The 70,000-angel-procession block-revelation tradition** (Ibn ʿAbbās via al-Ṭabarānī, Abū ʿUbayd in *Faḍāʾil al-Qurʾān*, al-Bayhaqī in *Shuʿab al-īmān*; reported also from Anas, Abū b. Kaʿb, Mujāhid (500 angels), ʿAṭāʾ, ʿAlī (1,000-per-heaven from each of seven), Jābir, Ibn ʿUmar): the entire surah is said to have descended at once to Mecca at night, escorted by 70,000 (or 500, or 7,000) angels with *zajl bi'l-tasbīḥ wa'l-taqdīs* until "the earth shook." 
- al-Suyūṭī (in *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 14 *mā nazala mushayyaʿan wa-mā nazala mufradan*) catalogs five surahs/passages with angelic procession: Q 1 (80,000 angels), Q 6 (70,000), Q 36 (30,000), Q 2:255 *āyat al-kursī* (30,000), Q 16:36 (20,000).
- al-Suyūṭī cites **Ibn al-Ṣalāḥ** (in *Fatāwā*): "The ḥadīth that it descended in one instalment we have transmitted via Ubayy b. Kaʿb. **In its chain there is weakness; we have not seen for it a sound chain,**" and cites a counter-tradition that Q 6 was revealed in stages.
- al-Suyūṭī cites the **al-Ḥākim** narration via Jābir as "ṣaḥīḥ ʿalā sharṭ Muslim" but al-Dhahabī immediately notes "**fīhi inqiṭāʿ wa-aẓunnuhu mawḍūʿan**" — "in it is a [chain] discontinuity and **I think it is fabricated**."

The chain audit is performed in detail in `04-hadith-corpus.md`. **Provisional verdict: the famous 70,000-angel-procession ḥadīth is ḌAʿĪF / probably FABRICATED per al-Dhahabī.**
