# Q 49 al-Ḥujurāt — Verse-by-verse content analysis

## 1. Architectural overview

Q 49 al-Ḥujurāt is structured as **four thematic blocks** of progressively widening scope:

| Block | Verses | Theme | Address-formula |
|:--|:--:|:--|:--|
| A | vv. 1-5 | Prophet-deference (etiquette toward Prophet) | *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* (vv. 1, 2) |
| B | vv. 6-8 | News-verification + favor-of-faith | *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* (v. 6) |
| C | vv. 9-13 | Brotherhood + universalism | *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* (vv. 11, 12); *yā-ayyuhā al-nāsu* (v. 13) |
| D | vv. 14-18 | Distinction īmān vs islām | (Bedouin claim and reply at v. 14) |

The address-formula scope is structurally a **chiastic widening-and-narrowing**: in-group (A,B) → in-group + universal (C) → in-group critique (D). The universalist apex at v. 13 functions as the theological-semantic center of gravity.

## 2. Block A — vv. 1-5: prophet-deference

### v. 1
> يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تقدموا بين يدي الله ورسوله ۖ واتقوا الله ۚ إن الله سميع عليم
>
> *O you who have believed, do not put yourselves forward before God and His Messenger; and fear God — God is All-Hearing, All-Knowing.*

The opening verse establishes the surah's framing as etiquette-paraenesis. The construction *bayna yaday Allāhi wa-rasūlih* ("between God's hands and His Messenger's hands") is a corpus-EXACT-singleton — the only Quranic occurrence of this exact 4-word construction. It carries dual reading:

- **Literal-spatial**: do not advance physically before God and the Prophet (anti-encroachment etiquette).
- **Figurative-judicial**: do not pre-empt God and the Prophet in pronouncing judgment or speaking on matters not yet revealed (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.; al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān*, vol. 16 p. 295).

The closing fāṣila *Allāha samīʿun ʿalīm* (a divine-attribute compound) is a Khawātim-style verse-final reminder of divine surveillance.

### v. 2
> يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا ترفعوا أصواتكم فوق صوت النبي ولا تجهروا له بالقول كجهر بعضكم لبعض أن تحبط أعمالكم وأنتم لا تشعرون
>
> *O you who have believed, do not raise your voices above the Prophet's voice, and do not address him loudly as you address one another, lest your deeds be made worthless while you do not perceive.*

Three constructions in this verse carry distinctive empirical weight:
- The root **Swt (ṣ-w-t / voice)** appears 3 times in this verse (`aṣwātakum`, `ṣawti al-nabī`, and indirectly via `kajahri`). Combined with v. 3's `aṣwātahum`, Q 49 carries **3 of the 8 corpus tokens** of root ṣ-w-t (37.5 %).
- The verb root **jhr (j-h-r / utter loudly)** appears here in the warning against loud-speech etiquette breach.
- The verb-stem `taḥbaṭa aʿmālukum` ("your deeds be made worthless") is a corpus-RARE construction tied to spiritual-capital nullification.

The asbāb al-nuzūl per al-Bukhārī (Ṣaḥīḥ #4845, Kitāb al-Tafsīr ad Q 49:2-3) reports that this verse was revealed regarding Abū Bakr and ʿUmar, who had each loudly advocated competing names of Banū Tamīm-delegation leaders (al-Aqraʿ b. Ḥābis vs al-Qaʿqāʿ b. Maʿbad) before the Prophet.

### v. 3
> إن الذين يغضون أصواتهم عند رسول الله أولئك الذين امتحن الله قلوبهم للتقوى ۚ لهم مغفرة وأجر عظيم
>
> *Indeed those who lower their voices in the presence of God's Messenger — those are the ones whose hearts God has tested for taqwā; for them is forgiveness and a great reward.*

The verb-root **g̲h-ḍ-ḍ** (لِغَضَّ / to lower [voice/gaze]) appears here, parallel to the lowered-gaze etiquette of Q 24:30-31. The construction *imtaḥana Allāhu qulūbahum li-l-taqwā* ("God has tested their hearts for taqwā") is a corpus-EXACT-singleton — the only Quranic occurrence of this verb-form on hearts.

### v. 4
> إن الذين ينادونك من وراء الحجرات أكثرهم لا يعقلون
>
> *Indeed those who call you from behind the Chambers — most of them do not reason.*

⭐ This verse contains the surah-titular noun **al-ḥujurāt** (الحجرات, "The Chambers") — the only verse in Q 49 (and one of very few corpus instances) of the noun *ḥujra* in plural construct. The noun denotes the Prophet's Madīnan compound's wives' chambers (per al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb*, ad loc.; al-Ṭabarī ad loc.).

The asbāb al-nuzūl: Banū Tamīm delegation arrived at noon during the Prophet's siesta and called out for him from outside the chambers. The verse is a direct-correction of their etiquette breach.

The construction *akṯaruhum lā yaʿqilūn* ("most of them do not reason") is a Quran-corpus-formulaic phrase (29 corpus occurrences of the syntagm, per substring scan), but applied here to a SPECIFIC delegation (al-aʿrāb / al-Tamīmiyyīn).

### v. 5
> ولو أنهم صبروا حتى تخرج إليهم لكان خيرا لهم ۚ والله غفور رحيم
>
> *And if they had been patient until you came out to them, it would have been better for them; and God is Forgiving, Merciful.*

Hortatory close: had they waited, it would have been better — anticipating divine forgiveness. The closing fāṣila *al-Ghafūr al-Raḥīm* is the most-frequent Quranic divine-attribute pair (47 corpus occurrences as compound) — softening the rebuke with mercy.

## 3. Block B — vv. 6-8: news-verification + favor-of-faith

### v. 6
> يا أيها الذين آمنوا إن جاءكم فاسق بنبإ فتبينوا أن تصيبوا قوما بجهالة فتصبحوا على ما فعلتم نادمين
>
> *O you who have believed, if a fāsiq (transgressor) brings you news, verify [it] (`fa-tabayyanū`), lest you wrong a people in ignorance and become regretful for what you have done.*

⭐ This verse is the **classical anchor of the qadiyat al-tabayyun** (jurisprudential rule of news-verification), derived in al-Jaṣṣāṣ *Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (vol. 5 p. 271-275), al-Qurṭubī (vol. 16 p. 311-314), Ibn al-ʿArabī *Aḥkām al-Qurʾān* (ad Q 49:6).

The asbāb al-nuzūl per al-Bukhārī, *Adab al-Mufrad* #399, and al-Ṭabarī ad loc.: al-Walīd ibn ʿUqba was sent to collect zakāt from Banū al-Muṣṭaliq. Old enmity caused him to fear them; he reported back falsely that they had refused (apostasy implicit). The Prophet was about to dispatch a punitive expedition under Khālid b. al-Walīd when the verse was revealed, vindicating the tribe.

The variant reading `fa-tathabbatū` (verify-firmly) in lieu of `fa-tabayyanū` (verify-with-clarity) is reported in some qirāʾāt; the Hafs-Kūfan reading uses `fa-tabayyanū`.

The root **fsq (f-s-q / transgress)** appears 3 times in Q 49 (vv. 6, 7, 11) — 5.6 % of the 54 corpus tokens. The semantic field of fisq-vs-īmān is foundational to the surah's Block B and Block C linkage.

### v. 7
> واعلموا أن فيكم رسول الله ۚ لو يطيعكم في كثير من الأمر لعنتم ولكن الله حبب إليكم الإيمان وزينه في قلوبكم وكره إليكم الكفر والفسوق والعصيان ۚ أولئك هم الراشدون
>
> *And know that the Messenger of God is among you. If he were to obey you in much of [your] affairs, you would be in distress; but God has endeared faith to you and made it pleasing in your hearts, and made hateful to you disbelief and transgression and disobedience. Those are the rightly-guided.*

Two rhetorical movements:

1. *wa-ʿlamū anna fīkum rasūla Allāh* — affirm the Messenger's presence among you (a corrective to vv. 1-5: do not push him forward and do not shout above him; rather know that you have him among you, a privilege).

2. *Allāhu ḥabbaba ilaykum al-īmān...* — God has *endeared* (root **ḥ-b-b**) faith to your hearts and *zayyana* (made-pleasing) it. Parallel verb *karraha* (made-hateful) for the triplet *al-kufru, al-fusūqu, al-ʿiṣyānu* (disbelief, transgression, disobedience).

The triplet *al-kufru / al-fusūqu / al-ʿiṣyānu* (disbelief / transgression / disobedience) is itself a corpus-RARE compound — the only Quranic occurrence of this exact 3-noun cluster (cf. Q 9:84 *al-kufr* alone, Q 33:73 *al-mushrikūn wa-l-mushrikāt al-kufru* alone).

⭐ The closing word *al-rāshidūn* (the rightly-guided ones) becomes the eponymous title for the Sunnī Khulafāʾ al-Rāshidūn doctrine. Quranic occurrences of root **r-sh-d** in Form-1 active participle are limited to this verse + Q 21:51 (regarding Ibrāhīm). The Q 49:7 occurrence with the active-plural-definite is a corpus-LOCUS for the doctrine.

### v. 8
> فضلا من الله ونعمة ۚ والله عليم حكيم
>
> *A bounty (faḍl) from God and a grace (niʿma); and God is All-Knowing, Wise.*

Brief closing of Block B as a **divine-bestowal coda**. The construction *faḍlan min Allāhi wa-niʿmatan* is a corpus-FORMULAIC pairing of *faḍl* and *niʿma* — a core rhetoric of divine gift-language. The closing fāṣila *al-ʿAlīmu al-Ḥakīm* is one of the 47 most-frequent compound divine-attribute pairs.

## 4. Block C — vv. 9-13: brotherhood + universalism

### v. 9
> وإن طائفتان من المؤمنين اقتتلوا فأصلحوا بينهما ۖ فإن بغت إحداهما على الأخرى فقاتلوا التي تبغي حتى تفيء إلى أمر الله ۚ فإن فاءت فأصلحوا بينهما بالعدل وأقسطوا ۖ إن الله يحب المقسطين
>
> *And if two parties of believers fight, reconcile (`fa-aṣliḥū`) between them. If one of them transgresses against the other, fight the one transgressing until it returns to God's command. If it returns, reconcile between them with justice and act equitably; God loves those who act equitably.*

A foundational verse on **intra-Muslim conflict ethics** — the basis for classical Islamic war-doctrine (al-Māwardī, *al-Aḥkām al-Sulṭāniyya*) on the *bāghī* (rebel) class. The verb-root **bgy (b-gh-y / transgress, rebel)** appears 4 times in this verse — a corpus-DENSE concentration.

The verb-root **fyʾ (f-y-ʾ / return)** appears twice — significant because *fayʾ* in Quranic-juristic usage denotes "what returns to God" or "what is given back to the Muslim community" (Q 59:7).

The closing *al-Muqsiṭīn* (those who act equitably) is one of the divine-attribute-class words that appears in some catalogues of al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā (Tirmidhī's list; the canonical 99-name list places *al-Muqsiṭ* at rank 86).

### v. 10
> إنما المؤمنون إخوة فأصلحوا بين أخويكم ۚ واتقوا الله لعلكم ترحمون
>
> *Indeed the believers are brothers (*ikhwa*); so reconcile between your two brothers; and fear God so that you may be shown mercy.*

⭐ The architectonic verse on **brotherhood (*ukhuwwa*) of the believers**. The corpus-RARE construction *innamā al-muʾminūna ikhwa* ("indeed, the believers are brothers") is a corpus-EXACT-singleton — the only Quranic occurrence of this exact phrase (substring-search confirmed).

The dual-form *bayna akhawaykum* ("between your two brothers") is grammatically interesting — the dual is rare in this kind of paraenesis. It refers back to the two warring parties of v. 9 (*ṭāʾifatāni*), now identified as "your two brothers" (a softening of warring-parties-of-believers to brothers-in-conflict).

The closing *laʿallakum turḥamūn* (so that you may be shown mercy) anchors the social-ethics injunction in eschatological mercy — fear God to be shown mercy.

### v. 11
> يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا يسخر قوم من قوم عسى أن يكونوا خيرا منهم ولا نساء من نساء عسى أن يكن خيرا منهن ۖ ولا تلمزوا أنفسكم ولا تنابزوا بالألقاب ۖ بئس الاسم الفسوق بعد الإيمان ۚ ومن لم يتب فأولئك هم الظالمون
>
> *O you who have believed, let not [one] people mock another — perhaps they are better than them; nor [one] women [mock] other women — perhaps they are better than them; and do not insult your own kind, and do not call each other by [insulting] nicknames. How evil is the name "fāsiq" after [accepting] faith. Whoever does not repent — those are the wrongdoers.*

Three discrete prohibitions on social-conduct breaches:
- *lā yaskhar* (do not mock) — root **s-kh-r** with corpus-rarity profile.
- *lā talmizū anfusakum* (do not insult your own kind / each other) — the construction *lamz* (slander) is corpus-rare.
- *lā tanābazū bi-l-alqāb* (do not call each other by nicknames) — root **n-b-z** is corpus-EXACT-singleton; the only Quranic occurrence of root n-b-z is here.

The construction *biʾsa al-ismu al-fusūqu baʿda al-īmāni* ("how evil is the name 'fāsiq' after [accepting] faith") is a memorable epithet that anchors the surah's broader fisq-vs-īmān dichotomy.

⭐ The closing predicate *fa-ulāʾika humu al-ẓālimūn* echoes Q 5:45 and Q 5:47 ("those are the wrongdoers"); a recurring Medinan-paraenesis verse-final.

### v. 12
> يا أيها الذين آمنوا اجتنبوا كثيرا من الظن إن بعض الظن إثم ۖ ولا تجسسوا ولا يغتب بعضكم بعضا ۚ أيحب أحدكم أن يأكل لحم أخيه ميتا فكرهتموه ۚ واتقوا الله ۚ إن الله تواب رحيم
>
> *O you who have believed, avoid much suspicion (`ẓann`); indeed some of suspicion is sin. And do not spy (`tajassasū`); and do not backbite (`yagtab`) one another. Would any of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? You would detest it. And fear God; indeed God is Accepter-of-Repentance, Merciful.*

Three prohibitions in cascade:
- *ijtanibū kathīran min al-ẓann* (avoid much suspicion).
- *lā tajassasū* (do not spy) — corpus-RARE root **j-s-s**.
- *lā yagtab baʿḍukum baʿḍā* (do not backbite one another) — corpus-RARE root **gh-y-b** in this Form-VIII reflexive sense.

⭐ The shock-rhetoric: *a-yuḥibbu aḥadukum an yaʾkula laḥma akhīhi maytā* ("Would any of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother?") — a corpus-RARE rhetorical device of grotesque-imagery analogy. The metaphor of *al-ghība = laḥm al-akhī al-mayyit* (backbiting = one's dead brother's flesh) is the LOCUS classicus for this metaphor in classical Arabic ethical literature; it shapes al-Ghazālī's *Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn* book on the tongue (kitāb āfāt al-lisān) and Imām al-Nawawī's *Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn* chapter on backbiting.

Closing fāṣila: *al-Tawwāb al-Raḥīm* — divine-attribute pair on repentance-acceptance, softening the rebuke.

### v. 13
> يا أيها الناس إنا خلقناكم من ذكر وأنثى وجعلناكم شعوبا وقبائل لتعارفوا ۚ إن أكرمكم عند الله أتقاكم ۚ إن الله عليم خبير
>
> *O humanity, We have created you from a male and a female and made you peoples (*shuʿūb*) and tribes (*qabāʾil*) so that you may know one another. Indeed the most honored of you in God's sight is the most god-fearing among you. Indeed God is All-Knowing, All-Aware.*

⭐ **The corpus's most universalist single verse.** Three architectonic features:

1. **Address-formula widening**: from the in-group *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* (vv. 1, 2, 6, 11, 12) to the universal *yā-ayyuhā al-nāsu*. This is the only verse in Q 49 with the widened address.

2. **Corpus-EXACT lexical feature**: the root **shaʿb (ش-ع-ب)** is a corpus-EXACT-doubleton (total = 2 occurrences corpus-wide); Q 49:13 carries 1 of them. The phrase *shuʿūban wa-qabāʾila* ("peoples and tribes") is a corpus-LOCUS for the universalist creational-equality grounding.

3. **Universalist creational ethics**: the verse grounds human dignity in pre-tribal-pre-ethnic creational equality (`khalaqnākum min dhakar wa-unthā`), then explicitly licenses ethnic and tribal diversity as instrumentally good (`li-taʿārafū` — so that you may come to know one another).

Empirical: of 14 unique roots in Q 49:13, **3 are corpus-rare ≤ 50** (sh-ʿ-b at 2, unthā at 30, krm at 47). The verse is lexically distinctive in addition to its theological universalism. See Q049-F-04 (CONFIRMED-VERSE-ANOMALY).

The closing fāṣila *Allāha ʿalīmun khabīr* — divine-attribute pair on knowledge.

## 5. Block D — vv. 14-18: distinction īmān vs islām

### v. 14
> ۞ قالت الأعراب آمنا ۖ قل لم تؤمنوا ولكن قولوا أسلمنا ولما يدخل الإيمان في قلوبكم ۖ وإن تطيعوا الله ورسوله لا يلتكم من أعمالكم شيئا ۚ إن الله غفور رحيم
>
> *The Bedouins said: "We have believed." Say: "You have not [truly] believed; rather say 'we have submitted (`aslamnā`)' — faith has not yet entered your hearts. But if you obey God and His Messenger, He will not deduct anything from your deeds. Indeed God is Forgiving, Merciful.*

⭐ This verse contains the architectonic distinction between **īmān (inward faith) and islām (outward submission)** that runs through al-Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Īmān*, al-Ashʿarī's *Maqālāt*, and Ibn Taymiyya's *al-Īmān*. The verse's force: the al-aʿrāb (Bedouins) claim *āmannā* (we have believed) but are corrected — at this stage, only the lower-bar *aslamnā* (we have submitted) applies; *al-īmānu lammā yadkhul fī qulūbikum* (faith has not yet entered your hearts).

Asbāb al-nuzūl per al-Wāḥidī ad loc. and al-Ṭabarī: regarding Banū Asad b. Khuzayma who entered Madīnah seeking food during a drought year. They claimed īmān to extract benefits; the verse corrects them.

The juristic implication of vv. 14-15: Islam-as-mere-public-profession is real but distinct from īmān-as-inward-conviction; this is the LOCUS for the Maturidi/Ashʿari/Ḥanafī/Ḥanbalī debate on whether the Muslim is *ḥukman* (legally) the same as the *muʾmin* (true believer). Q 49:14-15 is the central textual anchor.

The verse-final divine-attribute pair *al-Ghafūr al-Raḥīm* is identical to v. 5's closing — a structural echo across Block A and Block D.

### v. 15
> إنما المؤمنون الذين آمنوا بالله ورسوله ثم لم يرتابوا وجاهدوا بأموالهم وأنفسهم في سبيل الله ۚ أولئك هم الصادقون
>
> *Indeed, the [true] believers are those who have believed in God and His Messenger, then have not doubted, and have struggled (`jāhadū`) with their wealth and selves in God's path. Those are the truthful.*

The 3-criterion definition of true believers:
1. *āmanū bi-Allāhi wa-rasūlih* (believed in God and His Messenger)
2. *thumma lam yartābū* (then did not doubt) — root **r-y-b** (doubt) in Form-VIII
3. *jāhadū bi-amwālihim wa-anfusihim fī sabīl Allāh* (struggled with wealth and selves in God's way)

The verb-root **j-h-d** (struggle/jihād) appears 41 times in the Quran in this Form-III meaning of struggle-in-God's-path. Q 49:15 is one of the corpus-LOCI for the inner-struggle dimension (since "wealth and selves" is the inner+material commitment, not exclusively military).

Closing predicate *ulāʾika humu al-ṣādiqūn* (those are the truthful) — the corpus-FORMULAIC parallel to v. 7's *al-rāshidūn*.

### v. 16
> قل أتعلمون الله بدينكم والله يعلم ما في السماوات وما في الأرض ۚ والله بكل شيء عليم
>
> *Say: do you teach God your religion? Yet God knows what is in the heavens and what is in the earth, and God is All-Knowing of every thing.*

A direct rebuke against epistemic-pretension. The construction *a-tuʿallimūna Allāha bi-dīnikum* ("do you teach God your religion?") — corpus-EXACT-singleton; the only Quranic occurrence of this exact 4-word interrogative.

The 3-fold *Allāh-ʿalim* claim (verse contains 3 forms: *Allāh* twice + *ʿalīm*) anchors divine-omniscience as the antidote to human-pretension.

### v. 17
> يمنون عليك أن أسلموا ۖ قل لا تمنوا علي إسلامكم ۖ بل الله يمن عليكم أن هداكم للإيمان إن كنتم صادقين
>
> *They consider you obliged (`yamunnūna ʿalayka`) by their having submitted. Say: "Do not consider me obliged by your submission; rather God obliges you (`yamunnu ʿalaykum`) by having guided you to faith — if you are truthful."*

⭐ The root **mnn (m-n-n / favor-bestowing or impose-as-favor)** is a corpus-RARE root with 27 corpus tokens. Q 49 carries 3 of them (vv. 17 (twice), 8) — **11.1 % of corpus** in 0.29 % of corpus verse-real-estate.

The semantic shift between human-mannan (false claim of obligation) and divine-mannan (true source of every gift) is one of the surah's strongest polemical contrasts. *Allāhu yamunnu ʿalaykum an hadākum li-l-īmān* ("God obliges you by having guided you to faith") inverts the al-aʿrāb's framing of v. 14 — they thought their submission was a favor TO the Prophet; in fact God's guidance to faith is a favor TO them.

### v. 18
> إن الله يعلم غيب السماوات والأرض ۚ والله بصير بما تعملون
>
> *Indeed, God knows the unseen (*ghayb*) of the heavens and the earth; and God is All-Seeing of what you do.*

Terminal closing. The construction *ghayba al-samāwāti wa-l-arḍ* ("the unseen of the heavens and the earth") appears in 4-5 corpus instances; Q 49:18 is one. The closing fāṣila *al-Baṣīrun bi-mā taʿmalūn* (All-Seeing of what you do) is a Khawātim-style divine-surveillance affirmation that mirrors v. 1's *Allāha samīʿun ʿalīm* — bracketing the surah with **divine-omniscience opening + divine-omniscience close**.

## 6. Inclusio architecture

The verse-1 ↔ verse-18 echo:
- v. 1: *Allāha samīʿun ʿalīm* (All-Hearing, All-Knowing)
- v. 18: *Allāha baṣīrun bi-mā taʿmalūn* (All-Seeing of what you do)

Pattern: opening = hearing+knowing → closing = seeing. The 3-fold divine-perception bracket (samīʿ + ʿalīm + baṣīr) is one of the corpus's tighter inclusio-frames in a Medinan paraenesis surah. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar* ad Q 49) explicitly notes this "*munāsabah-faṣla-ila-faṣla*" framing.

This inclusio is a candidate for empirical testing — it is a Medinan-inclusio of the kind detected in H-NEW-189 (Medinan inclusio at 8.5× Meccan rate, p < 0.0001).
