## 2. Classical name and titling

The surah is named **al-Ḥujurāt** ("The Chambers / Inner Apartments") after a noun appearing only at v. 4 — the only verse where the word *ḥujurāt* (الحجرات) occurs. The construction `min warāʾi al-ḥujurāt` ("from behind the chambers") refers to a delegation of Banū Tamīm calling out for the Prophet from beyond the partitioned wives' chambers in the Prophet's Madīnan compound (asbāb al-nuzūl per al-Wāḥidī, *Asbāb*, ad loc.).

The surah is also known by alternative classical titles in tafsīr commentary:
- **Sūrat al-akhlāq** ("Surah of Manners") — al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar* ad loc.
- **Sūrat al-ādāb** ("Surah of Etiquette") — colloquial titling reflecting the surah's content focus.

There is no classical disagreement on the surah's Medinan attribution; all major mufassirūn (al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī, al-Biqāʿī) place it in the Medinan period, with al-Suyūṭī's revelation-rank ordering placing it at position 106 of 114 — late-Medinan.

## 3. The opening formula — direct address Medinan paraenesis

Q 49:1 opens with the canonical Medinan address-formula:

> *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū lā tuqaddimū bayna yaday Allāhi wa-rasūlih wa-ttaqū Allāha inna Allāha samīʿun ʿalīm*
>
> "O you who have believed, do not put yourselves forward before God and His Messenger; and fear God — God is All-Hearing, All-Knowing."

This is the canonical Medinan address-formula that opens 89 verses corpus-wide, exclusively in 27 Medinan surahs (zero Meccan attestations). Q 49 contains 5 attestations of this formula (vv. 1, 2, 6, 11, 12) plus a one-time use of the broader-address `yā-ayyuhā al-nāsu` at v. 13 — six "yā-ayyuhā" address-formulas in 18 verses.

⭐ **Q 49 is the corpus-rank-1 surah by per-verse density of yā-ayyuhā-alladhīna-āmanū**, at 5/18 = 0.2778. See Q049-F-01 (CONFIRMED, primary verdict).

## 4. Surah-level structural blocks (4 thematic units)

Classical mufassirūn (al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar*, Ibn ʿĀshūr *al-Taḥrīr wa-l-Tanwīr*, al-Suyūṭī *Asrār al-Tanzīl*) divide Q 49 into **four etiquette-thematic blocks**:

### Block A — vv. 1-5: prophet-deference

Five verses on conduct in the presence of the Prophet:
- v. 1: do not put yourselves forward before God and His Messenger
- v. 2: do not raise your voices above the Prophet's
- v. 3: those who lower their voices have hearts tested for taqwā by God
- v. 4: those calling from behind the chambers (Banū Tamīm asbāb)
- v. 5: had they waited until you came out, it would have been better

The reported asbāb-al-nuzūl context: the Banū Tamīm delegation arrived in al-Madīnah after the conquest of Makkah (year 9 AH per al-Wāḥidī) and called out to the Prophet from outside the chambers (*ḥujurāt*) of his Madīnan compound. The opening five verses are a direct-address paraenesis of formal mosque/court etiquette in the Prophet's presence.

### Block B — vv. 6-8: news-verification + favor-of-faith

Three verses on epistemic responsibility in conflict and the divine gift of īmān:
- v. 6: if a fāsiq (transgressor) brings you news, verify (`fa-tabayyanū`) lest you wrong a people in ignorance
- v. 7: know that the Messenger of God is among you; God has endeared faith to you and made it pleasing in your hearts
- v. 8: a bounty (faḍl) and a grace (niʿma) from God

The asbāb-al-nuzūl context: al-Walīd ibn ʿUqba's report on Banū al-Muṣṭaliq's alleged apostasy (per al-Bukhārī, *Jāmiʿ al-Ṣaḥīḥ*, kitāb al-tafsīr ad Q 49:6). Empirically the verb-stem `tabayyana` (verify) at v. 6 carries one of the strongest classical legal anchors in the Quran (al-Jaṣṣāṣ, *Aḥkām al-Qurʾān*; al-Qurṭubī ad loc.).

### Block C — vv. 9-13: brotherhood + universalism

Five verses on intra-Muslim conflict, social ethics, and the universalist verse:
- v. 9: if two parties of believers fight, reconcile (`fa-aṣliḥū`) between them
- v. 10: only believers are brothers (*ikhwa*); reconcile between your brothers
- v. 11: do not mock; do not call by bad nicknames; how evil is the name "fāsiq" after faith
- v. 12: avoid suspicion (*ẓann*); do not spy; do not backbite; would any of you eat his dead brother's flesh?
- v. 13: ⭐ *yā-ayyuhā al-nāsu* — "O humanity, We have created you from a male and female and made you peoples (*shuʿūb*) and tribes (*qabāʾil*) so you may know one another; the most honored among you in God's sight is the most god-fearing"

⭐ Q 49:13 is the corpus's **most universalist single verse**, opening with the broader-address formula `yā-ayyuhā al-nāsu` (rather than the in-group `yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū`) and explicitly grounding human dignity in pre-tribal-pre-ethnic creational equality. The word *taʿārafū* ("come to know one another") is a hapax-context: this is the only Quranic occurrence of the Form-VI reciprocal *taʿārafa* verb in this universalist sense.

### Block D — vv. 14-18: distinction īmān vs islām

Five verses distinguishing surface-level submission (*aslamnā*) from inward faith (*āmannā*):
- v. 14: the Bedouins (al-aʿrāb) said "we have believed"; say: "you have not believed; rather say 'we have submitted (*aslamnā*)' — faith has not yet entered your hearts"
- v. 15: the [true] believers are those who have believed in God and His Messenger, then have not doubted, and have struggled with their wealth and selves in God's path
- v. 16: do you teach God your religion? — God knows what is in the heavens and earth
- v. 17: they consider you obliged (*yamunnūna ʿalayka*) by their having submitted; say: "do not consider me obliged by your submission; rather God has obliged you (*Allāhu yamunnu ʿalaykum*) by guiding you to faith"
- v. 18: indeed God knows the unseen (*ghayb*) of the heavens and earth

The classical anchor: this block is one of the strongest Quranic passages distinguishing **Islam (outward submission)** from **īmān (inward conviction)**, an architectonic distinction that runs through al-Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Īmān*, al-Ashʿarī's *Maqālāt*, and Ibn Taymiyya's *al-Īmān*. The Banū Tamīm asbāb-al-nuzūl context is reported here as well: the Bedouin delegation's casual claim of belief is corrected to a more honest framing of submission-without-yet-realized-faith.

## 9. The dual-address architecture

A unique structural feature: Q 49 is the only Quranic surah with **BOTH address-formulas in close proximity**:
- 5 occurrences of *yā-ayyuhā alladhīna āmanū* (in-group address — vv. 1, 2, 6, 11, 12)
- 1 occurrence of *yā-ayyuhā al-nāsu* (universal address — v. 13)

The universalist address at v. 13 functions as the **theological center of gravity** of the surah: just when the narrative has moved through prophet-deference → epistemic-care → brother-reconciliation, it broadens to humanity-as-whole, then narrows back to the īmān-vs-islām distinction.

This dual-address structure is rare. The pattern (in-group → universal-broadening → in-group) is structurally a **chiastic widening-and-narrowing of address-scope** that mirrors al-Biqāʿī's reading of the surah as moving from particular-mosque-etiquette to universal-human-equality and back to communal-sincerity.
