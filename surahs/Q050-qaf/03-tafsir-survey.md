## 1. The opening letter ق — five classical positions

### 1.1 al-Ṭabarī (d. 310/923) on Q 50:1 — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

`spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafsir-al-tabari/50/1.json` records four classical opinions on the meaning of ق, with isnād-chains:

> "The interpreters disagreed on (ق):
> - Some said: it is a name from the names of Allah (taʿālā) by which He swore. ʿAlī b. Dāwūd → Abū Ṣāliḥ → Muʿāwiya → ʿAlī → Ibn ʿAbbās: 'For (ق) and (ن) and the like — it is an oath that Allah took, and it is a name from the names of Allah.'
> - Others said: it is a name from the names of the Qurʾān. Ibn ʿAbd al-Aʿlā → Ibn Thawr → Maʿmar → Qatāda: 'In (ق): a name from the names of the Qurʾān.'
> - Others said: (ق) is the name of the mountain encompassing the earth.
> - We have already explained (in the introduction) the muqaṭṭaʿāt-letters at the start of surahs sufficiently to forgo repeating it here."

al-Ṭabarī then notes the disagreement on the *jawāb al-qasam* (the response of the oath): the Basran grammarians say the *jawāb* is at v. 4 *qad ʿalimnā mā tanquṣu al-arḍu minhum*; the Kufan grammarians say the meaning is implicit (a *muqaddar*). The verse 1 *al-Majīd* he glosses as *al-Karīm* via Saʿīd b. Jubayr.

**Project commentary**: al-Ṭabarī's three-way framing of ق (divine-name / Qurʾān-name / mountain) is the classical taxonomy. He explicitly DECLINES to assert which is correct — *al-Ṭabarī's epistemic humility* is consistent with the broader cross-finding-001 / al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ on muqaṭṭaʿāt-meaning being unknowable.

### 1.2 al-Rāzī (d. 606/1209) on Q 50:1 — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

Per `razi-muqattaat-surah-qaf.md`:

> "Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī gives the most extensive classical discussion of the ḥurūf al-muqaṭṭaʿāt, listing some twenty opinions. He notes that:
> - The letters may be names of the surahs they open.
> - They may be among God's names.
> - They spell words when combined.
> - The Arabs would name things after single letters (ʿayn for 'eye', ghayn for 'clouds', nūn for 'whale').
> - **al-Rāzī does NOT report anyone computing the letter-frequency density. The classical range of explanation is semantic and symbolic, not statistical.**"

### 1.3 al-Qurṭubī (d. 671/1273) on Q 50:1 — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām*

`spa5k-tafsir-api/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi/50/1.json`:

> "Meccan, all of it (it is 45 verses). Meccan in the view of al-Ḥasan and ʿAṭāʾ and ʿIkrima and Jābir. Ibn ʿAbbās and Qatāda said: except one verse, which is His saying *And we created the heavens and the earth and what is between them in six days and no weariness touched us* (v. 38).
>
> In Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, on the authority of Umm Hishām bint Ḥāritha b. al-Nuʿmān: 'Our oven and the oven of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ were one, for two years — or one year and part of a year. I learned [the surah] *Qāf wa-l-Qurʾān al-majīd* only from the tongue of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, who used to recite it every Friday day on the *minbar* when he addressed the people.'
>
> And from ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb (raḍiya allāhu ʿanhu) — he asked Abū Wāqid al-Laythī: 'What did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ recite during the [prayer of] Eid al-Aḍḥā and Eid al-Fiṭr?' He said: 'He used to recite in both [prayers] *Qāf wa-l-Qurʾān al-majīd* and *Iqtarabati al-sāʿatu wa-nshaqqa al-qamar* (Q 54).'
>
> And from Jābir b. Samura: that the Prophet ﷺ used to recite in the dawn prayer (Fajr) *Qāf wa-l-Qurʾān al-majīd*, and his prayer thereafter was abbreviated."

al-Qurṭubī then catalogues seven recitation variants:
- The general (al-ʿāmma): qāf, with sukūn (jazm) on the last letter.
- al-Ḥasan + Ibn Abī Isḥāq + Naṣr b. ʿĀṣim: qāfi, with kasra on the fāʾ (treating sukūn-kasra equivalence).
- ʿĪsā al-Thaqafī: qāfa, with fatḥa on the fāʾ.
- (al-Qurṭubī continues with the meaning).

## 5. The "*qāf*-mountain" interpretation (al-Ṭabarī's third position)

al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, and al-Suyūṭī all catalogue the popular interpretation that ق is "the name of the mountain encompassing the earth" (*ism al-jabal al-muḥīṭ bi-l-arḍ*). al-Ṭabarī declines to endorse it; al-Qurṭubī notes it; al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* records its appearance among the twenty muqaṭṭaʿāt-interpretations al-Rāzī catalogues.

**Modern academic position**: this *qāf*-as-mountain interpretation is widely regarded as a folk-etymological accretion (cf. Wikipedia entry on Muqaṭṭaʿāt, cited in `razi-muqattaat-surah-qaf.md` Sources). The empirical project's stance: this is *not testable* in the data we have (no mountain-related vocabulary cluster in Q 50), and it does not affect the architectural empirical profile.

## 6. Major exegetical positions on Q 50:16 — *naḥnu aqrabu ilayhi min ḥabli al-warīd*

Three classical positions on the metaphysics of *qurb* (closeness):

1. **Knowledge-closeness** (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī, al-Jalālayn): *qurb* is *qurb al-ʿilm* — closeness of knowledge. al-Jalālayn writes "We are nearer to him *in knowing him* than his jugular vein."

2. **Power-closeness** (some later mutakallimūn): *qurb* is *qurb al-qudra* — by His power, He could destroy the man's life faster than the jugular vein could deliver blood.

3. **Angelic-mediation** (a minority position cited by al-Qurṭubī): the *aqrabu* refers to the angel-recorders (vv. 17-18) whose presence is closer than the jugular vein. al-Qurṭubī notes this view but does not endorse it.

The project's empirical body-part-density finding ([[01-empirical-profile|Q 50 §10]]) is *register-neutral* across these interpretations: it confirms that *whatever* the metaphysics, Q 50's body-part vocabulary is corpus-extreme — and the *ḥabl al-warīd* image is the most concentrated example.
