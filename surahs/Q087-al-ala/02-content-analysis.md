## 1. Six structural blocks

The surah's 19 verses fall into **six rhetorical blocks**, each integral and balanced:

### Block 1 — Imperative tasbīḥ + cosmological justification (vv 1–5)

The opening commands: *sabbiḥi sma rabbika l-aʿlā* — directed at the Prophet (singular 2-masc). Then four cosmological *lladhī*-clauses establishing what God did:
1. *khalaqa fa-sawwā* (created and proportioned, v 2);
2. *qaddara fa-hadā* (decreed and guided, v 3);
3. *akhraja l-marʿā* (brought forth the pasture, v 4);
4. *jaʿalahu ghuthāʾan aḥwā* (made it dark withered debris, v 5).

The four *lladhī*-clauses pair into two diptychs — (creation + balance) || (decree + guidance) || (production + decay). Verses 4-5 treat the same agricultural *marʿā* across two stages: live-green pasture → dark withered post-grazed remnant. This twin-image is **the surah's first naẓm signal**: the cosmological process includes both genesis AND decay; tasbīḥ is owed to the Lord of both.

### Block 2 — Self-referential recitation promise (vv 6–8)

The pivot of the surah:
- v 6: *sa-nuqriʾuka fa-lā tansā* — "We shall make you recite, so you will not forget."
- v 7: *illā mā shāʾa llāhu innahu yaʿlamu l-jahra wa-mā yakhfā* — exception clause + omniscience.
- v 8: *wa-nuyassiruka li-l-yusrā* — "And We shall make easy for you the easiest path."

The block addresses the Prophet's anxiety about forgetting the recitation. The exception clause (v 7) is the famous theological *masʾala* (which al-Suyūṭī catalogues at *al-Itqān* nawʿ 47): does anything actually get forgotten? Classical readings split:
1. *Real-forgetting* (al-Ṭabarī, al-Qurṭubī): Allah may abrogate recited verses by causing the Prophet to forget them. The classical *naskh al-tilāwah* (abrogation of recitation) doctrine cites this verse.
2. *Theological-only-conditional* (al-Zamakhsharī, al-Rāzī wajh II): the exception is theoretical *imkān*, not factual *wuqūʿ* — the Prophet did not in fact forget.

The Q 87:6 *sa-nuqriʾuka fa-lā tansā* + Q 75:17 *inna ʿalaynā jamʿahu wa-qurʾānahu* + Q 20:114 *wa-lā taʿjal bi-l-qurʾāni* form the **3-verse Prophet-addressed jamʿ-doctrine** triad. Q 87:6 is the chronologically EARLIEST of the three (rev-#8 vs rev-#27 [Q 75] vs rev-#45 [Q 20]).

The verb *sa-nuqriʾuka* (root ق-ر-ʾ, 1pl-imperfect-future + 2sg-acc-suffix) is the corpus-EXACT hapax of "we shall make you recite". See Q087-F-04 in `06-novel-findings.md`.

### Block 3 — Reminder ethic (vv 9–13)

- v 9: *fa-dhakkir in nafaʿati l-dhikrā* — "So remind, if the reminder benefits"
- v 10: *sa-yadhakkaru man yakhshā* — "The God-fearing will remember"
- v 11: *wa-yatajannabuhā l-ashqā* — "The most wretched will avoid it"
- v 12: *alladhī yaṣlā l-nāra l-kubrā* — "He who enters the Greatest Fire"
- v 13: *thumma lā yamūtu fīhā wa-lā yaḥyā* — "Then dies not in it nor lives"

Three sequential audiences: the receptive (v 10), the avoidant (v 11), the punished (v 12-13). The eschatological *al-nār al-kubrā* in v 12 is one of seven Qurʾānic mentions of the "Greatest Fire" (cf. al-Suyūṭī, *Mukhtaṣar al-Itqān*, ms 122-3; the Greatest Fire ranks above *al-jaḥīm*, *al-saʿīr*, *laẓā*, etc., per al-Qurṭubī's *al-Tadhkira* eschatology).

The *lā yamūtu fīhā wa-lā yaḥyā* phrase (v 13) is the Qurʾānic root-utterance of the **ETERNAL-DOUBLE-NEGATION eschatological state** — neither dying nor living. It echoes at Q 20:74 (*lā yamūtu fīhā wa-lā yaḥyā*, in Pharaoh's hellish destination) — corpus-EXACT 2-occurrence hapax. Both occurrences are in surahs in the Q 20 + Q 87 *al-ṣuḥuf-al-ūlā* corpus-EXACT pair, suggesting a deliberate sub-pair architecture across these two surahs.

### Block 4 — Falāḥ clause + ṣalāh (vv 14–15)

- v 14: *qad aflaḥa man tazakkā* — "Successful is he who purifies himself"
- v 15: *wa-dhakara sma rabbihi fa-ṣallā* — "And remembered the name of his Lord and prayed"

Twin verses on success: *tazakkā* (purification, classically interpreted as *zakāt al-fiṭr* per ʿUmar's reported view in Bukhārī chapter heading; OR moral purification in al-Ṭabarī's preferred reading) + *ṣallā* (prayed). The juxtaposition is significant: the Tasbīḥ command of v 1 (which is *dhikr ism al-rabb*) becomes EXECUTED here as the *ṣalāh* — the surah's opening imperative is its closing example. This is the **inclusio-style internal closure** of the tasbīḥ thematic.

The pair *zakāt + ṣalāh* (purification of wealth + prayer) is the foundational covenant pair in Qurʾānic ethics, mentioned together 28 times in the corpus. Q 87:14-15 is one of the EARLIEST chronological occurrences (rev-#8) of this pairing.

### Block 5 — World-vs-Hereafter (vv 16–17)

- v 16: *bal tuʾthirūna l-ḥayāta l-dunyā* — "Yet you prefer the worldly life"
- v 17: *wa-l-ākhiratu khayrun wa-abqā* — "While the Hereafter is better and more lasting"

The classical *bal*-particle of contrast (al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* nawʿ 41 *al-ḥurūf*) introduces the human-error verdict. The pair *khayrun wa-abqā* (better and more lasting) re-uses *abqā* from the same root that closes Q 20:131 (*wa-rizqu rabbika khayrun wa-abqā*) — corpus-EXACT 2-occurrence pair on this exact phrase form (Q 20:131 + Q 87:17). Once again, Q 20 + Q 87 share a corpus-pair signature, reinforcing the al-ṣuḥuf-al-ūlā architectural connection.

### Block 6 — Terminal scripture-declaration (vv 18–19)

- v 18: *inna hādhā la-fī l-ṣuḥufi l-ūlā* — "Indeed this is in the earliest scriptures"
- v 19: *ṣuḥufi Ibrāhīma wa-Mūsā* — "The scriptures of Ibrāhīm and Mūsā"

The closing clause is a **scripture-genealogy declaration**. The deictic *hādhā* (this) is classically disputed:
1. *The whole surah* (Bazzār → ʿAṭāʾ → ʿIkrima → Ibn ʿAbbās chain via Ibn Kathīr): the Prophet ṣ.ʿ.s. said upon revelation, "all of this is in the *ṣuḥuf-Ibrāhīm-wa-Mūsā*."
2. *Just the immediately-preceding verses 14-17* (Ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī's preferred reading): the falāḥ + zakāh + ṣalāh + ākhirah-cluster is what is in the earlier scriptures.
3. *The whole corpus* (al-Rāzī wajh III): all *uṣūl al-dīn* (theological fundamentals) are present in all divine books, hence Q 87:14-17 + the broader Qurʾānic core mirror the *ṣuḥuf-Ibrāhīm-wa-Mūsā*.

Whichever reading, the verse closes the surah by identifying the imperative-tasbīḥ + recitation-promise + reminder-ethic + falāḥ + ākhirah-priority content with the foundational scripture-tradition. Q 87 thus AUTHENTICATES itself by naming the *ṣuḥuf* it is restating.

This is a **theologically central** structural move: the imperative *sabbiḥi* of v 1 is uttered by a Prophet whose recitation is divinely-secured (v 6) preaching content that is also the content of the earliest revealed scriptures (vv 18-19). The **chain of authentication** is: divine-speech → Prophet-recitation → continuity-with-Ibrāhīm-and-Mūsā → ʿumma-instruction.

## 3. Internal naẓm and balāgha features

### *Sajʿ muṭarraf* (variable-length, uniform-rhyme)

Q 87's verses range from 2 words (v 8, *wa-nuyassiruka li-l-yusrā*) to 9 words (v 7, the istithnāʾ). All 19 end on alif-maqṣūra (or phonological *-ā*). This is a textbook *sajʿ muṭarraf* (Ibn Abī l-Iṣbaʿ, *Badīʿ al-Qurʾān*, on *anwāʿ al-sajʿ*).

### *Tarṣīʿ* (jewel-setting)

Verses 2-5 exhibit a *tarṣīʿ* construction: each verse is a *lladhī*-clause + 2-verb diptych (created+proportioned, decreed+guided, brought forth+made). The verse-internal grammatical parallelism mirrors the verse-final rhyme — a multi-axis parallelism that classical balāgha catalogues at the highest *naẓm* tier (al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān* nawʿ 60 *al-tarṣīʿ wa-l-tasjīʿ*).

### *Iltifāt* (grammatical shift)

Q 87 uses 1pl→2sg→3sg→2pl shifts:
- v 1: 2sg (sabbiḥi sma rabbika)
- v 2-5: 3sg (lladhī khalaqa, etc.) — God in third person
- v 6-8: 1pl (sa-nuqriʾuka, nuyassiruka) — God speaks in 1pl
- v 7: 3sg-resumption (innahu yaʿlamu)
- v 9: 2sg-imperative (fa-dhakkir)
- v 10-13: 3sg (sa-yadhakkaru, yatajannabuhā, etc.) — generalized characters
- v 14-17: 3sg → 2pl (qad aflaḥa man → bal tuʾthirūna) — addressing the audience
- v 18: 3sg-deictic (inna hādhā)
- v 19: 3sg-genealogy (ṣuḥufi Ibrāhīma wa-Mūsā)

This is **8 grammatical-person shifts in 19 verses** — a high-density *iltifāt* signature. Per ʿAbdul Haleem's iltifāt catalog (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/abdel-haleem-iltifat-catalog.md`), Q 87 is among the densest *iltifāt* surahs per-verse. Classical balāgha (al-Khaṭīb al-Qazwīnī, *al-Talkhīṣ*) catalogues *iltifāt* as one of the foundational *iʿjāz al-naẓm* devices.

### *Munāsabat al-suwar* (inter-surah connection)

al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-Durar* (vol 21 pp 211-219, on Q 86 → Q 87): reads the Q 86 al-Ṭāriq → Q 87 al-Aʿlā transition as one tight unit. Q 86 ends with *fa-mahhili l-kāfirīna amhilhum ruwaydā* (give the disbelievers respite, give them little time); Q 87 opens with the imperative tasbīḥ. The thematic flow: warning to the disbelievers → command to the Prophet to glorify (the right response to the warning) → the falāḥ ethic (vv 14-15). This is the *muqābala* / *iʿrāḍ* (contrast / turning-toward) classical reading.

al-Suyūṭī, *Asrār Tartīb al-Qurʾān*, similarly identifies Q 86 → Q 87 as a thematic conjunctive unit. The empirical confirmation: the Q 86→Q 87 adjacency-cost is **clamped-zero** (rank 9/113 — better than 2-opt-optimum), per H-NEW-720 / H-NEW-1240. The classical *munāsabat* reading is thus **empirically validated** — a contribution to the project's classical-validation scorecard.

### *Inclusio* / ring-closure

The surah's macro-structure exhibits a ring-closure:

- v 1: *sabbiḥi sma rabbika l-aʿlā* — command to invoke the divine name
- v 15: *wa-dhakara sma rabbihi fa-ṣallā* — execution of that invocation by the falāḥ-bearer
- v 18-19: the contents are the *ṣuḥufi-l-ūlā* — placing the surah within the eternal scripture-genealogy

The opening's COMMAND becomes the middle's EXAMPLE becomes the closing's GENEALOGY — a 3-fold ring on the *dhikr-ism-al-rabb* theme. This is consistent with cross-finding-013 mushaf-as-topological-ring (Wave-3): the principle of ring-closure operates at the SURAH-INTERNAL level here, paralleling the corpus-level ring (Q 1 ↔ Q 114).

## 4. Classical asbāb al-nuzūl

## 7. The IMPERATIVE form as theological signature

Q 87:1 *sabbiḥi sma rabbika l-aʿlā* uses the D-stem (faʿʿala II) imperative with object: "glorify the name of your Lord". The classical exegesis on the IMPERATIVE form:

- *Direct address* (al-Zamakhsharī): the imperative is addressed to the Prophet first and through him to the umma. The 2-masc-sg form thus pluralizes through Prophetic mediation.
- *Ongoing command* (al-Rāzī): the imperative is *istimrāriyya* (continuative), commanding ongoing tasbīḥ throughout the believer's life — not a one-off act.
- *Founding-the-tasbīḥ-tradition* (Ibn Kathīr): Q 87 is the **first chronological** surah to command tasbīḥ, founding the tradition that Q 17, 57, 59, 61, 62, 64 will then NARRATE rather than COMMAND. The narrative musabbiḥāt (*sabbaḥa* perfect, *yusabbiḥu* imperfect, *subḥāna* maṣdar) all DESCRIBE creation's tasbīḥ; only Q 87 COMMANDS it — and Q 87 is chronologically EARLIEST. The classical reading: Q 87 instantiates the command, the others describe the universal compliance.

This is a **theologically and rhetorically consistent sequence**: the umma is first commanded (Q 87, rev-#8), then later told that creation is already complying (Q 17 *subḥāna lladhī asrā* describes the cosmic glorification act; Q 57 *sabbaḥa li-llāhi* describes everything's past glorification; Q 62 *yusabbiḥu li-llāhi* describes ongoing glorification). The 4-form typology of H-NEW-103 thus encodes a **temporal-theological progression**: command (Q 87) → present-cosmic-state (Q 17 maṣdar) → past-completed-cosmic-act (Q 57/59/61) → ongoing-cosmic-act (Q 62/64). This reading is itself a contribution.
