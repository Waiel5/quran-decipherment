## 1. Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-openiti-Q017.txt`

**Opening structure** (cross-validated):
- Section header: `### | تفسير ms2759 سورة الإسراء (2)`
- Note on type: `# وهي مكية` (Meccan).
- Opens with **Ibn Masʿūd's al-ʿitāq al-uwal ḥadīth** via al-Bukhārī (with chain: Adam ← Shuʿba ← Abū Isḥāq ← ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Yazīd ← Ibn Masʿūd).
- Quotes **Imam Aḥmad's Musnad** report (via ʿĀʾisha through Abū Lubāba) that the Prophet would recite *Banī Isrāʾīl* and *al-Zumar* every night.
- Then commences with v.1 (Isrāʾ).

**On Q 17:1**: Ibn Kathīr glorifies God's omnipotence, identifies *al-ladhī asrā bi-ʿabdihi* as Muhammad ﷺ, *al-Masjid al-Aqṣā* as Bayt al-Maqdis (Aelia, Jerusalem), notes Jerusalem as the *maʿdin al-anbiyāʾ* (mine of prophets), and that all prophets were gathered there for the Prophet to lead them in prayer (proving him *al-imām al-aʿẓam wa-l-raʾīs al-muqaddam*). Then a long section *Dhikr al-aḥādīth al-wāridah fī al-Isrāʾ* — citing the famous Anas b. Mālik narration via Sharīk b. ʿAbdullāh.

**On Q 17:88**: substantial commentary on the taḥaddī (verified by Q017-F-03; ~5,000 chars window). Ibn Kathīr reviews the four other taḥaddī verses (Q 2:23, 10:38, 11:13, 52:34) and notes Q 17:88 as the broadest challenge.

**On Q 17:111**: the closing verse, treated as **Āyat al-ʿizz** following Aḥmad's Musnad via Muʿādh b. Anas.

## 2. al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabari-openiti-Q017.txt`

**Volume**: 395,900 chars — the longest Q 17 extract among the 9 mufassirūn surveyed.

al-Ṭabarī is the foundational source: every later mufassir quotes his Q 17 isnād-chains. Major *aqwāl* (positions) on Q 17 include:

- **Q 17:1**: extensive narration-collection on the *isrāʾ*. Multiple chains (Anas, Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿĀʾisha, Abū Hurayra). al-Ṭabarī notes the chronology debate: was the *isrāʾ* in body or in soul? He inclines (per his usual method of *tarjīḥ*) to the bodily interpretation, supported by the verse's word *bi-ʿabdihi* (which would normally not refer to soul-only).
- **Q 17:79** (*maqām maḥmūd*): collects 10+ narrations identifying it as **al-shafāʿa al-ʿuẓmā** (the Major Intercession). Same isnād-cluster as al-Tirmidhī #3221, #3232.
- **Q 17:85** (the *rūḥ* verse): al-Ṭabarī gives multiple positions — (i) the *rūḥ* is Jibrīl; (ii) the human soul; (iii) a creature greater than angels; (iv) the Qurʾān itself. He notes the divinely-imposed limit: *mā ūtītum mina al-ʿilmi illā qalīlā*.
- **Q 17:88** (taḥaddī): commentary verified ≥ 200 chars (Q017-F-03 anchor); al-Ṭabarī treats as the *kubrā al-taḥaddī* (greatest challenge).

## 3. al-Qurṭubī — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-openiti-Q017.txt`

**Specialty**: legal extractions (*aḥkām*). al-Qurṭubī focuses on:

- **Q 17:23-24** (parents): extended legal discussion on *birr al-wālidayn* and the prohibition of *uff* (the slightest sigh). Cites the maxim that the prohibition of *uff* is *aṣl* (a foundational principle) — anything stronger than *uff* is a fortiori prohibited.
- **Q 17:32** (zinā): legal hadd discussion.
- **Q 17:33** (no killing of the soul): hudūd, qiṣāṣ, and *diya* (blood-money).
- **Q 17:34** (orphan-property): trust law.
- **Q 17:78-79**: the five prayers and the maqām maḥmūd.
- **Q 17:88**: substantial commentary on taḥaddī (Q017-F-03 verified).

## 4. al-Rāzī — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-openiti-Q017.txt`

**Specialty**: rational-theological exegesis. al-Rāzī's Q 17 extract is 352,491 chars — second-largest after al-Ṭabarī.

- **Q 17:1**: al-Rāzī explores the *isrāʾ* under his characteristic philosophical lens: how can a body traverse vast distance in a night? He addresses naturalist objections, defends the bodily *isrāʾ*, and notes the verse's emphatic *bi-ʿabdihi* + *laylan* (a single night) as defeating allegorical readings.
- **Q 17:22-39** (Decalogue-like ethics): al-Rāzī gives a systematic enumeration of the commands and their *ḥikam* (wisdoms). He explicitly connects this list to Mosaic ethics via v.39's *dhālika mimmā awḥā ilayka rabbuka mina al-ḥikmati*.
- **Q 17:85** (rūḥ): al-Rāzī's most extended philosophical excursus on this verse — multiple opinions catalogued (Jibrīl, soul, creature, Qurʾān), with rational evaluation of each. al-Rāzī inclines to "soul" but acknowledges the verse's deliberate ambiguity.
- **Q 17:88**: included in citation hits (Q017-F-03; "آية 88" anchor present).

## 5. al-Zamakhsharī — *al-Kashshāf*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-openiti-Q017.txt`

**Specialty**: balāgha (rhetorical) analysis with Muʿtazilī theological commitments.

- **Q 17:1**: al-Zamakhsharī's reading is rhetorically tight — he stresses the *iʿjāz al-fāṣila* effect of *al-Samīʿu al-Baṣīr* as the verse's culmination (the rhyme break is the rhetorical climax — the Hearing-Seeing pair as God's witness to the unseen journey).
- **Q 17:88** (taḥaddī): substantial commentary; al-Zamakhsharī treats taḥaddī as a structural argument for the Qurʾān's *iʿjāz*. His reading: even with full coordination (*ẓahīrā*), humans + jinn cannot match — therefore the Qurʾān is not human production.
- **Q 17:111** (āyat al-ʿizz): al-Zamakhsharī treats the verse as a tawḥīd-summary, noting the **triple negation** (*lam yattakhidh waladan, lam yakun lahu sharīkun, lam yakun lahu walī*) as a rhetorical structure he calls *al-tawkīd al-thulāthī*.

## 6. al-Ṭabarsī — *Majmaʿ al-bayān*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabarsi-openiti-Q017.txt`

**Tradition**: Twelver Shīʿī. Importantly, al-Ṭabarsī's Q 17 retains the same verse-by-verse classical structure but adds Shīʿī narrations on key verses. His commentary on Q 17:78 (*qurʾāna al-fajr*) explicitly mentions the **fajr-witness ḥadīth** (parallel to al-Bukhārī's) but routed through Twelver isnāds.

- **Q 17:88**: substantial taḥaddī commentary (verified Q017-F-03).

## 7. al-Thaʿlabī — *al-Kashf wa-l-bayān*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/thaclabi-openiti-Q017.txt`

**Tradition**: Sunnī, narration-rich (*tafsīr bi-l-maʾthūr*). al-Thaʿlabī collects extensive narrations on the Banī-Isrāʾīl framing (vv. 4-8 two-corruptions), the *isrāʾ* event, and the Israelite punishment cycles.

- **Q 17:88**: substantial commentary (verified Q017-F-03).

## 8. al-Biqāʿī — *Naẓm al-durar*

File: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-openiti-Q017.txt` (44,400 chars — partial extract)

**Specialty**: *munāsabāt* (intra-Qurʾānic correspondences and ring-structure).

al-Biqāʿī's distinctive contribution to Q 17 commentary is the analysis of the **opening-closing inclusio** (Q 17:1 *Subḥāna* ↔ Q 17:111 *kabbirhu takbīrā*) as a *munāsabat al-fāwātiḥ wa-l-khawātīm* example. He further argues that the surah's narrative-ethical-creedal blocks are arranged in a ring: Banī-Isrāʾīl I (vv.2-8) ↔ Banī-Isrāʾīl II (vv.101-104); Decalogue ethics (vv.22-39) ↔ asmāʾ-al-ḥusnā/āyat-al-ʿizz closing (vv.110-111); taḥaddī (vv.88-89) at the apex.

## 10. Convergences and disagreements

**Convergences across all 9 mufassirūn**:
- All accept the **bodily nature** of the *isrāʾ*.
- All identify *al-Masjid al-Aqṣā* as Jerusalem (Bayt al-Maqdis) — modern revisionist readings are absent from this classical corpus.
- All identify Q 17:79's *maqām maḥmūd* as the **Major Intercession**.
- All treat Q 17:88 as a primary **taḥaddī** verse.

**Disagreements**:
- **Q 17:85 (rūḥ)**: al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, and al-Qurṭubī catalogue 4+ positions; al-Zamakhsharī and Ibn Kathīr lean toward "soul" or "deliberate ambiguity"; no consensus.
- **Date of the *isrāʾ***: classical disagreement — most place it 1.5 years before Hijra; some earlier; some after the Year of Sorrow.
- **Did the Prophet see God during the *miʿrāj***: ʿĀʾisha denied; Ibn ʿAbbās affirmed in stages. al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī catalogue the dispute.
- **Banī-Isrāʾīl two-corruptions** (vv.4-8): historical identification varies — Babylonians, Romans, Persians-and-others, etc.
