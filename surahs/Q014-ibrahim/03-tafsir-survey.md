# Q 14 Ibrāhīm — Classical Tafsir Survey (≥ 5 mufassirūn)

This file surveys classical commentaries on Q 14, organized around three high-attention loci: (1) the Mecca-prayer of Abraham (vv. 35-41), (2) the good-word parable (vv. 24-27), (3) Iblīs's eschatological speech (v. 22). Per the project's anti-hallucination protocol (`INVESTIGATION-PROTOCOL.md` §2.11), every claim carries a verification status. Many positions are SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED via Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr*, and the modern Mafātīḥ-survey literature — flagged accordingly.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān ʿan taʾwīl āy al-Qurʾān*

**On Q 14 chronology**: al-Ṭabarī classifies Q 14 as **Meccan** (consensus uncontested in his survey; intro to Sūrat Ibrāhīm). He notes a single-tradition exception held by some companions for vv. 28-30 (which mentions disbelievers exchanging God's grace) — said to refer to the Quraysh leaders at Badr — but the surah as a whole is uncontestedly Meccan.

**On Q 14:4 (*bi-lisān qawmihi*)**: al-Ṭabarī notes this verse establishes the principle that revelation is given in the language of the people receiving it. He cites a Companion-tradition (via Ibn ʿAbbās → Mujāhid) that this principle did NOT diminish the Quran's universal applicability — the Arabic-revelation is the seal-revelation for all humanity.

**On Q 14:24-25 (good-word parable)**: al-Ṭabarī endorses the *date-palm* identification of the good-word tree, citing the hadith from Ibn ʿUmar (Bukhārī #61, see `04-hadith-corpus.md`). He notes that the parable's *aṣluhā thābit* (firm root) is the *kalimat al-tawḥīd* (the word "lā ilāha illā Allāh"), and *farʿuhā fī al-samāʾ* (its branch in heaven) is the deeds-rising-to-God doctrine.

**On Q 14:35-41 (Mecca-prayer)**: al-Ṭabarī treats the prayer as Abraham's foundational prayer at the Kaʿba site, AFTER he settled Hagar and Ishmael there. He cites the foundational Hagar-Ishmael narrative (Bukhārī #3364 — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`) as the asbāb al-nuzūl framework, though noting the prayer itself is in Late-Meccan revelation, not Abraham-period. The prayer functions in late-Meccan revelation as the Kaʿba-anchor reaffirmation against Quraysh idolatry.

## 2. al-Rāzī (Fakhr al-Dīn) — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (al-Tafsīr al-kabīr)

**On Q 14 chronology**: al-Rāzī agrees with the Meccan classification, noting the surah's themes (cosmology, prophet-cycle, Mecca-foundation prayer) align with the late-Meccan period.

**On Q 14:4 (*bi-lisān qawmihi*)**: al-Rāzī develops a substantial epistemological argument: revelation must be intelligible to its first audience for the obligation-of-belief to apply. He links this to the *ḥujja* (proof) doctrine — God does not impose obligation without the means of comprehension. al-Rāzī notes the implication: the Arab audience of the Qurʾān has heightened *ḥujja* (proof) because the Qurʾān is in their tongue.

**On Q 14:22 (Iblīs's eschatological speech)**: al-Rāzī devotes a long section to whether Iblīs's *speech* on Judgment Day is (a) literal-eschatological event, (b) anticipatory revelation of what will happen, or (c) a symbolic reflection of the disbelievers' eventual self-recognition. al-Rāzī favours (a) — Iblīs literally addresses the damned at the moment of judgment, declaring his own deception. al-Rāzī notes the verse's rhetorical force in producing self-recognition in the disbelievers (*fa-lā talūmūnī wa-lūmū anfusakum*).

**On Q 14:24-27 (good-word parable)**: al-Rāzī is exhaustive — the *kalimat al-tawḥīd* is the firm-rooted tree; alternative interpretations include "the word 'subḥān Allāh'" and "the word of any sincere believer in any era". The good-tree is the date-palm per Bukhārī #61, but al-Rāzī notes the parable can be applied to any *thābit* good-saying. He links the parable to Q 14:27 (*yuthabbitu Allāhu alladhīna āmanū bi-l-qawli al-thābiti*) — the firm-word is what God establishes in believers' hearts at moments of crisis (e.g., the questioning in the grave per the *ʿadhāb al-qabr* tradition).

**On Q 14:35-41 (Mecca-prayer)**: al-Rāzī's analysis of the prayer's logical sequence is one of the surah's classical landmarks. He identifies **8 sequential prayer-acts**:
1. Mecca-safety (corporate, v. 35a).
2. Personal-and-progeny protection from idolatry (v. 35b-36).
3. Mecca-foundation declaration (v. 37a).
4. Hearts-toward-them prayer for the Mecca settlers (v. 37b).
5. Sustenance-from-fruits prayer (v. 37c).
6. Cosmological confession (v. 38).
7. Praise-for-late-life-progeny *al-ḥamd* (v. 39).
8. Prayer-establishment-for-self-and-progeny + acceptance of prayer (v. 40).
9. Closing intercession for self, parents, and believers (v. 41).

al-Rāzī emphasizes the **prayer-establishment as the central petition** (vv. 37b "li-yuqīmū al-ṣalāt", v. 40 "muqīm al-ṣalāt"). The Mecca-foundation is structurally subordinate to the prayer-foundation.

## 3. al-Qurṭubī — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**On Q 14 chronology**: al-Qurṭubī confirms Meccan classification consensus.

**On Q 14:4**: al-Qurṭubī cites the universal-revelation principle, noting al-Tirmidhī's hadith (per Bukhārī #335 / Tirmidhī #1553 - mass-prayer-with-others-in-different-language tradition is referenced) on the Prophet's universal mission to all peoples (despite Arabic revelation).

**On Q 14:35-41**: al-Qurṭubī devotes an extensive section to the **Hagar-Ishmael Mecca-settling tradition**, citing Bukhārī's foundational *zamzam* narrative (#3364, the Hajar-and-Ishmael abandonment-and-rescue) as the historical setting. He notes the linguistic detail: *bi-wādin ghayri dhī zarʿ* (a barren valley) explicitly sets the prayer at pre-Mecca-foundation. He also notes Abraham's prayer *waj-nubnī wa-banīya an naʿbuda al-aṣnām* — the **specific naming of "my children" alongside himself** — as a corpus-distinctive grandparent-child intercessory framing.

**On Q 14:39 (the *al-ḥamd* for sons)**: al-Qurṭubī notes the verse's chronological-specificity: Abraham was elderly (*ʿalā al-kibar*) when both sons were granted to him. The verse implicitly rebuts Christian-tradition's emphasis on Isaac alone — Q 14:39 names *both* Ishmael (mentioned first; the elder son) and Isaac, an Islamic theological-literary tradition asserting both as God-gift sons.

## 4. Ibn Kathīr — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

**On Q 14 chronology**: Ibn Kathīr classifies Meccan, citing al-Ṭabarī.

**On Q 14:24-27 (good-word parable)**: Ibn Kathīr extensively cites **Bukhārī #61**:
> Ibn ʿUmar said: while we were sitting with the Prophet (ﷺ), some date-palm hearts were brought to him. The Prophet said: "Among the trees there is one whose leaves don't fall, and which is like the believer. Tell me, which tree is this?" The people fell silent. I (Ibn ʿUmar) thought: 'It is the date-palm,' but I was shy. Then they said: 'Tell us, Messenger of God.' He said: 'It is the date-palm.'

Ibn Kathīr also cites Q 14:24 itself as the verse the Prophet was alluding to. The good-tree-as-date-palm interpretation is anchored at this hadith, with #61 verified at `data/literature/hadith/ahmedbaset-json/db/by_book/the_9_books/bukhari.json` (idInBook=61).

**On Q 14:27 (firm-word doctrine)**: Ibn Kathīr cites **Bukhārī #4699** (the *qawl al-thābit*-as-questioning-in-the-grave hadith via al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib) as the eschatological-anchor for v. 27. The verse promises God's establishing the believers with the firm-word in this life and in the next — the next-life "establishing" being the moment of grave-questioning.

**On Q 14:35-41**: Ibn Kathīr cites Bukhārī #3364-3365 (the foundational Hagar-Ishmael narrative) as the asbāb al-nuzūl framework. He notes Q 14:39's *Ismāʿīl wa-Isḥāq* explicitly elevates Ishmael as Abraham's first-named son, contra the Genesis-tradition's emphasis on Isaac.

## 5. al-Suyūṭī — *al-Durr al-manthūr fī al-tafsīr al-maʾthūr* + *al-Itqān*

**On Q 14 chronology** (al-Itqān, nawʿ 1 — Meccan-Medinan classification):
- al-Suyūṭī's catalog classifies Q 14 as **Meccan** (rev #72, in the chronological catalog).
- Source: `data/revelation-order.csv` Q 14 row carries `period=Meccan` (Tanzil Egyptian Standard, sourced from the al-Suyūṭī tradition, late-Meccan).

**On Q 14:35-41 (the Mecca-prayer)**: al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* assembles multiple chains for the asbāb al-nuzūl framework, drawing on Bukhārī's Hagar-Ishmael narrative + various Companion-traditions on Abraham's specific prayer-acts. He notes the prayer is structurally one of the **two Abrahamic Mecca-prayer blocks** in the corpus, the other being Q 2:127-129 (the *idh yarfaʿu Ibrāhīmu al-qawāʿid* prayer-block). al-Suyūṭī treats Q 14:35-41 as the **late-Meccan revelation extension** of the al-Baqara prayer-block.

**On Q 14:39's *Ismāʿīl wa-Isḥāq*** ordering: al-Suyūṭī notes the corpus-distinctive ordering (Ishmael first), citing it as a polemical-anchor against the Christian-Jewish primacy-of-Isaac tradition.

## 6. al-Zamakhsharī — *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq ghawāmiḍ al-tanzīl*

**On Q 14:1 (الر + book-reference)**: al-Zamakhsharī catalogs the muqaṭṭaʿāt of Q 14 within the ALR cluster (Q 10/11/12/14/15) and notes the standard *iʿjāz*-of-the-letters position: their meaning is unknowable but their function is challenge.

**On Q 14:24-27 (good-word parable)**: al-Zamakhsharī's *Kashshāf* is one of the principal balagha treatments. He identifies the parable as a **double-tree parable** — the good-tree (vv. 24-25) and the bad-tree (v. 26) — and notes the chiastic structure: the good-tree is *aṣluhā thābit* + *farʿuhā fī al-samāʾ* (rooted-and-rising); the bad-tree is *ujtuthat* + *mā lahā min qarār* (uprooted-and-no-stability). The chiasmus is a balagha hallmark.

**On Q 14:35-41**: al-Zamakhsharī highlights the **vocative variation** between *rabbi* (singular, my Lord, in vv. 35-36, 40) and *rabbanā* (plural, our Lord, in vv. 37-38, 41) — the prayer alternates between personal and corporate framing. al-Zamakhsharī treats this alternation as a balagha device of *iltifāt* (grammatical-shift) within prayer-discourse — a topic well-documented in Abdel Haleem 1992 (`data/literature/balagha/1992-abdel-haleem-grammatical-shift-iltifat-bsoas.md`) and in al-Suyūṭī's *Itqān* nawʿ 53.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī — *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

**On Q 13 → Q 14 → Q 15 *munāsabah***: al-Biqāʿī (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/biqai-nazm-al-durar.pdf`) treats the head-mushaf transitions as a **register-cluster**:
- Q 13 (ALMR, didactic-cosmological) → Q 14 (ALR, cosmological-prayer): the seam is "from corporate cosmological reflection to the personal-prophetic prayer of Abraham". al-Biqāʿī's qualitative claim of strong munāsabah corresponds to the empirical Q 13→Q 14 canonical-adjacency cost = **0.0497** (very low; H-NEW-720) and the Q013-F-05 + Q014-F-02 bilateral architectural-twin pair (d_arch = 0.486 — empirically vindicated).
- Q 14 (ALR, prayer-cosmological) → Q 15 (ALR, iterative-prophet-narrative): the seam is "from the prophetic prayer-affirmation to the prophetic-narrative cycle (Lūṭ + the Hijr-tribe + the Iblīs-rebellion-creation narrative)". al-Biqāʿī treats this as thematically-connected, BUT empirically the Q 14→Q 15 canonical-adjacency cost is **0.1988** (rank ≈13/113, top-15 EXPENSIVE). al-Biqāʿī's munāsabah works at the THEME level (prophet-cycles) but the architectural-axis level shows a register-shift (multi-rāwī + sig_A-positive Q 14 → near-monorhyme + sig_A-negative Q 15).

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī — *Majmaʿ al-bayān fī tafsīr al-Qurʾān* (Imāmī)

**On Q 14:39 (*Ismāʿīl wa-Isḥāq*)**: al-Ṭabarsī's *Majmaʿ al-bayān* on Q 14 is similar in coverage to the Sunnī tafsirs but adds Imāmī-tradition material on the Abrahamic-genealogy theme — the Imāmī tradition emphasizes the prophetic-progeny-line via Ishmael as the proto-Hāshimite line connecting to the Prophet Muḥammad.

**On Q 14:41**: al-Ṭabarsī flags the prayer's *li-wālidayya* (for my parents). Both Sunnī and Imāmī tradition discuss the theological problem: how can Abraham pray for his idolatrous father (compare Q 9:114, where Abraham eventually disavows him)? Classical resolution: the prayer in Q 14:41 is offered before the disavowal of Q 9:114 (chronologically late). But this raises text-theological tensions discussed in al-Rāzī and al-Ṭabarsī.

**Summary**: 8 of 8 surveyed mufassirūn classify Q 14 as Meccan (uncontested). The Mecca-prayer (vv. 35-41) is treated as the **structural and theological centre** by al-Ṭabarī, al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Suyūṭī. The good-word parable (vv. 24-27) is treated as a balagha hallmark by al-Zamakhsharī and al-Rāzī, with the date-palm hadith (Bukhārī #61) as the anchoring tradition. al-Biqāʿī's munāsabah analysis empirically aligns with the Q013-F-05 + Q014-F-02 architectural-twin finding for the Q 13→Q 14 seam, but predicts a closer-than-empirical Q 14→Q 15 seam (the empirical seam is top-15 expensive).
