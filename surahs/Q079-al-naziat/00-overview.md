## 2. Classical name

The surah is named **al-Nāziʿāt** ("the Pullers-Out") after the opening word of v.1 *wa-l-nāziʿāti gharqā* — "by those that pull-out drowningly." The Arabic *al-nāziʿāt* is the active feminine-plural participle from the root n-z-ʿ ("to pull out, tear forth, pull violently"); the cognate accusative *gharqā* (or *gharqan*) intensifies the action. The classical commentators differ on the referent:

- **ANGELS pulling souls of the damned** (al-Ṭabarī majority; Ibn ʿAbbās per al-Ṭabarī chain; al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī; Mujāhid; al-Suddī): *al-nāziʿāt* = the angels of death extracting the souls of disbelievers violently (with *gharqan* = "to the depths" / "drowningly," from gh-r-q "to drown, plunge"). The 5-element oath then reads as a 5-fold typology of angelic activity:
  - v.1 *wa-l-nāziʿāti gharqā* — angels pulling out souls of disbelievers violently
  - v.2 *wa-l-nāshiṭāti nashṭā* — angels pulling out souls of believers gently
  - v.3 *wa-l-sābiḥāti sabḥā* — angels swimming through the heavens
  - v.4 *fa-l-sābiqāti sabqā* — angels racing forward (perhaps to Allah's commands)
  - v.5 *fa-l-mudabbirāti amrā* — angels distributing the divine decree
- **WINDS / STARS (cosmic phenomena)** (Ibn Kathīr secondary; al-Bāqillānī minority): the 5 elements as 5 phenomena of the cosmic order — the winds plucking trees, the gentle breezes, the celestial-sphere-bodies swimming, the racing planets, the angels distributing the divine decrees. A mixed cosmic-and-angelic reading.
- **FIVE STAGES of one cosmic-soteriological process** (al-Rāzī *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* on Q 79:1-5; al-Zamakhsharī *Kashshāf*): violent extraction → gentle release → swimming through the celestial spheres → racing toward Allah → distributing the verdict. al-Rāzī's preferred "integrative" reading.
- **ANGELS at the moment-of-death + scribal angels at the moment-of-judgment** (Ibn al-Qayyim; al-Suyūṭī): the 5 elements split into 2 groups — vv.1-2 = the angels at the moment-of-death; vv.3-5 = the angels at the moment-of-cosmic-judgment.
- **The verses' own self-reflexive reading** (al-Bāqillānī tertiary): minority reading, cf. Q 51 al-Dhāriyāt classical disagreement (where one minority reads the *al-dhāriyāt* as the *verses scattering reminders*).

The **ANGELIC reading is the dominant majority view** and is empirically anchored on the broader Q 79 narrative content: vv.6-9 develop the trumpet/cataclysm; vv.15-26 narrate Mūsā's confrontation with Pharaoh (a paradigmatic prophet-vs-tyrant antinarrative the angels mediate); vv.27-33 catalog the cosmic creation order (earth, mountains, water, pasture); vv.34-46 close with the eschatological tableau. The 5-fold angelic-typology in the opening *jawāb al-qasam* anchors the surah's eschatological argument.

## 4. Thematic structure — the 5-block architecture

Q 79 unfolds in **five thematic blocks**, each marked by a discourse shift:

### Block 1 — The 5-element angelic oath (vv.1-5, 5 verses)

The opening 5-fold oath. Discussed in §3.

### Block 2 — Trumpet + earth-trembling (vv.6-9, 4 verses)

The eschatological trigger:
- v.6 *yawma tarjufu al-rājifa* — "the Day when the trembling-thing trembles"
- v.7 *tatbaʿuhā al-rādifa* — "[and] is followed by the following-thing"
- v.8 *qulūbun yawmaʾidhin wājifa* — "Hearts on that Day will be apprehensive"
- v.9 *abṣāruhā khāshiʿa* — "Their eyes humbled"

This 4-verse block uses 4 different participles (rajafa, radafa, wajafa, khashaʿa) for the eschatological-tremor and the human reaction. The vocabulary cluster includes 1 corpus-unique root: **n$T** (n-sh-ṭ, "to tease/pull gently") in v.2 (in Block 1), but wait — n-sh-ṭ is in the angelic-oath block.

### Block 3 — The disbelievers' question (vv.10-14, 5 verses)

The disbelievers' rhetorical question about resurrection:
- v.10 *yaqūlūna a-innā la-mardūdūna fī al-ḥāfira* — "They say: 'Are we to be returned to our previous state?'"
- v.11 *a-idhā kunnā ʿiẓāman **nakhirah*** — "Even when we have become decayed bones?" (root **n-kh-r** = corpus-unique to Q 79:11)
- v.12 *qālū tilka idhan karratun khāsira* — "They said: 'That, then, is a return at a loss!'"
- v.13 *fa-innamā hiya zajratun wāḥida* — "It is but a single shout"
- v.14 *fa-idhā hum bi-l-***sāhira*** — "And suddenly they will be on the wakeful-plain" (root **s-h-r** in nominal *al-sāhira* = corpus-unique to Q 79:14, hapax)

**Two corpus-hapax root-words in Block 3 alone**: *nakhirah* (decayed) v.11, *al-sāhira* (the wakeful-plain) v.14. This block is ETYMOLOGICALLY DISTINCT.

### Block 4 — The Mūsā vs Pharaoh narrative (vv.15-26, 12 verses)

The Mūsā-Pharaoh confrontation, narrated in extreme compression:
- v.15 *hal atāka ḥadīthu Mūsā* — "Has the story of Mūsā reached you?" (the *hal-atāka* opening is shared with Q 88:1 *hal atāka ḥadīthu al-ghāshiya*)
- v.16 — Mūsā called by his Lord at Ṭuwā, the sacred valley
- v.17 — "Go to Pharaoh; he has transgressed"
- v.18 — "Say: 'Will you be purified?'"
- v.19 — "And I will guide you to your Lord, that you may fear"
- v.20 — "Then he showed him the great sign"
- v.21 *fa-kadhdhaba wa-ʿaṣā* — "But he denied and disobeyed" (the kadhdhaba-and-disobey formula)
- v.22 *thumma adbara yasʿā* — "Then he turned back, hurrying"
- v.23 *fa-ḥashara fa-nādā* — "Then he gathered and called out"
- v.24 *fa-qāla anā rabbukumu al-aʿlā* — "And he said: 'I am your Lord most-high'" (Pharaoh's blasphemy, corpus-unique to Q 79:24 in this exact form; also at Q 28:38 *mā ʿalimtu lakum min ilāhin ghayrī*)
- v.25 *fa-akhadhahu allāh nakāla al-ākhirati wa-l-ūlā* — "So Allah seized him in the punishment of the latter and the former"
- v.26 *inna fī dhālika la-ʿibratan li-man yakhshā* — "Truly in that is a lesson for whoever fears"

This is the **shortest Mūsā-Pharaoh narrative in the corpus** (12 verses; cf. Q 26:10-69 = 60 verses, Q 28:7-43 = 37 verses, Q 7:103-156 = 54 verses, Q 20:9-89 = 81 verses, Q 10:75-93 = 19 verses, Q 11:96-99 = 4 verses, Q 51:38-40 = 3 verses, Q 73:15-16 = 2 verses, Q 85:17-20 = 4 verses). At 12 verses, Q 79's Mūsā-Pharaoh narrative is **rank ~5 in length-economy** among the corpus's Mūsā-Pharaoh tellings — short, dramatic, retributive.

The **Pharaoh self-deification declaration** *anā rabbukumu al-aʿlā* (v.24) is corpus-distinctive in this exact form. The closest parallel is Q 28:38 *mā ʿalimtu lakum min ilāhin ghayrī* ("I do not know of any god for you other than me"), but the Q 79:24 form is more compact and rhetorically punchy — it is the *jawāb al-faʿl* response to the prophetic invitation in v.18-19.

### Block 5 — Cosmic creation order (vv.27-33, 7 verses)

The "creation order" rhetorical sequence. After the Mūsā-Pharaoh narrative, the surah pivots to a cosmological-rebuke argument:
- v.27 *a-antum ashaddu khalqan ami al-samāʾu banāhā* — "Are you stronger in creation, or the heaven? He built it"
- v.28 *rafaʿa **samkahā** fa-sawwāhā* — "He raised its **height** and proportioned it" (root **s-m-k** in nominal *samk* = corpus-unique to Q 79:28, hapax)
- v.29 *wa-***aghṭasha*** laylahā wa-akhraja ḍuḥāhā* — "And **darkened** its night and brought out its forenoon" (root **gh-ṭ-sh** = corpus-unique to Q 79:29, hapax)
- v.30 *wa-l-arḍa baʿda dhālika **daḥāhā*** — "And the earth, after that, He **spread out**" (root **d-ḥ-w** = corpus-unique to Q 79:30, hapax)
- v.31 *akhraja minhā māʾahā wa-marʿāhā* — "He brought forth from it its water and its pasture"
- v.32 *wa-l-jibāla arsāhā* — "And the mountains, He anchored them"
- v.33 *matāʿan lakum wa-li-anʿāmikum* — "[All] as a benefit for you and your livestock"

**Three corpus-unique roots in Block 5**: *samk* (height) v.28, *aghṭasha* (darken) v.29, *daḥā* (spread out) v.30. This is the highest hapax-root density per-verse for any block in the surah (3 hapax in 7 verses = 0.43 per-verse).

The **aghṭasha + daḥā** pairing is theologically resonant: aghṭasha = the dimming-of-night, daḥā = the spreading-of-earth. These are creation-action verbs that occur ONCE in the corpus, both in Q 79:29-30. The "creation order" cosmological sequence at vv.27-33 has the **highest hapax-density** of any creation-order passage in the corpus.

### Block 6 — Eschatological tableau + closing question (vv.34-46, 13 verses)

The closing block. After the cosmological argument, the surah returns to eschatology:
- v.34 *fa-idhā jāʾat al-***ṭāmma*** al-kubrā* — "When the great **calamity** comes" (root **ṭ-m-m** in nominal *al-ṭāmma* = corpus-unique to Q 79:34, hapax)
- v.35 — "the Day when man recalls all his strivings"
- v.36 — "and Hell is brought forth for him who sees"
- vv.37-39 — the transgressor's fate (Hell as abode)
- vv.40-41 — the God-fearing one's fate (Paradise as abode)
- vv.42-46 — the closing rhetorical question about "the Hour"
  - v.42 *yasʾalūnaka ʿani al-sāʿati ayyāna mursāhā* — "They ask you about the Hour: when is its anchor?"
  - v.43 *fīma anta min dhikrāhā* — "On what (basis) are you (regarding) its remembrance?"
  - v.44 *ilā rabbika muntahāhā* — "To your Lord is its termination"
  - v.45 *innamā anta mundhirū man yakhshāhā* — "You are but a warner of him who fears it"
  - v.46 *kaʾannahum yawma yarawnahā lam yalbathū illā ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā* — "On the Day they see it, [it will seem] they had not lingered except for an evening or its forenoon"

The closing v.46 is the **time-foreshortening / eschatological-distance-collapse** trope — the disbelievers, on judgment-day, will perceive their entire worldly life as having lasted only "an evening or a forenoon." This trope is shared with **Q 23:113** (*qālū labithnā yawman aw baʿḍa yawmin*), **Q 30:55** (*lam yalbathū ghayra sāʿatin*), **Q 46:35** (*lam yalbathū illā sāʿatan min nahār*), **Q 75:35** (*kāna labitha mā labitha aḥadan*), and **Q 78:8** (mid-section). The Q 79:46 form is corpus-distinctive in its specific *ʿashiyyatan aw ḍuḥāhā* phrasing.
