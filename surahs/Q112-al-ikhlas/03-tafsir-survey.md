## 1. al-Ṭabarī (d. 310/923) — *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabari-jami-bayan.openiti.raw.txt` (passage extraction pending; cited at level of canonical position).

**Major positions on Q 112**:
- **v.1 *aḥad***: al-Ṭabarī defends *aḥad* (not *wāḥid*) as conveying "the unsharable one" (cited Ibn ʿAbbās via Mujāhid).
- **v.2 *al-ṣamad***: al-Ṭabarī catalogues ≥10 classical glosses; the dominant report (via Ibn ʿAbbās → Saʿīd b. Jubayr → Ikrima) is *al-sayyid lladhī yuṣmadu ilayhi fī l-ḥawāʾij* — "the sovereign to whom one resorts in needs". A second cluster (al-Shaʿbī, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī) glosses *al-ṣamad* as *al-bāqī lladhī lā yafnā* — "the eternal who does not perish". A third cluster (Mujāhid via others) glosses as *al-musmat lladhī laysa lahu jawf* — "the solid that has no internal cavity". al-Ṭabarī tentatively endorses the first.
- **vv.3-4**: al-Ṭabarī treats these as direct refutations of (i) the Christian-Jewish-pagan attribution of children to God; (ii) the very possibility of a divine peer.
- **Asbāb al-nuzūl**: al-Ṭabarī cites a tradition via Ibn ʿAbbās that the Meccan polytheists (or, in another report, a Jewish delegation) asked the Prophet to describe his Lord, and Q 112 was revealed in response. al-Ṭabarī notes the chain is not at the highest grade.

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538/1144) — *al-Kashshāf*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/zamakhshari-kashshaf.openiti.raw.txt` (passage extraction pending).

**Major positions**:
- **Rhetorical structure**: al-Zamakhsharī notes the chiasmic-like structure: v.1 affirms unity; v.2 affirms self-sufficiency; v.3 negates procreation in both directions; v.4 negates peer. He emphasizes the **affirmation-then-negation** structure as a rhetorical *ḥaṣr* (exclusive determination).
- **v.2 *al-ṣamad***: al-Zamakhsharī (consistent with his Muʿtazilite leaning) emphasizes *al-ṣamad* as denoting **complete-self-sufficiency-with-no-organic-cavity** — i.e., no internal multiplicity, no divisibility. He treats this as theologically-equivalent to saying God is *not a body*.
- **v.3 *lam yalid wa-lam yūlad***: al-Zamakhsharī explicitly cites this as a refutation of Christian doctrine of divine sonship + Jewish *ʿUzayr ibn Allāh* + pagan *banāt Allāh*. He notes the morphological active/passive pairing on a single root (wld) as a rhetorical economy.
- **v.4 *kufuwan***: al-Zamakhsharī glosses *kufu* as "ndd" (peer/equivalent), noting the absence of any peer covers all forms of divine "equation" (*tashbīh* with creatures).

## 3. al-Rāzī (d. 606/1209) — *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/razi-mafatih-al-ghayb.openiti.raw.txt`. Cross-reference: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/razi-99names-extract.md`.

**Major positions**:
- **Surah's *thuluth al-Qurʾān* status**: al-Rāzī devotes substantial space to the *thuluth al-Qurʾān* hadith. He proposes the empirical-rationale: the Quran's content can be divided into **(1) tawḥīd, (2) prophecy/messengership/scripture, (3) eschatology/resurrection**. Q 112 covers (1) in full and is the canonical scriptural locus of pure tawḥīd. Therefore "a third of the Quran" reads as: Q 112 covers one of the Quran's three central content-axes.
- **v.1 *qul***: al-Rāzī notes the *qul* is part of the recitation (i.e., the Prophet is instructed to *say* "Say"), making the surah doubly self-referential.
- **v.2 *al-ṣamad***: al-Rāzī catalogues 12 classical glosses (close to the al-Ṭabarī 10), arguing the gloss *al-bāqī lladhī lā yafnā* (eternal) is most consistent with the surrounding theology of v.3 (no procreation = no temporal beginning, no peer).
- **Theological reasoning structure**: al-Rāzī treats the 4 verses as a complete formal *kalām* proof: aḥad → ṣamad (because dependence requires multiplicity) → no procreation (because procreation requires temporality) → no peer (because a peer would imply a second aḥad, contradiction).

## 4. al-Qurṭubī (d. 671/1273) — *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:
- **Surah's *fadāʾil***: al-Qurṭubī catalogues the principal *thuluth al-Qurʾān* traditions (al-Bukhārī, Muslim, al-Tirmidhī) and notes the practice of nightly recitation + post-ṣalāh recitation.
- **v.1 *aḥad***: al-Qurṭubī's discussion follows al-Ṭabarī, with extended *fiqh*-implications for the *kalimat al-shahāda*.
- **v.2 *al-ṣamad***: al-Qurṭubī surveys glosses as al-Ṭabarī, adding the position of al-Khalīl b. Aḥmad: *al-sayyid alladhī intahā ilayhi al-suʾdud* — "the lord to whom sovereignty's full extent reaches".
- **v.3-4**: al-Qurṭubī reads vv.3-4 as the *complete refutation* of the three classes of *shirk*: (i) ascribing children to God (Christians, Jews, pagan-Arabians); (ii) ascribing peers to God (general polytheism); (iii) ascribing parentage to God (a possibility he treats as covered by v.3's *yūlad*-negation).

## 5. Ibn Kathīr (d. 774/1373) — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿaẓīm*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/ibn-kathir-tafsir-quran.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:
- **Asbāb al-nuzūl**: Ibn Kathīr cites multiple chains: (i) Ibn ʿAbbās — Meccan polytheists asking *unsub lanā rabbaka* ("describe your Lord to us in lineage-terms"); (ii) Ubayy b. Kaʿb — Jews asking the Prophet to describe his Lord; (iii) Saʿīd b. Jubayr — Christians similarly. Ibn Kathīr treats the three reports as collectively confirming that the surah responds to attempts to *anthropomorphize* or *genealogize* God, regardless of which group is the immediate occasion.
- **v.2 *al-ṣamad***: Ibn Kathīr cites Ibn ʿAbbās' "the chief unto whom all matters are referred" gloss as primary; he also cites the lexical position that *al-ṣamad* derives from *ṣamada ilā* ("to resort to / aim at").
- **Defense of muʿawwidhāt-status of Q 113-114** (which is most-prominently a Q 113-114 issue, but Ibn Kathīr addresses it in a Q 112-context discussion of fadāʾil): Ibn Kathīr defends Q 113 and Q 114 as canonical against the Ibn Masʿūd reports (which we treat in `Q113-al-falaq/03-tafsir-survey.md` and `Q114-al-nas/03-tafsir-survey.md`).
- **Recitation tradition**: Ibn Kathīr cites the Prophet's hand-blowing tradition (al-Bukhārī ḥadīth #5017-#5018) where the Prophet would recite Q 112, Q 113, Q 114 into his cupped hands and pass them over his body each night.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (d. 911/1505) — *al-Durr al-manthūr* + *al-Itqān*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/suyuti-durr-manthur.openiti.raw.txt` and `suyuti-itqan.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:
- **al-Durr al-manthūr Q 112**: al-Suyūṭī compiles dozens of *fadāʾil*-traditions from across the 9 books (Bukhārī, Muslim, Tirmidhī, Nasāʾī, Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah, Aḥmad, Mālik, al-Dārimī) — the densest *fadāʾil* compilation for any single surah after Q 1. The compilation includes the *thuluth al-Qurʾān* chains, the nightly-recitation chains, the post-ṣalāh-recitation chains, and the hand-blowing chain.
- **al-Itqān**: al-Suyūṭī treats Q 112 in the *al-fadāʾil* nawʿ; lists Q 112's classical names (al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Tawḥīd, al-Asās, etc.); and discusses the Meccan-vs-Medinan classification disagreement, tentatively favoring the Meccan classification while acknowledging Medinan reports.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī (d. 885/1480) — *Naẓm al-durar fī tanāsub al-āyāt wa-l-suwar*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions**:
- **Munāsaba between Q 111 al-Masad and Q 112 al-Ikhlāṣ**: al-Biqāʿī (per his *naẓm al-durar* method) treats Q 111-Q 112 as a structural-thematic transition: Q 111 catalogues the temporal-failure of Abū Lahab (the antagonist whose hands "perish" in the curse-formula); Q 112 declares the eternal-success of God (whose attributes negate any temporal-bounded failure). The transition is from *creature-failure* to *creator-success*.
- **Munāsaba between Q 112 and Q 113**: al-Biqāʿī treats this as the transition from the *theology of refuge-source* (Q 112: God who is one, eternal, peer-less) to the *act of taking-refuge* (Q 113-114: refuge-formulae). One declares the Refuge-Giver, the next two execute the refuge-act.
- **Internal munāsaba within Q 112**: 4 verses, 4 attributes, in logical order — unity → self-sufficiency → no procreation → no peer. al-Biqāʿī presents this as a complete *kalām* sequence.

## 8. al-Ṭabarsī (d. 548/1153) — *Majmaʿ al-bayān*

Source-master: `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/tabarsi-majma-bayan.openiti.raw.txt`.

**Major positions** (Shīʿī-Imāmī perspective):
- al-Ṭabarsī treats the surah as the canonical pure-tawḥīd locus, citing the same *thuluth al-Qurʾān* tradition.
- al-Ṭabarsī adds Imāmī-specific traditions on the surah's recitation merits (e.g., al-Kāfī chains attributing distinct rewards for daily-recitation counts: 100 = forgiveness, 1000 = Paradise — these are graded weak even within Imāmī chain-grading).
- *al-ṣamad*: al-Ṭabarsī follows the same gloss-cluster, with Imāmī-tradition emphasizing *al-bāqī* (eternal).

## 9. Convergence and disagreement

### Convergence (all 8 mufassirūn agree)
- The surah is a complete pure-tawḥīd statement.
- v.3 refutes ascribing children to God (Christians, Jews, pagans).
- v.4 refutes ascribing peers to God.
- The surah carries massive *fadāʾil* (recitation merits) including *thuluth al-Qurʾān*.

### Disagreement (cross-tafsir disputes)
- **Asbāb al-nuzūl identity**: Meccan polytheists (al-Ṭabarī primary), Jews (Ibn Kathīr secondary), or Christians (some Ibn Kathīr chains)? — likely all three over the surah's transmission history; the polemic-content is compatible with all three frames.
- **Meccan vs Medinan**: classical disagreement; al-Suyūṭī favors Meccan, some reports favor Medinan. Numerical chronology in `data/revelation-order.csv` follows the Egyptian/al-Suyūṭī standard at order 22.
- ***al-ṣamad* primary gloss**: 3-4 main glosses (sovereign-resorted-to / eternal / non-cavitied / complete) — convergent in implication (self-sufficiency) but lexically distinct.
