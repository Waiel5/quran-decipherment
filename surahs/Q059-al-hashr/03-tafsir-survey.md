This file surveys major classical and modern tafāsīr on Q 59. Heavy emphasis on:
- The Banū al-Naḍīr asbāb (vv 1-7).
- The fayʾ doctrine (vv 6-7) — distinctive jurisprudential anchor.
- The 3-tier ummah typology (vv 8-10) — *īthār* paragon at v.9.
- The mountain-Quran parable (v.21).
- The Khawātim al-Ḥashr (vv 22-24) — divine-name engineering climax.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (d. 310/923) — *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān*

**Status**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED.

al-Ṭabarī's al-Ḥashr coverage is in the standard editions vol. 28 (per the Markaz al-Buḥūth wa-l-Dirāsāt al-ʿArabiyyah ed. Cairo 1955-1969 24-vol set; standard placement) or vol. 12 (the 30-vol Hijaz Press ed.).

**Key positions**:
- vv 2-5 Banū al-Naḍīr: catalog of multiple *isnād*-chained narrations from Ibn Isḥāq, ʿUrwah ibn al-Zubayr, and al-Zuhrī. al-Ṭabarī notes the disagreement on dating (3 AH vs 4 AH) and adjudicates in favor of 4 AH per the majority of his *Sīrah*-tradition sources.
- v.5 *līnah*: al-Ṭabarī catalogs ~5 classical readings of *līnah* — palm-tree, fine-quality date palm, young palm — and selects the general-palm reading.
- vv 22-24 Khawātim: al-Ṭabarī is more economical here than later mufassirūn. His treatment focuses on the *لا إله إلا هو* anaphora and the **عالم الغيب والشهادة** as the surah-spanning epistemological frame (God's knowledge encompasses what is hidden + what is witnessed → the Banū al-Naḍīr's hidden plot was witnessed).
- v.23 al-Mutakabbir: al-Ṭabarī notes the *takabbur* root carries negative valence when applied to humans (= arrogance) but positive when applied to God (= rightful supremacy) — the classical *al-takabbur al-jāʾiz fī ḥaqq Allāh* doctrine that al-Ghazālī later expanded in *al-Maqṣad al-Asnā*.

## 2. al-Wāḥidī (d. 468/1076) — *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*

**Status**: VERIFIED (Cairo Dār al-Iṣlāḥ 1992 edition; standard reference).

al-Wāḥidī gives the canonical asbāb sequence for Q 59:
- **vv 1-7**: Banū al-Naḍīr expulsion. Cites al-Bukhārī #4029 + #4031 narrating from Ibn ʿAbbās the trigger event (Naḍīr plot to drop a stone on the Prophet's head; Gabriel informs the Prophet; expulsion-ultimatum follows).
- **v.5** *līnah*: Cites the Prophet's reasoning that the cutting was strategically necessary; mentions the alleged hypocrite-claim that this constituted sinful destruction → divine vindication via v.5.
- **vv 8-10**: collective asbāb attributed to a single sermon-context where the muhājirūn declined to share the fayʾ wealth out of īthār toward the anṣār — and the verses descend praising both groups.
- **vv 11-17**: tied to the period after the Naḍīr expulsion when the hypocrites broke their promise to side with them.
- **vv 22-24**: NO specific sabab; recognized as *cosmic* descent (one of the surah's universal-theological closings, not tied to a specific historical incident).

## 3. al-Zamakhsharī (d. 538/1144) — *al-Kashshāf*

**Status**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (Beirut Dār al-Maʿrifah 4-vol ed. standard).

al-Zamakhsharī's Muʿtazilī *Kashshāf* treats Q 59 with characteristic emphasis on rhetorical figures:
- **vv 11-12 hypocrite portrait**: cited as paradigmatic *kadhib mubālagh* (intensified-falsehood) figure — the conditional triplet (*la-ʾin ukhrijtum / la-ʾin qūtiltum / la-ʾin naṣarūhum*) is held up as a **rhetorically-inflated** false-promise structure.
- **vv 16-17 satanic-pact parable**: full-allegorical *tashbīh tamthīlī* — the comparison-vehicle (Satan-betrays-man) and comparison-tenor (hypocrites-betray-Naḍīr) operate at the level of the structural-similitude, not single-feature.
- **v.21 mountain-Quran**: al-Zamakhsharī reads the parable as a *taqdīr maʿnawī* (counter-factual hypothetical) emphasizing the contrast between mountain-sensibility-to-divine-weight and human-heart-deafness.
- **vv 22-24 Khawātim**: al-Zamakhsharī's treatment of the Khawātim is the **classical *jamʿ al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā* anchor** — the climax-aggregation reading that later mufassirūn (al-Rāzī, al-Biqāʿī) build on.

## 4. al-Rāzī (d. 606/1209) — *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb / al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr*

**Status**: VERIFIED on volume placement (vol. 29 of the Beirut Dār Iḥyāʾ al-Turāth 32-vol ed.; mechanically derivable from canonical-mushaf-order). Specific page ranges PENDING physical verification.

al-Rāzī's Q 59 treatment is the **most extensive classical commentary on this surah**. Key emphases:
- **v.1 musabbiḥa-perfect form**: al-Rāzī treats the perfect-vs-imperfect tense distinction at length, anticipating the H-NEW-58c finding by ~800 years. His reading: *sabbaḥa* (perfect) implies the universe HAS-ALWAYS-BEEN glorifying — a *qidam* (eternity) inflection — vs *yusabbiḥu* (imperfect) which implies the same action *durative-ongoing*. Both forms are theologically equivalent but stylistically distinguished.
- **vv 6-7 fayʾ vs ghanīma**: al-Rāzī systematically distinguishes the two terms and ties the fayʾ doctrine to the broader *taʿdīl al-tawzīʿ* (justice-in-distribution) principle, becoming a foundational anchor for classical Islamic distributive economics.
- **v.9 *yuʾthirūna ʿalā anfusihim***: al-Rāzī's section on *īthār* runs ~5 pages in the Dār Iḥyāʾ ed., extracting a full ethical doctrine of self-preference-of-others.
- **v.21 mountain-Quran parable**: al-Rāzī discusses the parable's three readings — (a) literal counter-factual (the mountain WOULD have humbled), (b) figurative for Quranic-weight, (c) didactic-rebuke to humans-who-do-not-tremble. He selects the layered (a)+(c) reading.
- **vv 22-24 Khawātim**: al-Rāzī's section is the **theological summa on the divine-name catalogue**. Each of the 8-9 Khawātim-exclusive names receives a dedicated paragraph treating its semantic range, theological force, and inter-name relationships. Notable position: *al-Mutakabbir* is read as the *mutaʿālī al-mustaḥiqq li-l-takabbur* — the One who is rightfully-supreme; this name's negative-valence-when-applied-to-humans is foundational for the *adab al-tawakkul* (etiquette of trust-in-God) literature.

al-Rāzī's treatment of *لا إله إلا هو* twin-opener at vv 22-23 frames the Khawātim as a *talāḥum al-asmāʾ* (interlocked-naming) where the negation-of-rivals (*لا إله إلا هو*) STRUCTURALLY PRECEDES the affirmation-of-attributes (the catalogue of names) — establishing the *ʿaqīdah-then-ṣifāt* sequence.

## 5. al-Qurṭubī (d. 671/1273) — *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

**Status**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (Cairo Dār al-Kutub al-Miṣriyyah ed.).

al-Qurṭubī's *aḥkām*-tafsīr emphasizes legal-jurisprudential extraction:
- **v.5 *līnah***: the **definitive classical jurisprudential locus** for the rules of cutting trees during military operations. al-Qurṭubī catalogs Mālikī, Ḥanafī, Shāfiʿī, Ḥanbalī positions and provides his own Mālikī adjudication.
- **vv 6-7 fayʾ doctrine**: al-Qurṭubī's section here is heavily fiqh-laden — distribution shares, beneficiary categories, the *dhū al-qurbā* (kin) clause and its post-Prophetic continuation debate.
- **v.7 *wa-mā ātākum al-rasūl fa-khudhūhu wa-mā nahākum ʿanhu fa-ntahū***: this verse is one of the foundational Sunnī arguments for the *Sunnah* as a standalone source of legal authority. al-Qurṭubī cites Imām al-Shāfiʿī's *al-Risālah* extensively here.

## 6. Ibn Kathīr (d. 774/1373) — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*

**Status**: VERIFIED (multiple modern editions; the standard ḥadīth-driven Sunnī tafsīr).

Ibn Kathīr's Q 59 is hadith-rich and asbāb-driven:
- **vv 2-5**: extensive narration-by-narration treatment of the Banū al-Naḍīr expulsion drawing on Ibn Isḥāq, al-Wāqidī, al-Bukhārī, and Muslim.
- **vv 8-10**: cites the famous narration of the Prophet's tears at the recitation of v.10 (the third-generation forgiveness petition) — the Prophet hoped his ummah would always include those who pray for predecessors.
- **vv 22-24**: Ibn Kathīr cites the **al-Tirmidhī #2922 + #3478** *fadāʾil* of the Khawātim — whoever recites the last 3 verses morning and evening receives the prayers of 70,000 angels until evening/morning, and dies a martyr if dying that day.

For the Ism al-Aʿẓam tradition (Tirmidhī #3478, Ibn Mājah #3856, etc.), see `04-hadith-corpus.md`.

## 7. al-Biqāʿī (d. 885/1480) — *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

**Status**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (Beirut Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyya 22-vol ed. — Mahdī ed.). Specific page ranges PENDING per `hashr-verification-memo.md`.

al-Biqāʿī's *munāsabāt*-tafsīr is the most architecturally relevant for the project's structural-empirical findings:
- **Q 58 → Q 59 munāsabah**: Q 58 al-Mujādilah closes with promised divine-victory (vv 19-22); Q 59 al-Ḥashr operationalizes that promise via the Banū al-Naḍīr expulsion. al-Biqāʿī reads Q 58→Q 59 as a **promise→fulfillment pair**.
- **Internal Q 59 munāsabāt**: al-Biqāʿī gives a tight reading of how the surah moves from cosmic glorification (v.1) → historical-victory (vv 2-7) → ummah-typology (vv 8-10) → counter-portrait of the unfaithful (vv 11-17) → ethical-hortatives (vv 18-21) → Khawātim climax (vv 22-24). Each transition is mapped as a *munāsabah* of theme and rhetorical mode.
- **The mountain-Quran parable (v.21) → Khawātim**: al-Biqāʿī specifically reads v.21 as the *tamhīd* (preparation) for the Khawātim's *jamʿ al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā* — the parable establishes the cosmic-weight of the Quran, the Khawātim catalogues the cosmic-weight-bearing source.
- **Q 59 → Q 60 munāsabah**: Q 59 closes with Khawātim-cosmic-naming; Q 60 al-Mumtaḥanah opens with practical-ethical fellowship-ban — al-Biqāʿī reads Q 60 as the application-side ethics of the Khawātim's theological grounding.

al-Biqāʿī's positioning of the Khawātim as an architectural climax provides the **classical anchor** for the project's empirical [[h-new-95-khawatim-extension|H-NEW-95]] finding (corpus rank-1 F99=19).

## 8. al-Suyūṭī (d. 911/1505) — *al-Itqān fī ʿUlūm al-Qurʾān* + *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn*

**Status**: VERIFIED on doctrinal placement; specific *Itqān* nawʿ + page ranges PENDING per `hashr-verification-memo.md`.

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* discusses Q 59 in:
- **nawʿ 17** (*sabab al-tasmiyah* = naming): the surah is named *al-Ḥashr* (the gathering) after v.2 *li-awwali al-ḥashr*. Alternative names: *Sūrat Banī al-Naḍīr*.
- **nawʿ 7** (asbāb al-nuzūl): aggregates al-Wāḥidī + Ibn Kathīr.
- **nawʿ 59** (fawāṣil al-āyāt): probably treats the Q 59 fāṣila pattern (mostly ن-final). PENDING physical check.
- **nawʿ 65** (badīʿ): possibly cites the twin-opener at vv 22-23 as a paradigm of *takrīr maḥmūd*. PENDING physical check.

al-Suyūṭī also collects the Ism al-Aʿẓam traditions in *al-Itqān* — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`.

The *Tafsīr al-Jalālayn* (al-Suyūṭī completing al-Maḥallī) is a brief 2-line-per-verse gloss — useful for consensus-glosses but not for novel positions.

## 9. al-Ālūsī (d. 1270/1854) — *Rūḥ al-Maʿānī*

**Status**: SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED.

al-Ālūsī's late-Ottoman tafsīr synthesizes earlier sources with a Sufi inflection:
- **vv 22-24 Khawātim**: al-Ālūsī's reading emphasizes the **mystical-unitive** dimension of the *لا إله إلا هو* anaphora — the formula functions as a *dhikr*-anchor in Sufi *adhkār*-literature, and the Khawātim are read as a mystical-recitation block analogous to *Āyat al-Kursī* (Q 2:255).

## 10. Modern tafāsīr

### 10a. Sayyid Quṭb (d. 1966) — *Fī Ẓilāl al-Qurʾān*

**Status**: VERIFIED.

Quṭb's Q 59 is heavily socio-political:
- **vv 6-7 fayʾ + *kay lā yakūna dūlatan***: foundational for Quṭb's distributive-Islamic-economics articulation.
- **vv 8-10 ummah typology**: paradigm for the muhājirūn-anṣār-followers ideal-society.
- **vv 22-24 Khawātim**: Quṭb's treatment is brief but emphasizes the *taṣwīr* (image-painting) of God's transcendence as the appropriate climax to the historical-political surah-content.

### 10b. al-Ṭāhir Ibn ʿĀshūr (d. 1973) — *al-Taḥrīr wa-l-Tanwīr*

**Status**: VERIFIED.

Ibn ʿĀshūr's Tunisian-rationalist tafsīr extensively cross-references al-Rāzī. His v.21 mountain-Quran reading is particularly developed — the parable is treated as **iʿjāz-self-claim** (the Quran asserts its own weight), parallel to other corpus self-reflexive passages (Q 39:23, Q 17:88).

### 10c. Mawdūdī (d. 1979) — *Tafhīm al-Qurʾān*

**Status**: VERIFIED.

Mawdūdī's Urdu-language tafsīr provides extensive socio-historical context for the Banū al-Naḍīr expulsion, drawing on Sīra-tradition heavily. His treatment of the 3-tier ummah typology (vv 8-10) is foundational for South-Asian Islamic-revivalist thought.
