## 2. Classical name

Named after the opening word of v.1 *wa-l-shams* ("by the sun"). Arabic *al-shams* is the universal-cosmic-luminary feminine-grammatical noun. al-Suyūṭī *al-Itqān* nawʿ 17 lists Q 91 in the *aqsām al-Qurʾān* taxonomy as a paradigmatic early-Meccan oath-surah. The classical convention names every short-Meccan oath-surah after the FIRST sworn-by NP — hence al-Shams (sun) over the equally-prominent qamar (moon), nahār (day), layl (night), samāʾ (heaven), arḍ (earth), or nafs (soul).

## 10. Quick content structure

- **vv. 1-7**: SEVEN-OATH OPENER (sun, moon, day, night, heaven, earth, soul) — the corpus's longest contiguous oath-cluster.
- **vv. 8-10**: ETHICAL-AXIOM JAWĀB — God inspires the soul with both *fujūr* (transgression) and *taqwā* (piety); the one who purifies the soul prospers, the one who corrupts it fails.
- **vv. 11-15**: THAMŪD-NARRATIVE — the proof-text. Thamūd denied; the wretched-one was dispatched; the messenger warned (the she-camel and her water-share); they hamstrung her; God destroyed them; He fears not the consequence.

The two halves are causally bound: vv 1-10 establish the *moral cosmos* (the ordered-cosmos as warrant for the soul-purification axiom); vv 11-15 supply the *historical proof* (Thamūd as exemplary failure of soul-purification → divine destruction).

## 11. Q 91:13 — the Prophet-Messenger formula and divine-naming pattern

Q 91:13 contains a unique syntactic pattern: *fa-qāla la-hum rasūlu llāh: nāqata llāh wa-suqyā-hā* ("And the messenger of God said to them: [Beware!] the she-camel of God and her water-share!"). The naming pattern *rasūlu llāh* + *nāqatu llāh* in a single verse is **corpus-unique outside Muḥammadan-context verses**.

Compare the parallel construction at Q 7:73 (*hādhihi nāqatu llāhi la-kum āyatan*), Q 11:64 (*hādhihi nāqatu llāh*), Q 26:155 (*hādhihi nāqatun*), Q 54:27 (*innā mursilū l-nāqati fitnatan la-hum*). Q 91:13 is the **only Thamūd-witness** to combine *rasūlu llāh* + *nāqatu llāh* in genitive-construct apposition within a single verse — compressing the messenger-warrant and the camel-sign into a single 7-word phrase.
