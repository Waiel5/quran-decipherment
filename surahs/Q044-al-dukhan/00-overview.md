## 2. Position in HM-7 cluster

Q 44 is the **shortest HM-7 surah** by word count (364 words; the next-shortest is Q 46 al-Aḥqāf at 676 words, almost 2× longer). It is the **most semantically dense** HM-7 surah on a per-verse basis.

It is also **the Q 43 ↔ Q 44 opening twin**: both surahs open with حم + *wa-l-Kitābi al-mubīn* (Q 43:1-2 = Q 44:1-2 verbatim). This makes Q 43-44 a **direct opening-formula pair** — classical exegetes (al-Biqāʿī, *Naẓm al-durar* ad Q 44:1) note this as a deliberate liturgical-paired-pair structure.

## 3. Distinctive features

### 3.1 The *laylatin mubāraka* / Night of Decree (Q 44:3-6)
*Innā anzalnāhu fī laylatin mubāraka innā kunnā mundhirīn. Fīhā yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm.* — "We sent it down on a blessed night… therein every wise affair is decreed".

Two classical readings:
- **Layla al-Qadr** reading (al-Ṭabarī ad loc.; Ibn Kathīr ad loc.; majority): the "blessed night" is the Night of Decree (Q 97:1) in Ramaḍān.
- **Layla al-Niṣf min Shaʿbān** reading (minority; ʿIkrima via al-Ṭabarsī, *Majmaʿ al-bayān*): the blessed night is the night of mid-Shaʿbān.

The majority Layla al-Qadr reading dominates Sunni-mainstream and is reinforced by Q 97:1 *innā anzalnāhu fī laylati al-qadr*. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān*, nawʿ 16 (the night of revelation), prefers the Layla al-Qadr reading.

### 3.2 The *yawm yaʾtī al-samāʾu bi-dukhān mubīn* — the smoke-sign (Q 44:10-11)
*Fa-rtaqib yawma taʾtī al-samāʾu bi-dukhānin mubīn. Yaghshā al-nāsa hādhā ʿadhābun alīm.* — "Watch for the day when the sky will bring an obvious smoke. It will cover the people. This is a painful punishment."

Classical-exegetical disagreement (Bukhārī, ḥadīth #4774-4823 series; *Tafsīr Sūrat al-Dukhān* in Bukhārī's *Kitāb al-Tafsīr*):
- **Past-event reading** (Ibn Masʿūd via Bukhārī): the smoke is a **historical** event — the Quraysh famine after the Prophet's curse, when they were so hungry they saw "smoke" between earth and sky from weakness of vision.
- **Future-eschatological reading** (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī, al-Ḥasan via al-Ṭabarī ad loc.): the smoke is a *yet-to-come* sign of the Hour, one of the *ʿalāmāt al-sāʿa al-kubrā*.

al-Bukhārī's *Tafsīr* book preserves both, with the historical reading attributed to Ibn Masʿūd narrating the famine-causation. The eschatological reading is supported by the Ibn ṣayyād hadith (Ibn Kathīr ad Q 44, opening) where the Prophet hides the surah-mention as a test for Ibn Ṣayyād's prophetic-claim, and Ibn Ṣayyād answers "*huwa al-dukh*" — confirming the eschatological reading was active in the Prophet's environment.

### 3.3 The Mūsā / Pharaoh narrative (Q 44:17-33)
Compact (vs. Q 43's longer Mūsā section): the night-departure of Banū Isrāʾīl, the drowning of Pharaoh, the inheritance-of-blessings. v. 25-28 is the elegiac account of Pharaoh's lost wealth: *kam tarakū min jannātin wa-ʿuyūn* — "How many gardens and springs they left behind" — classical *qaṣīda*-style elegy.

### 3.4 The eschatological *zaqqūm* description (Q 44:43-50)
*Inna shajarata al-zaqqūm. Ṭaʿāmu al-athīm. Ka-l-muhli yaghlī fī al-buṭūn ka-ghalyi al-ḥamīm.* — The *zaqqūm* tree, food of the sinner, like molten metal boiling in the bellies. Q 44 is one of the **three primary zaqqūm-locus surahs** in the Meccan corpus (with Q 37:62-66 and Q 56:52).

### 3.5 The Paradise description (Q 44:51-57)
*Inna al-muttaqīna fī maqāmin amīn… yalbasūna min sundusin wa-istabraq…* — Paradise: silk and brocade, *ḥūr ʿīn* (paired-companions), security. The Q 44 paradise-description is the **prototype for HM-B Paradise material** (Q 43:71-73 and Q 45:30 develop similar but shorter accounts).
