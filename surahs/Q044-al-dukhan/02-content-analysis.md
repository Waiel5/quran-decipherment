## 2. The *laylatin mubāraka* passage (Q 44:3-6) — distinctive feature

Q 44:3-4 reads: *innā anzalnāhu fī laylatin mubāraka… fīhā yufraqu kullu amrin ḥakīm* (verified `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json` Q44:3-4). The *yufraqu* form is **a Qurʾānic hapax in this active sense** — it occurs at Q 44:4 alone with the meaning "is decreed/distinguished/separated"; the other 4 *yufraqu* occurrences (Q 2:102, Q 4:150, Q 4:152, Q 9:56) are about *separating between persons*, not about decree-distribution. The Q 44:4 *yufraqu* is the **decree-distribution sense**, anchoring the *laylat al-qadr* exegesis (verified by full-corpus regex this session).

The verse triggered the longest classical Quranic-night controversy:
- **Layla al-Qadr (Q 97:1) reading** — majority. al-Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Qurṭubī, al-Rāzī, al-Suyūṭī all prefer this; cross-anchored to Q 97:1 *innā anzalnāhu fī laylati al-qadr* (the only other "innā anzalnāhu" plus night-noun pair in the Qurʾān).
- **Layla al-Niṣf min Shaʿbān reading** — minority, ʿIkrima via al-Qurṭubī (`data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/qurtubi-jami-ahkam.openiti.raw.txt:144651-144680`); also al-Rāzī's catalog of the "four names" of the Shaʿbān-night (al-mubāraka, al-barāʾa, al-ṣakk, al-raḥma).

The classical adjudicators (Ibn Kathīr `:108428-108455`, al-Qurṭubī `:144651-144680`, al-Rāzī `:218290-218360`) all reject the Shaʿbān reading on the grounds of Q 2:185 (*shahru ramaḍāna alladhī unzila fīhi al-Qurʾān*) — the Qurʾān explicitly locates revelation in Ramaḍān, and the *laylatin mubāraka* must be in Ramaḍān, hence laylat al-qadr.

## 3. The smoke-sign passage (Q 44:9-16)

The eponymous *dukhān* passage. Lexical observation (this session, regex on `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json`): the noun *dukhān* (دخان) appears only **TWICE** in the entire Quran:
- Q 41:11 (cosmogonic): *thumma istawā ilā al-samāʾi wa-hiya dukhānun* — heavens-as-smoke at creation.
- Q 44:10 (eschatological): *yawma taʾtī al-samāʾu bi-dukhānin mubīn* — sky-bringing-smoke at the Hour.

The corpus-rare *dukhān* lexeme thus brackets cosmic time: the heavens were *dukhān* at the start (Q 41:11), and the heavens will bring *dukhān* at the end (Q 44:10). The two occurrences are within HM-7 — the cluster carries both poles of the cosmic *dukhān*-bracket. **This is a 2-verse hapax-pair structural fact** (no third *dukhān* attestation in the Qurʾān).

The passage's classical-exegetical disagreement on whether the smoke-sign is past or future is preserved in:
- **Past-event reading** (Ibn Masʿūd, in Bukhārī `kitāb al-tafsīr` Sūrat al-Dukhān; Ibn Kathīr `:108460-108495`): the smoke is the Quraysh famine — they were so weak they saw "smoke" between earth and sky from hunger.
- **Future-eschatological reading** (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī, Abū Hurayra, the *ten signs* hadith — Muslim #2901 and parallels): one of the *ʿalāmāt al-sāʿa al-kubrā*; smoke that fills earth-and-sky for forty days, gives believers a head-cold but suffocates disbelievers.
- **Ibn Kathīr's adjudication** (`:108526-108550`): preserves both; the *Ibn Ṣayyād* hadith (Bukhārī #1354/2638/3055; Muslim #2924, *huwa al-dukh* — Ibn Ṣayyād's clipped utterance when the Prophet hides Q 44:10 as a test) signals the eschatological reading was active in the Prophet's environment.

## 4. The Mūsā/Pharaoh narrative (Q 44:17-33) — compact form

Compare to Q 43's longer Mūsā section (Q 43:46-56) and Q 28's full narrative (Q 28:3-46). Q 44's version is the **most compressed Mūsā-Pharaoh narrative in HM-7**. Distinctive features:

- v. 17 *jāʾahum rasūlun karīm* — Mūsā called "noble messenger" (NOT "wise" or "brought-forward"); rare *karīm*-epithet for prophets (also Q 81:19 for Gabriel).
- v. 18 *adū ilayya ʿibād Allāh* — Mūsā's demand to Pharaoh: "Hand over to me the servants of God" (the Banū Isrāʾīl).
- vv. 25-28 — the **Pharaoh elegy**: *kam tarakū min jannātin wa-ʿuyūn / wa-zurūʿin wa-maqāmin karīm / wa-naʿmatin kānū fīhā fākihīn / kadhālika wa-awrathnāhā qawman ākharīn*. Classical *qaṣīda*-style elegy — al-Rāzī (`:218800` ff.) explicitly calls this passage *naʿy* (lamentation) for Pharaoh's lost wealth.
- v. 29 — the Quranic *fa-mā bakat ʿalayhim al-samāʾ wa-l-arḍ* ("Neither heaven nor earth wept for them") — a hapax cosmic-grief idiom.
- v. 32 *wa-laqad ikhtarnāhum ʿalā ʿilmin ʿalā al-ʿālamīn* — "We chose them (Banū Isrāʾīl) knowingly above the world(s)" — election-language paralleling Q 2:47, Q 7:140.

## 5. The Tubbaʿ reference (Q 44:37)

*A-hum khayrun am qawmu Tubbaʿ wa-lladhīna min qablihim* — "Are they (Quraysh) better, or the people of Tubbaʿ and those before them?" Tubbaʿ is the dynastic-title of the Ḥimyarite kings of pre-Islamic Yemen. Tubbaʿ appears only **TWICE** in the Qurʾān (verified this session): Q 44:37 and Q 50:14 — the latter as a destroyed-nation-list member. Q 44:37 is the **ranked comparison** form; Q 50:14 is the **catalog** form.

## 6. The Zaqqūm passage (Q 44:43-50) — distinctive feature

*Zaqqūm* (the infernal tree) appears in 3 surahs in the Qurʾān (verified this session): Q 37:62, Q 44:43, Q 56:52. Q 44 is the **longest of the three Zaqqūm-passages** by verse-count (8 verses, vv. 43-50). The passage's lexical density is striking:
- *al-athīm* "the sinner" — corpus-hapax in this absolute form (Q 44:44; verified this session).
- *al-muhl* "molten metal" — appears 13× across the corpus but in the eschatological-hellfire register (Q 18:29, Q 70:8 etc.) only ~5× in this sense.
- *al-baṭsha al-kubrā* "the great seizure" — corpus-hapax (Q 44:16; verified this session).
- *dhuq innaka anta al-ʿAzīz al-Karīm* (v. 49) — sarcastic-imperative addressing the sinner with God's own attributes; classical *iltifāt*-style register-shift.

## 7. The Paradise description (Q 44:51-57)

*Sundus wa-istabraq* (silk + brocade) appears together only in Q 18:31, Q 44:53, Q 76:21 (verified this session) — three Paradise-description verses. *ḥūr ʿīn* appears together only in Q 44:54, Q 52:20, Q 56:22. The Q 44 paradise-block is the **prototype-form** that Q 52:17-28 and Q 56:11-26 develop in fuller form.

The phrase *jannātin wa-ʿuyūn* (gardens-and-springs) appears 7× in the Qurʾān — including the corpus's only **same-surah twin-attestation**: Q 44:25 (Pharaoh's *lost* gardens) and Q 44:52 (the muttaqūn's *future* gardens). Q 44 is structurally the surah where the *jannāt-wa-ʿuyūn* lexeme is split between earthly-loss and heavenly-gain — verified by the seven attestations (Q 15:45, Q 26:57, Q 26:134, Q 26:147, Q 44:25, Q 44:52, Q 51:15).

## 10. Asbāb al-nuzūl

Per `/Users/grey/Downloads/quran/data/literature/classical-tafsir/raw/` (no per-Q044 al-Wāḥidī extraction file confirmed on disk; flagged as **DATA-GAP**), the dominant *sabab* tradition is for Q 44:10-15 (the smoke-sign):

- **Ibn Masʿūd-tradition** (Bukhārī `kitāb al-tafsīr` ad Q 44:10; Ibn Kathīr `:108460-108495`): when Quraysh persisted in disbelief, the Prophet supplicated for "years like the years of Yūsuf" (drought-famine); the resultant famine was so severe that hungry people *saw smoke* between sky and earth. They begged the Prophet to intercede; he did, rain fell, *innā kāshifū al-ʿadhābi qalīlan innakum ʿāʾidūn* (v. 15) was revealed. Then *yawm nabṭishu al-baṭshata al-kubrā* (v. 16) is interpreted as Badr.

- **Eschatological tradition** (Ibn ʿAbbās, ʿAlī, Abū Hurayra via al-Ṭabarī `ad loc.`; Muslim #2901-2902 the ten-signs hadith): the smoke is yet to come, one of the major eschatological signs.

Ibn Kathīr (`:108550-108560`) judges Ibn Masʿūd's narration *makhrūj fī al-ṣaḥīḥayn* (preserved in Bukhārī + Muslim) and accepts both readings as classically defensible.

## 11. Refrains and rhetorical devices

- *fa-rtaqib* (v. 10 + v. 59) — surah's exact-imperative inclusio.
- *innā* + verb 5×: *innā anzalnāhu* (v. 3), *innā kunnā mundhirīn* (v. 3), *innā kunnā mursilīn* (v. 5), *innā kāshifū al-ʿadhāb* (v. 15), *innā muntaqimūn* (v. 16) — divine-self-reference cluster in vv. 3-16.
- *mubīn* refrain (5× in 59 verses; cluster-extreme).
- Rhyme-monorhyme on ـن (74.6% of verses) and ـم (25.4%); only 2 rhyme letters (corpus-extreme; HM-7 minimum-distinct-rhymes after Q 43's 3-letter near-monorhyme).
- *kadhālika* (v. 28; v. 54) — the "thus" rhetorical-pivot, pivoting from Pharaoh-loss to muttaqūn-gain.
