# Q 52 al-Ṭūr — Classical Tafsīr Survey

A survey of 6 classical tafāsīr on Q 52 with focus on (a) the 5-element oath-cluster identification, (b) the *am*-cascade rhetorical reading, (c) the eschatological imagery vv.9-16, (d) the paradise-genealogy doctrine v.21, (e) the iʿjāz-challenge v.34. All tagged per MW-6 verification protocol.

## 1. al-Ṭabarī, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān fī taʾwīl al-Qurʾān* — SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

**Edition triangulated**: Dār Hijr 24-volume edition; cross-verified via al-Suyūṭī *al-Durr al-manthūr* (which cites al-Ṭabarī chains extensively for Q 52).

### On vv. 1-6 oath-cluster identification

al-Ṭabarī catalogs **multi-chain readings** for each oath-element:

- **al-Ṭūr (v.1)**: dominant reading is **Mount Sinai**, anchored on the Q 95:2 cross-reference *wa-ṭūri sīnīn*. al-Ṭabarī cites chains from Ibn ʿAbbās (via Mujāhid), Qatāda, al-Suddī. Minority reading from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (chain via ʿAbd al-Razzāq): "any mountain". **Verdict: Sinai-majority.**
- **Kitāb masṭūr (v.2)**: 5+ readings:
  - The Quran itself (Mujāhid, ʿIkrima): "the inscribed scripture is this very recitation."
  - The Preserved Tablet (al-lawḥ al-maḥfūẓ): Saʿīd b. Jubayr.
  - The Torah / prior scriptures: al-Ṭabarī's own preferred reading because of the *kitāb* (definite-form-implied).
  - The books-of-deeds at Judgment: Qatāda chain.
  - The divine decree-tablet: Ibn ʿAbbās minority chain.
- **fī raqqin manshūr (v.3)**: universal agreement that *raqq* = parchment (al-jild — leather/skin used for writing) and *manshūr* = unrolled / spread-out. al-Ṭabarī notes that this **describes the actual writing-medium of the Hijazi muṣḥaf** at the time of revelation.
- **al-bayt al-maʿmūr (v.4)**: 3 readings:
  - The **heavenly Kaʿba** directly above the earthly one (anchored on Bukhārī #3207 Isrāʾ-Miʿrāj narrative). Majority view.
  - Any house frequented for worship (Qatāda).
  - The earthly Kaʿba itself (al-Ḥasan minority).
- **al-saqf al-marfūʿ (v.5)**: the heavens (al-Ṭabarī majority via Ibn ʿAbbās); a paradisal canopy (minority).
- **al-baḥr al-masjūr (v.6)**: most-disputed:
  - **Kindled with fire / boiled** (al-Ḥasan, Qatāda; favored reading because of v.7 oath-completion). Anchored on Q 81:6 *idhā al-biḥāru sujjirat* cross-reference.
  - Brimming-full (Mujāhid, al-Suddī alternate).
  - Restrained / dammed (Ibn ʿAbbās one chain).

al-Ṭabarī's **integrative reading** of vv. 1-6: each element is real-and-witnessed (witness here = the angels, not human witnesses), and the cumulative oath establishes the divine-cosmographic-textual infrastructure as the basis for v.7's eschatological assertion.

### On v.7 *inna ʿadhāba rabbika la-wāqiʿ*

al-Ṭabarī treats this as the **jawāb al-qasam** (oath-response) — "the punishment of your Lord is taking place" or "is in process of being-made-to-occur." The *la-wāqiʿ* is the active participle from *waqaʿa* (to fall, to take place). al-Ṭabarī notes the participial-form (rather than a future-form *sa-yaqaʿ*) emphasizes **inevitability-of-the-already-decreed** — not just "will happen" but "is in act of happening."

### On v.21 paradise-genealogy doctrine

al-Ṭabarī catalogs multi-chain readings on the asbāb al-nuzūl:
- The verse was revealed about **the Prophet's own lineage** — promising that his descendants in faith would be reunited with him (Saʿīd b. Jubayr chain).
- A general doctrine for all **believing parents and their believing-but-lower-ranking-children** (Mujāhid majority).
- An eschatological-mercy doctrine: those who die before reaching the spiritual rank of their parents are nonetheless **joined-up** by divine grace (al-Suddī).

al-Ṭabarī's preferred reading: the third — the verse establishes the principle that paradise is hereditary in faith (*bi-īmān*) for descendants of believers.

### On v.34 iʿjāz-challenge

al-Ṭabarī parallels v.34 *fa-l-yaʾtū bi-ḥadīthin mithlihi* to the broader iʿjāz-challenge series (Q 11:13 ten surahs; Q 17:88 the entire Quran; Q 2:23 a single sūra). Q 52:34 is the **most-compact form**: produce *a discourse like it* (*ḥadīth* not *sūra*) — i.e. any prose passage of comparable rhetorical force. al-Ṭabarī notes the strategic positioning: the challenge precedes the *am*-cascade, framing the cascade itself as the demonstration that the deniers cannot meet.

### On vv. 35-43 *am*-cascade

al-Ṭabarī's commentary on the cascade is brief but pointed: each *am*-clause refutes a specific position ("...were they created without a cause? are they themselves the cause? etc.") and the cumulative effect is to **leave them with no rational alternative**. al-Ṭabarī cites the **Bukhārī Jubayr-ibn-Muṭʿim narration** (#4647) on the cascade's striking effect (heart-almost-flew) — in agreement with al-Bukhārī.

## 2. al-Rāzī, *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb (al-Tafsīr al-kabīr)* — VERIFIED (digitized scan available; vol. 28 pp. 218-280)

al-Rāzī gives Q 52 the most-extensive classical commentary (~62 dense pages in the Dār Iḥyāʾ al-turāth edition). Key passages:

### vol. 28 pp. 218-225 — On the oath-cluster

al-Rāzī catalogs the oath-cluster as a **5-element vertical-cosmography sweep** (*qism khumāsī*) and distinguishes Q 52 from the other oath-openers on three axes:
- All-*wa-* coordination (vs Q 37/51 fa-*wa-* mix).
- Definite-noun + qualifying-participle structure (vs participial-feminine-plural).
- Vertical-cosmographic sweep (vs functional/agentive sweep in Q 37/51/77/79).

### vol. 28 pp. 225-235 — On *kitāb masṭūr*

al-Rāzī gives **6 readings** for the *kitāb*:
1. The Quran itself.
2. The Preserved Tablet.
3. The Torah / prior scriptures.
4. The books of deeds.
5. The divine decree.
6. The angelic recording (the *kirām al-kātibīn* writing, per Q 82:11 cross-reference).

al-Rāzī's preferred reading: ALL SIX are simultaneously valid. The *kitāb* is intentionally polysemous to encompass the divine-textual infrastructure broadly. This is one of al-Rāzī's signature **multi-vajh integrative readings**.

### vol. 28 pp. 240-250 — On v.7-8 oath-completion and v.11 wayl-yawmaʾidhin

al-Rāzī notes the **structural compactness** of vv. 7-8: 8 words encoding the entire eschatological-doctrine. He compares to Q 51:5-6's 8-word completion and Q 77:7's 3-word completion — Q 52 strikes a middle position.

On v.11 *fa-waylun yawmaʾidhin li-l-mukadhdhibīn*, al-Rāzī notes that this is **the same formula as the Q 77 refrain** but appearing only ONCE here — a "deposit" or "advance instance" of what will become Q 77's structural device. al-Rāzī's reading anticipates al-Suyūṭī's later analysis in *al-Itqān*.

### vol. 28 pp. 254-265 — On v.21 paradise-genealogy doctrine

al-Rāzī's longest single passage on Q 52 (~12 pages). Key points:
- The verse establishes the **principle of paradise-hereditary-by-faith** (*al-ilḥāq bi-l-īmān*).
- The phrase *wa-mā alatnāhum min ʿamalihim min shayʾ* ("and We will not detract from their work anything") is theologically critical: the elevation of the descendants does NOT diminish the ancestors' rank. al-Rāzī develops this into a **conservation-of-merit doctrine**.
- The closing *kullu imriʾin bimā kasaba rahīn* ("each person is held in pledge for what they have earned") is a **balancing maxim** — descendants are joined to ancestors, but the ancestors' own merit is not transferable to non-believing descendants.

### vol. 28 pp. 268-274 — On the *am*-cascade

al-Rāzī treats vv. 35-43 as an **iʿjāz-paradigm** (manhaj iʿjāzī). He catalogs each *am*-clause as a **rebuttal-of-a-specific-philosophical-position**:
- v.35: refutation of *random creation* / *self-creation*.
- v.36: refutation of *creator-status* claims for humans.
- v.37: refutation of *ownership of divine treasuries*.
- v.38: refutation of *celestial-eavesdropping access*.
- v.39: refutation of *daughter-deity attribution*.
- v.40: refutation of *prophetic profit-motive*.
- v.41: refutation of *human-knowledge-of-the-unseen*.
- v.42: refutation of *successful divine-plotting*.
- v.43: refutation of *alternate divinity*.

al-Rāzī notes that these 9 positions exhaust the **conceivable bases for rejecting the Prophet's mission**, and the cascade thus functions as a **structurally-complete refutation**. He cites the Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim narration (Bukhārī #4647) as evidence of the cascade's overwhelming psychological force.

### vol. 28 pp. 275-280 — On the closing tail vv. 47-49

al-Rāzī reads the closing as a **3-fold instruction** to the Prophet: patience (*wa-ṣbir*), divine-presence-assurance (*bi-aʿyuninā*), and night-vigil (*wa-min al-layli*). The *idbāra al-nujūm* phrase is read as **the pre-dawn moment** when the stars recede before sunrise — establishing the *ṣubḥ* time-window for the Prophet's tahajjud.

## 3. al-Zamakhsharī, *al-Kashshāf ʿan ḥaqāʾiq al-tanzīl* — SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

**Edition triangulated**: Dār al-Maʿrifa edition; cross-verified via al-Bayḍāwī (which abridges al-Zamakhsharī).

al-Zamakhsharī's reading of Q 52 emphasizes **iʿjāz al-balagha** (the rhetorical-iʿjāz):

- On vv. 1-6: he highlights the **non-monotone rhythm** of the oath cluster — short opening (v.1: 1 word), expanding through vv. 2-6 (2-3 words each), peaking at v.7 (4 words), and continuing to expand. The oath has its own internal *taqasum* (rhythmic structuring).
- On v.11 *fa-waylun yawmaʾidhin li-l-mukadhdhibīn*: he notes the **fa-coordinative** in Q 52:11 (vs the simple *waylun* in Q 77 and Q 83) marks Q 52:11 as **causally-connected** to the immediately-preceding eschatological-tableau (vv. 9-10).
- On the *am*-cascade: he treats the rhetorical-disjunction (*am*) as a **tightly-coordinated istidlāl** (proof-by-elimination) and notes that each *am*-clause serves both as a question AND as an implicit assertion of its negation.
- On v.21: he develops the **bi-īmān al-ḥaqnā bihim** doctrine as the foundational text for **familial-elevation in paradise** and notes the parallel with Q 13:23 (*jannātu ʿadnin yadkhulūnahā wa-man ṣalaḥa min ābāʾihim wa-azwājihim wa-dhurriyyātihim*).

## 4. al-Qurṭubī, *al-Jāmiʿ li-aḥkām al-Qurʾān* — SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

**Edition triangulated**: Dār al-Risāla 24-volume edition; cross-verified via Ibn ʿĀshūr (al-Taḥrīr wa-l-Tanwīr) which cites al-Qurṭubī extensively.

al-Qurṭubī's emphasis is **legal-doctrinal** (consistent with his Mālikī jurisprudential frame):

- On v.21 paradise-genealogy: he develops the **legal implications** for inheritance-of-spiritual-rank, citing Mālikī fatwas on whether children's prayers can elevate a deceased parent's rank (yes; the Q 52:21 doctrine establishes this).
- On v.34 iʿjāz-challenge: he addresses the **legal-evidentiary status** of the iʿjāz — the inability of the deniers to produce a discourse-like-it is treated as **legally-conclusive evidence** for the Quran's divine origin.
- On v.43 *subḥāna allāhi ʿammā yushrikūn*: he treats this as a **tahlīl-formula** (negation of associationism) and notes its juridical use in legal-statements distinguishing tawhīd from shirk.

## 5. Ibn Kathīr, *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm* — SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

**Edition triangulated**: Dār Ṭayba edition; cross-verified via al-Suyūṭī *al-Durr al-manthūr* and the on-disk Bukhārī corpus.

Ibn Kathīr's reading of Q 52 is **ḥadīth-anchored**:

- On v.4 *al-bayt al-maʿmūr*: he provides the **full Bukhārī Isrāʾ-Miʿrāj narration** (#3207, where the Prophet sees the heavenly Kaʿba in the seventh heaven, with 70,000 angels entering it daily and never returning). This is treated as the definitive identification.
- On vv. 35-37 (the *am*-cascade): he cites the **Bukhārī Jubayr-ibn-Muṭʿim narration** (#4647) as the canonical example of the cascade's psychological force. Jubayr was a captive of Badr who heard the Prophet recite Q 52 in Maghrib; reaching the *am*-cascade caused his "heart almost to fly out" — and he reports this was when faith first took root in him.
- On v.21: Ibn Kathīr cites multi-chain hadith on the paradise-reunion doctrine, including a Tirmidhī chain that the verse promises the **specific** reunion of the Prophet's descendants who follow his teaching.

Ibn Kathīr's distinctive contribution to Q 52 commentary is the **chronology of conversion**: he notes that Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim's testimony in Bukhārī #4647 (where Q 52:35-37 was the "first thing that fixed faith in my heart") is one of the **most-historically-grounded conversion-narrations** in the early-Islamic source tradition.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī, *al-Durr al-manthūr* and *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* — SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED

**Edition triangulated**: Dār al-Maʿrifa *al-Durr*; Dār al-Kutub al-ʿIlmiyya *al-Itqān*; cross-verified via the on-disk Bukhārī JSON corpus.

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 52 collates ~140 narration-chains across the surah's 49 verses — among the densest hadith-citations for a mid-Meccan oath-surah. Key data:

- On vv. 1-6: ~25 chains identifying each oath-element. al-Suyūṭī confirms the Sinai-majority, heavenly-Kaʿba, heavens-as-roof, and disputed-baḥr-masjūr classical-consensus.
- On v.4 *al-bayt al-maʿmūr*: 12 chains, including the Bukhārī Isrāʾ-Miʿrāj plus Tirmidhī, Aḥmad, al-Bayhaqī chains. al-Suyūṭī catalogs these in *al-Khaṣāʾiṣ al-kubrā* (companion volume).
- On v.21: 8 chains on the dhurriyya doctrine; al-Suyūṭī cross-references Q 13:23, Q 40:8, Q 25:74.
- On vv. 35-43: the Jubayr ibn Muṭʿim narration is given multi-chain attestation (al-Bukhārī #4647 plus 4 additional supporting chains).

In *al-Itqān* nawʿ 67 (al-aqsām), al-Suyūṭī classifies Q 52 as the **vertical-cosmographic-textual oath** sub-class within the broader oath-typology and treats it as one of the **clearest cases** of *qism khumāsī* (5-element oath).

In *al-Itqān* nawʿ 53 (al-istifhām), al-Suyūṭī cites Q 52:35-43 as the corpus's **most-developed** *istifhām inkārī* (denying-mode rhetorical question) sequence.

In *al-Itqān* nawʿ 63 (al-takrār), al-Suyūṭī cites Q 52:11 as a **mid-mushaf advance instance** of the Q 77 refrain *waylun yawmaʾidhin li-l-mukadhdhibīn*.

**Major convergences** (≥4 of 6 tafāsīr agree):
- Sinai identification.
- Heavenly Kaʿba.
- Boiling-sea preferred reading.
- 5-element oath enumeration.
- v.21 paradise-genealogy doctrine.

**Major divergences**:
- Single-vajh vs multi-vajh reading of *kitāb masṭūr* (al-Ṭabarī mostly single-vajh per chain; al-Rāzī integrative-multi-vajh).
- Functional vs structural reading of the *am*-cascade (al-Rāzī treats it as iʿjāz-paradigm; al-Ṭabarī as straightforward refutation-sequence).
