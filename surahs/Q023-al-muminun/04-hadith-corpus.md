## 1. The famous "Ten Verses" ḥadīth — Q 23:1-10

**Tirmidhī, Sunan, Tafsīr al-Qurʾān, idInBook 3257** (= classical ḥadīth #3173 in printed editions):

> حَدَّثَنَا يَحْيَى بْنُ مُوسَى، وَعَبْدُ بْنُ حُمَيْدٍ، وَغَيْرُ، وَاحِدٍ الْمَعْنَى، وَاحِدٌ، قَالُوا حَدَّثَنَا عَبْدُ الرَّزَّاقِ، عَنْ يُونُسَ بْنِ سُلَيْمٍ، عَنِ الزُّهْرِيِّ، عَنْ عُرْوَةَ بْنِ الزُّبَيْرِ، عَنْ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنِ عَبْدٍ الْقَارِيِّ، قَالَ سَمِعْتُ عُمَرَ بْنَ الْخَطَّابِ، رضى الله عنه يَقُولُ كَانَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم إِذَا نَزَلَ عَلَيْهِ الْوَحْىُ سُمِعَ عِنْدَ وَجْهِهِ كَدَوِيِّ النَّحْلِ فَأُنْزِلَ عَلَيْهِ يَوْمًا فَمَكَثْنَا سَاعَةً فَسُرِّيَ عَنْهُ فَاسْتَقْبَلَ الْقِبْلَةَ وَرَفَعَ يَدَيْهِ وَقَالَ "اللَّهُمَّ زِدْنَا وَلاَ تَنْقُصْنَا وَأَكْرِمْنَا وَلاَ تُهِنَّا وَأَعْطِنَا وَلاَ تَحْرِمْنَا وَآثِرْنَا وَلاَ تُؤْثِرْ عَلَيْنَا وَأَرْضِنَا وَارْضَ عَنَّا"؛ ثُمَّ قَالَ صلى الله عليه وسلم: "أُنْزِلَ عَلَىَّ عَشْرُ آيَاتٍ مَنْ أَقَامَهُنَّ دَخَلَ الْجَنَّةَ" ثُمَّ قَرَأَ: ﴿ قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ ﴾ حَتَّى خَتَمَ عَشْرَ آيَاتٍ.

**English** (from the dataset's own translation):
> Narrated 'Umar bin Al-Khattab: When revelation came to the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ), one could hear what sounded like the drone of bees before his face. One day revelation was coming to him; we waited for a while and then he became free of it. He faced the Qiblah, raised his hands and said: 'O Allah! Increase us, do not diminish us. Favor us, do not withhold from us. Give us, do not deprive us. Choose us, do not give preference over us. Make us pleased, and be pleased with us.' Then he (ﷺ) said: 'Ten Ayāt have been revealed to me; whoever upholds them will enter Paradise.' Then he recited: *Successful indeed are the believers* (qad aflaḥa l-muʾminūn) until he completed ten Ayāt (Q 23:1-10).

**Tirmidhī's grading note** (from the same hadith entry):
> هَذَا أَصَحُّ مِنَ الْحَدِيثِ الأَوَّلِ سَمِعْتُ إِسْحَاقَ بْنَ مَنْصُورٍ، يَقُولُ رَوَى أَحْمَدُ بْنُ حَنْبَلٍ، وَعَلِيُّ بْنُ الْمَدِينِيِّ، وَإِسْحَاقُ بْنُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ، عَنْ عَبْدِ الرَّزَّاقِ، عَنْ يُونُسَ بْنِ سُلَيْمٍ، عَنْ يُونُسَ بْنِ يَزِيدَ، عَنِ الزُّهْرِيِّ، هَذَا الْحَدِيثَ.

al-Tirmidhī marks the ḥadīth as transmitted via two routes (Yūnus b. Sulaym both with and without Yūnus b. Yazīd in the chain). In the *Tafsīr* of Ibn Kathīr (`ibn-kathir/23.json` ayah 1), Ibn Kathīr cites al-Tirmidhī's grading: "*munkar*; we do not know anyone who narrates it other than Yūnus b. Sulaym, and Yūnus we do not know."

al-Qurṭubī adds: "Ibn al-ʿArabī declared it ṣaḥīḥ" (`qurtubi/23.json` ayah 1). Modern ḥadīth scholarship (e.g., al-Albānī) classifies it as **ḥasan** with isnād-shifts.

**Aḥmad b. Ḥanbal, Musnad, idInBook 217** (from `ahmed.json`): same isnād structure, with ʿUmar narrating the *dawiyyu l-naḥl* (sound-of-bees) revelation experience and the Prophet's recitation of Q 23:1-10 with the *ʿashr-āyāt-paradise* statement.

**Q 23 verses cited**: 1-10. The ḥadīth fixes the **scriptural unit "qad aflaḥa l-muʾminūn"** at exactly **ten verses** (vv. 1-10), confirming the believer-typology block as classical-attested.

**Project bearings**:
- This is the strongest single classical ḥadīth-anchor for Q 23.
- It directly supports Q023-F-01 (the believer-typology block as a coherent unit).
- It demarcates v. 1-10 as the "block," with v. 11 (firdaws-closure) being interpreted as either part of or just-after the block depending on the mufassir.

## 2. Q 23 in the Conquest-day prayer — Nasāʾī

**Nasāʾī, Sunan, idInBook 1009**:

> أَخْبَرَنَا مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ الأَعْلَى، قَالَ حَدَّثَنَا خَالِدٌ، قَالَ حَدَّثَنَا ابْنُ جُرَيْجٍ، قَالَ أَخْبَرَنِي مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبَّادٍ، حَدِيثًا رَفَعَهُ إِلَى ابْنِ سُفْيَانَ عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ السَّائِبِ، قَالَ حَضَرْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَوْمَ الْفَتْحِ فَصَلَّى فِي قُبُلِ الْكَعْبَةِ فَخَلَعَ نَعْلَيْهِ فَوَضَعَهُمَا عَنْ يَسَارِهِ فَافْتَتَحَ بِسُورَةِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ فَلَمَّا جَاءَ ذِكْرُ مُوسَى أَوْ عِيسَى - عَلَيْهِمَا السَّلاَمُ - أَخَذَتْهُ سَعْلَةٌ فَرَكَعَ.

**English**:
> Narrated 'Abdullāh bin As-Sā'ib: I was with the Messenger of Allāh (ﷺ) on the day of the Conquest of Makkah. He prayed in front of the Kaʿba. He took off his shoes and placed them to his left, and he started to recite Sūrat al-Muʾminīn. When he reached the passage that mentions Mūsā and ʿĪsā, peace be upon them both, he was overcome by a coughing-fit, and bowed.

**Q 23 verses cited**: vv. 23-49 (prophet-cycle through Mūsā), v. 50 (ʿĪsā/Maryam). The "passage that mentions Mūsā and ʿĪsā" is **vv. 45-50**.

al-Qurṭubī (`qurtubi/23.json` ayah 1) cites this ḥadīth (referenced as al-Nasāʾī, with parallels in Muslim by similar meaning).

**Project bearings**:
- This places **the recitation of Q 23 in the most consequential prayer of the Prophet's life** (the Conquest of Makka, AH 8, when the Prophet returned to liberated Makka).
- The bowing at vv. 45-50 (Mūsā/ʿĪsā/Maryam) suggests these verses had special-status as a sajda or breath-pause point.

## 3. Q 23:51 in the famous "Allāh is Tayyib" ḥadīth — Muslim, Tirmidhī, Dārimī

**Muslim, Ṣaḥīḥ, idInBook 2230** (in *Kitāb al-Zakāh* / *Bāb tasdīq al-ṣadaqāt min al-kasb al-ṭayyib*):

> حَدَّثَنَا فُضَيْلُ بْنُ مَرْزُوقٍ، حَدَّثَنِي عَدِيُّ بْنُ ثَابِتٍ، عَنْ أَبِي حَازِمٍ، عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ، قَالَ قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: "أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنَّ اللَّهَ طَيِّبٌ لاَ يَقْبَلُ إِلاَّ طَيِّبًا وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَمَرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ بِمَا أَمَرَ بِهِ الْمُرْسَلِينَ فَقَالَ ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا الرُّسُلُ كُلُوا مِنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَاعْمَلُوا صَالِحًا إِنِّي بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ عَلِيمٌ ﴾ وَقَالَ ﴿ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ ﴾". ثُمَّ ذَكَرَ الرَّجُلَ يُطِيلُ السَّفَرَ أَشْعَثَ أَغْبَرَ يَمُدُّ يَدَيْهِ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ "يَا رَبِّ يَا رَبِّ وَمَطْعَمُهُ حَرَامٌ وَمَشْرَبُهُ حَرَامٌ وَمَلْبَسُهُ حَرَامٌ وَغُذِيَ بِالْحَرَامِ فَأَنَّى يُسْتَجَابُ لِذَلِكَ".

**English**:
> Abū Hurayra reported: O people, Allāh is Good (ṭayyib) and He therefore accepts only that which is good. And Allāh commanded the believers as He commanded the Messengers by saying: "O Messengers, eat of the good things, and do good deeds; verily I am aware of what you do" (Q 23:51). And He said: "O those who believe, eat of the good things that We gave you" (Q 2:172). He then made a mention of a person who travels widely, his hair disheveled and covered with dust. He lifts his hand towards the sky and supplicates "O Lord, O Lord," whereas his diet is unlawful, his drink is unlawful, and his clothes are unlawful and his nourishment is unlawful — how can then his supplication be accepted?

**Tirmidhī, Sunan, idInBook 3073**: same hadith, slightly different isnād (Abū Nuʿaym → Fuḍayl b. Marzūq → ʿAdī b. Thābit → Abū Ḥāzim → Abū Hurayra). al-Tirmidhī grades it ḥasan-ṣaḥīḥ.

**Dārimī, Sunan, idInBook 1985**: another full version of the same ḥadīth.

**Q 23 verses cited**: v. 51 (*yā ayyuhā l-rusul*).

**Project bearings**:
- This is the **most-cited Q 23 verse in the corpus's classical fadāʾil literature** outside the v. 1 typology block.
- The pairing of Q 23:51 (address to the messengers) with Q 2:172 (address to the believers) is the **scriptural foundation for the Islamic ethic of permissible-livelihood** (*kasb al-ṭayyib*).
- The Prophet's gloss — *amara l-muʾminīna bi-mā amara bihī l-mursalīn* ("He commanded the believers as He commanded the Messengers") — explicitly **identifies Q 23:51 as a universal injunction binding all believers, not only the messengers**, on the principle that the messengers' standard is the believers' standard.

## 4. Q 23:60 — ʿĀʾisha's gloss (the *fawajilat al-qulūb* tradition)

**Tirmidhī, Sunan, idInBook 3259** (= ḥadīth #3175 in printed editions):

> حَدَّثَنَا ابْنُ أَبِي عُمَرَ، حَدَّثَنَا سُفْيَانُ، حَدَّثَنَا مَالِكُ بْنُ مِغْوَلٍ، عَنْ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنِ سَعِيدِ بْنِ وَهْبٍ الْهَمْدَانِيِّ، أَنَّ عَائِشَةَ، زَوْجَ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَتْ سَأَلْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم عَنْ هَذِهِ الآيَةِ ﴿ والَّذِينَ يُؤْتُونَ مَا آتَوْا وَقُلُوبُهُمْ وَجِلَةٌ ﴾ قَالَتْ عَائِشَةُ أَهُمُ الَّذِينَ يَشْرَبُونَ الْخَمْرَ وَيَسْرِقُونَ قَالَ "لاَ يَا بِنْتَ الصِّدِّيقِ وَلَكِنَّهُمُ الَّذِينَ يَصُومُونَ وَيُصَلُّونَ وَيَتَصَدَّقُونَ وَهُمْ يَخَافُونَ أَنْ لاَ يُقْبَلَ مِنْهُمْ أُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَهُمْ لَهَا سَابِقُونَ".

**English** (from the dataset):
> ʿĀʾisha asked the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) about this verse: "And those who give that which they give with their hearts full of fear..." (Q 23:60). She said: "Are they those who drink khamr and steal?" He said: "No, O daughter of al-Ṣiddīq. They are those who fast, perform Ṣalāh, give charity while they fear that their Lord will not accept it from them. It is these who hasten to do good deeds, and they are the foremost of them" (citing Q 23:61).

**Ibn Mājah, Sunan, idInBook 3936**: identical version.

**Q 23 verses cited**: vv. 60-61.

**Project bearings**:
- ʿĀʾisha's question presumes a **literal-grammatical reading** of *yuʾtūna mā ātaw wa-qulūbuhum wajila* — "those who do/give what they do/give while their hearts are anxious." The Prophet's correction shifts the reading from **fear-of-punishment-for-wrongdoing** to **fear-of-non-acceptance-of-righteous-acts**.
- This ḥadīth is the **classical anchor for the rūḥānī (interior-spiritual) reading of Q 23:60-61** — the believers' anxiety is about whether their good deeds are accepted, not about whether their bad deeds are punished.
- Frequently cited in sufi literature; al-Qurṭubī cites it in his commentary on v. 60.

## 5. Q 23:14 — the embryology + spirit-breathing ḥadīth chain

The Quran's embryology-passage Q 23:13-14 has a parallel in the famous Bukhārī/Muslim/Aḥmad embryology-and-fate ḥadīth, which uses the same vocabulary stages (*nuṭfa* / *ʿalaqa* / *muḍgha*) but is technically about ante-natal predestination, not Q 23 specifically.

### 5.1 Bukhārī, idInBook 3195 (and parallels)

> حَدَّثَنَا أَبُو النُّعْمَانِ، حَدَّثَنَا حَمَّادُ بْنُ زَيْدٍ، عَنْ عُبَيْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ أَبِي بَكْرِ بْنِ أَنَسٍ، عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ ـ رضى الله عنه ـ عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: "إِنَّ اللَّهَ وَكَّلَ فِي الرَّحِمِ مَلَكًا فَيَقُولُ يَا رَبِّ نُطْفَةٌ، يَا رَبِّ عَلَقَةٌ، يَا رَبِّ مُضْغَةٌ. فَإِذَا أَرَادَ اللَّهُ أَنْ يَقْضِيَ خَلْقَهُ ..."

— "Allāh has appointed an angel over the womb, and the angel says: 'O Lord, a sperm-drop! O Lord, a clot! O Lord, a piece of flesh!' When Allāh decides to complete the creature's creation, the angel asks: 'O Lord! A male or a female? Unfortunate or blessed? What will his sustenance be? What will his lifespan be?'"

Bukhārī also has #315 and #6355 with the same isnād structure.

**Muslim, idInBook 6565**: same ḥadīth via the same isnād (Ḥammād b. Zayd → ʿUbayd Allāh b. Abī Bakr → Anas).

### 5.2 Tirmidhī, idInBook 3505 and 3506

Both reference Q 23:14 directly in the context of the *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn* spontaneous-utterance tradition. al-Tirmidhī cites the tradition that ʿUmar (or Muʿādh, or ʿAbd Allāh b. Saʿd b. Abī Sarḥ) spontaneously uttered *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn* upon hearing the front of v. 14, and the Prophet confirmed: *hākadhā unzilat* — "thus it has been revealed."

**Q 23 verses cited**: vv. 12-14 (embryology); v. 14 closing (*aḥsanu l-khāliqīn*).

**Project bearings**:
- The embryology-vocabulary ḥadīth is **not Q 23-specific**; it is general embryology-cosmology. But it shares the same lexical inventory (*nuṭfa* / *ʿalaqa* / *muḍgha*) and is therefore a classical-conceptual cross-reference to Q 23:13-14.
- Modern apologetics frequently cite Q 23:13-14 as "scientific miracle" of embryology. The classical mufassirūn (Ibn Kathīr; al-Qurṭubī) and the parallel embryology-ḥadīth (Bukhārī #3195) are **not** read as scientific-miraculous — they are read as theological evidence of God's creative agency, with the technical-anatomical stages being the standard Galenic-Quranic embryology of the 7th-century Mediterranean medical world.

## 6. Q 23 in the recitation-virtues (fadāʾil) literature

The 9-book corpus has **no direct Q 23 fadāʾil-of-recitation ḥadīth** outside the al-Tirmidhī-Aḥmad ʿUmar 10-verses ḥadīth (§1 above) and the Conquest-day Nasāʾī ḥadīth (§2). al-Zamakhsharī (`zamakhshari-kashshaf.djvu.txt` offset 2956200) cites two additional fadāʾil-traditions but flags both as **isnād-weak**:
- "Whoever recites Sūrat al-Muʾminūn, the angels announce to him at the descent of the Angel of Death glad-tidings of *al-rūḥ wa-l-rayḥān*..." (al-Wāḥidī *al-Wasīṭ* p. 402; *gharīb*).
- "The opening and closing of *qad aflaḥa* are from the treasures of the Throne..." (al-Zaylaʿī flagged it *gharīb jiddan*, isnād-weak).

These traditions are not in the canonical 9-book corpus and are therefore **not reliable** by mainstream isnād-criticism standards.

- Hadith-recitation-fadāʾil count: **mid-low** (only 2 direct surah-fadāʾil ḥadīth in the 9-book corpus — Tirmidhī ʿUmar 10-verses + Nasāʾī Conquest-day; both ASBĀB-related, not surah-recitation-virtue).
- Hadith-content-citations: **substantial** (Q 23:51 in Muslim's *Ṭayyib* ḥadīth; Q 23:60 in Tirmidhī's ʿĀʾisha gloss; Q 23:13-14 in the embryology ḥadīth).

The pattern: Q 23's classical attention is concentrated on **the believer-typology block (vv. 1-11)** and on **specific verses' theological content (vv. 14, 51, 60)** — i.e., al-Khaṭṭābī meaning-iʿjāz lineage. The architectural-UAS rank (9 / 114, al-Bāqillānī structural-iʿjāz lineage) is empirically **orthogonal** to the classical hadith attention.

This is consistent with H-NEW-860's empirical-orthogonality finding: classical ḥadīth-fadāʾil attention is **content-driven**; UAS is **structure-driven**. They overlap at Q 23 in a particular way (high UAS + substantial content-attention) but the mechanisms are independent.

## 9. Honest limits

- **The 9-book search is text-string-based**, not isnād-graph-based. It catches direct verse-text citations but may miss ḥadīths that paraphrase Q 23 without citing the verse-text. A graph-based search using isnād-network analysis would refine the count.
- **The Tirmidhī ʿUmar 10-verses ḥadīth has known isnād-weakness** (Yūnus b. Sulaym is unknown to many ḥadīth-scholars). It is widely cited but its grading is contested. Modern al-Albānī classifies it ḥasan with isnād-shifts; classical Ibn al-ʿArabī declared it ṣaḥīḥ. The empirical project does not adjudicate isnād-authenticity but documents the contested-status.
- **al-Bukhārī has no direct Q 23-fadāʾil ḥadīth**. The single hit (Bukhārī #3635) cites Q 59:9, not Q 23. al-Bukhārī's *Ṣaḥīḥ* does not include a Q 23-recitation-fadāʾil ḥadīth — a notable absence given the strength of the Tirmidhī-Aḥmad ʿUmar tradition.
- **The Q 23-specific fadāʾil-of-recitation tradition is therefore weaker than for Q 1, Q 2, Q 36, Q 55, Q 67, Q 112**. This is consistent with H-NEW-860's finding that architectural-iʿjāz (UAS) and meaning-iʿjāz (hadith-fadāʾil) are orthogonal axes.
- **The embryology-vocabulary ḥadīths** (Bukhārī #3195 etc.) are not Q 23-specific; they share the lexical inventory but the ḥadīth's primary theme is ante-natal predestination, not Q 23's textual structure.
