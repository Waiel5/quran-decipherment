# Q 23 al-Muʾminūn — Content Analysis

## 1. Five-movement architecture

I read Q 23 as a five-movement architecture, with each movement marked by a rhetorical hinge or refrain. The full Arabic was extracted from `quran-text/quran-no-tashkeel.json` (and cross-validated against `quran-min-tashkeel.json` and `quran-full-tashkeel.json`).

| Movement | Verses | Hinge / opening formula | Theme |
|:--|:-:|:--|:--|
| I | 1-11 | *qad aflaḥa l-muʾminūn* | The believer-typology |
| II | 12-22 | *wa-laqad khalaqnā l-insāna min sulālatin min ṭīn* | Creation, embryology, sustenance, signs |
| III | 23-54 | *wa-laqad arsalnā nūḥan ilā qawmihi* | Prophet-cycle |
| IV | 55-77 | *a-yaḥsabūna annamā numiddu-hum bihi min mālin* | Wealth-test, eschatology, sealing of unbelief |
| V | 78-118 | *wa-huwa lladhī anshaʾa lakumu l-samʿa wa-l-abṣāra* | Sense-evidence, judgment, ring-closure |

## 2. Movement I — The believer-typology block (vv. 1-11)

The famous "ten verses" of the ʿUmar–Tirmidhī ḥadīth (#3173 / idInBook 3257; see `04-hadith-corpus.md` §1).

Cross-validated text (no-tashkeel, with min-tashkeel and full-tashkeel cross-checks):

| v | Arabic (no-tashkeel) | Trait |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | قد أفلح المؤمنون | Performative: "they have prospered" |
| 2 | الذين هم في صلاتهم خاشعون | khushūʿ in prayer |
| 3 | والذين هم عن اللغو معرضون | turning away from idle-speech (laghw) |
| 4 | والذين هم للزكاة فاعلون | doing/giving zakāh |
| 5 | والذين هم لفروجهم حافظون | guarding the private parts (ḥifẓ al-furūj) |
| 6 | إلا على أزواجهم أو ما ملكت أيمانهم فإنهم غير ملومين | (clause): exception for spouses / what right hands possess |
| 7 | فمن ابتغى وراء ذلك فأولئك هم العادون | (closer): seeking beyond → transgressors |
| 8 | والذين هم لأماناتهم وعهدهم راعون | keeping trusts and covenants |
| 9 | والذين هم على صلواتهم يحافظون | guarding their prayers (ḥifẓ al-ṣalawāt) |
| 10 | أولئك هم الوارثون | "those are the inheritors" — closing |
| 11 | الذين يرثون الفردوس هم فيها خالدون | inheriting Firdaws, abiding eternally |

**Note on counting**: classical mufassirūn count exactly **6 distinct trait-clauses** within the ten verses (khushūʿ, iʿrāḍ-laghw, zakāh, ḥifẓ-furūj, riʿāyat-amāna, ḥifẓ-ṣalawāt) — vv. 6-7 are sub-clauses of trait 4 (ḥifẓ-furūj), and vv. 10-11 are the closing benediction. The ʿUmar ḥadīth's *ʿashr āyāt* refers to the **ten verses** (v. 1 through v. 10), with v. 11 (firdaws-closure) sometimes included as the unit's semantic-completion.

**Key root-clusters in the typology block** (extracted from QAC for vv. 1-11):
- *Slw* (prayer): vv. 2, 9 — opening + closing of trait-list, forming an inner mini-inclusio.
- *flḥ* (prosper): v. 1 only in this block; recurs at v. 102, v. 117 (see `01-empirical-profile.md` §7).
- *Hf*Z* (guard): vv. 5, 9 — *guard the privates* + *guard the prayers*.
- *zwj* (spouse): v. 6.
- *zkw* (purify / alms): v. 4.
- *Ehd* (covenant): v. 8.
- *wrv* (inherit) + *wry* (firdaws as proper noun, root not in QAC standard list): vv. 10-11.

The trait-list has a deliberate **prayer-frame** (v. 2 khushūʿ-in-prayer ↔ v. 9 ḥifẓ-of-prayers), enclosing the social-ethical traits (vv. 3-8: laghw / zakāh / furūj / amāna). This is a **chiastic mini-structure** classically noted (al-Biqāʿī *Naẓm al-Durar* on Q 23 §1).

## 3. Movement II — Creation, embryology, sustenance (vv. 12-22)

The "embryology block" (vv. 12-16) plus the sustenance block (vv. 17-22).

**vv. 12-14 — the famous embryology sequence**:

| v | Arabic | Stage |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 12 | ولقد خلقنا الإنسان من سلالة من طين | sulāla min ṭīn (extract from clay — Ādam-allusion) |
| 13 | ثم جعلناه نطفة في قرار مكين | nuṭfa fī qarārin makīn (drop in a settled lodging — uterus) |
| 14 | ثم خلقنا النطفة علقة فخلقنا العلقة مضغة فخلقنا المضغة عظاما فكسونا العظام لحما ثم أنشأناه خلقا آخر فتبارك الله أحسن الخالقين | ʿalaqa → muḍgha → ʿiẓām → laḥm → "another creation"; closing *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn* |
| 15 | ثم إنكم بعد ذلك لميتون | (you will die) |
| 16 | ثم إنكم يوم القيامة تبعثون | (you will be raised) |

**Embryology-cluster roots** (QAC v. 12-16):
- *Tyn* (clay), *sll* (extract / origin), *nTf* (drop / sperm), *Elq* (clot / clinging-thing), *mDg* (lump of flesh), *EZm* (bone), *lHm* (flesh), *xlq* (create — 4 attestations in v. 14 alone), *An$A* (bring forth as new creation), *Axr* (other / next), *brk* (blessed / multiplied), *Hsn* (best / good), *mwt* (death), *bEv* (resurrect), *qrr* (settled-place), *mkn* (firmly-placed).

**Asbāb al-nuzūl note**: classical sources (al-Qurṭubī ad loc. citing Musnad al-Ṭayālisī) report a tradition that when v. 14 was first revealed up to *khalqan ākhar*, ʿUmar (or in another isnād, Muʿādh b. Jabal, or ʿAbd Allāh b. Saʿd) spontaneously responded *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn*, and the closing clause was then revealed in confirmation of his utterance. al-Qurṭubī notes this report and discusses isnād-disagreements; see `03-tafsir-survey.md` §3.1 for the full survey.

**vv. 17-22 — sustenance / signs**:
- v. 17: seven heavens above (sabʿ ṭarāʾiq).
- v. 18: water from sky in measured-amount (qadar).
- v. 19: gardens of palms and grapes.
- v. 20: olive tree from Mt Sinai (Ṭūr Saynāʾ) producing oil.
- v. 21: cattle as a "sign" (āya).
- v. 22: ships and animals as transport.

This is a classic Meccan **āyāt al-takwīn** (cosmological-signs) catalogue — the rhetorical move "see the world; it is full of evidence."

## 4. Movement III — The prophet-cycle (vv. 23-54)

This is Q 23's longest movement and the empirical anchor of Q023-F-04 (`06-novel-findings.md`), which compares Q 23's prophet-cycle structure to Q 11 Hūd's *wa-ilā [tribe] akhāhum* lattice.

### 4.1 Nūḥ (vv. 23-30)

- v. 23: *wa-laqad arsalnā nūḥan ilā qawmihī* — "We sent Nūḥ to his people"
- v. 23: *fa-qāla yā qawmi aʿbudū llāha mā lakum min ilāhin ghayruhū* — **the Q 11 *yā qawmi aʿbudū* formula**
- v. 24: the chiefs of his people who disbelieved replied: "this is only a man like you wishing to gain superiority..."
- v. 25: "he is just a man with madness in him; wait on him for a while"
- v. 26: Nūḥ's prayer: *Rabbi unṣurnī bimā kadhdhabūn* — "Lord, help me; they have called me a liar"
- vv. 27-28: ark-construction + flood + the *qul al-ḥamdu li-llāhi lladhī najjānā mina l-qawmi l-ẓālimīn* refrain.
- v. 29: *Rabbi anzilnī munzalan mubārakan* — "Lord, land me in a blessed landing"
- v. 30: *inna fī dhālika la-āyātin wa-in kunnā la-mubtalīn* — closing formula: "in this are signs; we are testers"

### 4.2 The unnamed second prophet (vv. 31-41) — Hūd or Ṣāliḥ?

- v. 31: *thumma anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qarnan ākharīn* — **"then We raised up another generation after them"**
- v. 32: *fa-arsalnā fīhim rasūlan minhum an-i ʿbudū llāha mā lakum min ilāhin ghayruhū a-falā tattaqūn* — same *aʿbudū llāha* formula as v. 23, but the messenger is **unnamed**.
- vv. 33-38: dialogue + standard Meccan-prophet rejection.
- v. 39: same prayer formula as v. 26: *Rabbi unṣurnī bimā kadhdhabūn*.
- v. 41: *fa-akhadhathumu l-ṣayḥa bi-l-ḥaqq* — "the cry seized them rightfully" — al-ṣayḥa is the punishment associated with **Thamūd** (Ṣāliḥ's people), suggesting the unnamed prophet is **Ṣāliḥ**. Some classical mufassirūn (al-Ṭabarī ad loc. via Mujāhid) read Hūd; others (al-Qurṭubī, citing al-Wāqidī) read Ṣāliḥ; al-Rāzī notes the ambiguity is **deliberate** — the surah deliberately collapses the post-Nūḥ messengers into a generic-pattern.

### 4.3 The "another generations" interlude (vv. 42-44)

- v. 42: *thumma anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qurūnan ākharīn* — same formula as v. 31, but plural *qurūn* (generations, not generation). This is a **broadening** rhetorical move: from single named prophets to multiple unnamed ones.
- v. 43: each ummah has its appointed term.
- v. 44: *thumma arsalnā rusulanā tatrā* — "then We sent Our messengers in succession". This compresses the entire prophetic history between Ṣāliḥ-or-Hūd and Mūsā into a single sentence.

### 4.4 Mūsā and Hārūn (vv. 45-49)

- v. 45: *thumma arsalnā mūsā wa-akhāhu hārūna bi-āyātinā wa-sulṭānin mubīn* — "Then We sent Mūsā and his brother Hārūn with Our signs and clear authority"
- v. 46: *ilā firʿawna wa-malaʾihī fa-stakbarū wa-kānū qawman ʿālīn* — "to Pharaoh and his chiefs; they were arrogant and a haughty people"
- v. 47: *fa-qālū a-nuʾminu li-basharayni mithlinā wa-qawmuhumā lanā ʿābidūn* — "shall we believe in two humans like ourselves while their people serve us?" (echo of v. 24's basher-objection)
- v. 48: they denied them and were destroyed.
- v. 49: *wa-laqad ātaynā mūsā l-kitāba laʿallahum yahtadūn* — "We gave Mūsā the Book that they might be guided"

### 4.5 ʿĪsā / Maryam (v. 50)

- v. 50: *wa-jaʿalnā bna maryama wa-ummahu āyatan wa-āwaynāhumā ilā rabwatin dhāti qarārin wa-maʿīn* — "We made the son of Maryam and his mother a sign, and We gave them refuge in a high-ground place of settled-rest and a flowing spring"

This is **the briefest ʿĪsā / Maryam reference in the corpus**. No nativity narrative, no miracle-list, no crucifixion-issue. Just an *āya* + a place of refuge. The "high-ground with settled-rest and a spring" (rabwa dhāt qarār wa-maʿīn) is identified by al-Ṭabarī ad loc. with Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem) per Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Qurṭubī also cites traditions identifying it with Damascus (Bāniyās), or Egypt, or Palestine — classical mufassirūn disagreed.

### 4.6 Universal address to the messengers (vv. 51-54)

- v. 51: *yā ayyuhā l-rusulu kulū mina l-ṭayyibāti wa-ʿmalū ṣāliḥan innī bimā taʿmalūna ʿalīm* — "O messengers, eat from the good things and act righteously; I am Knower of what you do"
- v. 52: *wa-inna hādhihī ummatukum ummatan wāḥidatan wa-anā rabbukum fa-ttaqūn* — "this community of yours is one community; I am your Lord; so reverence Me"
- v. 53: *fa-taqaṭṭaʿū amrahum baynahum zuburā* — "they cut up their religion among themselves into sects"
- v. 54: *fa-dharhum fī ghamratihim ḥattā ḥīn* — "leave them in their dazzling-confusion until a time"

This is a unique **collective second-person plural address to all the messengers** — a Quranic rhetorical move classified by Abdel Haleem (1992) as *iltifāt* of person + address-target (see `data/literature/balagha/1992-abdel-haleem-grammatical-shift-iltifat-bsoas.md`). The rusul (plural messengers) are addressed as if all simultaneously present — the famous Darimi #1985 ḥadīth (`04-hadith-corpus.md` §3) cites v. 51 as a universal-Islamic injunction binding both the messengers and the community.

### 4.7 Q 23 prophet-cycle vs Q 11 Hūd's *wa-ilā [tribe]* lattice

Q 11 (Hūd) uses the *wa-ilā [tribe] akhāhum [prophet]* formula:
- Q 11:50: *wa-ilā ʿādin akhāhum hūdā*
- Q 11:61: *wa-ilā thamūda akhāhum ṣāliḥā*
- Q 11:84: *wa-ilā madyana akhāhum shuʿaybā*

Q 23 uses a different lattice — **two refrains**:
- *yā qawmi aʿbudū llāha mā lakum min ilāhin ghayruhū* (vv. 23, 32) — direct preaching formula.
- *thumma anshaʾnā min baʿdihim qarnan / qurūnan ākharīn* (vv. 31, 42) — generation-succession formula.

**Q 11's lattice names the tribes (ʿĀd, Thamūd, Madyan)**; **Q 23's lattice abstracts to "another generation"**. Q 11 is **3 named prophets** (Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb) named explicitly with tribal anchors; Q 23 is **3 named prophets** (Nūḥ, an unnamed-Hūd-or-Ṣāliḥ, Mūsā/Hārūn) + the brief Maryam-ʿĪsā reference. **Q 23's prophet-cycle is more abstract and universalizing**; **Q 11's is more concrete and localized**. Pre-registered test Q023-F-04 (`06-novel-findings.md`) tests this lexical-distinction quantitatively.

## 5. Movement IV — Wealth-test, eschatology, sealing of unbelief (vv. 55-77)

- v. 55-56: *a-yaḥsabūna annamā numiddu-hum bihi min mālin wa-banīn nusāriʿu lahum fī l-khayrāt* — "do they reckon that the wealth and sons We give them are a hastening to good?"
- vv. 57-61: the believers' fear (the celebrated "those who give what they give while their hearts are afraid that they will return to their Lord" — v. 60 *wa-lladhīna yuʾtūna mā ātaw wa-qulūbuhum wajila*).
- vv. 62-67: scrolls of deeds + their excuses on the Day.
- vv. 68-71: *a-fa-lā yatadabbarūna l-qawl* — "do they not reflect upon the Word?"
- vv. 72-75: the Prophet's ajr is from God; the disbelievers refuse the truth even when Mercy is offered.
- vv. 76-77: when punishment comes they bow down.

ʿĀʾisha's well-known interpretation (cited in `04-hadith-corpus.md` §6) clarifies v. 60: it does NOT refer to those who "do things and fear punishment" but to "those who pray and fast and pay zakāh fearing it may not be accepted from them."

## 6. Movement V — Sense-evidence, judgment, ring-closure (vv. 78-118)

### 6.1 vv. 78-83 — sense-organs as evidence

- v. 78: *wa-huwa lladhī anshaʾa lakumu l-samʿa wa-l-abṣāra wa-l-afʾidata qalīlan mā tashkurūn* — "He gave you hearing and sight and hearts; little do you give thanks"
- v. 79-80: He multiplied you on earth; He gives life and death.
- v. 81-83: their counter-argument: "this is what our forefathers were promised before — these are only fables"

### 6.2 vv. 84-89 — three rhetorical *qul* questions

Three parallel *qul li-man (...)* questions, each receiving the answer *sayaqūlūna li-llāh*:

| v | Question (cleaned) | Answer in v |
|:-:|:--|:-:|
| 84 | *li-man al-arḍu wa-man fīhā* — "to whom belongs the earth and what is in it?" | 85 |
| 86 | *man rabbu l-samāwāti l-sabʿi wa-rabbu l-ʿarshi l-ʿaẓīm* — "who is the Lord of the seven heavens and the great Throne?" | 87 |
| 88 | *man biyadihī malakūtu kulli shayʾ* — "in whose hand is the dominion of every thing?" | 89 |

The answer in each case is *sayaqūlūna li-llāh* — "they will say: Allāh"; *qul a-fa-lā tattaqūn* / *a-fa-lā tadhakkarūn* / *a-fa-lā tudhakkarūn*. Three *qul* + three rhetorical questions + three answers + three closings = a **rhetorical triplet structure** (al-Biqāʿī notes this in *Naẓm al-Durar*).

### 6.3 vv. 99-115 — the eschatology + barzakh + judgment

- vv. 99-100: the dying disbeliever's plea: *Rabbi rjiʿūni laʿallī aʿmalu ṣāliḥan fīmā taraktu* — "Lord, send me back, that I may do righteously what I left behind"
- v. 100: refusal — *kallā innahā kalimatun huwa qāʾiluhā wa-min warāʾihim barzakhun ilā yawmi yubʿathūn* — "no, it is just a word he speaks; behind them is a barzakh until the Day they are raised". This is **the Quran's only explicit *barzakh* mention by name**.
- vv. 101-115: the Day of Judgment scene; the **scales** (*al-mawāzīn*) at vv. 102-103.
- v. 102: *fa-man thaqulat mawāzīnuhū fa-ulāʾika humu l-mufliḥūn* — **the second *flḥ*-anchor** (see §7).

### 6.4 vv. 116-118 — the ring-closure

- v. 116: *fa-taʿālā llāhu l-maliku l-ḥaqq lā ilāha illā huwa rabbu l-ʿarshi l-karīm* — "Exalted is Allāh, the True King; no deity but He, Lord of the noble Throne"
- v. 117: *wa-man yadʿu maʿa llāhi ilāhan ākhara lā burhāna lahū bihī fa-innamā ḥisābuhū ʿinda rabbihī innahū lā yufliḥu l-kāfirūn* — "and whoever invokes another god alongside Allāh, has no proof for it; his accounting is with his Lord; the disbelievers do not prosper" — **the third *flḥ*-anchor, INVERTED**.
- v. 118: *wa-qul Rabbi-ghfir wa-irḥam wa-anta khayru l-rāḥimīn* — "And say: Lord, forgive and have mercy; You are the best of the merciful"

### 6.5 The *flḥ* triple-inclusio

| v | Form | Polarity |
|:-:|:--|:--|
| 1 | *qad aflaḥa l-muʾminūn* | positive perfective; prosperity already-achieved by believers |
| 102 | *al-mufliḥūn* | positive participle; identifies the prosperous as those whose scales weigh heavy |
| 117 | *lā yufliḥu l-kāfirūn* | negative imperfective; disbelievers do not prosper |

This is a **structurally-precise opening / mid-late / closing inversion**. The empirical fact that *flḥ* appears exactly **3 times in Q 23** and at exactly these positions is verified in QAC. Pre-registered test Q023-F-03 (`06-novel-findings.md`) confirms.

## 8. Cross-surah content references

Q 23 has measurable lexical overlap with:
- **Q 70 al-Maʿārij vv. 22-35**: a parallel believer-typology block. al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar* on Q 70) explicitly compares the two. Both lists include khushūʿ-in-prayer, ḥifẓ-al-furūj, riʿāyat-amāna-wa-ʿahd, ḥifẓ-al-ṣalawāt. The pre-registered test Q023-F-01 (`06-novel-findings.md`) measures the lexical overlap.
- **Q 11 Hūd**: shares the *yā qawmi aʿbudū llāha* prophet-preaching formula but uses a different lattice-structure (*wa-ilā [tribe] akhāhum*). Pre-reg Q023-F-04.
- **Q 22 al-Ḥajj**: the immediate prior surah; al-Qurṭubī ad loc. v. 14 says "the discussion of nuṭfa and ʿalaqa and muḍgha and the rulings on them have already been treated at the start of al-Ḥajj" — meaning Q 22:5, which is the corpus's other major embryology-reference.
- **Q 75 al-Qiyāma vv. 37-40**: another major embryology-passage (*a-lam yaku nuṭfatan min maniyyin yumnā*). Q 23:12-14 + Q 75:37-40 + Q 22:5 together form the three classical Quranic embryology-loci.
- **Q 29 al-ʿAnkabūt v. 14**: the *fa-akhadhahumu l-ṭūfān wa-hum ẓālimūn* Nūḥ-flood reference, parallel to Q 23:27-28.

## 9. Content register

**Mixed Meccan-narrative-creedal-eschatological**:
- Movement I: **creedal** (typology of the prosperous believer).
- Movement II: **cosmological / embryological** (creation as evidence).
- Movement III: **narrative-prophetic** (Nūḥ → Ṣāliḥ-or-Hūd → Mūsā/Hārūn → ʿĪsā/Maryam → universal address to messengers).
- Movement IV: **wisdom + eschatological** (wealth-test, hearts-trembling, scrolls of deeds).
- Movement V: **rhetorical-eschatological + closing benediction** (sense-organs, three *qul* questions, barzakh, scales, ring-closure).

This **five-register sequence within a single surah** is unusual. Q 11 Hūd has a similar mixed register but is dominated by movement III (the prophet-cycle). Q 23 is more **balanced across all five registers** — a structural feature consistent with its top-decile UAS rank.

## 10. Vocabulary distinctness — summary

Per `01-empirical-profile.md` §6: Q 23 has 616 root-tokens / 255 distinct roots. Top roots are *qwl* (35), *rbb* (23), *Alh* (19), *kwn* (17), *qwm* (13), *xlq* (10), *rsl* (9), *Amn* (8) — a Meccan-narrative-prophet-creation profile. Notably, the *Amn* (believe) root is **not** over-concentrated despite the surah's title (8 tokens, 1.30% / 1000 root-tokens).

The five FR-nearest neighbours (Q 7, Q 21, Q 25, Q 36, Q 43) are all Meccan-narrative-prophet-cycle. The farthest neighbour Q 55 al-Raḥmān is Meccan-monorhyme-fawāṣil-supreme — confirming the project's anti-twin lock that monorhyme + content are orthogonal axes.

## 11. Honest limits

- The "five-movement" structure I propose is not the unique classical reading. al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar* on Q 23 reads it as four sections (typology / creation / prophet-history / ring-closure); al-Rāzī as three (introduction / proofs / conclusion). The five-movement reading I use is descriptively useful but is one of several defensible structures.
- The "Q 23 is more abstract than Q 11" claim (§4.7) is computed empirically in Q023-F-04; the magnitude of the abstraction is mild, not extreme.
- The "barzakh is mentioned only in Q 23:100 by name in the Quran" is correct; the cognate root *brz* (separate / present) appears in other contexts but the technical-noun *barzakh* (intermediate-state) appears at Q 23:100 + Q 25:53 (where it refers to the freshwater-saltwater barrier between two seas, not the eschatological barzakh) + Q 55:20 (same physical sense). The eschatological-barzakh sense is **uniquely Q 23:100**.
