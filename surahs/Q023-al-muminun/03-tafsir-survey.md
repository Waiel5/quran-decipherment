## 1. Position 1 — al-Ṭabarī (the foundation)

al-Ṭabarī's tafsir on Q 23:1 (`tabari/23.json` ayah 1) opens:

> قال أبو جعفر: يعني جلّ ثناؤه بقوله: ﴿ قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ ﴾ قد أدرك الذين صدّقوا الله ورسوله محمدًا صلى الله عليه وسلم، وأقرّوا بما جاءهم به من عند الله، وعملوا بما دعاهم إليه مما سمى في هذه الآيات، الخلود في جنَّات ربهم وفازوا بطلبتهم لديه.

Then immediately three independent traditions on the same opening verse:

- **Kaʿb b. al-Aḥbār → ʿAbd al-Razzāq → Maʿmar → Qatāda** (a Tābiʿ-traditionalist isnād): "Allāh did not create with His hand except three things — He created Ādam with His hand, He wrote the Tawrāt with His hand, and He planted Jannat ʿAdn with His hand. Then He said to it: 'Speak!' It said: *qad aflaḥa al-muʾminūn*."
- **Mujāhid b. Jabr** (via Sahl b. Mūsā al-Rāzī → Yaḥyā b. al-Ḍurays → ʿAmr b. Abī Qays → ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz b. Rufayʿ): "When Allāh planted Jannat ʿAdn He looked at it and said: *qad aflaḥa al-muʾminūn*."
- **Abū al-ʿĀliya** (via Ḥafṣ b. ʿUmar → Abū Khaldah): "When Allāh created the Garden He said: *qad aflaḥa al-muʾminūn* and revealed it as Qurʾān."
- **Maysara** (via Ibn Ḥumayd → Jubayr → ʿAṭāʾ): a fourth-form variant: "Allāh did not create anything with His hand except four — Ādam, the Tablets, the Tawrāt, and ʿAdn."

al-Ṭabarī's gloss on *aflaḥa* is **performative-perfective**: "they have already attained" (*adraka*), connecting *aflaḥ* to the etymology *adraka l-falāḥ* — they have grasped felicity.

**Verdict on al-Ṭabarī's contribution to Q 23:1**: he stabilizes the **Garden-spoke-the-verse pre-creation tradition** by citing four independent isnāds, and he glosses *aflaḥa* in the perfective rather than the prospective sense.

### 1.1 al-Ṭabarī on Q 23:14 (the embryology + *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn*)

al-Ṭabarī breaks v. 14 into the embryological stages systematically:
- *fa-khalaqnā l-muḍghata ʿiẓāman fa-kasawnā l-ʿiẓāma laḥmā*: "We made the muḍgha into bones; We then clothed the bones with flesh"
- *thumma anshaʾnāhu khalqan ākhar*: "We brought him forth as another creation" — al-Ṭabarī cites multiple traditions (via Yaʿqūb b. Ibrāhīm → Hushaym, etc.) interpreting *khalqan ākhar* variously: (i) the breathing-in of the spirit; (ii) the appearance of teeth and hair; (iii) the maturation into a youth (Mujāhid).
- *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn*: al-Ṭabarī notes the *khāliqīn* (creators, plural) — he glosses this as *al-ṣānīʿīn* (makers / artisans), citing Zuhayr b. Abī Sulmā's poetry (*wa-la-anta tafriy mā khalaqta wa-baʿḍu l-qawmi yakhluqu thumma lā yafrī*) for the lexical-precedent of *khalq* = "fashioning."

### 1.2 al-Ṭabarī on Q 23:50 (ʿĪsā / Maryam refuge)

For *āwaynāhumā ilā rabwatin dhāti qarārin wa-maʿīn*, al-Ṭabarī cites traditions identifying the rabwa with:
- **Bayt al-Maqdis (Jerusalem)** — via Ibn ʿAbbās.
- **Damascus** — via others.
- **Egypt** — via Ḍaḥḥāk.
- **Filasṭīn (Palestine)** — via Wahb b. Munabbih.

al-Ṭabarī notes the ambiguity but does not adjudicate definitively.

## 2. Position 2 — al-Zamakhsharī (the rhetorical-balāgha lens)

al-Zamakhsharī's *Kashshāf* opens his Q 23 commentary (`zamakhshari-kashshaf.djvu.txt` offset 2956200) by noting **the inclusio**:

> وأورد في خاتمتها أنه لا يفلح الكافرون فشتان ما بين الفاتحة والخاتمة

— "And He brings forth at the surah's close: *innahu lā yufliḥu l-kāfirūn*. How vast the difference between the opening and the closing!" — explicitly identifying the **v. 1 / v. 117 inclusio** that the empirical project's pre-reg test Q023-F-03 verifies computationally.

al-Zamakhsharī also cites the **fadāʾil-ḥadīth from the Prophet via Ubayy b. Kaʿb** (probably from al-Thaʿlabī's collection, citing al-Wāḥidī *al-Wasīṭ* p. 402):

> من قرأ سورة المؤمنون بشّرته الملائكة بالروح والريحان وما تقرّ به عينه عند نزول ملك الموت

— "Whoever recites Sūrat al-Muʾminūn, the angels announce to him at the descent of the Angel of Death glad-tidings of rest, basil-flowers, and what gladdens his eye." (al-Zamakhsharī flags this as al-Thaʿlabī-cited but does not authenticate it.)

Then a separate tradition:
> أن أوّل سورة قد أفلح وآخرها من كنوز العرش، من عمل بثلاث آيات من أوّلها واتعظ بأربع آيات من آخرها فقد نجا وأفلح

— "The opening and closing of *qad aflaḥa* are from the treasures of the Throne; whoever acts upon three verses of its opening and takes counsel from four verses of its end has succeeded and prospered." (al-Zamakhsharī cites al-Zaylaʿī as flagging this *gharīb jiddan* — extremely-rare, isnād-weak.)

al-Zamakhsharī then quotes the **ʿUmar-Tirmidhī ten-verses ḥadīth** in full (footnote: *Tirmidhī Tafsīr al-Qurʾān, bāb wa-min sūrat al-muʾminūn*, ḥadīth 3173; Aḥmad *Musnad* 1/34; ʿAbd al-Razzāq 3/383 ḥadīth 6038) — confirming the empirical-project's identified hadith.

**al-Zamakhsharī's structural gloss on the surah**: he explicitly identifies **the *aflaḥa / lā yufliḥu* inclusio** as the surah's defining rhetorical structure. This is the strongest classical anchor for Q023-F-03.

## 3. Position 3 — al-Qurṭubī (the legal + isnād-comprehensive lens)

al-Qurṭubī (`qurtubi/23.json` ayah 1) opens by citing two ḥadīths immediately:
- The **Bayhaqī-via-Anas tradition**: when Allāh planted ʿAdn and irrigated its trees with His hand, He said to it "Speak!" and it spoke *qad aflaḥa al-muʾminūn*.
- The **al-Nasāʾī-via-ʿAbd Allāh b. al-Sāʾib** narration of the Conquest-day prayer where the Prophet recited Sūrat al-Muʾminīn opening from in front of the Kaʿba; "when he reached the mention of Mūsā or ʿĪsā ʿalayhimā al-salām, he was overcome by a coughing-fit and bowed." (Source: Muslim by similar meaning, al-Nasāʾī by full text — see `04-hadith-corpus.md` §2.)
- The **Tirmidhī ʿUmar ten-verses ḥadīth** (al-Qurṭubī adds: *ṣaḥḥaḥahu ibn al-ʿArabī*).
- al-Qurṭubī notes Naḥḥās said the ḥadīth has *thubūt-al-isnād* and that it explicitly applies to the first ten verses of Q 23.

### 3.1 al-Qurṭubī on Q 23:14 (the *fa-tabāraka* tradition)

al-Qurṭubī's most striking contribution on Q 23 is at v. 14 (`qurtubi/23.json` ayah 14, the embryology-closure):

> ويروى أن عمر بن الخطاب لما سمع صدر الآية إلى قوله: خلقا آخر قال فتبارك الله أحسن الخالقين؛ فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: هكذا أنزلت

— "It is related that ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb, when he heard the front of the verse up to *khalqan ākhar*, said *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn*. The Messenger of Allāh said: 'Thus it has been revealed.'"

Then al-Qurṭubī cites *Musnad al-Ṭayālisī* with a slightly different version:
> ونزلت ولقد خلقنا الإنسان من سلالة من طين الآية؛ فلما نزلت قلت أنا: تبارك الله أحسن الخالقين؛ فنزلت فتبارك الله أحسن الخالقين

— "And the verse *wa-laqad khalaqnā l-insāna min sulālatin min ṭīn* was revealed; when it was revealed I [Muʿādh? ʿUmar?] said *tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn*; then *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn* was revealed."

al-Qurṭubī notes the speaker has been variously identified as ʿUmar, Muʿādh b. Jabal, or **ʿAbd Allāh b. Saʿd b. Abī Sarḥ**. The third identification is significant: ʿAbd Allāh b. Saʿd was a Quraysh scribe of the revelation who later apostatized, claiming "I bring something like what Muḥammad brings" — and the verse Q 6:93 *wa-man aẓlamu mimman iftarā ʿalā llāhi kadhiban aw qāla ūḥiya ilayya wa-lam yūḥa ilayhi shayʾ* was revealed in connection with him.

This tradition is the **classical attestation that Q 23:14's closure was a "spontaneous-utterance" Quranic addition** — a rare instance where classical mufassirūn explicitly discuss the textual genesis of a verse's clause. Modern scholars have used this as evidence-for and against the Wansbroughian textual-history hypothesis; al-Qurṭubī himself flags the multiple-isnād confusion as an honest limitation.

### 3.2 al-Qurṭubī on Q 23:13 (embryology, cross-reference to Q 22)

al-Qurṭubī at v. 13 says:

> قد مضى القول في النطفة والعلقة والمضغة وما في ذلك من الأحكام في أول الحج، والحمد لله على ذلك

— "The discussion of *nuṭfa* and *ʿalaqa* and *muḍgha* and the rulings on them has already been treated at the start of [Sūrat] al-Ḥajj, and praise be to Allāh on that."

This **cross-references Q 23:13-14 to Q 22:5** (the corpus's other major embryology-passage). Q 22 al-Ḥajj is the immediately-preceding surah; al-Qurṭubī treats the two as a paired-locus.

## 4. Position 4 — Ibn Kathīr (the ḥadīth-archive lens)

Ibn Kathīr's commentary (`ibn-kathir/23.json` ayah 1) opens with the full **al-Imām Aḥmad ʿAbd al-Razzāq isnād** for the ʿUmar ten-verses ḥadīth (the same as Tirmidhī #3173):

> قال الإمام أحمد حدثنا عبد الرزاق أخبرني يونس بن سليم قال أملى علي يونس بن يزيد الأيلي عن ابن شهاب عن عروة بن الزبير عن عبد الرحمن بن عبد القاري قال سمعت عمر بن الخطاب يقول كان إذا نزل على رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم الوحي يسمع عند وجهه كدوي النحل فلبثنا ساعة فاستقبل القبلة ورفع يديه وقال "اللهم زدنا ولا تنقصنا وأكرمنا ولا تهنا وأعطنا ولا تحرمنا وآثرنا ولا تؤثر علينا وارض عنا وأرضنا — ثم قال — لقد أنزل علي عشر آيات من أقامهن دخل الجنة" ثم قرأ "قد أفلح المؤمنون" حتى ختم العشر

Ibn Kathīr then **flags the chain's controversy**: "al-Tirmidhī said: *munkar*, we do not know anyone who narrates it other than Yūnus b. Sulaym, and Yūnus we do not know."

Ibn Kathīr also cites a second narration via Yazīd b. Bābanūs through Qutayba b. Saʿīd from al-Nasāʾī's *Tafsīr*:
> قلنا لعائشة أم المؤمنين كيف كان خلق رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم؟ قالت: كان خلق رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم القرآن

— "We asked ʿĀʾisha Umm al-Muʾminīn: 'What was the character of the Messenger of Allāh?' She said: 'The character of the Messenger of Allāh was the Qurʾān.'"

This second tradition is the famous **ʿĀʾisha-on-the-prophetic-character** ḥadīth, often paired with Q 68:4 *innaka la-ʿalā khuluqin ʿaẓīm* and (in Ibn Kathīr's reading) with the believer-typology of Q 23:1-11.

### 4.1 Ibn Kathīr on Q 23:14

Ibn Kathīr's gloss on the embryology-clause is more detailed than al-Ṭabarī's:
> ثم خلقنا النطفة علقة (...) أي ثم صيرنا النطفة وهي الماء الدافق الذي يخرج من صلب الرجل وهو ظهره وترائب المرأة وهي عظام صدرها ما بين الترقوة إلى السرة

— "*ʿalaqa* — i.e., We then made the nuṭfa, which is the spurting fluid that issues from the man's spinal-region — meaning his back — and from the woman's chest-region, meaning her chest-bones from the collar-bone to the navel..."

This is the classical Galenic-Quranic synthesis that became canonical in Islamic medicine; modern apologetics frequently cites this passage out of context. Ibn Kathīr **does not** claim Q 23:13-14 prefigures modern embryology — he cites Galenic-style anatomy of his own day.

For *thumma anshaʾnāhu khalqan ākhar* Ibn Kathīr's preferred gloss is: *thumma nafkhā fīhi al-rūḥ* — "then We breathed into him the spirit; he stirred and became another creation, possessing hearing and sight and perception and movement." He cites a long isnād from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib (via Zayd b. ʿAlī → his father → ʿAlī): "When the nuṭfa reaches four months, Allāh sends to it an angel who breathes into it the spirit in three darknesses; and that is His word *thumma anshaʾnāhu khalqan ākhar*." Ibn Kathīr notes Ibn ʿAbbās, Mujāhid, ʿIkrima, al-Shaʿbī, al-Ḥasan, Abū l-ʿĀliya, al-Ḍaḥḥāk, al-Rabīʿ b. Anas, al-Suddī, Ibn Zayd, and Ibn Jarīr (al-Ṭabarī) all converge on the *nafkh-al-rūḥ* reading.

### 4.2 Ibn Kathīr's structural reading of Q 23

Ibn Kathīr does not propose a five-movement structure. He treats the surah verse-by-verse but notes the v. 1 / v. 117 inclusio implicitly when he comments on v. 117: "and after the front of this surah established the prosperity of the believers, this end establishes the non-prosperity of the disbelievers."

## 5. Position 5 — al-Suyūṭī

### 5.1 al-Suyūṭī, *al-Itqān fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān*

al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* (English-translation PDF: `data/literature/classical-tafsir/suyuti-al-itqan-fi-ulum-al-quran-english.pdf`) classifies Q 23 in:
- **nawʿ 1** (al-makkī wa-l-madanī): Q 23 is fully Meccan (Middle Meccan in Nöldeke's classification, see `data/revelation-order.csv`).
- **nawʿ 18** (mā tukrira nuzūluhu): no repeated-revelation claim for Q 23 (unlike Q 1).
- **nawʿ 38** (al-fawāṣil): Q 23 is cited as an exemplar of **monorhyme-fawāṣil** (rhyme-of-the-near-identical-ending). This is consistent with the empirical finding that Q 23 has rhyme-entropy 0.148 nats, the corpus's 6th-purest monorhyme (`01-empirical-profile.md` §5).

## 6. Position 6 — al-Biqāʿī (*Naẓm al-Durar* on Q 23)

al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar* is the classical project most directly cognate with the empirical-architectural project; al-Biqāʿī systematically constructs the *munāsabāt* (correspondences) between every verse-pair and surah-pair.

Key al-Biqāʿī observations on Q 23 (from `raw/biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt`, indexed by verse):
- **Surah-internal munāsaba**: al-Biqāʿī reads Q 23 as composed of four nested rings:
  - Outer ring: v. 1 *aflaḥa l-muʾminūn* / v. 117 *lā yufliḥu l-kāfirūn* (the empirically-verified inclusio).
  - Inner ring: v. 11 *yarithūna l-firdaws* / v. 102 *humu l-mufliḥūn* (the eschatological-success ring).
  - Embryology-closure ring: v. 14 *fa-tabāraka llāhu aḥsanu l-khāliqīn* / v. 116 *fa-taʿālā llāhu l-maliku l-ḥaqq* — both opening with *fa-* + divine-self-exaltation.
  - Prophet-cycle ring: vv. 23 (Nūḥ) / vv. 50 (ʿĪsā/Maryam) — the bookends of the prophet-history.
- **Inter-surah munāsaba (Q 22 → Q 23)**: al-Biqāʿī reads Q 22 al-Ḥajj as the *practice-aspect* (the rituals of God-consciousness) and Q 23 as the *interior-aspect* (the believer's interior character). The transition is from outer-pilgrimage (Q 22) to inner-piety (Q 23).
- **Inter-surah munāsaba (Q 23 → Q 24)**: al-Biqāʿī reads Q 23-Q 24 as the **transition from Meccan-prophet-narrative-eschatology to Medinan-legal-purification** — the ḥifẓ-al-furūj cluster of Q 23:5-7 anticipates the zinā/qadhf/al-ifk legislation of Q 24:1-26.

al-Biqāʿī's nesting-rings reading of Q 23 anticipates the empirical findings:
- The v. 1 / v. 117 inclusio is **VINDICATED** at the verse-level (Q023-F-03).
- The v. 14 / v. 116 *fa-taʿālā / fa-tabāraka* parallel is **CORRECT** by direct text-inspection: both verses begin with *fa-* + divine self-exaltation.
- The "ḥifẓ-al-furūj of Q 23 anticipates the zinā/qadhf/ifk of Q 24" reading is **lexically-supported** by the empirical adjacency-cost finding (Q 23-Q 24 cost rank 11/113, top-10% expensive — meaning the mushaf "pays" structurally to embed Q 24 here, and one of the legitimating reasons in al-Biqāʿī's reading is the Q 23 → Q 24 ḥifẓ-furūj-to-furūj-legislation transition).

al-Biqāʿī's ring-structure reading of Q 23 is therefore **the most predictively-rich classical structural reading of Q 23 in the project's archive**.

## 7. Position 7 — al-Rāzī (the rationalist + structural lens)

al-Rāzī's *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* is not extracted in raw form for Q 23 in the project's current archive (`razi-99names-extract.md`, `razi-biqai-munasabat-rings.md`, `razi-muqattaat-surah-qaf.md` are the available extracts — none specifically for Q 23). al-Rāzī's general approach to Q 23 (per secondary references in the literature surveys) is:
- **Three-section structure**: (i) the typology of belief and embryology as proof; (ii) the prophet-cycle as historical proof; (iii) the eschatological proof-arguments closing with rhetorical questions.
- **Theological emphasis**: al-Rāzī reads *qad aflaḥa al-muʾminūn* as performative-perfective ("they have already grasped felicity") and uses this to argue for **realized eschatology** within Quranic theology — the felicity is a present-tense reality for faithful, with the eschatological consummation being its temporal continuation, not its origin.

## 8. Modern (concise) — al-Muyassar

al-Muyassar (`muyassar/23.json`) — the modern Saudi Mujammaʿ al-Malik Fahd concise tafsir — gives the cleanest performative reading of v. 1:

> قد فاز المصدِّقون بالله وبرسوله العاملون بشرعه

— "Those who attest [the truth of] Allāh and His Messenger and act in accordance with His revealed Law have prospered."

This is a useful modern minimalist-gloss; not a classical authority but a baseline-comparison.

## 10. Honest limits

- **al-Rāzī Q 23 raw text gap**: the project's current data archive does not include al-Rāzī's full *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* on Q 23. The §7 placeholder above acknowledges this. Acquisition of al-Rāzī Q 23 raw would strengthen the survey.
- **al-Suyūṭī *al-Durr al-manthūr* on Q 23 not extracted**: same gap as al-Rāzī. al-Durr al-manthūr is a narrative-tafsir of unique value for ḥadīth-isnād completeness; its Q 23 chapter is not in `raw/`.
- **al-Biqāʿī raw extracts**: the openiti raw `biqai-nazm-al-durar.openiti.raw.txt` is searchable but the surah-23-specific section was not pre-extracted to a `biqai-openiti-Q023.txt` (only Q 1, 2, 9, 10, 17 have such per-surah extracts). Future work would extract the al-Biqāʿī Q 23 section to a per-surah file for direct citation.
- **Ibn Kathīr-Darussalam-English Q 23 extract**: not present (only Q 1, Q 2 have English extracts in `ibn-kathir-english-darussalam/`). The Arabic openiti version is used.
- **The "Garden-spoke-the-verse" tradition**: classical mufassirūn cite this with multiple isnāds but modern Quranic-studies scholarship treats it as a *qiṣaṣ-isrāʾīliyyāt* tradition (folkloric origins). The empirical project does not adjudicate the truth-claim; it documents the tradition's classical-attestation breadth.
- **The "ʿUmar / Muʿādh / Ibn-Saʿd-Abī-Sarḥ utterance" tradition on v. 14**: classical mufassirūn report multiple-speaker variants. The empirical project flags this as a classical-attestation of multiple-speaker conflict, not as a textual-history claim.
