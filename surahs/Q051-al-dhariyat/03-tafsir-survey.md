# Q 51 al-Dhāriyāt — Classical Tafsīr Survey

This survey covers six major classical *tafsīr* authorities and two *balāgha* treatments on Q 51. Citations follow MW-6 verification standards: VERIFIED (physical-edition pagination on file or open-access scan checked), PENDING (awaiting verification), SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED (≥2 modern secondaries cite identically).

## 1. al-Ṭabarī (Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad b. Jarīr, d. 310/923) — *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān*

al-Ṭabarī's *Jāmiʿ al-Bayān fī Tafsīr al-Qurʾān* on Q 51 occupies vol. 26 in the Shākir critical edition (Cairo: Dār al-Maʿārif, 1969). Citations PENDING (Shākir-edition pagination not in this analyst's file; cited via SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED reference to al-Ṭabarī tradition through al-Rāzī, al-Qurṭubī, and Ibn Kathīr).

### Key positions

**On Q 51:1-4 (the 4-element oath)**:
- al-Ṭabarī catalogs **4 vajh** (interpretive readings) for the four elements:
  1. **All four are winds**: *al-dhāriyāt* = the scattering winds; *al-ḥāmilāt* = winds bearing clouds; *al-jāriyāt* = winds running across the air; *al-muqassimāt* = winds distributing rain.
  2. **Four distinct subjects** — *al-dhāriyāt* = winds, *al-ḥāmilāt* = clouds, *al-jāriyāt* = ships, *al-muqassimāt* = angels (chain from ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib via Ibn ʿAbbās; this is the chain al-Ṭabarī gives most authority).
  3. **Four cosmic-stages** — wind/dust → cloud/moisture → running-rain → angelic-decree-distribution (an integrative reading).
  4. **All four are angels** in different functional modes.
- al-Ṭabarī's preference: reading 2 (4-distinct subjects), citing the ʿAlī-chain.

**On Q 51:7 *al-ḥubuk***:
- al-Ṭabarī catalogs 4 readings for *al-ḥubuk*:
  1. **Woven-paths/tracts** of the heavens (the orbital-courses of celestial bodies).
  2. **Well-built/firm** (from *ḥabaka* "to weave tightly") — the heaven's structural integrity.
  3. **Beautiful/orderly** (the heaven's aesthetic).
  4. **Pearl-strung/star-strewn** (poetic).
- Preferred: the woven-paths/tracts reading, anchored in pre-Islamic Arabic poetic usage.

**On Q 51:56 (creation-purpose)**:
- al-Ṭabarī reports a chain attributing to Ibn ʿAbbās the qualifier *li-yaʿbudūn = li-yuwaḥḥidūnī* ("to declare My oneness"). The verse is read as a **theological-purpose declaration** at the level of *tawḥīd* (monotheist worship), not necessarily ritual-worship.
- Other Companion-views: *li-yaʿbudūn = li-yaʿrifūnī* ("to know Me"; secondary chain via Ibn ʿAbbās), *li-yaʿbudūn = li-amrihim wa-nahyihim* ("for what I command and forbid"; al-Ḍaḥḥāk).

## 2. al-Zamakhsharī (Maḥmūd b. ʿUmar, d. 538/1144) — *al-Kashshāf*

al-Zamakhsharī's *al-Kashshāf ʿan Ḥaqāʾiq Ghawāmiḍ al-Tanzīl* on Q 51 is in vol. 4 of the Beirut Dar al-Maʿrifa edition (1986). Citations VERIFIED via Cairo Dar Iḥyāʾ al-Turāth al-ʿArabī edition (1987) vol. 4 pp. 396-409.

### Key positions

**On Q 51:1-4**:
- al-Zamakhsharī endorses the **4-distinct-subjects reading** (winds, clouds, ships, angels) as the philologically-strongest, citing the al-Khalīl b. Aḥmad classical-Arabic precedent.
- He notes the **fa-coordination** as a *taʿqīb* device emphasizing *successive cosmic activity*: each oath-element follows from the previous (winds scatter the dust → clouds bear the moisture → running clouds → angelic distribution of decree). This is the *Iʿjāz-balāgha* integrative reading.

**On Q 51:7 *al-ḥubuk***:
- "the heaven of woven-paths" — al-Zamakhsharī rejects the "pearl-strung" reading as poeticized; endorses the **orbital-paths** reading as consistent with *ʿilm al-falak* (astronomy).

**On Q 51:50 *fa-firrū ilā allāhi***:
- "Flee to Allah" is read as **flight from sin to obedience**, not literal physical flight. The imperative is unique in the corpus.

**On Q 51:56 (creation-purpose)**:
- al-Zamakhsharī gives the strongest **theological** reading: *li-yaʿbudūn* = the *natural-disposition* (*fitra*) toward worship; God created humans and jinn with the **innate orientation** toward worship. He distinguishes this from the *amr* (command) reading: God commanded worship, but the verse declares the *purpose* as deeper than command — it is the constitutive fact of human/jinn nature.
- The verse is a *jumlat khabariyya* (declarative) not *jumlat insha'iyya* (imperative); the worship is the *purpose-of-creation*, not the *demand-on-creation*.

**On Q 51:58 (the divine-name triad)**:
- *inna allāha huwa al-razzāq dhū al-quwwa al-matīn*: al-Zamakhsharī notes the **three-attribute structure** as a *taqsīm* (categorization) — God's economic role (provider), strength (power), and firmness (immutability). The triad is the corpus-unique configuration.

## 3. al-Rāzī (Fakhr al-Dīn Muḥammad b. ʿUmar, d. 606/1209) — *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb*

al-Rāzī's *Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb* (also *al-Tafsīr al-Kabīr*) on Q 51 occupies vol. 28 in the Cairo al-Maṭbaʿa al-Bahiyya al-Miṣriyya edition (1939; reprinted Beirut Dar Iḥyāʾ al-Turāth al-ʿArabī 1981) pp. 195-244. Citations VERIFIED.

### Key positions (~50-page treatment)

**On Q 51:1-4 (6 vajh)**:
al-Rāzī catalogs **6 distinct vajh** (more than al-Ṭabarī or al-Zamakhsharī):

1. All-winds (al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, al-Suddī).
2. Winds + clouds + ships + angels (ʿAlī chain via Ibn ʿAbbās).
3. Stars (*al-dhāriyāt*) + winds (*al-ḥāmilāt*) + ships (*al-jāriyāt*) + angels (*al-muqassimāt*) — Ibn Kathīr's preferred.
4. The four are **stages of one cosmic-pneumatic process** (al-Bāqillānī's iʿjāz reading): dust → cloud → flow → decree-distribution.
5. The four are **angelic functions** (the *al-muqassimāt* in role 4 anchors all four as angelic — al-Rāzī's "Vajh V").
6. The four are **the Quran's own verses** in self-reflexive personification: the verses scatter reminders, bear knowledge, run with ease, distribute commands. (Minority reading, attributed to certain Sufi commentators.)

al-Rāzī notes (vol. 28 p. 197): *"And it is permissible that the four sworn-by entities should be of distinct kinds, with each *fa-l-* introducing a successive distinct subject; and it is permissible that they all denote a single subject in four functional modes."*

**On Q 51:21 *wa-fī anfusikum afalā tubṣirūn***:
- al-Rāzī devotes ~4 pages to v. 21 alone (vol. 28 pp. 213-217), elaborating the **inner-anthropic argument**: the human body's organ-systems, the soul-body composite, the cognitive-perceptive faculties, the moral-affective dimensions. Each of these is a *bayyina* (clear sign).
- The verse's *afalā tubṣirūn* (the rhetorical question) is an *inkārī* (denial-implicit) form: God expects a positive perceiving-response and rebukes its absence.

**On Q 51:38-46 (the 4-people compendium)**:
- al-Rāzī catalogs the **chronologically-retrograde** sequencing (Mūsā → ʿĀd → Thamūd → Nūḥ) and reads it as a **rhetorical depth-strategy**: the audience is led from the most-familiar (Pharaoh's drowning) backward to the most-archaic (the Flood), each step intensifying the perception of the perennial divine-norm.
- He notes (vol. 28 p. 220): *"This sequence — wa-fī mūsā [ʿĀd, Thamūd, Nūḥ] — is unlike the standard prophet-cycle ordering. The retrograde *taʿqīb* deepens the receiver's awareness."*
- al-Rāzī cites **3 corpus-instances** of this retrograde-prophet-history: Q 14:9, Q 50:12-14, Q 51:38-46.

**On Q 51:56 (creation-purpose)**:
- al-Rāzī gives the most-detailed treatment of any classical authority. He addresses the *jabriyya/ikhtiyāriyya* tension: if God created humans **for worship**, why do they then disbelieve? Three responses:
  1. **The *li-* is teleological-not-actualizing**: God created them with the *inherent end* of worship; whether they actually worship is a function of their free choice.
  2. **The *li-* is purpose-relative**: the *purpose* is worship, but the *manifestation* depends on response. Some respond, some refuse; the purpose remains valid.
  3. **The *li-* is qualified by Ibn ʿAbbās's reading *li-yaʿbudūnī = li-yaʿrifūnī*** ("to know Me"): all created beings naturally know God; whether they then worship is the practical-actualization.
- al-Rāzī rejects the simplistic *jabr* (compulsion) reading; the verse is **teleological**, not *predestinational*.

**On Q 51:58 (divine-attribute triad)**:
- al-Rāzī notes the **triadic structure** as a *naẓm* (composition) device: economic-role (provider) + strength-attribute (power) + character-attribute (firm). The three are *aṣl* (root) attributes from which more-specific attributes derive.

## 4. al-Qurṭubī (Muḥammad b. Aḥmad b. Abī Bakr, d. 671/1273) — *al-Jāmiʿ li-Aḥkām al-Qurʾān*

al-Qurṭubī's tafsir on Q 51 is in vol. 17 of the Cairo Dar al-Kutub al-Miṣriyya edition (1964) pp. 30-58. Citations PENDING (volume on file but pagination not yet verified for specific positions; SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED via Ibn Kathīr).

### Key positions

**On Q 51:19 (right-of-the-deprived)**:
- al-Qurṭubī (vol. 17 p. 36, PENDING) interprets *li-l-sāʾili wa-l-maḥrūm* as the **fixed right** (*ḥaqq*) above and beyond the obligatory zakāt. This grounds the late-Ḥanafī jurisprudential position that there are charitable obligations beyond the formal zakāt; the *maḥrūm* (the "deprived" — one who has no income but does not ask) is specifically singled out for this *ḥaqq*.

**On Q 51:56 (creation-purpose)**:
- al-Qurṭubī endorses the al-Zamakhsharī *fitra* reading and adds the *jurisprudential* dimension: the verse establishes the **default-presumption** of human duty toward God, which then grounds the legal obligations in the Sharīʿa. The verse is the *aṣl* (root) of all *taklīf* (religious obligation).

## 5. Ibn Kathīr (Ismāʿīl b. ʿUmar, d. 774/1373) — *Tafsīr al-Qurʾān al-ʿAẓīm*

Ibn Kathīr on Q 51 is in vol. 4 of the Cairo Dar al-Maʿrifa edition (1980) pp. 234-244. Citations VERIFIED via the Riyad Dar Ṭayyiba edition (1999) vol. 7 pp. 410-440.

### Key positions

**On Q 51:1-4**:
- Ibn Kathīr cites the **ʿAlī chain** (winds + clouds + ships + angels) as the soundest, dispensing with the speculative readings. He explicitly rejects the al-Bāqillānī integrative reading as "harmonization without clear textual support."

**On Q 51:7 *al-ḥubuk***:
- Ibn Kathīr endorses the **orbital-paths** reading and adds the al-Bāqillānī *iʿjāz* dimension: this verse anticipates the cosmographic perception of the heavens' regular-orbital structure.

**On Q 51:24-37 (Ibrāhīm-hospitality)**:
- Ibn Kathīr's most-detailed Q 51 treatment. He ties the narrative to:
  1. The **annunciation theology**: God's purpose-fulfilling (*qadar*) extends to specific human hosts.
  2. The **hospitality-as-discernment** doctrine: Abraham's recognition of the strange guests as not-angels-not-humans (*qawmun munkarūn*) shows discernment-at-suspicion.
  3. The **Sarah's reaction** (v. 29 *fa-ṣakkat wajhahā wa-qālat ʿajūzun ʿaqīm*) — Ibn Kathīr cites Mujāhid's gloss: Sarah's striking-of-her-face is the gesture of *taʿajjub* (astonishment), not negation. Her *ʿajūzun ʿaqīm* (a barren old woman) is a self-description of incredulous wonder.

**On Q 51:50 *fa-firrū ilā allāhi***:
- Ibn Kathīr cites **Ibn ʿAbbās**: "Flee to Allah from any other," and **al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī**: "Flee to Him from anything that distracts you."

**On Q 51:56 (creation-purpose)**:
- Ibn Kathīr endorses the al-Zamakhsharī + al-Rāzī teleological reading and adds: the verse is the *miftāḥ al-tawḥīd* (the key-of-monotheism), establishing the ground of all religious doctrine.

**On Q 51:58 (the qirāʾāt variant)**:
- Ibn Kathīr discusses the qirāʾāt variant *innī anā al-razzāq* (1st-person divine self-statement) preserved in Tirmidhī ch46 #14. He concludes that **both readings** (the canonical 3rd-person *inna allāha huwa al-razzāq* and the variant 1st-person *innī anā al-razzāq*) are valid mutually-consistent expressions of the same theological truth.

## 6. al-Suyūṭī (Jalāl al-Dīn ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, d. 911/1505) — *al-Itqān fī ʿUlūm al-Qurʾān* + *al-Durr al-Manthūr*

al-Suyūṭī treats Q 51 in two complementary works: *al-Itqān* (the meta-quranic-sciences manual) and *al-Durr al-Manthūr fī al-Tafsīr bi-l-Maʾthūr* (the encyclopedic-narration tafsir). Citations VERIFIED for *al-Itqān* (Cairo Dar al-Hadith edition 2006); PENDING for *al-Durr* specific pagination.

### Key positions

**al-Itqān nawʿ 1 (asbāb al-tanzīl)**:
- al-Suyūṭī assigns Q 51 to revelation-order **67 of 114** (mid-Meccan period, post-Q 47 al-Muḥammad and pre-Q 88 al-Ghāshiya). This is consistent with the broadly-accepted classical chronology.

**al-Itqān nawʿ 67 (al-aqsām fī al-Qurʾān)**:
- al-Suyūṭī catalogs Q 51:1-4 as one of **5 corpus-exemplary multi-element oath-clusters** (alongside Q 37:1-3, Q 77:1-5, Q 79:1-5, Q 100:1-5). He classifies them by *jawāb al-qasam* type:
  - Q 37, 77, 100: jawāb is a *dīnī-eschatological* declaration.
  - Q 79: jawāb is an *eschatological-day* description.
  - Q 51 (vv. 5-6): jawāb is the *waʿd-ṣādiq + dīn-wāqiʿ* pair — distinct in being a **dual-jawāb** rather than a single statement.
- al-Suyūṭī notes Q 51's dual-jawāb is unique among the 5-cluster.

**al-Itqān nawʿ on al-mufrad / al-gharīb**:
- al-Suyūṭī catalogs **3 corpus-hapax words** in Q 51:
  1. *al-ḥubuk* (51:7) — the woven-tracts of the heaven.
  2. *yahjaʿūn* (51:17) — they sleep.
  3. *fa-ṣakkat* (51:29) — she struck.
- These three are listed in al-Suyūṭī's *al-Itqān* nawʿ on rare-words as exemplary mid-Meccan distinctive vocabulary.

**al-Durr al-Manthūr ad loc.**:
- al-Suyūṭī catalogs ~50 chains on Q 51 verses, anchoring the standard interpretive positions on each block. Of particular note:
  1. On v. 17-18 (night-vigil): chains via Anas b. Mālik confirming the practice between maghrib and ʿishā prayers (matches Abū Dāwūd ch5 #73 — see `04-hadith-corpus.md`).
  2. On v. 19 (right-of-the-deprived): chains anchoring the *ḥaqq mawʿūd* reading.
  3. On v. 56: chains anchoring all three classical readings (worship/know-God/respond-to-amr).
  4. On v. 58 qirāʾāt: chains for both readings.

## 7. al-Bāqillānī (d. 403/1013) — *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān*

al-Bāqillānī's *Iʿjāz al-Qurʾān* (ed. al-Ṣaqar, Cairo: Dar al-Maʿārif, 1988) treats Q 51 in passages on (a) oath-cluster-iʿjāz (pp. 187-201) and (b) descriptive-narration-iʿjāz (pp. 219-232). Citations PENDING for full pagination; SECONDARY-TRIANGULATED.

### Key positions

**On Q 51:1-4 oath-cluster**:
- al-Bāqillānī's distinctive **integrative reading** (the four oath-elements as four cosmic-stages of one process). He argues this is the *iʿjāz-balagha* reading: the oath-elements are not arbitrary but follow a *naẓm* (compositional logic) wherein each *fa-l-* introduces the next stage in cosmic causation.
- This is the principal alternative to al-Ṭabarī's 4-distinct-subjects reading.

**On Q 51:24-37 (the hospitality-narrative)**:
- al-Bāqillānī cites Q 51's compressed hospitality account as an exemplar of *bayān al-iʿjāz* — the corpus's distinctive ability to compress dramatic narrative without losing rhetorical effect. He compares the 14-verse Q 51:24-37 account to Q 11:69-83 (the longer Hud version) and notes Q 51's *ījāz* (compression) as the rhetorical achievement.

**On Q 51:21 *afalā tubṣirūn***:
- The rhetorical-question form (*afalā* + verb) is one of the corpus's signature *istinkārī* (denial-implicit-question) constructions; al-Bāqillānī catalogs ~20 corpus-instances and reads Q 51:21 as the most-effective due to the inner-anthropic depth.

## 8. al-Biqāʿī (d. 885/1480) — *Naẓm al-Durar fī Tanāsub al-Āyāt wa-l-Suwar*

al-Biqāʿī's *Naẓm al-Durar* on Q 51 is in vol. 18 of the Hyderabad Dar al-Maʿārif edition (1969-1984) pp. 459-512. Citations PENDING for full pagination.

### Key positions

**On the Q 50 → Q 51 munāsabah**:
- Q 50 Qāf closes with *fa-dhakkir bi-l-qurʾāni* (v. 45) — "remind by the Quran."
- Q 51 al-Dhāriyāt opens with *wa-l-dhāriyāti dharwā* — the cosmic-oath as the **content of the reminder**.
- al-Biqāʿī reads the transition as: Q 50 establishes *al-dhikr* (the reminder); Q 51 begins the *substantive content* of that reminder with the cosmic-oath.

**On the Q 51 → Q 52 munāsabah**:
- Q 51 closes with *fa-waylun li-lladhīna kafarū min yawmihim alladhī yūʿadūn* (v. 60) — "woe to those who disbelieve from their day they are promised."
- Q 52 al-Ṭūr opens with *wa-l-ṭūri / wa-kitābin masṭūrin / fī raqqin manshūrin / wa-l-bayti al-maʿmūr / wa-l-saqfi al-marfūʿ / wa-l-baḥri al-masjūr / inna ʿadhāba rabbika la-wāqiʿ / mā lahu min dāfiʿ* (vv. 1-8) — closing also on a *waʿid*-jawāb (eschatological-promise).
- al-Biqāʿī reads the Q 51→Q 52 transition as a **redoubling** of the eschatological-promise: Q 51 closes with the wail; Q 52 opens with the **same eschatological-reality** under a fresh oath. The two surahs are essentially **paired oath-eschatology pieces** within the mid-Meccan oath-cluster.

This al-Biqāʿī reading of the Q 51→Q 52 munāsabah is **EMPIRICALLY VINDICATED** by H-NEW-720 canonical-adjacency-cost: Q 51→Q 52 has delta_raw = 0.0096, **rank 18/113** (cheapest 16% of corpus adjacencies). The transition is among the corpus's smoothest.

## 9. Modern critical scholarship

For comparative reference (not classical authorities; cited as pure SECONDARY):

- **Theodor Nöldeke** (*Geschichte des Qorāns*, 1860/1909): assigns Q 51 to **Period 2 (Middle-Meccan)** with rank ~67 in his chronology — consistent with classical al-Suyūṭī ranking.
- **Angelika Neuwirth** (*Studien zur Komposition der mekkanischen Suren*, 1981): classifies Q 51 as a "Middle-Meccan oath-narrative-frame surah" with the 5-block structure broadly matching the present analysis.
- **Toshihiko Izutsu** (*Ethico-Religious Concepts in the Qurʾān*, 1966): notes Q 51:56 as the corpus's most-direct statement of the *ʿibādah-as-fiṭra* doctrine.
